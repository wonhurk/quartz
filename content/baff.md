**BAFF** (B-cell Activating Factor) is a soluble cytokine essential for B-cell survival and innate immune activation, functioning as a critical regulator of adaptive immunity in both health and autoimmune disease. In autoimmune kidney disease—particularly [[iga-nephropathy]] and [[lupus-nephritis]]—BAFF becomes pathologically dysregulated, frequently in cooperation with [[april|a proliferation-inducing ligand (APRIL)]], driving aberrant B-cell responses and chronic glomerular inflammation.¹ The dual BAFF and APRIL antagonist [[telitacicept]] has emerged as a validated therapeutic strategy for BAFF-mediated kidney disease, achieving substantial proteinuria reduction and restoring immune homeostasis through mechanisms that extend beyond simple B-cell depletion to encompass expansion of regulatory B-cell populations and suppression of pro-inflammatory innate lymphoid cells.²³

---

## Mechanism

BAFF exerts its effects primarily through receptor engagement on B lymphocytes and innate immune cells, providing critical survival signals that sustain humoral immune responses. The cytokine functions cooperatively with [[april]], its structural homolog, which shares overlapping receptor specificity and amplifies B-cell activation and antibody class-switching in response to antigenic challenge.¹ In autoimmune kidney disease, dysregulation of BAFF—manifested as either increased production or aberrant signaling—leads to expansion of autoreactive B-cell clones and production of pathogenic autoantibodies. Critically, BAFF dysregulation in [[iga-nephropathy]] and [[lupus-nephritis]] is not merely a secondary consequence of disease but an active driver of pathogenesis, sustaining the B-cell and innate immune responses that perpetuate glomerular inflammation.

---

## IgA nephropathy pathogenesis

[[iga-nephropathy]] represents the most prevalent primary glomerulonephritis globally, characterized by dominant IgA deposition in the glomerulus and progressive renal function decline. The pathogenesis of IgA nephropathy is mediated by dysregulation of the BAFF-[[april]] signaling axis, which drives expansion and activation of autoreactive B cells that produce IgA-containing immune complexes deposited in the kidney.¹ Recognition of BAFF's central pathogenic role has led to development of [[telitacicept]], a fusion protein that simultaneously neutralizes both BAFF and APRIL, effectively interrupting the cooperative signaling sustaining disease-driving immune responses.

Clinical trials demonstrate the efficacy of telitacicept monotherapy in IgA nephropathy, achieving a mean 24-hour proteinuria reduction of 71.4% from baseline at 6 months.² Combination therapy with [[glucocorticoids]] substantially enhances this effect, yielding a mean proteinuria reduction of 84.1% at 6 months, indicating synergistic benefit from dual immunosuppression.² Telitacicept-treated patients also demonstrate reductions in serum IgA and IgG levels, reflecting suppression of the pathogenic antibody response, alongside improvements in hematuria and significant decreases in urinary soluble CD163, a biomarker of disease activity and macrophage-driven kidney inflammation.²

## Lupus nephritis and immune modulation

[[lupus-nephritis]] represents a serious systemic manifestation of systemic lupus erythematosus in which BAFF dysregulation contributes to pathogenesis through promotion of B-cell survival and aberrant self-reactive antibody production. Beyond conventional mechanisms of B-cell depletion, BAFF-pathway inhibition alters fundamental aspects of immune balance in lupus nephritis through two complementary mechanisms.³ First, therapeutic BAFF blockade expands populations of regulatory B cells, which suppress inflammatory responses through interleukin-10 and transforming growth factor-beta production and provide critical immune tolerance functions.³ Second, BAFF inhibition concurrently suppresses type 1 innate lymphoid cells, pro-inflammatory innate effectors that drive kidney injury through interferon-gamma production and amplify both adaptive and innate inflammatory responses.³ This dual mechanism—simultaneous expansion of tolerogenic regulatory B cells coupled with suppression of pro-inflammatory innate lymphoid cells—reveals that BAFF-pathway inhibition operates through immunomodulatory principles broader than B-cell depletion alone, potentially explaining its durable clinical benefits and disease-modifying properties in autoimmune kidney disease.

---

## Telitacicept: dual BAFF and APRIL antagonism

[[telitacicept]] represents a mechanistically distinct therapeutic approach to BAFF-driven autoimmune disease by simultaneously neutralizing both BAFF and its cooperative ligand [[april]].¹² This dual-target strategy provides superior disease control compared to BAFF-alone inhibition because it blocks the redundant pathways through which both cytokines sustain pathogenic B-cell responses and innate immune activation. In clinical practice across IgA nephropathy trials, telitacicept demonstrates robust proteinuria reduction with favorable safety profiles, supporting its use as either monotherapy or in combination with conventional immunosuppressive agents such as [[glucocorticoids]].² The flexibility of telitacicept dosing and its compatibility with existing therapies enable personalized treatment strategies adapted to individual patient responses and baseline disease severity.
