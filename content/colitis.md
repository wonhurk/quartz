**Colitis** is an inflammatory bowel disease characterized by colon inflammation, epithelial barrier dysfunction, and dysregulation of innate and adaptive immunity. Intestinal homeostasis is essential for systemic health and longevity; its disruption precipitates both local and systemic inflammation while compromising tissue integrity and regenerative capacity.¹ The disease pathophysiology centers on dysfunctional [[macrophage]] infiltration and pro-inflammatory polarization, accompanied by loss of epithelial [[tight-junctions]] and dysbiotic microbiota shifts that deplete butyrate-producing commensals. Recent therapeutic innovations target colitis through mechanistically distinct pathways: dietary N-acetylneuraminic acid (Neu5Ac) supplementation preserves epithelial barrier integrity and repolarizes [[macrophage|macrophages]] toward tissue-reparative phenotypes while selectively enriching butyrate-producing bacteria; inhibition of [[ptpro|protein tyrosine phosphatase receptor type O (PTPRO)]] suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokine production through [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] pathway modulation; and in vivo functional engineering of colonic epithelial cells via [[lipid-nanoparticles]]-delivered mRNA enhances their [[efferocytosis]] to accelerate inflammation resolution.

---

## Mechanism

Colitis arises from a cascade of epithelial and immune dysfunction. The intestinal epithelium forms a critical barrier separating luminal antigens and microbiota from the systemic circulation; loss of this barrier — marked by decreased expression of [[tight-junctions]] — permits bacterial translocation, aberrant antigen sampling, and mucosal immune hyperactivation.¹ Concurrently, dysbiotic alterations deplete populations of butyrate-producing bacteria, reducing the short-chain fatty acid production that sustains colonocyte metabolism and epithelial regeneration. The resulting inflammatory cascade — characterized by elevated IL-6, interferon-gamma, and interleukin-2 — perpetuates epithelial damage and establishes a pathogenic feed-forward loop. Infiltrating [[macrophage|macrophages]] adopt pro-inflammatory (M1-like) phenotypes while tissue-reparative macrophage populations are depleted, further impairing epithelial regeneration and resolution of inflammation. This epithelial-immune dysfunction underpins the clinical manifestations of colitis and guides emerging therapeutic strategies.

## Dietary barrier preservation

Oral N-acetylneuraminic acid (Neu5Ac) supplementation addresses colitis through multi-level targeting of pathogenic mechanisms. Neu5Ac, a sialic acid enriched in bird's nest and human milk, directly reinforces epithelial barrier integrity by increasing the abundance of [[tight-junctions]] and preserving mucosal architecture.¹ At the immune level, Neu5Ac suppresses [[macrophage]] infiltration into the colonic lamina propria and actively repolarizes resident macrophages away from the pro-inflammatory phenotype toward tissue-reparative states. Critically, Neu5Ac selectively enriches butyrate-producing bacterial taxa — including Butyricimonas synergistica and Parabacteroides goldsteinii — thereby increasing fecal [[butyrate]] levels without globally perturbing overall microbial diversity.¹ These coordinated anti-inflammatory effects are mechanistically mediated through the [[hif-1|HIF-1 signaling pathway]], which is activated by Neu5Ac and suppresses pro-inflammatory gene expression in infiltrating immune cells. In both natural aging and dextran sulfate sodium-induced colitis models, Neu5Ac supplementation restores intestinal homeostasis, reduces senescence-associated epithelial damage, and preserves the tissue's intrinsic regenerative and barrier-maintenance functions.

## PTPRO-targeted immunomodulation

[[ptpro|Protein tyrosine phosphatase receptor type O (PTPRO)]] has emerged as a central regulator of macrophage activation and the pro-inflammatory cytokine response in colitis. Selective [[ptpro]] inhibitors, exemplified by benzofuran-2-carboxylic acid derivatives such as compound 10j, potently suppress IL-6 production through modulation of the [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] signaling pathway in macrophages.² These compounds exhibit favorable oral bioavailability, enabling systemic delivery and sustained in vivo efficacy. In murine dextran sulfate sodium-induced colitis models, compound 10j significantly attenuated disease severity, restored normal colon length, and suppressed interferon-gamma and interleukin-2 levels — key markers of pathogenic Type 1 and Type 2 T cell responses.² Further structural optimization yielded compound 30k, which combines enhanced potency (IC50 = 0.16 μM) with superior in vivo efficacy and an improved toxicological profile. These [[ptpro]] inhibitors establish a new pharmacological class for phosphatase-targeted immunomodulation and represent a distinct therapeutic strategy for inflammatory bowel disease.

## Epithelial cell engineering

Functional in vivo editing of colonic epithelial cells represents a mechanistically novel therapeutic approach that directly targets the epithelial defects underlying colitis. This strategy employs [[lipid-nanoparticles]] (LNPs) to deliver mRNA encoding factors that enhance the [[efferocytosis|efferocytic]] capacity of epithelial cells — their intrinsic ability to internalize apoptotic immune cells. Enhanced epithelial [[efferocytosis]] promotes inflammation resolution and restoration of tissue homeostasis by clearing apoptotic leukocytes before they release pro-inflammatory contents into the lamina propria.³ In murine colitis models, intraperitoneal administration of mRNA-loaded nanoparticles specifically designed to boost epithelial [[efferocytosis]] markedly attenuated intestinal inflammation and halted disease progression.³ This approach addresses a critical therapeutic deficit in existing inflammatory bowel disease treatments: insufficient precision and efficacy. By engineering epithelial cells to actively promote inflammation resolution rather than broadly inhibiting individual inflammatory mediators, this strategy restores the tissue's intrinsic capacity for homeostatic regeneration.

## Aging and intestinal dysfunction

Intestinal homeostasis and barrier function decline with advancing age, contributing to age-related gut dysfunction and increased susceptibility to inflammatory disease. The epithelial barrier becomes progressively compromised, [[tight-junctions]] decline, and the microbiota shifts toward dysbiotic compositions enriched in pro-inflammatory taxa.¹ These age-associated changes elevate baseline systemic inflammation and impair the resolution of acute colonic injury, creating a microenvironment permissive to colitis development. Importantly, oral Neu5Ac supplementation preserves epithelial barrier integrity in natural aging models, suggesting that dietary supplementation with barrier-protective sialic acids can counteract age-related intestinal dysfunction and reduce age-related colonic inflammation.¹ By maintaining epithelial regenerative capacity and suppressing senescence-associated epithelial damage, Neu5Ac-based interventions may offer a preventive or therapeutic strategy for maintaining intestinal health and reducing colitis risk in aging populations.
