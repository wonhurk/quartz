**Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC)** is the most prevalent thyroid malignancy, characterized by heterogeneous clinical behavior ranging from indolent to aggressive metastatic disease.¹ Recent multi-omics profiling has identified [[lactate]] as a critical metabolic driver of PTC progression, operating through lactylation of [[cpt1a|carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1A (CPT1A)]], the rate-limiting enzyme in fatty acid β-oxidation (FAO).¹ Environmental factors, particularly [[iodine-131]] exposure from nuclear accidents, establish distinct molecular signatures that stratify carcinogenic risk independently of exposure history.²

---

## Mechanism

The [[lactate]]-[[cpt1a]] axis amplifies fatty acid β-oxidation to fuel PTC cell migration and metastatic dissemination. Exogenous lactate enhances PTC cell migration in a [[cpt1a]]-dependent manner through a dual-pronged molecular mechanism.¹ First, lactate promotes histone H3K18 lactylation, a post-translational modification that upregulates [[cpt1a]] transcription. Simultaneously, lactate stabilizes [[cpt1a]] protein via direct lactylation at lysine residues K180 and K285, preventing its ubiquitin-proteasomal degradation and prolonging its functional half-life in the cell.¹ This coordinate regulation of both [[cpt1a]] expression and protein stability synergistically amplifies fatty acid catabolism, enabling cancer cells to extract metabolic energy from lipid substrates at accelerated rates. Genetic or pharmacological inhibition of [[cpt1a]] abolishes lactate-driven migration and FAO activity, establishing the enzyme as essential for lactate-mediated progression.¹

---

## Radiation-induced etiology

Papillary thyroid carcinomas arising from [[iodine-131]] exposure exhibit molecularly distinct etiological signatures compared to sporadic PTCs. Analysis of tumors from Chornobyl-exposed individuals stratified by thyroid radiation dose—high exposure (>0.5 Gy) versus low exposure (<50 mGy)—combined with their matched normal thyroid tissues, revealed that exposure history imprints characteristic miRNA and transcriptomic signatures on both tumor and normal tissue.² Using partial least squares discriminant analysis (sPLS-DA), radiation-exposed normal tissues and PTCs shared common multi-omic signatures independent of the carcinogenic process itself, enabling separation of exposure markers from carcinogenic mechanisms.² This stratified molecular approach demonstrates that [[iodine-131]] creates dose-dependent tissue remodeling, with distinct transcriptional and epigenetic landscapes across the radiation spectrum. These findings provide a framework for improved risk stratification in radiation-exposed populations and potential early detection strategies.

---

## Metabolic biomarkers and in vivo progression

Metastatic PTCs display elevated [[lactate]] levels and activated fatty acid β-oxidation, both identifiable through multi-omics profiling and validated in TCGA datasets.¹ The [[lactate]]-[[cpt1a]] lactylation axis functions as a critical metabolic vulnerability in vivo. Lactylation-deficient [[cpt1a]] mutants harboring K180R/K285R substitutions substantially attenuated lung metastasis and subcutaneous tumor growth in xenograft models, establishing that lactate-dependent [[cpt1a]] modification is necessary for full metastatic potential.¹ These findings position [[cpt1a]] lactylation as a therapeutic target for metabolic intervention, with potential applications in suppressing metastatic dissemination in aggressive PTC subtypes and enabling rational design of CPT1A-directed inhibitors to block the lactate-driven progression axis.
