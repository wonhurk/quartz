**CAR-T cell therapy** is a form of adoptive immunotherapy in which patient T cells are genetically engineered to express chimeric antigen receptors that recognize and eliminate cells bearing specific tumor-associated antigens. The chimeric receptor fuses an extracellular single-chain variable fragment (scFv) derived from a monoclonal antibody with intracellular T cell signaling domains, enabling major histocompatibility complex-independent tumor recognition and targeted cytotoxic responses.¹ Originally transformative in hematologic malignancies, CAR-T therapy has now demonstrated efficacy in solid tumors—including [[gbm|glioblastoma]], pancreatic cancer, and colorectal cancer—and is being extended to pediatric autoimmune diseases through [[cd19-car-t|CD19-directed]] constructs that induce durable remission by eliminating autoreactive B cells.

---

## Mechanism

CAR-T cells are generated by harvesting autologous T cells, transducing them with viral or [[nonviral-genome-editing|nonviral]] vectors encoding the chimeric receptor, expanding the modified population ex vivo, and infusing them back into the patient. Upon antigen engagement, the CAR triggers T cell activation, proliferation, cytokine secretion, and cytolytic killing via perforin and granzyme release. The specificity of the scFv domain determines target selectivity, while intracellular costimulatory domains—typically CD28 or 4-1BB—enhance persistence and memory formation.² Second- and third-generation CAR constructs incorporate these costimulatory signals to improve long-term efficacy, and emerging fourth-generation "armored" CARs secrete immune-modulatory cytokines to reshape the tumor microenvironment.

## Hematologic malignancies

CD19-directed CAR-T therapy has achieved transformative outcomes in B-cell malignancies. [[brexucabtagene-autoleucel|Brexucabtagene autoleucel (brexu-cel)]], a CD19 CAR-T product, demonstrates 78% complete remission rates in relapsed/refractory mantle cell lymphoma patients aged ≥70 years, with one-year overall survival of 74% and progression-free survival of 62%.² Importantly, efficacy is preserved in patients over 75 years, with Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status—not age—serving as the strongest independent predictor of outcomes. Cytokine release syndrome occurs in 80% of patients but is manageable, with grade ≥3 events in only 9%.²

To address antigen escape—a major resistance mechanism—bispecific CD19/20 CAR-T constructs simultaneously target both antigens, reducing the selective pressure that drives CD19-negative relapse.⁸ In [[bcma|BCMA]]-directed therapies for multiple myeloma, co-targeting CD74 mitigates immune evasion features and enhances CAR-T activity in preclinical models of relapsed/refractory disease.⁷ [[cd33|CD33]] CAR-T cells engineered with constitutively active [[rac1v12|Rac1 (Rac1V12)]] exhibit enhanced bone marrow infiltration and retention, achieving superior leukemia suppression and prolonged survival in xenograft models of acute myeloid leukemia.⁹

## Solid tumor applications

Solid tumors present unique challenges including antigen heterogeneity, immunosuppressive microenvironments, and physical barriers to T cell infiltration. [[upar|Urokinase plasminogen activator receptor (uPAR)]] has emerged as a promising target expressed on both glioblastoma tumor-initiating cells and tumor-associated macrophages. uPAR CAR-T cells eliminate both populations, inducing durable regressions across glioblastoma patient-derived xenografts.³ Beyond direct tumor cell killing, uPAR is upregulated in senescent, pro-fibrotic stromal cells; uPAR-directed CAR-T cells therefore dismantle the tumor-supporting niche by targeting uPAR-positive cancer-associated fibroblasts, eradicating systemic metastases and achieving complete responses in diverse solid tumor models enriched for TP53 and RAS pathway mutations.⁵

B7-H3 is broadly overexpressed across solid tumors including pancreatic cancer, neuroblastoma, and glioblastoma. Fully human antibody-derived B7-H3 CAR designs eliminate murine immunogenicity that can provoke immune-mediated rejection of earlier CAR-T products. The lead fully human binder Y111 demonstrates superior antitumor activity compared to humanized murine-derived constructs (376.96 and MGA271), inducing complete responses and significant survival benefits with excellent tolerability.¹⁰

In colorectal cancer, [[persister-tumor-cells|quiescent persister tumor cells (PTCs)]] survive neoadjuvant chemotherapy and orchestrate immunosuppressive microenvironments characterized by CD96⁺CD8⁺ T cell infiltration. [[epcam|EpCAM]]-targeted CAR-T cells deficient in CD96 expression robustly eliminate PTCs by diverting CD8⁺ T cells from exhaustion trajectories and promoting memory-like phenotypes through enhanced mitochondrial function.⁴

## Overcoming resistance

Antigen loss, CAR-T exhaustion, and tumor microenvironmental suppression drive therapeutic failure. Multispecific targeting addresses antigen heterogeneity: CD19/CD20 bispecific CAR-T cells spatially resolve tumor-intrinsic and microenvironmental determinants of response, mitigating the selective pressure that drives single-antigen escape.⁸ In multiple myeloma, CD74 co-targeting enhances BCMA CAR-T activity against residual disease with immune-escape features.⁷

Engineering strategies targeting T cell migration and persistence improve efficacy. Rac1V12 expression in CD33 CAR-T cells enhances cytoskeletal dynamics, promoting bone marrow retention and immunological memory phenotypes while reducing tonic signaling—a combination that prolongs T cell persistence and anti-tumor efficacy in acute myeloid leukemia models.⁹ CD96 knockout prevents CD8⁺ T cell exhaustion, enabling more effective elimination of chemotherapy-resistant persister populations in colorectal cancer.⁴

Immunogenicity of murine-derived scFvs can provoke host anti-CAR immune responses, leading to CAR-T rejection. Fully human antibody-based CAR designs eliminate residual foreign epitopes within variable domains, improving persistence and therapeutic durability.¹⁰

## Autoimmune diseases

CD19 CAR-T therapy is being repurposed for severe [[pediatric-autoimmune-diseases|pediatric autoimmune diseases]] by depleting autoreactive B cells. In a clinical case series, CD19 CAR-T treatment induced rapid and durable remissions with manageable toxicity; patients discontinued standard immunosuppressive therapies, and clinical benefits persisted even after B cell reconstitution.¹ This suggests a fundamental reset of the immune system rather than mere B cell depletion, positioning CAR-T therapy as a potentially curative intervention for refractory autoimmune conditions beyond oncology.

---

## Engineering innovations

In vivo CAR-T generation using [[mrna|mRNA]]-loaded [[113-aa-c8c14|lipid nanoparticles (LNPs)]] represents a paradigm shift from ex vivo manufacturing. The spleen-tropic ionizable lipid 113-AA-C8C14 achieves 57-fold higher mRNA expression in the spleen compared to benchmark selective organ-targeting formulations. When loaded with CD19 CAR mRNA, 113-AA-C8C14 LNPs generate 2.7-fold more CAR-T cells in vivo, accompanied by 2.9-fold and 3.7-fold increases in interferon-γ and tumor necrosis factor-α secretion, respectively, resulting in 2.3-fold greater tumor growth inhibition in pancreatic cancer models.⁶ This approach eliminates the need for apheresis, ex vivo culture, and cryopreservation, potentially democratizing access to CAR-T therapy.

Constitutively active Rac1 engineering enhances CAR-T migration into protective bone marrow niches where leukemia cells evade therapy. Rac1V12 CD33 CAR-T cells demonstrate transwell migration-dependent cytotoxicity in vitro and achieve superior leukemia suppression with prolonged survival in vivo by promoting immunological memory phenotypes and reducing tonic signaling.⁹
