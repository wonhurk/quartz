**YAP (Yes-Associated Protein)** is a transcriptional coactivator that functions as the central downstream effector of the Hippo signaling pathway, orchestrating diverse biological programs including cell fate determination, tissue regeneration, tumor progression, and immune microenvironment remodeling. Its activity is governed by [[lats|LATS kinase]]-mediated phosphorylation at Ser127, which promotes cytoplasmic sequestration and functional inactivation, yet YAP frequently escapes this negative regulation in cancer through post-translational modifications—most notably symmetrical dimethylation at arginine 124 (R124me2s) catalyzed by [[prmt5|protein arginine methyltransferase 5 (PRMT5)]].¹ This methylation event shields YAP from inhibitory phosphorylation, enabling nuclear translocation and sustained transcriptional output that drives tumorigenicity, metabolic addiction, and therapy resistance across multiple malignancies.

---

## Mechanism

YAP activation is canonically suppressed by the Hippo pathway through [[lats|LATS]]-mediated phosphorylation at conserved serine residues within HXRXXS motifs, which mark YAP for cytoplasmic retention and degradation. However, [[prmt5|PRMT5]]-catalyzed symmetrical dimethylation at arginine 124—positioned within one of five conserved LATS-targeting motifs—serves as a physiological protective mechanism that blocks LATS-mediated inhibitory phosphorylation at Ser127.¹ This R124me2s modification is promoted by [[methionine]] availability and establishes a feedforward amplification loop wherein active YAP transcriptionally upregulates [[prmt5|PRMT5]] itself alongside the [[methionine]] transporters [[slc3a2|SLC3A2]] and [[slc7a5|SLC7A5]], thereby entrenching metabolic dependency and sustained YAP signaling.¹ In non-small cell lung cancer, [[protein-kinase-c-iota|protein kinase Cι (PKCι)]] acts as an upstream activator of YAP1, driving its transcriptional program to orchestrate chemokine secretion and immune evasion.⁴

---

## Cancer applications

YAP hyperactivation is a hallmark of multiple aggressive malignancies and confers broad resistance to standard-of-care therapies. In glioblastoma, [[prmt5|PRMT5]]-dependent YAP methylation drives tumor [[methionine]] addiction and confers radioresistance by upregulating the [[methionine]] transporter axis [[slc3a2|SLC3A2]]/[[slc7a5|SLC7A5]], which sustains methionine supply and enables continued YAP signaling under radiation stress.¹ Combination therapy targeting PRMT5-YAP methylation alongside radiotherapy impairs intracranial xenograft growth and significantly extends survival in preclinical models.¹

In ovarian cancer, YAP1 suppresses ferroptosis and promotes cisplatin resistance through transcriptional activation of [[gls1|GLS1]], a metabolic enzyme governing glutaminolysis and glutathione biosynthesis.² This YAP1/[[gls1|GLS1]] axis is further reinforced by ZDHHC14-mediated S-palmitoylation of the transcription factor RUNX2, which enhances its stability and drives YAP1 expression in a feedforward regulatory circuit.² Disruption of RUNX2 palmitoylation restores ferroptosis sensitivity and chemotherapy response both in vitro and in vivo.²

---

## Immunotherapy resistance

YAP orchestrates immunosuppression through multiple mechanisms, most notably in non-small cell lung cancer where high YAP expression correlates with resistance to anti-PD-1 checkpoint blockade. Mechanistically, [[protein-kinase-c-iota|PKCι]] stabilizes YAP1-dependent transcription of the macrophage-recruiting chemokine [[ccl7|CCL7]], which promotes tumor-associated macrophage (TAM) infiltration while suppressing CD8+ T cell accumulation.⁴ Pharmacological inhibition of [[protein-kinase-c-iota|PKCι]] with auranofin disrupts the PKCι-YAP1-[[ccl7|CCL7]] axis, reduces TAM infiltration, promotes CD8+ T cell recruitment, and restores sensitivity to anti-PD-1 therapy in preclinical models.⁴ Combination therapy with auranofin and anti-PD-1 antibodies triggers robust CD8+ T cell-dependent antitumor immunity and controls tumor growth in multiple transplantable models.⁴

---

## Non-cancer contexts

Beyond oncology, modulation of YAP activity holds therapeutic promise in regenerative medicine. In postmenopausal osteoporosis, inhibition of upstream Hippo pathway components by the natural alkaloid [[dendrobine]] enhances YAP nuclear translocation in bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells, promoting coupled osteogenic and angiogenic differentiation programs that accelerate bone repair.³ [[Dendrobine]] treatment suppresses apoptosis, upregulates osteogenic markers Runx2, OCN, and OPN, and stimulates angiogenic factor secretion including VEGF, MMP2, and MMP9, resulting in improved bone mineral density and microarchitecture in ovariectomized rat models.³

Conversely, YAP downregulation is therapeutically beneficial in fibrotic wound healing contexts. In diabetic wound repair, a dual pH/ROS-responsive [[gt-pba-egcg-opu-tob-pdlscs-hydrogel|hydrogel encapsulating periodontal ligament stem cells]] markedly reduces scar formation by downregulating the profibrotic gene YAP alongside En1, facilitating regenerative rather than fibrotic healing and achieving accelerated wound closure with minimal scarring in diabetic animal models.⁵
