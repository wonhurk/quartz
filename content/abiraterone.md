**Abiraterone** is an androgen-synthesis inhibitor used in the treatment of metastatic prostate cancer across hormone-sensitive and castration-resistant disease states. The drug functions by potently inhibiting the CYP17A1 enzyme, blocking the conversion of pregnenolone and 17-hydroxypregnenolone to their 17,20-lyase products, thereby suppressing intratesticular and adrenal androgen production. Abiraterone is characteristically administered with prednisone, a glucocorticoid required to mitigate mineralocorticoid-related toxicities, and represents a cornerstone of early-line androgen-deprivation therapy in metastatic disease.¹

---

## Mechanism and pharmacology

Abiraterone inhibits the 17α-hydroxylase and 17,20-lyase activities of CYP17A1, a key enzyme in the androgen biosynthetic pathway. This dual inhibition prevents the conversion of C21 steroids (pregnenolone and 17-hydroxypregnenolone) into the C19 androgens — testosterone and androstenedione — that drive prostate cancer cell growth. Unlike castration, which reduces testicular androgen production, abiraterone additionally suppresses adrenal synthesis of androgens, lowering total circulating testosterone to castrate levels even in patients with chemically or surgically castrated baseline status. The concurrent administration of prednisone serves a dual purpose: it replaces the cortisol lost via CYP17A1 inhibition and suppresses the secondary rise in ACTH-driven adrenal steroid synthesis that otherwise occurs, preventing symptoms related to mineralocorticoid excess.¹

---

## Treatment of metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer

Abiraterone is a cornerstone of early-line therapy for [[metastatic-hormone-sensitive-prostate-cancer|metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC)]]. Emerging evidence demonstrates that combining abiraterone with [[olaparib]], a poly-ADP-ribose polymerase (PARP) inhibitor, improves outcomes in mHSPC patients harboring homologous recombination repair (HRR) gene mutations. In a phase II trial, olaparib plus abiraterone and prednisone was evaluated in this molecularly defined subset, offering a strategy to target both androgen signaling and DNA repair deficiency simultaneously.² This combination approach reflects a growing paradigm of precision oncology in prostate cancer, where pharmacogenomic stratification — particularly HRR status — guides therapeutic decision-making to optimize disease control.

---

## Castration-resistant disease and progression

While abiraterone provides initial disease control in [[castration-resistant-prostate-cancer|metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC)]], patients inevitably develop progressive disease despite continued therapy. The combination of olaparib plus abiraterone and prednisone has demonstrated significantly prolonged radiographic progression-free survival in mCRPC patients, establishing PARP inhibition as a synergistic strategy in this heavily treated population.¹ Progression on abiraterone serves as a clinical milestone defining the transition to next-generation therapeutic approaches, including [[enzalutamide]] and [[prostate-specific-membrane-antigen|PSMA]]-targeted radioligand therapy. Patients who progress on prior abiraterone therapy are candidates for subsequent agents such as iodine-131-labeled PSMA-targeting compounds in combination with second-generation androgen receptor antagonists, exemplifying sequential treatment strategies in advanced disease.²
