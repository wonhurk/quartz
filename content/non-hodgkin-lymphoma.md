**Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL)** is a diverse group of malignant lymphoid neoplasms representing approximately 90% of all lymphoid malignancies worldwide.¹ A significant subset is driven by [[ebv|Epstein-Barr virus (EBV)]] infection, which propels highly aggressive disease with rapid therapeutic resistance and poor patient outcomes. Recent research has identified [[nek2|NEK2]], a cellular serine/threonine kinase, as a critical driver of EBV-positive NHL pathogenesis through direct upregulation by the [[ebv|EBV]] latency proteins [[ebna1|EBNA1]], [[lmp1|LMP1]], and [[ebna2|EBNA2]].¹ [[nek2|NEK2]] inhibition selectively triggers cell death in lymphoma cells through reactive oxygen species accumulation and [[gasdermin-d|gasdermin D]] cleavage, while simultaneously restoring chemosensitivity by suppressing drug resistance transporters. For relapsed or refractory B-cell NHL, bispecific [[car-t|CAR-T]] cells simultaneously targeting [[cd19|CD19]] and [[cd20|CD20]] address antigen escape and enhance therapeutic persistence.²

## EBV-Associated Pathogenesis

EBV-positive NHL represents a biologically distinct subset of non-Hodgkin lymphomas characterized by aggressive disease progression and early treatment failure. The three major [[ebv|EBV]] latency proteins — [[ebna1|EBNA1]], [[lmp1|LMP1]], and [[ebna2|EBNA2]] — each independently upregulate the cellular kinase [[nek2|NEK2]], establishing a central oncogenic axis.¹ [[nek2|NEK2]] expression is markedly elevated in primary lymphocytes following [[ebv|EBV]] infection and is essential for growth and survival of EBV-positive NHL cells.¹ This kinase is necessary for maintaining the transformed phenotype, and its inhibition selectively induces cell death in malignant cells while sparing normal lymphoid populations.

[[nek2|NEK2]] inhibition triggers inflammatory cell death characterized by accumulation of reactive oxygen species and cleavage of [[gasdermin-d|gasdermin D]], a pyroptotic effector molecule.¹ This death pathway is particularly potent in [[ebv|EBV]]-driven disease because it disrupts both viral and cellular oncogenic programs. Expression of the viral oncoprotein [[lmp1|LMP1]] and the cellular oncogene [[myc|c-MYC]] are both suppressed following [[nek2|NEK2]] inhibition, indicating that this kinase governs a convergent pathway critical to [[ebv|EBV]]-positive lymphoma maintenance.¹

## Drug Resistance and NEK2-Mediated Chemosensitization

Drug resistance remains a central obstacle in [[ebv|EBV]]-positive NHL, arising through both intrinsic tumor factors and upregulated drug efflux mechanisms. The multidrug resistance-associated protein 1 ([[mrp1|MRP1]]) serves as the most active drug resistance transporter in EBV-positive NHL, actively pumping chemotherapeutics from malignant cells and enabling rapid drug escape.¹ [[nek2|NEK2]] expression drives upregulation of [[mrp1|MRP1]] and other cellular drug transporters, contributing to early treatment failure and disease progression. [[nek2|NEK2]] inhibition reduces both the expression and activity of cellular drug resistance transporters including [[mrp1|MRP1]], thereby resensitizing lymphoma cells to chemotherapy and overcoming acquired resistance.¹ This dual mechanism — direct induction of cell death through gasdermin-mediated pyroptosis combined with restoration of chemosensitivity — positions [[nek2|NEK2]] inhibition as a rational therapeutic strategy for overcoming treatment failure in [[ebv|EBV]]-positive NHL.

## Relapsed/Refractory B-Cell NHL and Bispecific CAR-T Strategy

Relapsed or refractory B-cell NHL represents a major clinical challenge despite the approval of [[cd19|CD19]]-directed [[car-t|chimeric antigen receptor T (CAR-T)]] cell therapies. Treatment failure frequently arises through antigen escape, whereby lymphoma cells downregulate or completely lose [[cd19|CD19]] surface expression, rendering them invisible to [[cd19|CD19]]-directed [[car-t|CAR-T]] cells.² Limited [[car-t|CAR-T]] persistence further contributes to disease relapse and poor long-term survival.² To address these mechanisms of resistance, bispecific [[cd19-cd20-car-t|CD19/CD20 CAR-T cells]] have been engineered to simultaneously target both [[cd19|CD19]] and [[cd20|CD20]] antigens, substantially reducing the probability of escape through loss or downregulation of a single target.² This dual-targeting strategy enhances [[car-t|CAR-T]] persistence and improves clinical outcomes in patients with relapsed or refractory B-NHL, overcoming limitations of single-antigen approaches.

Spatial transcriptomics has emerged as a powerful approach for understanding [[car-t|CAR-T]] response and resistance mechanisms within the lymphoma microenvironment. Spatially resolved single-cell transcriptomics identifies both tumor-intrinsic factors — such as [[cd19|CD19]] and [[cd20|CD20]] antigen density, metabolic state, and immunosuppressive phenotypes — and microenvironmental features including immune cell composition, T cell exhaustion state, and stromal support — that predict clinical response to [[car-t|CAR-T]] therapy.² These spatial biomarkers enable personalized patient stratification and optimize therapy selection, improving outcomes in relapsed or refractory disease.
