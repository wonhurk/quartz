**Conductive multifunctional hydrogels** are advanced biomaterials engineered to combine electrical conductivity with mechanical strength, biocompatibility, and responsive functionality for wound healing and wearable sensor applications. Two distinct design paradigms have emerged: a dopamine-functionalized [[reduced-graphene-oxide]]/[[alginate]] matrix enhanced with [[thymol]] and [[chitosan]] nanoparticles for antimicrobial wound dressing,¹ and a pearl-inspired dual-network polymer combining [[pva]], acrylic acid, [[gelatin]], [[tannic-acid]], and quaternary ammonium [[chitosan]] reinforced with [[acetylene-black]] for flexible sensing.² Both architectures achieve exceptional performance through mussel-inspired chemistry and nanoreinforcement strategies, delivering superior mechanical properties alongside real-time electrical responsiveness for clinical and wearable applications.

---

## Design and mechanism

Conductive multifunctional hydrogels employ two complementary engineering strategies to achieve their multifunctional capabilities. In the wound-dressing formulation, dopamine-grafted [[reduced-graphene-oxide]] (PDA-rGO) serves as the conductive bioadhesive matrix backbone, synthesized via Ca²⁺-mediated radial diffusion crosslinking with [[alginate]].¹ This foundational architecture is then loaded with [[thymol]] and [[chitosan]] nanoparticles to enhance antimicrobial potency and antioxidant capacity. The dopamine moiety imparts mussel-inspired adhesive properties essential for sustained contact with moist wound surfaces.

The alternative wearable-sensor design implements a dual-network architecture inspired by the brick-and-mortar assembly of pearl oyster shells, combining [[pva]], acrylic acid, [[gelatin]], and [[tannic-acid]] with quaternary ammonium [[chitosan]] reinforced by [[acetylene-black]] nanoparticles.² This polymer blend is manufactured via photopolymerization followed by solvent-exchange treatment to maximize both mechanical and electrical performance. The layered network architecture distributes stress across hierarchical scales, preventing catastrophic failure and enabling outstanding cyclic durability exceeding 2000 strain cycles even at frigid temperatures.

## Wound healing and antimicrobial applications

The dopamine-functionalized [[reduced-graphene-oxide]]/[[alginate]] hydrogel with [[thymol]]/[[chitosan]] nanoparticles (Ty/CsNPs@PDA-rGO/Alg) demonstrates exceptional promise for advanced antibacterial wound dressings, addressing both infection prevention and tissue repair.¹ In vitro analysis confirmed excellent hemocompatibility with hemolysis rates below 0.2%, establishing safety for direct blood contact during wound healing. Cellular biocompatibility exceeded 90% viability in standard assays, while bioadhesion strength reached approximately 8.26 kPa — sufficient for reliable adhesion to moist wound beds without excessive force during removal. The hydrogel drove robust cell migration in 3T3 fibroblasts at 71.41%, indicating promotion of re-epithelialization.

The [[thymol]]/[[chitosan]] nanoparticles confer superior antimicrobial activity critical for infected or contaminated wounds. The hydrogel exhibited inhibition zones of approximately 20 mm against [[staphylococcus-aureus]] and approximately 23 mm against [[e-coli]], alongside strong antioxidant activity exceeding 80% — mechanisms that suppress biofilm formation and accelerate wound clearance of pathogenic burden. The electrical conductivity of 3.95 mS·cm⁻¹ approximates human skin, enabling bioelectrical integration and potentially supporting endogenous electrical signaling during healing.

## Wearable sensor applications

The pearl-inspired gelatin/[[chitosan]]-reinforced hydrogel (PAGTH-AB) excels as a flexible, real-time motion-monitoring wearable sensor, combining mechanical robustness with rapid electrical responsiveness across extreme environmental conditions.² The hydrogel achieves a tensile strength of 5.49 MPa with elongation at break of 736% and toughness of 22.07 MJ/m³ — mechanical properties rivaling traditional elastomers — alongside a Young's modulus of 0.69 MPa permitting soft integration with skin. Electrical conductivity of 0.83 S/m enables sensitive detection with response times of 336 milliseconds, sufficient for real-time capture of dynamic human motions including finger, wrist, elbow, and knee joint articulation, as well as precise gesture recognition and accurate tracking of dynamic writing trajectories.

The sensor architecture maintains exceptional performance across diverse environmental stresses. Freeze resistance and superior water retention enable reliable operation in frigid conditions without mechanical degradation or loss of electrical sensitivity. Outstanding cyclic stability across 2000+ strain cycles ensures durability for long-term wearable deployment. The hydrogel demonstrates a linear sensor response over a broad strain range of 0–500%, establishing reliable calibration across diverse motion amplitudes and biomechanical contexts.

## Performance characteristics

Both hydrogel designs achieve superior mechanical properties through nanoreinforcement and architectural innovation. The wearable sensor variant achieves compressive strength and tensile performance exceeding conventional hydrogels, while the wound-dressing formulation exhibits a 3.5-fold increase in storage modulus (G') compared to the pristine [[alginate]] matrix alone.¹ Electrical conductivity in the wearable platform (0.83 S/m) enables rapid electrochemical transduction, while the wound-dressing formulation maintains biologically compatible conductivity (3.95 mS·cm⁻¹) that mirrors native tissue. Both formulations maintain high biocompatibility and demonstrate stability under physiological conditions, with the wearable variant showing exceptional durability under repeated mechanical and thermal cycling that would degrade conventional polymer-based sensors. The integration of functional additives — [[thymol]], [[tannic-acid]], and quaternary ammonium [[chitosan]] — provides multifunctional benefits spanning antimicrobial activity, mechanical reinforcement, and cell interaction without compromising electrical or mechanical performance.
