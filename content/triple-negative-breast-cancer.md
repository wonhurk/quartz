**Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)** is a highly aggressive breast cancer subtype defined by the absence of estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2 expression, resulting in limited druggable targets and substantially worse prognosis compared to other breast cancer subtypes.¹ Recent investigations have revealed that [[ferroptosis]] and [[cuproptosis]]—iron- and copper-dependent forms of regulated cell death—represent exploitable therapeutic vulnerabilities in TNBC, particularly when integrated with conventional chemotherapy and immune checkpoint blockade.²⁻⁴ The disease is characterized by high invasiveness, intrinsic chemotherapy resistance, and an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment that renders many patients refractory to single-agent therapies.⁵

---

## Molecular characteristics and resistance

TNBC cells exhibit remarkable adaptability to oncogene-induced replication stress and maintain proliferative capacity through overexpression of DNA damage response machinery.⁶ The catalytic subunit of RNase H2 (RNASEH2A), which removes misincorporated ribonucleotides from genomic DNA, is significantly overexpressed in TNBC tumors and correlates with poor survival outcomes.⁶ Type I protein arginine methyltransferases ([[tagln|PRMTs]]) similarly play crucial roles in maintaining TNBC proliferation by regulating epigenetic modifications essential for DNA replication and damage repair.⁷ Environmental pollutants including per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) have been causally linked to TNBC through dysregulation of [[cathepsin-d|Cathepsin D]], a lysosomal protease that promotes perineural invasion and epithelial-mesenchymal transition.⁸

---

## Ferroptosis-based therapy

Ferroptosis induction has emerged as a potent therapeutic approach in TNBC by exploiting vulnerabilities in iron metabolism and lipid peroxidation defense systems. The solute carrier family 7 member 5 (SLC7A5) functions as a ferroptosis-suppressing transporter; its pharmacological inhibition with [[jph203|JPH203]] effectively blocks leucine uptake, suppresses mTORC1 signaling, disrupts iron and redox homeostasis, and synergizes with mitochondrial reactive oxygen species to induce ferroptosis, achieving 81.2% tumor growth suppression in orthotopic 4T1 models.⁵ A complementary strategy employs [[sorafenib]], [[rhein]], and ferrocene co-delivered via glutathione-responsive disulfide-linked nanoprodrugs (SOR@RSSF), where ferrocene generates hydroxyl radicals through the Fenton reaction while sorafenib impairs cellular ferroptosis defense machinery, triggering synchronous lipid peroxide accumulation and glutathione depletion.¹ [[thioredoxin-reductase|Thioredoxin reductase]] (TrxR), which is overexpressed in TNBC cells, has been validated as a dual therapeutic target; TrxR-targeted Pt(IV) prodrugs simultaneously induce ferroptosis through glutathione depletion and lipid peroxidation while triggering DNA damage, demonstrating remarkable activity against both cisplatin-sensitive and cisplatin-resistant TNBC cells.⁴

## Cuproptosis and copper-based strategies

Cuproptosis represents an emerging therapeutic modality whose efficacy in TNBC is significantly enhanced when combined with Wnt/β-catenin pathway inhibition to overcome intrinsic tolerance mechanisms. Twinborn metallic polymer nanoparticles (NP(Pt-Cu)) engineered from copolymerized cuproptosis inducer (TriPy-Cu) and oxidized cisplatin enable on-demand release of copper ions and cisplatin, synergizing cuproptosis with chemotherapy while functioning as type II immunogenic cell death inducers.³ The Wnt/β-catenin pathway confers cuproptosis tolerance, but NP(Pt-Cu) suppresses this pathway via endoplasmic reticulum stress-triggered calcium overload, thereby reversing resistance.³ Copper peroxide nanoparticles embedded within biodegradable polymersomes similarly exploit copper-based Fenton-like reactions; upon reaching the acidic tumor microenvironment, CuO₂ decomposes to generate hydrogen peroxide and Cu²⁺ ions, producing robust reactive oxygen species that simultaneously suppress cytidine deaminase (enhancing [[compound-57|gemcitabine]] activation) and deplete glutathione.⁹

## Molecular targets and selective inhibitors

Multiple mechanistically diverse molecular targets have been validated through genetic and pharmacological approaches. [[usp1|Ubiquitin-specific protease 1 (USP1)]] regulates the DNA damage response by deubiquitinating PCNA; its inhibition with the scaffold-hopped compound 57 demonstrates nanomolar potency, favorable oral bioavailability (76% in rats), and synergistic antitumor activity when combined with the PARP inhibitor olaparib, resulting in enhanced DNA damage and significantly improved tumor growth inhibition compared to monotherapy.¹⁰ RNase H2 is overexpressed in TNBC cells that have escaped oncogene-induced senescence; genetic silencing or pharmacological inhibition of RNase H2 selectively impairs TNBC viability while sparing non-tumorigenic mammary epithelial cells by increasing replication stress, DNA damage, and cytosolic single-stranded DNA accumulation.⁶ Type I PRMTs represent another promising target; the oral bioavailable inhibitor SKLB06489 exhibits low nanomolar inhibition of PRMT1, PRMT6, and PRMT8 (IC₅₀ values 64.55, 4.21, and 51.27 nM respectively), suppressing TNBC proliferation by impairing DNA replication, compromising DNA damage repair, and inducing G0/G1-phase cell cycle arrest and apoptosis.⁷

## Immunogenic cell death and checkpoint synergy

A central therapeutic strategy involves triggering immunogenic cell death to convert immunosuppressive "cold" tumors into immunologically active "hot" tumors, thereby enhancing the efficacy of checkpoint blockade. Ferroptosis inducers targeting SLC7A5 promote dendritic cell maturation, activate CD8⁺ T-cell responses, and significantly improve survival outcomes when integrated with anti-PD-1/PD-L1 therapy.⁵ Similarly, biomimetic nanoplatforms comprising self-assembled rosmarinic acid and selenium ions coated with homologous cancer cell membranes (RA-Se@M) leverage immune evasion and enhanced tumor targeting while inducing potent apoptosis and robust immunogenic cell death characterized by calreticulin exposure, HMGB1 release, and dendritic cell maturation; when combined with anti-PD-1 therapy, RA-Se@M promotes profound intratumoral infiltration of cytotoxic T cells.¹¹ TrxR-targeted Pt(IV) prodrugs induce excessive accumulation of intracellular reactive oxygen species, triggering endoplasmic reticulum stress and subsequent immunogenic cell death, establishing a foundation for chemoimmunotherapy.⁴ RNase H2 inhibition triggers innate immune activation and upregulation of T cell-recruiting chemokines, synergizing with ATR inhibitors, PARP inhibitors, and immune checkpoint blockade.⁶

## Advanced nanoparticle delivery systems

Tumor microenvironment-responsive nanoplatforms have been engineered to overcome intracellular barriers and inefficient drug accumulation. Biodegradable, reactive oxygen species-responsive polymersomes functionalized with hyaluronic acid ([[ha-pgc|HA-PGC]]) target CD44-overexpressing TNBC cells and enable controlled release of [[compound-57|gemcitabine]] and copper peroxide nanoparticles, inducing oxidative stress that promotes immunogenic cell death and converts cold tumors into hot tumors.⁹ Surface-engineered fucoidan-docetaxel nanoparticles (FINAL) simultaneously target P-selectin-expressing cancer cells and tumor-associated macrophages, achieving dual-cell orchestration through enhanced cellular uptake, disruption of tumor-macrophage adhesion, promotion of M1 macrophage polarization, and suppression of VEGF-A-mediated angiogenesis, resulting in doubled survival duration in TNBC models.¹² Biomimetic lipid nanoparticles cloaked with [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|TNBC]] cell membrane components leverage both homotypic and heterotypic adhesion, exhibiting higher affinity for cancer-associated fibroblasts and enabling efficient RNA delivery to tumor microenvironment cells.¹³

---

## Combination therapy strategies

Synergistic approaches integrating multiple therapeutic modalities demonstrate substantially superior antitumor efficacy compared to monotherapy. Camptothecin-peptide conjugate nanoassemblies delivering JPH203 integrate active tumor targeting, enzyme-responsive disassembly, and mitochondrial reactive oxygen species generation to induce ferroptosis while concurrently engaging innate and adaptive immunity.⁵ USP1 inhibitors synergize with PARP inhibitors by enhancing DNA damage and suppressing tumor survival pathways.¹⁰ RNase H2 inhibition synergizes with ATR and PARP inhibitors while enhancing immune checkpoint blockade efficacy through increased replication stress and innate immune activation.⁶ The twinborn metallic polymer NP(Pt-Cu) addresses cuproptosis tolerance via Wnt pathway inhibition while providing potent chemo-immunotherapeutic activity.³ These combination strategies systematically address adaptive resistance mechanisms and tumor microenvironment complexity that undermine single-agent approaches.
