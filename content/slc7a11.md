**Solute Carrier Family 7 Member 11** (SLC7A11), also known as xCT, is a cystine/glutamate antiporter that mediates the import of cystine into cells for [[glutathione]] (GSH) synthesis, functioning as the primary gatekeeper of cellular antioxidant defense. In the context of [[ferroptosis]]—an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death—SLC7A11 acts as a major resistance factor, enabling tumor cells to maintain [[gpx4|glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4)]] activity and suppress lipid peroxidation-induced cell death. In hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) and other solid tumors with elevated baseline antioxidant systems, SLC7A11 overexpression drives intrinsic ferroptosis resistance, positioning the transporter as a compelling therapeutic target for overcoming treatment resistance and inducing [[immunogenic-cell-death|immunogenic cell death]].¹

---

## Mechanism

SLC7A11 catalyzes the Na⁺-independent symport of cystine and glutamate across the plasma membrane, maintaining continuous cystine availability for [[glutathione]] biosynthesis. The imported cystine is reduced to cysteine, the rate-limiting precursor for GSH synthesis via successive reactions catalyzed by γ-glutamylcysteine synthetase and glutathione synthetase.² Once synthesized, [[glutathione]] functions as the essential cofactor for [[gpx4|GPX4]], which reduces lipid hydroperoxides to their corresponding lipid alcohols, thereby blocking ferroptotic chain reactions. Elevated SLC7A11 expression establishes a self-sustaining antioxidant loop: high [[glutathione]] pools support maximal GPX4 activity, suppressing [[ferroptosis]] even under conditions of active iron-catalyzed lipid peroxidation. This mechanism proves especially potent in cells within [[hypoxia|hypoxic tumor microenvironments]], where limited oxygen availability and elevated lactate production favor GSH-dependent survival pathways over ferroptotic death.¹

---

## Ferroptosis resistance and therapeutic targeting

SLC7A11 upregulation in solid tumors represents a fundamental barrier to ferroptosis-based therapy. Suppression of SLC7A11—whether directly via nanoparticle-mediated knockdown or pharmacologically through [[metformin]]—depletes intracellular [[glutathione]] and sensitizes cells to ferroptotic collapse.¹ [[Metformin]] achieves dual therapeutic effect: inhibition of [[mitochondrial-complex-i|mitochondrial complex I]] alleviates tumor [[hypoxia]] by restoring adequate oxygen availability, while concurrent suppression of SLC7A11-mediated cystine uptake depletes the [[glutathione]] pool and disables the cell's primary ferroptosis defense mechanism.² 

Combined SLC7A11 suppression with ferroptosis induction demonstrates potent synergistic cytotoxicity across multiple therapeutic modalities. Iron nanocomposites loaded with ferroptosis inducers achieve substantial tumor growth inhibition in HCC by simultaneously downregulating both SLC7A11 and [[gpx4|GPX4]], while delivering photochemotherapy and photothermal therapy effects.¹ Lactate oxidase—an enzyme catalyzing intratumoral lactate into hydrogen peroxide—similarly requires SLC7A11 suppression for maximal ferroptotic efficacy, as elevated [[glutathione]] would otherwise neutralize the generated reactive oxygen species and prevent lipid peroxidation-driven cell death.²

---

## Immunotherapy synergy

SLC7A11 suppression combined with ferroptosis induction activates [[immunogenic-cell-death|immunogenic cell death]], a form of regulated cell death that primes adaptive immunity through the release of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) and tumor-associated antigens. Ferroptotic cells undergoing [[glutathione]] depletion and lipid peroxide accumulation release high concentrations of DAMPs that activate dendritic cells and promote [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cell]] infiltration into the tumor microenvironment. Disruption of antioxidant defenses via SLC7A11 downregulation amplifies this immunogenic signal, converting an otherwise immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment into one permissive for anti-tumor immunity.¹ Combination therapy targeting SLC7A11 and inducing ferroptosis alongside [[anti-pd-l1-antibody|anti-PD-L1 checkpoint blockade]] produces prolonged immunological memory and durable therapeutic responses in HCC xenograft models, overcoming the adaptive resistance mechanisms that limit checkpoint inhibitor monotherapy.¹
