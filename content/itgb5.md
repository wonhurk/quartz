**ITGB5** (integrin beta-5) is a cell-surface integrin subunit functioning as a critical node in extracellular matrix interactions and cancer progression across epithelial malignancies. In invasive lobular carcinoma (ILC), ITGB5 emerges as a synthetic lethal partner of [[cdh1]] and is suppressed by [[lox|lysyl oxidase (LOX)]] inhibition, disrupting a collagen-integrin-[[myc|MYC]] signaling axis that drives tumor expansion and metastatic seeding.¹ In gastric cancer, ITGB5 is transcriptionally activated by [[hif2a|HIF-2α]] under hypoxic conditions, becomes selectively enriched in tumor-derived exosomes, and directly promotes cancer progression and metastatic dissemination.²

---

## Mechanism

ITGB5 participates in a collagen-dependent signaling cascade essential to ILC biology. The protein forms functional integrin heterodimers that transduce signals from the collagen-rich extracellular matrix generated by elevated [[lox|LOX]] activity in CDH1-loss tumors. In this context, ITGB5 couples collagen engagement to downstream activation of [[myc|MYC]], NF-κB, and AP-1 transcriptional programs that orchestrate proliferation and invasive phenotypes.¹ 

In gastric cancer under hypoxic stress, ITGB5 expression is directly regulated at the transcriptional level through [[hif2a|HIF-2α]]-mediated promoter binding, as confirmed by chromatin immunoprecipitation.² Beyond its classical role in cell-matrix adhesion, ITGB5 undergoes selective enrichment in exosomal cargo released by hypoxic tumor cells, enabling paracrine signaling in the tumor microenvironment and facilitating metastatic dissemination.

---

## Invasive lobular carcinoma

Genome-wide CRISPR screening identified ITGB5 as a synthetic lethal partner of [[cdh1]] in ILC models, revealing an unexpected vulnerability created by loss of the CDH1 adhesion system.¹ When CDH1 is lost, ILC cells develop heightened dependency on [[lox|LOX]]-remodeled collagen and integrin signaling for survival and progression. The clinical pan-lysyl-oxidase inhibitor PXS-5505, currently in phase I/IIa trials for myelofibrosis, potently suppressed ITGB5 expression and halted tumor expansion and metastatic seeding across both estrogen receptor-positive and triple-negative ILC models.¹ PXS-5505 treatment remodeled fibrillar collagen and simultaneously downregulated ITGB5 alongside MYC, NF-κB, and AP-1 programs, identifying this collagen-integrin-signaling pathway as a tractable vulnerability in an understudied breast cancer subtype.

---

## Gastric cancer and hypoxia-driven progression

In gastric cancer, ITGB5 emerges as a hypoxia-responsive integrin regulated through direct transcriptional activation by [[hif2a|HIF-2α]].² Under hypoxic conditions, ITGB5 expression correlates positively with HIF-2α levels, with chromatin immunoprecipitation confirming direct binding of HIF-2α to the ITGB5 promoter.² Clinical analysis validated ITGB5 overexpression in gastric cancer tissues through RT-qPCR, western blot, and flow cytometry, establishing robust clinical correlation.² 

A striking finding was the selective enrichment of ITGB5 protein in exosomal cargo derived from hypoxic gastric cancer cells.² This exosomal ITGB5 likely functions in cell-cell communication, promoting cancer cell proliferation, invasion, and migration both in vitro and in vivo. The hypoxia-HIF-2α-ITGB5 axis thus represents a targetable driver of gastric cancer progression and metastatic dissemination, positioning ITGB5 as a candidate for intervention in this aggressive malignancy.

---

## Therapeutic strategies

Two complementary therapeutic approaches emerge from this biology. In ILC, [[lox|LOX]] inhibition via PXS-5505 directly downregulates ITGB5 expression and disrupts the collagen-integrin-[[myc|MYC]] axis, offering potential for single-agent or combination therapy with endocrine agents in window-of-opportunity trials.¹ In gastric cancer, modulation of the [[hif2a|HIF-2α]] pathway represents a means to suppress hypoxia-driven ITGB5 activation and its exosomal dissemination.² Because ITGB5 integrin function is intrinsically tied to extracellular matrix remodeling and paracrine signaling, combined targeting of integrin-collagen interactions alongside HIF-2α inhibition may prove synergistic in hypoxic tumors.
