**Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S)** is a gaseous signaling molecule emerging as a therapeutic agent across regenerative medicine and oncology, typically delivered through [[zinc-sulfide|pH-responsive zinc sulfide (ZnS) nanoparticles]] that enable controlled spatiotemporal release in pathological microenvironments. In chronic diabetic wounds, H₂S functions as a potent antioxidant, scavenging reactive oxygen species (ROS) and reducing inflammation to promote tissue regeneration within injectable hydrogel scaffolds.¹ In cancer, H₂S exhibits fundamentally different pro-oxidant properties as a key component of multi-modal therapies, disrupting mitochondrial respiration, destabilizing [[hif1a|hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α)]], and suppressing glycolytic metabolism to inhibit tumor growth and angiogenesis.² H₂S-releasing nanoplatforms integrate [[cuproptosis]], photothermal therapy, and immunomodulation to enhance therapeutic efficacy and overcome treatment resistance, particularly in hepatocellular carcinoma post-embolization settings.³

---

## Mechanism and Delivery

H₂S exerts context-dependent therapeutic effects through both antioxidant and pro-oxidant pathways, with its biological activity critically dependent on controlled delivery via bioengineered nanoplatforms. In wound healing, [[zinc-sulfide|ZnS nanoparticles]] release H₂S and Zn²⁺ ions in response to the acidic pH characteristic of chronic diabetic wounds, where H₂S acts as a potent ROS-scavenger, reducing oxidative stress that impairs cellular function.¹ The nanoparticles are typically encapsulated in methacrylated silk fibroin hydrogels—a biocompatible, injectable scaffold providing suitable mechanical strength, porosity, and biodegradability for in situ delivery and restoration of fibroblast and endothelial cell function.¹ In cancer, H₂S operates through metabolic reprogramming: it disrupts mitochondrial respiration, elevating intracellular oxygen and destabilizing [[hif1a]], thereby suppressing the hypoxia-driven transcription of [[vegf]] and glycolytic enzymes essential for tumor angiogenesis and glucose metabolism.² When integrated into cascade-responsive nanoplatforms, H₂S co-releases with Cu⁺ to enhance mitochondrial copper accumulation, triggering [[cuproptosis]] through inhibition of dihydrolipoamide S-acetyltransferase (DLAT) while downregulation of [[atp7a]] prevents Cu⁺ efflux and metabolic escape.²

## Diabetic Wound Healing

H₂S-releasing nanoparticles have demonstrated remarkable efficacy in accelerating closure of chronic diabetic wounds by restoring redox homeostasis and promoting tissue regeneration. In streptozotocin-induced diabetic mouse models, injectable silk fibroin hydrogels encapsulating pH-responsive ZnS nanoparticles significantly accelerated wound closure and enhanced the polarization of macrophages toward a reparative M2 phenotype, a critical shift toward pro-healing immunity.¹ The ZnS nanoparticles exhibited potent ROS-scavenging capacity in vitro and effectively restored fibroblast and endothelial cell function under oxidative stress, while the hydrogel's shear-thinning properties facilitated in situ application without requiring surgical implantation.¹ The system's tunable swelling behavior and biodegradability ensured sustained local H₂S delivery without systemic toxicity, positioning H₂S-releasing hydrogels as a functional regenerative platform superior to conventional static dressings that lack active redox modulation capacity.

## Cancer Therapy and Metabolic-Immune Reprogramming

H₂S has emerged as a versatile agent for overcoming treatment resistance in solid tumors, particularly when integrated into multi-modal nanoplatforms that coordinate metabolic disruption with immune activation. Cascade-responsive nanoparticles co-delivering H₂S and Cu⁺ simultaneously trigger [[cuproptosis]] while promoting photothermal therapy (PTT), tumor vascular normalization, and immune activation.² Within the tumor microenvironment, H₂S disrupts mitochondrial respiration and destabilizes [[hif1a]], suppressing the glycolytic reprogramming that sustains hypoxic tumors, while Zn²⁺ co-released from nanoparticles inhibits hexokinase and lactate dehydrogenase, depleting ATP and biosynthetic intermediates essential for tumor growth.² These metabolic disruptions synergize with H₂S-induced immunogenic cell death, which triggers dendritic cell maturation, robust cytotoxic T cell infiltration, and macrophage repolarization toward pro-inflammatory phenotypes, thereby enhancing the efficacy of [[pd1|anti-PD-1 checkpoint inhibitors]].²

## Hepatocellular Carcinoma Post-Embolization

Transarterial embolization (TAE) in hepatocellular carcinoma is limited by post-embolization hypoxia-driven angiogenesis and adaptive metabolic reprogramming that sustain residual tumor growth. pH-responsive gelatin microspheres encapsulating ZnS nanoparticles (ZnS@GMs) directly address this clinical challenge through dual-targeted suppression of the [[hif1a]]/[[vegf]] axis and glycolytic metabolism via localized H₂S and Zn²⁺ delivery.³ H₂S disrupts mitochondrial respiration, elevating intracellular oxygen and destabilizing HIF-1α, while Zn²⁺ inhibits glycolytic enzymes; together, these metabolic interventions suppress angiogenic signaling and normalize tumor vasculature, reducing the tissue hypoxia that normally drives escape from embolization.³ Critically, ZnS@GMs induce immunogenic cell death, triggering robust dendritic cell maturation and macrophage repolarization, thereby reversing the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment and synergizing with anti-PD-1 therapy to prevent recurrence.³ In rabbit orthotopic liver tumor models, intra-arterial infusion of ZnS@GMs demonstrated potent antitumor efficacy while maintaining effective embolic performance, establishing this platform as a multifunctional solution to TAE resistance in HCC.

## Formulation and Delivery Systems

The therapeutic utility of H₂S critically depends on controlled delivery to target tissues, as free H₂S gas is volatile and subject to rapid systemic metabolism. [[Zinc-sulfide|pH-responsive zinc sulfide nanoparticles]] serve as the primary platform, releasing H₂S specifically under acidic microenvironmental conditions while remaining chemically stable in neutral pH contexts, ensuring spatial restriction of therapeutic activity to diseased tissues.¹ Encapsulation in biocompatible polymers—including methacrylated silk fibroin for injectable wound healing applications¹ and gelatin microspheres for hepatic arterial delivery³—provides mechanical stability, appropriate biodegradation kinetics, and tunable release profiles tailored to tissue-specific needs. For cancer applications, cascade-responsive nanoplatforms incorporate multiple functional layers: a Cu₂O core serving as both a Cu⁺ source and near-infrared photothermal agent, a triphenylphosphine-functionalized chitosan shell for mitochondrial targeting and Cu⁺ stabilization, a dendritic mesoporous organosilica shell enabling GSH-responsive co-release of H₂S and Cu⁺, and an outer hyaluronic acid coating facilitating CD44-mediated tumor cell endocytosis.² These integrated engineering strategies ensure high tumor targeting, minimal off-target toxicity, and sustained local concentration sufficient to trigger multiple independent therapeutic mechanisms simultaneously—[[cuproptosis]], photothermal ablation, metabolic reprogramming, and immunogenic cell death.
