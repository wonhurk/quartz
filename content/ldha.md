**Lactate Dehydrogenase A (LDHA)** is a glycolytic enzyme catalyzing the conversion of pyruvate to lactate and serves as a critical metabolic hub linking glucose metabolism to epigenetic chromatin remodeling in cancer cells. In hyperglycolytic breast cancer, [[ldha|LDHA]] undergoes phosphorylation at tyrosine 239 by the [[ttk|TTK]] kinase, a modification orchestrated by the scaffolding protein [[sin1|SIN1 (MAPKAP1)]], which dramatically amplifies glycolytic flux and lactate accumulation.¹ The elevated lactate produced by p-LDHA^Tyr239 serves as a substrate for histone lactylation, particularly enriching histone H3K18 lactylation at the promoters of glucose transporters such as [[glut3|GLUT3]], thereby establishing a self-reinforcing metabolic-epigenetic feed-forward loop that sustains tumor metabolic addiction and invasion.¹ Beyond malignancy, [[ldha|LDHA]] participates in redox homeostasis maintenance during reproductive development, including oocyte maturation under hypoxic stress.

---

## Mechanism

[[ldha|LDHA]] catalyzes the terminal step of anaerobic glycolysis, converting pyruvate to lactate while regenerating NAD⁺ for continued glycolytic ATP production. In normoxic cancer cells, this reaction is amplified by post-translational phosphorylation. The [[ttk|TTK]] kinase, when recruited to [[ldha|LDHA]] through the scaffolding function of [[sin1|SIN1]], phosphorylates [[ldha|LDHA]] at tyrosine 239 (p-LDHA^Tyr239), a modification that enhances the enzyme's catalytic efficiency and lactate production capacity.¹ This phosphorylation-dependent amplification drives a profound elevation in intracellular lactate levels, which accumulates to concentrations sufficient to serve as a substrate for histone acetyltransferases and lactate-dependent histone lactylation machinery. [[hif-1-alpha|HIF-1α]] acts as a transcriptional activator of [[ldha|LDHA]] expression, positioning the enzyme as a central [[hif-1-alpha|HIF-1]] target gene in both normoxic and hypoxic cancer microenvironments.

---

## Metabolic-epigenetic axis in cancer

The phosphorylation of [[ldha|LDHA]] by [[ttk|TTK]] initiates a remarkable metabolic-epigenetic crosstalk axis that defines the most invasive breast cancer phenotypes. Accumulated [[ldha|LDHA]]-derived lactate is converted to acetyl-CoA and serves as a substrate for histone lactylation, particularly enriching histone H3K18 lactylation (H3K18la) at the promoters of glucose transporters.¹ [[glut3|GLUT3 (SLC2A3)]], a high-affinity glucose transporter critical for tumor cell glucose uptake, is directly upregulated by H3K18 lactylation at its promoter, creating a self-reinforcing SIN1/[[ttk|TTK]]/[[ldha|LDHA]]-H3K18la-[[glut3|GLUT3]] feed-forward loop.¹ This cycle amplifies both glycolytic flux and lactate production, establishing glucose and lactate as concurrent epigenetic signals that lock cancer cells into a hyperglycolytic state. Single-cell transcriptomic profiling reveals that [[sin1|SIN1]] is markedly co-enriched with epithelial-mesenchymal transition and proliferation programs in highly invasive breast tumors, positioning the [[sin1|SIN1]]/[[ttk|TTK]]/[[ldha|LDHA]] axis as a critical node linking metabolism to tumor aggression.¹

## Therapeutic targeting

The [[sin1|SIN1]]/[[ttk|TTK]]/[[ldha|LDHA]] complex represents a targetable node in cancer metabolism. Structure-function analyses and high-throughput virtual screening identified [[lr-90|LR-90]], a small-molecule inhibitor that directly disrupts the [[sin1|SIN1]]/[[ttk|TTK]]/[[ldha|LDHA]] scaffolding interaction.¹ Treatment with [[lr-90|LR-90]] reduces phosphorylated [[ldha|LDHA]] (p-LDHA^Tyr239), intracellular lactate levels, H3K18 lactylation occupancy at the [[glut3|GLUT3]] promoter, and glucose uptake, resulting in significant suppression of tumor cell invasion and xenograft growth.¹ Notably, [[lr-90|LR-90]] synergizes with standard chemotherapeutic agents in both patient-derived organoid models and mouse xenografts, suggesting therapeutic potential as a metabolic sensitizer to conventional treatment.¹

---

## Non-cancer contexts

Beyond oncology, [[ldha|LDHA]] plays a physiological role in maintaining redox homeostasis during developmental stages requiring profound metabolic remodeling. In porcine oocytes undergoing in vitro maturation under hypoxic stress, [[ldha|LDHA]] expression is regulated by the [[hif-1-alpha|HIF-1α]] pathway and contributes to the maintenance of intracellular redox balance critical for developmental competence.² Under normoxic conditions, suppression of [[hif-1-alpha|HIF-1α]]-target genes including [[ldha|LDHA]], alongside vascular endothelial growth factor and glucose transporters, can enhance oocyte developmental potential, suggesting context-dependent roles for [[ldha|LDHA]] in reproductive biology.²
