**Irreversible Electroporation (IRE)** is a non-thermal ablation technology that destroys tumors through precisely calibrated high-voltage electrical pulses while preserving adjacent neurovascular structures, avoiding the collateral tissue damage inherent to thermal ablation methods.¹ Beyond local tumor control, IRE triggers immunogenic cell death, initiating systemic antitumor immune responses detectable through comprehensive single-cell and T-cell receptor profiling in treated patients.¹ This dual mechanism—focal disease destruction coupled with durable immunological engagement—positions IRE as a tissue-sparing alternative to conventional radical surgery in organ-confined malignancies.

---

## Mechanism

IRE operates through application of electric fields of sufficient magnitude to overcome the transmembrane potential, disrupting cell membrane integrity and triggering apoptosis-independent cell death pathways. Unlike [[thermal-ablation|thermal ablation]], which generates heat-induced necrosis and risks thermal conduction injury to surrounding tissues, IRE preserves the extracellular matrix and vascular architecture, enabling rapid tissue remodeling and functional recovery.¹ The electrical energy induces cellular electroporation—transient or permanent loss of membrane impermeability—followed by irreversible cell death through osmotic imbalance and organellar dysfunction.

Critically, IRE-induced cell death liberates damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) and tumor-associated antigens that activate dendritic cells and prime both innate and adaptive immune responses. This immunogenic character distinguishes IRE from thermal methods and establishes a foundation for systemic antitumor immunity rather than isolated local control.

---

## Prostate cancer focal therapy

In [[prostate-cancer|prostate cancer]], IRE has emerged as a promising focal therapy reducing morbidity compared to radical prostatectomy while maintaining oncological efficacy. Radical prostatectomy, the gold standard for localized disease, carries well-documented complications including urinary incontinence, erectile dysfunction, and reduced quality of life due to surgical trauma and neurovascular disruption. IRE's preservation of the neurovascular bundles—critical for erectile function—and the urethral sphincter musculature substantially mitigates these complications.¹ For men with organ-confined, localized prostate cancer, focal IRE offers tissue-sparing tumor control without the functional deficits inherent to whole-gland surgical resection or extensive external-beam radiotherapy.

The selectivity of IRE application also permits iterative or multimodal treatment strategies, allowing salvage therapy in cases of recurrence while minimizing cumulative functional impairment—an advantage over radiation-based approaches where retreatment is constrained by normal tissue tolerance.

---

## Immunological mechanisms and systemic responses

IRE-treated patients exhibit significant peripheral immune remodeling detectable through high-resolution single-cell transcriptomics and T-cell receptor (TCR) sequencing before and after treatment.¹ Immunogenic cell death unleashed by IRE activates tumor-resident and draining lymph node-localized dendritic cells, licensing clonal expansion of tumor-specific [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cells]]. Characterization of systemic T-cell repertoires in IRE-treated prostate cancer patients reveals dynamic shifts in clonotype diversity and expansion of neoantigen-reactive T-cell populations, establishing a mechanistic link between local ablation and systemic immunological engagement.

This immune activation creates a platform for immunotherapy synergy. Patients undergoing IRE exhibit heightened responsiveness to concurrent or sequential [[immune-checkpoint-inhibitors|immune checkpoint blockade]], as the inflamed tumor microenvironment—generated by IRE's immunogenic cell death—primes dendritic cells and T cells for enhanced checkpoint inhibitor efficacy. The temporal dynamics of this immune priming remain an active area of clinical investigation, with ongoing studies mapping the optimal window for checkpoint inhibitor administration relative to IRE delivery.
