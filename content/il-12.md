**Interleukin-12 (IL-12)** is an immunostimulatory cytokine that orchestrates local and systemic antitumor immune responses through coordinated activation of innate and adaptive immunity via [[ifn-gamma|interferon-gamma (IFNγ)]] and [[fas|FAS]]-dependent signaling.¹ The cytokine has emerged as a powerful immunotherapeutic agent through two complementary delivery strategies: inducible [[aav|adeno-associated viral (AAV) vector]]-based platforms that achieve efficient tumor-localized IL-12 production without constitutive toxicity, and engineered integration into natural killer cell circuits governed by context-responsive promoters such as [[pfkfb4|PFKFB4]].¹²

---

## Mechanism

IL-12 drives antitumor immunity through dual mechanisms of local tumor microenvironment reprogramming and systemic immune priming. The inducible [[aav|AAV]]-IL-12 platform (AAV-iIL12) delivers transgenes that produce IL-12 specifically in response to interferon signaling within irradiated tumors, enabling efficient cytokine synthesis without the dose-limiting toxicity associated with constitutive expression systems.¹ The cytokine operates through [[ifn-gamma|IFNγ]]-dependent amplification of immune surveillance and [[fas|FAS]]-dependent triggering of tumor cell apoptosis, establishing a reinforcing cycle of cytotoxic lymphocyte activation and tumor cell death.¹ This coordinated signaling enables IL-12-driven immune responses to dismantle established immunosuppressive mechanisms and overcome resistance to immune checkpoint blockade.

---

## Radiotherapy synergy

Radiation exposure and inducible [[aav|AAV]]-delivered IL-12 combine to generate sustained local and systemic antitumor responses. Radiotherapy enhances [[aav|AAV]] tumor transduction through epigenetic modification of vector episomes, creating favorable conditions for transgene expression within the irradiated lesion.¹ Critically, the interferon-inducible promoter system restricts IL-12 production to radiated tumor tissue, simultaneously maximizing immunostimulation and minimizing systemic toxicity compared to constitutive delivery paradigms.¹ The resulting highly immunostimulatory tumor microenvironment activates both local lymphocyte infiltration and systemic immunity in an [[ifn-gamma|IFNγ]]- and [[fas|FAS]]-dependent manner, generating durable antitumor memory and preventing disease recurrence.¹

---

## NK cell engineering

IL-12 has been engineered as a hypoxia-responsive genetic circuit within natural killer cells, enabling context-dependent therapeutic efficacy. The circuit employs the [[pfkfb4|PFKFB4 promoter]] — a transcriptional marker activated selectively in hypoxic microenvironments — to gate IL-12 expression and restore NK cell cytotoxicity under conditions of oxygen depletion.² This design ensures that cytotoxic function is preserved and amplified precisely where tumor-imposed metabolic stress would otherwise suppress immune cells, expanding the therapeutic utility of NK cell-based approaches.² The nonviral genome-editing platform supporting this integration maintains full compatibility with good manufacturing practice (GMP) manufacturing workflows, enabling clinical-scale expansion of IL-12-engineered NK cell products for off-the-shelf immunotherapy.²
