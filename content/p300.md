**P300** is a transcriptional coactivator protein with intrinsic histone acetyltransferase (HAT) activity that catalyzes histone acetylation and enables chromatin remodeling during gene activation. As a molecular bridge between transcription factors and chromatin machinery, P300 integrates regulatory signals to coordinate transcriptional responses across diverse cellular contexts, including thyroid hormone signaling in [[kidney-disease|kidney pathology]] and synaptic plasticity in [[alzheimers-disease|Alzheimer's disease]].¹ Its activity is tightly regulated through multiprotein complexes such as the [[trb|TR-β]]-P300-[[brd4|BRD4]] regulatory axis, enabling precise transcriptional control under specific physiological conditions.

---

## Mechanism

P300 functions as a molecular integrator of transcriptional regulation through its intrinsic HAT activity and extensive protein–protein interaction domains. Upon recruitment to target gene enhancers and promoters by transcription factors, P300 catalyzes the acetylation of histone tails, creating an open chromatin state permissive to transcriptional machinery assembly.¹ The enzyme simultaneously serves as a scaffolding protein, recruiting additional coactivators and chromatin remodeling complexes to amplify transcriptional output. Its ability to bridge transcription factor-DNA binding to downstream effector machinery enables P300 to function as a central hub in multiprotein regulatory complexes that coordinate responses to diverse cellular signals, from hormonal stimulation to neuronal activity.

## Kidney disease and thyroid hormone signaling

P300 plays a critical role in a pathological kidney disease pathway activated by maternal obesity. In offspring of obese mothers, proximal tubule-derived 20-hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acid (20-HETE) drives macrophage to myofibroblast transition (MMT) through [[ffar1|FFAR1]] signaling, promoting progressive kidney pathology.¹ Thyroid hormone (T3) amplifies this pathway by enhancing [[ffar1|FFAR1]] expression in macrophages through the [[trb|TR-β]]-P300-[[brd4|BRD4]] regulatory axis, where [[trb|TR-β]] binds to an identified enhancer upstream of the [[ffar1|FFAR1]] gene and recruits P300 to catalyze histone acetylation, thereby promoting transcriptional activation of the pro-fibrotic program.¹ This T3 and 20-HETE co-activated MMT mechanism illustrates how P300-mediated epigenetic remodeling can amplify pathological cell state transitions in response to maternal metabolic perturbations, offering a potential therapeutic intervention point for offspring at risk of chronic kidney disease.

## Synaptic plasticity and Alzheimer's disease

P300 regulates synaptic function and neuronal homeostasis through histone acetylation-dependent mechanisms affecting [[ampar|AMPAR]] trafficking and synaptic internalization. During rhythmic neuromodulation in the hippocampus, P300-mediated histone acetylation coordinates with [[nfkb|NF-κB]] transcription factor activation to remodel memory-related network connectivity and restore impaired neural oscillations.² Specifically, P300 catalyzes histone modifications that regulate the balance between [[ampar|AMPAR]] synaptic delivery and internalization, processes central to synaptic plasticity and long-term potentiation. In early-stage [[alzheimers-disease|Alzheimer's disease]] models, where synaptic dysfunction and gamma oscillation loss precede neurodegeneration, restoration of P300-dependent histone acetylation through focused ultrasound-driven neuromodulation rescues synaptic plasticity, reestablishes gamma-frequency rhythms, and enhances hippocampal information processing capacity.² These findings position P300 as a critical epigenetic regulator whose activity state determines cognitive reserve and therapeutic responsiveness in [[alzheimers-disease|Alzheimer's disease]].
