## Lead

**mRNA vaccines** are nucleic acid-based immunotherapeutics that encode pathogen or tumor antigens and are delivered via [[lipid nanoparticles]] (LNPs) to stimulate potent humoral and cellular immune responses.¹²³⁴⁵ These platform technologies represent a paradigm shift in immunization strategy, enabling rapid response to emerging infectious threats while simultaneously extending therapeutic reach to genetic disorders and oncology. Unlike conventional vaccines that deliver inactivated virus or recombinant proteins, mRNA vaccines harness host cellular machinery to synthesize the protective antigen in situ, eliminating manufacturing complexity and enabling dose-sparing regimens that achieve complete protection at microgram quantities.²³⁴ The versatility of the mRNA vaccine platform—spanning from [[seasonal influenza]] to [[citrullinemia type I]] correction and [[tumor]]-directed immunotherapy—reflects both the modularity of antigen encoding and the potency of dendritic cell activation and CD8+ T cell priming achieved through engineered [[lipid nanoparticle]] formulations.⁵

## Molecular Platforms and RNA Modalities

mRNA vaccines have evolved beyond conventional nucleoside-modified mRNA to encompass diverse molecular architectures optimized for distinct therapeutic contexts. The foundational platform comprises chemically modified mRNA—typically incorporating pseudouridine or other nucleoside analogs—that reduces immunogenicity through pattern recognition receptor suppression while maintaining translational efficiency. However, clinical experience with [[seasonal influenza]] vaccination revealed strain-specific constraints in conventional mRNA immunogenicity, particularly for influenza B virus (IBV), which failed to elicit detectable antibodies at standard doses.² To overcome these limitations, self-amplifying RNA (saRNA) and circular RNA (circRNA) modalities have been systematically evaluated. SaRNA platforms, which encode their own replicase, achieve enhanced antigen expression from markedly reduced doses; a single 0.1 μg dose of trivalent saRNA influenza vaccine induced robust humoral immunity and conferred complete homologous protection in mouse models, outperforming the quadrivalent inactivated vaccine at 2 μg and exceeding the 14% survival achieved with conventional mRNA at the same dose.² CircRNA architectures, engineered for enhanced stability and resistance to cellular degradation, similarly enable dose-sparing with improved long-term antibody persistence.² These advances in RNA design directly address both immunogenicity constraints and manufacturing scalability.

## Delivery Systems and Surface Engineering

[[Lipid nanoparticles]] serve as the primary in vivo delivery vehicle for mRNA vaccines, comprising a lipid bilayer matrix, helper lipids, cholesterol, and surface-conjugated ligands that direct cellular uptake and lymphoid targeting. Conventional formulations employ poly(ethylene glycol) (PEG)-based surface modifications to enhance pharmacokinetics and colloidal stability; however, PEG moieties can elicit anti-PEG immune responses, reactogenicity, and hypersensitivity reactions that reduce vaccine efficacy and complicate repeated dosing.⁴ Recent advances in LNP surface engineering have established heparosan—a naturally occurring, biodegradable polysaccharide—as a biocompatible alternative that maintains immunological efficacy while minimizing tissue damage and immune activation.⁴ Systematically characterized heparosan-coated LNPs demonstrate nanoparticle stability, mRNA encapsulation efficiency, and delivery performance comparable to PEG-modified counterparts across cultured cells and mouse models, while eliminating the hypersensitivity concerns associated with synthetic polymer coatings.⁴ For tumor immunotherapy applications, cationic lipid-based LNP formulations—composed of ionizable lipids such as C1 and L-phenylalanine-based poly(ester amide) 8p4—enhance cellular uptake and promote robust activation of antigen-presenting cells.⁵

## Immunological Mechanisms and B Cell Breadth

mRNA vaccine immunogenicity is underpinned by two complementary mechanisms: robust dendritic cell maturation, which initiates innate sensing and adaptive priming, and the preferential generation of high-breadth B cell responses that recognize multiple viral variants. Analysis of 6,262 circulating B cells from previously naive mRNA vaccinees revealed that vaccination, compared with natural [[SARS-CoV-2]] infection, preferentially stimulated B cells expressing antibodies with inherently high antigen-binding breadth across up to 20 viral variants.¹ Post-germinal center memory B cells demonstrated progressively increasing variant recognition and somatic hypermutation over time, indicating sustained maturation of the humoral response.¹ This superior breadth in vaccine-induced B cells likely reflects the controlled temporal kinetics of antigen presentation and the immunodominance hierarchy imposed by synthetic antigen design, contrasting with the polyclonal antigenic landscape of natural infection. At the cellular level, [[lipid nanoparticle]] formulations containing cationic lipids promote maturation and expansion of type 1 conventional dendritic cells (cDC1), which are essential for efficient MHC-I presentation and CD8+ T cell priming.⁵ Antigen-immunogenic protein fusions—exemplified by tumor antigen fused with [[angiotensin II]]—further enhance dendritic cell licensing and intensify antigen-specific CD8+ T cell responses; in multiple mouse tumor models, such engineered mRNA vaccines effectively inhibited tumor growth in a manner dependent on cDC1-mediated immunity.⁵

## Clinical Applications and Therapeutic Landscape

The clinical reach of mRNA vaccines spans infectious diseases, oncology, and rare genetic disorders. In infectious disease, [[SARS-CoV-2]] mRNA vaccines have achieved widespread deployment and demonstrated durable protection, while dose-sparing saRNA platforms targeting [[influenza-b]] and other seasonal strains offer expedited vaccination campaigns with reduced antigen requirements.²³ Beyond classical immunization, mRNA-encoded therapies leverage the same [[lipid nanoparticle]] delivery platform for in situ protein synthesis in non-immune contexts; prime editing-based correction of [[citrullinemia type I]], mediated by mRNA-encoded prime editor protein (PE7) and synthetic guide RNA delivered via LNPs, achieved 24% correction in hepatocytes at a single 3 mg/kg dose and normalized blood citrulline and ammonia concentrations with full restoration of urea cycle function and survival.¹ Tumor immunotherapy applications employ antigen-angiotensin II fusion mRNA nanovaccines to simultaneously enhance antigen immunogenicity and promote cDC1-mediated CD8+ T cell response, achieving significant antitumor efficacy across multiple malignancy models.⁵ These expanding applications underscore both the flexibility of mRNA encoding and the clinical utility of engineered [[lipid nanoparticle]] formulations.

---

## Dose-Sparing Efficacy and Manufacturing Advantages

A defining advantage of advanced mRNA vaccine platforms is their dose-sparing capacity, which reduces manufacturing burden and enables rapid response to emerging pathogens. Conventional quadrivalent inactivated influenza vaccines require 2 μg of hemagglutinin per strain per dose; in contrast, nucleoside-modified mRNA influenza vaccines at 0.1 μg—a 20-fold reduction—induced equivalent or superior antibody responses and conferred complete homologous protection in mouse challenge models.² Self-amplifying RNA platforms amplify this advantage further, with a single 0.1 μg dose of saRNA trivalent influenza vaccine achieving robust immunity and complete IBV protection, while maintaining high antibody titers over 20 weeks of monitoring.² This dose-sparing capacity translates to substantial manufacturing and supply-chain efficiencies, particularly for pandemic preparedness or rare-disease applications where production volume may be constrained. The modularity of mRNA antigen design—requiring only the nucleotide sequence encoding the target antigen—further accelerates platform deployment for novel pathogens or tumor neoantigens, positioning mRNA vaccines as a foundational technology for adaptive immunization strategies.
