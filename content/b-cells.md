**B cells** are lymphocytes that form a critical component of the [[adaptive-immune-system|adaptive immune system]], distinguished by their capacity to produce antibodies and present antigens to T cells. The [[germinal-center|germinal center]] serves as the primary site of B cell maturation, where somatic hypermutation and class switching generate functionally distinct populations with varying antigen-recognition breadth.¹ B cell responses are markedly heterogeneous, encompassing atypical B cells, class-switched memory cells, and post-germinal center populations that differ substantially in antigen-binding patterns and somatic hypermutation frequency.¹ Dysregulation of B cell function underlies multiple autoimmune diseases, notably [[systemic-lupus-erythematosus|systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)]], and B cells also populate tumor microenvironments where they influence metastatic progression.

---

## Phenotypic diversity and antigen binding

B cell populations exhibit remarkable phenotypic heterogeneity in their capacity to recognize viral antigens and antigen variants. Atypical B cells and class-switched memory cells bearing evidence of recent [[germinal-center|germinal center]] exposure are preferentially enriched for antigen-binding capacity.¹ In contrast, post-germinal center B cells display a distinctive pattern: while initially showing narrower variant recognition, they progressively increase their breadth of antigen-binding against multiple viral variants over time, accompanied by accumulation of somatic hypermutation.¹ This temporal evolution reflects ongoing affinity maturation and diversification within the B cell compartment long after germinal center exit.

---

## Vaccination versus natural infection

The immunization route profoundly shapes the phenotype and functional properties of generated B cell populations. Vaccination, compared with natural infection, preferentially stimulates B cells expressing antibodies with inherently high antigen-binding breadth against viral variants.¹ This enhanced breadth in vaccinated populations suggests that [[mRNA-vaccine|mRNA vaccination]] engages distinct B cell maturation pathways or selects for B cell clones with superior capacity for cross-variant recognition, providing a mechanistic advantage for vaccine-based immunization strategies against rapidly mutating pathogens.

## Dysregulation in systemic lupus erythematosus

B cell dysregulation in [[systemic-lupus-erythematosus|systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)]] drives chronic inflammation, hypertension, and progressive renal injury in affected patients.² Aberrant B cell activity in SLE reflects impaired immune tolerance mechanisms and pathogenic autoantibody production, perpetuating organ-specific damage. The [[cholinergic|cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway]] provides neuroimmune feedback that normally constrains B cell activation; therapeutic enhancement of this pathway via acetylcholinesterase inhibitors such as [[galantamine]] reduces circulating CD19+ B cell populations and ameliorates inflammation-driven pathology in SLE models.² These observations highlight the reciprocal relationship between B cell homeostasis and neuroimmune regulation in autoimmune disease.

---

## CAR T cell therapeutic targeting

[[CD19|CD19+]] B cells represent a rational therapeutic target in severe pediatric autoimmune diseases, where conventional immunosuppression has failed. CD19-directed CAR T cell therapy induces rapid and durable clinical remissions with manageable toxicity, enabling patients to discontinue standard immunosuppressive medications.³ Notably, clinical benefits persist even after B cell reconstitution, indicating that CAR T cell-mediated immune reset establishes durable tolerance rather than merely depleting B cells transiently.³ This phenomenon suggests that CD19-targeted CAR T cells fundamentally reprogram pathogenic immune circuits, warranting further investigation of mechanisms underlying long-term immune tolerance post-treatment.

---

## Role in tumor microenvironment and metastasis

B cells populate breast cancer tumor microenvironments and are associated with axillary lymph node metastasis.⁴ Single-cell transcriptomic profiling reveals substantial B cell heterogeneity within primary tumors and metastatic lymph nodes, with distinct B cell subsets participating in ligand-receptor communication networks alongside monocytes, T cells, and epithelial compartments.⁴ This cellular crosstalk implicates B cells in lymphatic dissemination and metastatic progression, positioning them as potential biomarkers for lymph node involvement and candidates for future therapeutic intervention in metastatic disease.
