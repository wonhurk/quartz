**Farnesoid X receptor (FXR)** is a nuclear hormone receptor that orchestrates bile acid metabolism and lipid homeostasis, functioning as a central metabolic governor in both hepatic and intestinal tissues. Its pharmacological activation has emerged as a compelling therapeutic approach for metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), a progressive liver disease defined by hepatic steatosis, inflammation, and fibrosis.¹ FXR responds to [[bile-acids|primary bile acids]], which serve as endogenous ligands, and mediates adaptive responses that suppress bile acid synthesis, reduce hepatic lipid accumulation, and dampen inflammatory signaling. Dysregulation of FXR activity is increasingly recognized as a driver of MASH pathogenesis, with altered FXR signaling permitting dysbiosis and enrichment of pathogenic microbiota including [[turicibacter-sanguinis|Turicibacter sanguinis]], establishing FXR as a therapeutic nexus between hepatic metabolism and the gut-liver axis.²

---

## Mechanism

FXR regulates hepatic lipid metabolism through a multi-layered signaling network. Upon activation by [[bile-acids|bile acids]], FXR heterodimerizes with the retinoid X receptor and recruits co-activators to modulate the transcription of bile acid and lipid-metabolic genes.¹ Critically, FXR activity suppresses the expression of [[cyp7a1|CYP7A1]], the rate-limiting enzyme in bile acid synthesis, creating a negative feedback loop that prevents excessive bile acid accumulation. Simultaneously, FXR coordinates the downregulation of [[srebp1c|SREBP1c]], a master transcription factor that drives hepatic lipogenesis, thereby reducing de novo fatty acid and triglyceride synthesis.² The receptor also activates [[shp|SHP (NR0B1)]], an orphan nuclear receptor that further represses lipogenic gene expression. This multi-target regulation positions FXR as a potent metabolic brake capable of simultaneously addressing both bile acid cholestasis and hepatic steatosis.

## MASH therapeutics

FXR represents a validated therapeutic target for MASH, a condition affecting approximately one quarter of the global population and characterized by progressive hepatocellular damage resistant to lifestyle intervention alone. Activation of FXR with selective agonists reduces hepatic steatosis, attenuates inflammation, and prevents collagen deposition in fibrosis models.¹ In preclinical MASH models, FXR agonists normalize elevated hepatic triglycerides, suppress inflammatory cytokines, and restore the balance of pro-fibrotic and anti-fibrotic signals. The efficacy of FXR activation in reversing disease features stems from its dual mechanistic role: FXR agonists simultaneously address aberrant hepatic lipid metabolism and restore dysregulated FXR signaling at the gut-liver interface. This convergence of hepatic and microbial pathways distinguishes FXR from single-pathway therapeutics.

## Gut-liver axis and microbiota dysbiosis

Emerging evidence reveals that altered FXR signaling underlies dysbiosis in MASH, with loss of FXR-mediated bile acid homeostasis triggering pathogenic microbial blooms. [[turicibacter-sanguinis|Turicibacter sanguinis]], a gram-positive bacterium, is consistently enriched across multiple MASH models and correlates with increased severity of steatosis, inflammation, and fibrosis.² Mechanistically, overgrowth of T. sanguinis depletes unconjugated bile acids and elevates conjugated species, suppressing hepatic FXR signaling and driving upregulation of CYP7A1 and [[srebp1c|SREBP1c]], which promote bile acid synthesis and hepatic lipogenesis. The dysbiosis-driven FXR suppression thus establishes a pathogenic loop in which dysbiotic microbiota perpetuate impaired FXR signaling, steatosis, and fibrosis progression. Pharmacological FXR activation breaks this cycle by restoring bile acid homeostasis, rebalancing the microbiota, and suppressing the pro-inflammatory and pro-fibrotic signals emanating from dysbiotic species.

## Selectivity and safety

Early-generation FXR agonists such as [[oca|obeticholic acid (OCA)]] demonstrated efficacy against MASH but were associated with dose-limiting off-target activation of [[mrgprx4|MRGPRX4]] (formerly known as MRGX2), a G protein-coupled receptor on mast cells that triggers histamine release and pruritic side effects.¹ Modern FXR agonists achieve improved safety by minimizing MRGPRX4 engagement while simultaneously recruiting [[tgr5|TGR5]], a G protein-coupled bile acid receptor that potentiates anti-inflammatory and metabolic benefits. This dual-agonist strategy — enhanced FXR/TGR5 co-activation coupled with suppressed MRGPRX4 activity — overcomes the tolerability barriers encountered with first-generation agents and expands the therapeutic window for MASH treatment.

## Novel FXR agonists

[[compound-27|Compound 27]] represents a breakthrough non-carboxylic steroidal FXR agonist engineered through rational structure-activity relationship analysis.¹ The agent achieves nanomolar potency at FXR (TR-FRET EC₅₀ = 10 ± 3 nM) with exceptional oral bioavailability in rats (F = 70.30%), properties that translate to robust hepatoprotection in preclinical models. Compound 27 demonstrates selective [[tgr5|TGR5]] co-activation (HTRF EC₅₀ = 1360 nM) while markedly suppressing [[mrgprx4|MRGPRX4]], achieving a pharmacological profile superior to OCA-class agents. In vivo efficacy studies confirm its ability to attenuate collagen deposition in chemically-induced liver fibrosis and to improve both hepatic steatosis and inflammatory infiltration in MASH models, positioning it as a leading preclinical candidate for clinical advancement.
