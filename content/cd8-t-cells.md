## Definition and Role in Antitumor Immunity

**CD8+ T cells** are [[hla-i|major histocompatibility complex class I]]-restricted cytotoxic lymphocytes that execute the primary adaptive immune response against intracellular pathogens and malignant cells, and represent the essential cellular determinant of [[immune-checkpoint-inhibitors|checkpoint immunotherapy]] efficacy in multiple cancer types. These lymphocytes recognize [[tumor-associated-antigens|tumor-associated]] and [[neoantigen|neoantigenic]] peptides bound to HLA-I molecules on tumor cell surfaces through their clonally distributed T-cell receptors. Upon antigen engagement, CD8+ T cells rapidly execute cytotoxic function through release of perforin and granzyme-containing granules, simultaneously producing the pro-inflammatory cytokine interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) that amplifies anti-tumor immunity. In the clinical context of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer, CD8+ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) are the sole lymphocyte subset—distinguished from Th1 cells, regulatory T cells, and natural killer cells—that shows consistent association with durable benefit from [[pdcd1|PD-1]]-axis checkpoint blockade.¹ This clinical primacy reflects both the [[hla-i|HLA-I]]-mediated specificity of CD8+ T-cell recognition and the capacity of these cells to mount sustained, polyfunctional immune responses amenable to therapeutic re-activation through targeted monoclonal antibody therapy or precision cellular engineering.

## Phenotypic States and Biomarkers of Immunotherapy Responsiveness

CD8+ T-cell function spans a spectrum from highly activated effectors to progressively dysfunctional states, and the enrichment of specific naïve and activated phenotypes in pre-treatment tumors correlates strongly with therapeutic responsiveness across distinct immunotherapy modalities. Longitudinal single-cell transcriptomic analysis of patients treated with the anti-CD20 T-cell engager glofitamab for relapsed/refractory B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma identified the maintenance of fresher, naïve-like CD8+ T-cell states at early treatment timepoints as a significant predictor of clinical response, with responder CD8+ T cells exhibiting enhanced functional activity and sustained expansion compared to non-responders.² In parallel, integrated multi-omics analysis across 31 non-small cell lung cancer datasets revealed that elevated infiltration of [[znf683|ZNF683+]] CD8+ T cells distinguishes immunotherapy responders from non-responders, with ZNF683 serving as a robust and reproducible indicator of therapeutic sensitivity across cancer types.³ The [[znf683|ZNF683+]] CD8+ T-cell subset exhibits heightened antigen-specific expansion capacity, robust IFN-γ production, and enrichment in treatment-sensitive tumors, enabling development of ZNF683-based prognostic risk scores with superior predictive capability compared to conventional immune infiltration metrics.

Within the tumor microenvironment, CD8+ TILs exhibit a phenotype of exhaustion-yet-functionality characterized by co-expression of the inhibitory markers CD103, CD39, and the proliferation marker Ki-67.¹ This partially suppressed state, identified through spatial single-cell proteotyping of pre-treatment [[colon-cancer|colorectal]] and lung tumors, represents a distinct intermediate phenotype in which tumor-reactive capacity persists despite chronic antigenic stimulation and tissue-resident signaling constraints. The recognition that CD8+ T-cell dysfunction is heterogeneous and reversible—rather than a binary state of complete anergy—has fundamental implications for therapeutic design, opening avenues for targeted intervention via inhibitory receptor antagonism.

## Dysfunction and Microenvironmental Suppression

The tumor microenvironment employs multiple, redundant mechanisms to restrict CD8+ T-cell infiltration, effector function, and long-term memory formation, operating through direct cellular signaling, metabolic competition, and physical barriers. Elevated expression of the co-stimulatory molecule [[cd155|CD155]] and concurrent upregulation of the inhibitory receptor [[cd96|CD96]] on CD8+ T cells during interaction with quiescent persister tumor cells—which emerge following chemotherapy in [[colon-cancer|colorectal cancer]]—drives a trajectory toward exhaustion and reduced cytotoxic capacity.⁴ In pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, hypoxia-induced formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETosis) creates physical barriers that restrict CD8+ T-cell infiltration to immunogenic tumor regions, necessitating dual CXCR2-targeting blockade and [[pdcd1|anti-PD-1]] therapy to overcome this resistance mechanism and restore CD8+ T-cell recruitment and IFN-γ production.⁵ Gut dysbiosis-associated elevation of ammonia, produced by the ammonia-generating bacterium *Clostridium perfringens* in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis, disrupts intestinal barrier integrity and triggers FosB-mediated upregulation of the chemokine CCL5 in hepatic CD8+ T cells, creating a pathological CD8+ T-cell-mediated inflammation that can be attenuated through ammonia-reducing interventions such as the tripeptide [[dt-109|DT-109]].⁶ Lipid droplet accumulation in tumor stroma suppresses CD8+ T-cell maturation and [[cd107a|degranulation]] capability via type I interferon signaling while paradoxically preserving memory CD8+ T-cell differentiation, thereby generating a functionally "cold" tumor microenvironment resistant to single-agent checkpoint blockade.⁷ Additionally, SPP1-mediated signaling in immunotherapy-resistant tumors correlates with reduced CD8+ T-cell effector function and enhanced M2-like macrophage polarization, establishing SPP1 as a targetable molecular axis for restoring CD8+ T-cell responses in combination with checkpoint inhibitors.³

## Therapeutic Restoration and Cellular Engineering

The reversible nature of CD8+ T-cell exhaustion has enabled development of multiple therapeutic strategies to restore anti-tumor cytotoxicity. Direct [[cd96|CD96]] blockade or cellular depletion diverts CD8+ T cells from exhaustion trajectories and promotes memory-like phenotypes characterized by enhanced mitochondrial respiration, proliferative capacity, and long-term persistence.⁴ This [[cd96|CD96]]-targeted approach is particularly effective against quiescent persister tumor cells in [[colon-cancer|colorectal cancer]] that emerge after neoadjuvant chemotherapy and orchestrate an immunosuppressive microenvironment. Combination therapeutic approaches that simultaneously address microenvironmental suppressors—such as ferroptosis-inducing nanomaterials combined with [[immune-checkpoint-inhibitors|checkpoint blockade]]—enhance CD8+ T-cell infiltration and IFN-γ production by disrupting tumor redox balance and reducing lipid-droplet-mediated immunosuppression.⁸

Precision genetic engineering of CD8+ T cells enables generation of potent, antigen-specific, and durable cellular immunotherapy with substantially reduced oncogenic risk compared to random viral integration strategies. Targeted introduction of tumor-specific T-cell receptor genes into the endogenous T-cell receptor alpha constant (TRAC) [[genome-editing|locus via genome editing]] in iPSC-derived CD8+ T cells yields stable, monoclonal TCR expression under native regulatory control, with robust antigen-specific [[cd107a|degranulation]], rapid CD107a upregulation, and potent IFN-γ production against cognate peptide-expressing targets.⁹ This TRAC-mediated TCR replacement overcomes the heterogeneous expression profiles and transformation risk inherent to viral vector-mediated approaches, establishing a scalable and clinically translatable platform for generating therapeutic CD8+ T cells with precisely defined specificity. Complementary approaches such as epithelial cell adhesion molecule (EpCAM)-targeted CAR-T cell engineering additionally deficient in [[cd96|CD96]] expression further amplify anti-tumor activity against persister populations by simultaneously eliminating the cellular adhesion interaction and the [[cd96|CD96]]-mediated exhaustion signal.⁴

## Epigenetic and Metabolic Enhancement of CD8+ T-Cell Function

Recent in vivo CRISPR/Cas9 screening studies have identified epigenetic regulators, notably chromobox 4 (CBX4), as key negative regulators of the tumor microenvironment that actively suppress CD8+ T-cell infiltration and cytotoxic function.¹⁰ Genetic loss of CBX4 in both tumor cells and immunosuppressive tumor-associated macrophages derepresses endogenous retrotransposons, activating intracellular RNA-sensing pathways and amplifying type I interferon responses, thereby establishing a robustly inflamed tumor microenvironment characterized by enhanced CD8+ T-cell recruitment, activation, and cytotoxic capability. Therapeutic restoration of ammonia-driven CD8+ T-cell dysregulation through the [[dt-109|tripeptide DT-109]]—which reduces *Clostridium perfringens* abundance and intestinal ammonia levels—demonstrates the clinical feasibility of microenvironmental metabolite targeting to restore CD8+ T-cell function and prevent dysregulated cytotoxicity.⁶ These convergent findings position CD8+ T cells as highly sensitive orchestrators of anti-tumor immunity whose therapeutic fate can be precisely modulated through combined targeting of inhibitory receptors ([[cd96|CD96]], [[cd155|CD155]]), epigenetic suppressors, microenvironmental metabolites, and cellular redox pathways.
