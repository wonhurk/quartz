**Amyloid precursor protein (APP)** is a transmembrane glycoprotein that functions as a critical regulator of immune-mediated cell crosstalk through its interaction with the [[cd74|CD74]] receptor. The APP-CD74 axis mediates communication between epithelial or tubular cells and macrophages, with context-dependent effects ranging from proinflammatory macrophage activation in [[lung-adenocarcinoma|lung adenocarcinoma]] to pathogenic inflammation in [[autosomal-dominant-tubulointerstitial-kidney-disease|autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease (ADTKD)]].¹² The pathway is amenable to therapeutic targeting, as demonstrated by small-molecule APP inhibitors that attenuate renal inflammation and kidney injury in disease models.¹

---

## Mechanism

The APP-CD74 axis represents a key intercellular communication pathway between epithelial or tubular cells and myeloid populations. Engagement of [[cd74|CD74]] by APP on macrophages sustains activation of the [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] pathway, initiating downstream inflammatory responses.¹ The spatial organization of these interactions is critical to their function — in specific contexts, APP-expressing epithelial subpopulations are positioned adjacent to [[cd74|CD74]]-expressing myeloid cells within discrete niches, facilitating direct intercellular communication.² In genetic disease, mutations in [[umod|uromodulin (UMOD)]] can aberrantly activate this signaling axis, dysregulating the normal balance of tubular-macrophage crosstalk and causing sustained [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] pathway hyperactivation.¹

---

## Lung adenocarcinoma

In [[lung-adenocarcinoma|lung adenocarcinoma]] (LUAD), the APP-CD74 axis emerges as a marker of immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI) responsiveness. Single-cell spatial atlases of 38 LUAD patients identified enrichment of APP-expressing epithelial populations (the E3-IFI6 subpopulation) in tumors with high ICI response, where they co-localize with [[cd74|CD74]]-expressing myeloid populations (the Ma-MHC subpopulation) in specific spatial niches.² These APP-expressing epithelial cells display elevated expression of interferon- and antigen-presentation genes. Functional validation confirmed that overexpression of these interferon-related genes in LUAD cell lines promotes proinflammatory macrophage activation, suggesting that APP-CD74-mediated crosstalk amplifies anti-tumor immunity in ICI-responsive tumors.² This spatial organization and transcriptional coordination position APP-CD74 crosstalk as a key component of the tumor immune microenvironment in adenocarcinomas with rare actionable mutations.

---

## Kidney disease

The APP-CD74 axis plays a pathogenic role in [[autosomal-dominant-tubulointerstitial-kidney-disease|ADTKD-UMOD]], a monogenic form of progressive kidney disease driven by [[umod|uromodulin]] mutations. Certain UMOD variants, including p.His36Tyr and p.Trp31Cys, aberrantly activate APP-CD74 signaling in the crosstalk between mutant renal tubular cells and infiltrating macrophages.¹ This sustained [[cd74|CD74]] engagement perpetuates [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] pathway activation in macrophages, initiating macrophage pyroptosis — a lytic form of regulated cell death accompanied by release of pro-inflammatory cytokines including IL-1β and IL-18.¹ The ensuing pyroptotic inflammation drives progressive interstitial inflammation and fibrosis characteristic of ADTKD-UMOD. In mouse models of the UmodH36Y/+ mutation, multi-omics analyses revealed marked macrophage pyroptosis in the kidney, establishing the causal link between the [[umod|UMOD]] variant, APP-CD74 axis activation, and disease pathogenesis.

---

## Therapeutic targeting

Pharmacological inhibition of APP represents a viable therapeutic strategy for ADTKD-UMOD-driven kidney disease. The small-molecule APP inhibitor [[arn2966|ARN2966]] effectively suppresses APP-CD74 signaling in tubular-macrophage crosstalk, reducing [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] activation and macrophage pyroptosis.¹ In mouse models of UMOD p.His36Tyr disease, ARN2966 treatment attenuated renal injury, interstitial inflammation, and cyst formation, establishing APP as a druggable target in kidney disease.¹ This finding highlights the therapeutic potential of interrupting the APP-CD74 axis as a treatment strategy for inflammatory kidney diseases driven by dysregulated immune cell-cell communication.
