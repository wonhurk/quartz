**Mitochondrial dysfunction** is a central pathological driver across diverse disease contexts, characterized by impaired oxidative phosphorylation, excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, and disrupted energy homeostasis. The condition manifests through multiple interconnected mechanisms including dysregulated calcium transport between the endoplasmic reticulum and mitochondria, [[calcium-dysregulation|aberrant intracellular calcium signaling]], metabolic reprogramming from oxidative phosphorylation toward glycolysis, and activation of [[ferroptosis]] and inflammasome pathways.¹ Key regulatory proteins include [[pgc1a|PGC1α]] and [[ucp2|UCP2]], which govern mitochondrial biogenesis and uncoupling, respectively, while systemic factors such as [[igf-1|IGF-1]] modulate mitochondrial integrity in response to metabolic stress.²

---

## Mechanism

Mitochondrial dysfunction arises through convergent pathological mechanisms that disrupt both structural and functional integrity. At the cellular level, endoplasmic reticulum stress and mitochondrial damage engage in bidirectional reinforcement through dysregulated intracellular calcium transport, creating a self-amplifying cycle of organellar dysfunction.³ Transcriptomic analyses across disease models reveal characteristic metabolic reprogramming marked by suppression of TCA cycle–dependent oxidative phosphorylation and compensatory upregulation of glycolytic pathways, indicating fundamental impairment of mitochondrial ATP generation.² Concurrent ROS accumulation overwhelms endogenous antioxidant systems, triggering lipid peroxidation, NLRP3 inflammasome activation, and initiation of multiple cell death programs including apoptosis and ferroptosis.⁴ This oxidative cascade further compromises mitochondrial membrane potential and disrupts mitophagy, preventing the selective elimination of damaged organelles and perpetuating cellular injury.⁵

## Cardiovascular disease

In [[obesity]]-associated cardiomyopathy, mitochondrial dysfunction serves as a central mediator of cardiac remodeling and contractile failure. High-fat diet exposure induces hyperleptinemia, hypertriglyceridemia, and suppression of circulating [[igf-1|IGF-1]], culminating in cardiac hypertrophy, interstitial fibrosis, and profound contractile dysfunction characterized by reduced fractional shortening and ejection fraction.² Mechanistic studies demonstrate that obesity-induced cardiac injury involves coordinated downregulation of [[pgc1a|PGC1α]] and [[ucp2|UCP2]], key regulators of mitochondrial biogenesis and oxidative uncoupling, alongside activation of ferroptosis as a critical node in myocardial cell death.² Cardiac-specific [[igf-1|IGF-1]] overexpression mitigates these pathological changes through arachidylcarnitine-dependent mechanisms that simultaneously suppress ferroptosis, preserve mitochondrial membrane integrity, and restore calcium handling in cardiomyocytes.²

## Hepatic injury

Acetaminophen-induced acute liver injury represents a prototypical model of drug-induced mitochondrial toxicity, where hepatocellular damage proceeds through ROS-mediated organellar collapse. Prolyl hydroxylase domain enzyme 1 (PHD1) emerges as a therapeutic target in this context, with targeted degradation using the cereblon-recruiting PROTAC compound [[sh-26|SH-26]] attenuating hepatic inflammation and necrosis without detectable cytotoxicity.⁴ Mechanistically, [[sh-26|SH-26]]-mediated PHD1 degradation disrupts the acetaminophen-triggered cascade of ROS accumulation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and NLRP3 inflammasome activation, providing robust in vivo protection against acute liver injury.⁴ This approach highlights the utility of targeted protein degradation strategies for modulating mitochondrial stress responses in toxin-induced organ failure.

## Cancer cachexia

In [[cancer-cachexia|cancer cachexia]], mitochondrial dysfunction manifests as profound metabolic wasting driven by dysregulated lipid metabolism and elevated circulating ceramides. These bioactive sphingolipids accumulate through increased hepatic synthesis—specifically via upregulation of serine palmitoyltransferase (SPT)—and directly impair mitochondrial function and energy homeostasis in skeletal muscle and other target tissues.⁶ Pharmacological inhibition of ceramide synthesis using myriocin, an SPT inhibitor, or RNA interference targeting key synthesis enzymes improves markers of muscle atrophy in cachectic mice, establishing the ceramide biosynthetic pathway as a contributor to metabolic dysfunction and a viable therapeutic target.⁶ Notably, SPT enzyme expression correlates with disease severity in liver samples from patients with [[cancer-cachexia|cancer cachexia]], though elevation is organ-specific rather than systemic.⁶

## Renal and periodontal disease

Acute kidney injury (AKI) involves mitochondrial dysfunction across multiple cellular compartments, with oxidative stress overload, aberrant macrophage polarization, and renal tubular epithelial cell apoptosis converging to drive tissue damage.⁵ Mitophagy promotion represents an emerging therapeutic strategy, with mesoporous cerium dioxide nanospheres engineered to target injured renal tissue through integrin αvβ3 receptor binding, scavenge ROS via the Nrf2/HO-1/GPX4/SOD1 pathway, and deliver quercetin to stimulate mitophagy in tubular epithelial cells.⁵ Similarly, in [[periodontitis]], mitochondrial dysfunction and endoplasmic reticulum stress are mutually reinforced through [[calcium-dysregulation|dysregulated calcium transport]], driving macrophage proinflammatory activation and perpetuating periodontal tissue destruction.³ Dual-functional polydopamine nanoparticles that chelate excess calcium and deliver osteogenic peptides restore organellar homeostasis, remodel macrophage polarization, and accelerate periodontal bone regeneration when administered via osteoimmunomodulatory microneedles integrated with decellularized extracellular matrix.³

## Novel therapeutic approaches

Mitofusin-decorated extracellular vesicles (MFNEVs) have emerged as a natural-sourced nanoplatform for targeted delivery of nucleic acids to mitochondria, addressing the long-standing challenge of biocompatible mitochondrial therapeutics.⁷ Surface-displayed mitofusin proteins MFN1 and MFN2 direct MFNEVs to localize at mitochondria, enabling efficient delivery of siRNA to reduce mitochondrial DNA-encoded gene expression and sgRNA to achieve CRISPR-based mitochondrial genome editing with resultant reduction in mitochondrial DNA content.⁷ The delivery mechanism exploits the endogenous mitochondrial fusion machinery, establishing MFNEVs as a versatile platform for metabolic syndrome, neurodegeneration, and cancer therapeutics arising from mitochondrial genetic mutations or dysfunction.⁷
