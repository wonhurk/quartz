**Activated T cell extracellular vesicles (ATEVs)** are cell-free membrane-bound nanovesicles derived from activated T lymphocytes that function as vehicles for non-viral transfer of genomic DNA into recipient cells. ATEVs carry extracellular vesicular DNA (EVDNA) substantially enriched in genes encoding antigen processing and presentation (APP) machinery and other immune-related factors. The transfer mechanism depends on [[granzyme-b|granzyme B (GzmB)]], a serine protease packaged within ATEVs that disrupts the nuclear envelope of recipient dendritic cells and tumor cells, enabling intranuclear deposition of DNA and transient APP gene expression.¹ This DNA transfer establishes a positive feedback loop that enhances antigen presentation capacity, recruits effector T cells to tumors, and converts immunologically "cold" tumors into immunologically "hot" ones — a conversion that renders even immune-refractory cancers susceptible to checkpoint blockade.²

---

## Mechanism

The molecular basis of ATEV function rests on three integrated steps: DNA packaging, delivery, and transient gene expression. ATEVs encapsulate double-stranded genomic DNA at notably high abundance, with enrichment for genes involved in antigen processing and presentation rather than random genomic content. Mechanistically, packaged [[granzyme-b|GzmB]] disrupts the structural integrity of the nuclear envelope in target cells — both dendritic cells and tumor-infiltrating cells — allowing ATEV-associated DNA to cross the nuclear barrier and access the transcriptional machinery of recipient cell nuclei.¹ The resulting expression of APP genes, including those encoding major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules and proteasomal components, is transient, reflecting the episomal and non-integrating nature of the delivered DNA. Critically, DNase treatment removes the majority of ATEV-associated DNA and entirely abrogates APP upregulation, T cell activation, and tumor recruitment, demonstrating that the therapeutic effect is DNA-dependent and not mediated by ATEV lipids or protein cargo alone.²

---

## Tumor immunotherapy applications

ATEVs function as a bona fide immunotherapy by remodeling the tumor microenvironment away from immune tolerance toward active antitumor immunity. When administered to tumor-bearing mice, ATEVs enhance antigen presentation by resident and infiltrating dendritic cells, amplifying T cell recognition of tumor neoantigens and driving T cell recruitment and effector function within the tumor parenchyma. This mechanism is particularly potent in immunologically "cold" tumors — those characterized by sparse T cell infiltration and high expression of immune checkpoint ligands — which are otherwise refractory to [[pd-1|PD-1]] monotherapy. By restoring the antigen presentation capacity of the tumor microenvironment through granzyme B-mediated DNA delivery, ATEVs establish the immunological conditions necessary for effective checkpoint blockade.¹²

---

## Synergy with checkpoint blockade

ATEVs achieve maximal therapeutic efficacy when combined with [[pd-1|PD-1]] checkpoint blockade, demonstrating synergistic suppression of tumor growth and prolonged survival in immunotherapy-refractory tumor models.¹ The mechanistic basis of this synergy lies in the complementary functions: ATEVs restore antigen presentation and T cell priming through granzyme B-mediated APP gene delivery, while [[pd-1|PD-1]] blockade relieves the inhibitory signals that prevent activation of tumor-infiltrating T cells. Together, this combination overcomes the two-layered immune evasion strategy typical of advanced cancers — both the microenvironmental scarcity of antigenic stimulation and the presence of checkpoint-mediated T cell exhaustion. Clinical translation of this combination approach is supported by the finding that ATEV-treated tumors show increased infiltration of activated, non-exhausted [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cells]], indicating durable immunological reprogramming rather than transient T cell stimulation.

---

## Advantages as acellular immunotherapy

ATEVs offer distinct therapeutic advantages over conventional cell-based and viral vector-mediated immunotherapies. As acellular vesicles, they can be manufactured, cryopreserved, and deployed without the logistical constraints and safety concerns associated with adoptive T cell therapies or autologous cellular products. Unlike viral vectors or chemically transfected systems, ATEV-mediated DNA delivery is non-integrating and transient — the transferred genomic DNA does not persist or integrate into the host genome, limiting the risk of insertional mutagenesis and off-target autoimmunity. The DNA cargo is enriched for genes necessary for antigen presentation and immune activation rather than being random or heavily engineered, allowing ATEVs to harness the immunological "wisdom" encoded in the natural transcriptional signature of activated T cells. Furthermore, the transient nature of ATEV-driven APP gene expression enables precise temporal control over immune activation, reducing the risk of overwhelming systemic inflammation or collateral damage to normal tissues.²
