**Inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS)** is a pro-inflammatory enzyme catalyzing nitric oxide (NO) production and serving as a canonical marker of activated [[astrocyte|astrocytes]] and pro-inflammatory [[macrophage|macrophages]] in both neuroinflammatory and hepatic tissue injury contexts.¹² The enzyme is tightly regulated by the [[ahr|aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR)]] pathway, which suppresses iNOS expression through inhibition of [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] and [[mapk|MAPK]] signaling, and by the [[sirt1|NAD+-dependent SIRT1 deacetylase]], whose activity directly controls iNOS upregulation in response to metabolic stress and inflammatory signals.² Pharmacological restoration of NAD+ bioavailability via [[nmn|β-nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN)]] and activation of the AhR pathway through [[indole-3-propionic-acid|indole-3-propionic acid (IPA)]] both suppress iNOS expression and mitigate secondary tissue damage across distinct organ systems.

---

## Mechanism

iNOS expression is controlled by convergent signaling cascades responding to metabolic stress and innate immune activation. Under conditions of cellular NAD+ depletion—such as during ischemia-reperfusion injury—[[sirt1|SIRT1]] deacetylase activity is impaired, leading to hyperacetylation of [[eif2a|eIF2α]].² This acetylation event triggers endoplasmic reticulum stress and promotes the pro-inflammatory polarization of [[macrophage|macrophages]], driving robust iNOS upregulation as part of the acute inflammatory transcriptional program.² Conversely, activation of the [[ahr|AhR]] pathway by microbial metabolites suppresses iNOS through coordinated inhibition of [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] and [[mapk|MAPK]] cascades, attenuating the pro-inflammatory phenotype in [[astrocyte|astrocytes]] and other innate immune cells.¹

---

## Neuroinflammation in spinal cord injury

iNOS serves as a critical driver of secondary neurodegeneration following spinal cord injury (SCI). TNF-α stimulation of [[astrocyte|astrocytes]] triggers robust iNOS expression alongside other pro-inflammatory mediators—including IL-6, IL-1β, COX-2, CCL2, CXCL2, and CXCL10—that together establish a neurotoxic microenvironment.¹ Elevated iNOS correlates directly with neuronal loss, glial scar formation, and lesion expansion in SCI models, making the enzyme a key therapeutic target for improving neurological recovery.¹ The pro-inflammatory astrocyte phenotype is mediated through [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] and [[mapk|MAPK]] signaling, pathways susceptible to suppression by activation of the [[ahr|AhR]] pathway.¹

---

## Hepatic ischemia-reperfusion injury

In hepatic ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI), iNOS expression is elevated in pro-inflammatory [[macrophage|hepatic macrophages]] and correlates strongly with tissue dysfunction and late-phase injury exacerbation, particularly in aged livers with limited regenerative capacity.² The metabolic derangements accompanying IRI—marked by NAD+ depletion and [[sirt1|SIRT1]] activity loss—permit [[eif2a|eIF2α]] hyperacetylation and endoplasmic reticulum stress, establishing a feed-forward loop of iNOS upregulation and pro-inflammatory cytokine production.² This mechanism is especially pronounced in elderly transplant recipients, where age-associated metabolic fragility amplifies the hepatotoxic inflammatory cascade.²

---

## Therapeutic targeting via AhR activation

[[Indole-3-propionic acid (IPA)]], a tryptophan-derived metabolite produced by the gut microbiota, suppresses iNOS expression in [[astrocyte|astrocytes]] by activating the [[ahr|AhR]] pathway and inhibiting downstream [[nf-kb|NF-κB]]/[[mapk|MAPK]] signaling.¹ In mouse models of spinal cord injury, IPA treatment reduces glial scar formation, enhances neuronal survival, and improves long-term motor function recovery as measured by behavioral and imaging assessments.¹ The therapeutic effect is paralleled by marked reduction in pro-inflammatory mediator production and preserved tissue integrity, establishing IPA as a proof-of-concept metabolite-based approach to neuroinflammatory disease.¹

---

## Therapeutic targeting via NAD+ restoration

[[β-Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN)]] restores hepatic [[sirt1|SIRT1]] activity and suppresses iNOS expression in pro-inflammatory [[macrophage|macrophages]] during ischemia-reperfusion injury.² By restoring [[sirt1|SIRT1]] deacetylase function, NMN reduces [[eif2a|eIF2α]] acetylation, alleviates endoplasmic reticulum stress, and shifts the macrophage phenotype away from pro-inflammatory activation.² In aged mice, NMN supplementation significantly mitigates pro-inflammatory cytokine production—including TNF-α, iNOS, and IL-1β—and improves hepatocyte survival, demonstrating particular therapeutic promise for marginal liver graft viability and transplant outcomes in elderly recipients.²
