**Iba-1** (ionized calcium-binding adapter molecule 1) is a protein marker specifically expressed in [[microglia]], the resident immune cells of the [[central-nervous-system|central nervous system (CNS)]], and serves as a quantifiable indicator of microglial activation and [[neuroinflammation|neuroinflammatory]] status.¹ Elevated Iba-1 levels correlate directly with microglial M1 polarization and pathological neuroinflammatory responses in brain injury conditions, while its reduction by therapeutic interventions marks successful mitigation of microglial-driven neuropathology. The expression of Iba-1 is inversely related to the protective factor [[rbm47|Rbm47]], which co-localizes with microglia and is progressively downregulated as Iba-1 increases during injury-induced microglial activation.¹

---

## Microglial biology and activation

Iba-1 functions as a quantifiable marker of microglial presence and activation state within the nervous system. Under inflammatory or injury conditions, microglia become activated and undergo polarization toward a pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype, characterized by elevated Iba-1 expression alongside increased production of inflammatory cytokines and reactive oxygen species.¹ This microglial response is fundamental to the acute phase of many neuropathological conditions, though sustained or excessive activation can perpetuate tissue damage and contribute to chronic neurodegeneration. The dynamic regulation of Iba-1 during microglial state transitions makes it particularly valuable for monitoring the neuroinflammatory trajectory in disease models.

---

## Neonatal hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy

In neonatal hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy ([[nhie|NHIE]]) — a leading cause of neonatal morbidity and mortality — hypoxia-ischemia triggers robust microglial activation marked by elevated Iba-1 expression and M1 polarization.¹ This pathological microglial response correlates directly with cerebral infarction, neurological deficits, and impaired learning and cognitive function in neonatal rat models. Critically, Iba-1 levels increase inversely with protective factors such as [[rbm47|Rbm47]], which is downregulated in NHIE brain tissue and can be therapeutically restored to suppress microglial M1 activation and mitigate brain injury.

---

## Cerebral palsy

[[cerebral-palsy|Cerebral palsy]] (CP), a chronic motor disorder with high cognitive comorbidity, is characterized by sustained neuroinflammatory responses driven by pathological microglial activation. In moderate-to-severe CP models, elevated Iba-1 reflects ongoing microglial-mediated neuroinflammation that impairs motor coordination and cognitive function.² Therapeutic interventions that reduce Iba-1 expression correspondingly improve neurological outcomes, establishing microglial suppression as a core mechanism for addressing both motor and cognitive dysfunction in cerebral palsy.

---

## Therapeutic modulation and biomarker applications

[[dexmedetomidine|Dexmedetomidine]], an α₂-adrenergic agonist with neuroprotective properties, attenuates NHIE by upregulating [[rbm47|Rbm47]] and suppressing microglial M1 polarization, thereby reducing Iba-1 expression in both in vitro and in vivo models.¹ Similarly, human umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cell-derived exosomes ([[hucmsc-exos|hUCMSC-Exos]]) ameliorate cerebral palsy by suppressing microglial activation and reducing Iba-1 alongside other pro-inflammatory markers including TNF-α and IL-6, while simultaneously promoting remyelination and restoring brain-derived neurotrophic factor.² Across both therapeutic modalities, Iba-1 reduction correlates with improved neurological recovery, positioning it as a sensitive and actionable biomarker for monitoring microglial-driven neuropathology and therapeutic efficacy in brain injury conditions.
