**Epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EMT)** is a fundamental cellular process through which epithelial cells lose cell-cell adhesion and progressively acquire mesenchymal characteristics, including increased motility and invasive capacity.¹ EMT is essential in development, wound healing, and tissue remodeling, but aberrant activation drives cancer metastasis and disease progression. The process is tightly regulated by miRNA signatures that act as endogenous cellular state markers, and emerging biomarkers such as [[kirrel1|KIRREL1]] activate EMT pathways in gastric cancer, correlating with poor patient prognosis.² Exosomal communication—particularly through circular RNAs like [[circ0000549]]—propagates EMT signaling between transformed and normal cells, enabling intercellular transmission of pro-malignant phenotypes.³

---

## Mechanism

EMT is fundamentally characterized by the downregulation of epithelial adhesion molecules and the upregulation of mesenchymal markers, including vimentin, matrix metalloproteinases (MMP2 and MMP9), and morphological changes that facilitate cell migration. Distinct cell states, including EMT, are marked by unique miRNA expression signatures that encode critical information about cellular identity and transition status.¹ These endogenous miRNA landscapes have emerged as powerful tools for detecting and responding to EMT activation in real time. [[kirrel1|KIRREL1]], a cell-surface protein, acts as an oncogenic driver by directly activating the EMT transcriptional program, promoting the expression of mesenchymal markers while suppressing epithelial characteristics.²

Exosomal communication plays a pivotal role in spreading EMT signals between cell populations. Circular RNAs such as [[circ0000549]] are enriched in exosomes derived from transformed cells and function as microRNA sponges, sequestering [[miR-15b-5p]] and thereby de-repressing its target genes. Through the miR-15b-5p/[[kif1b|KIF1B]] axis, exosomal [[circ0000549]] promotes both EMT and cancer stem cell properties, establishing a bidirectional communication channel by which malignantly transformed cells reprogram normal epithelial neighbors toward mesenchymal and stem-like states.³

## Gastric cancer and environmental carcinogenesis

EMT activation represents a critical bottleneck in gastric cancer progression, particularly in contexts of environmental exposure. N-methyl-N'-nitro-N-nitrosoguanidine (MNNG), a potent environmental carcinogen, induces malignant transformation of normal gastric epithelial cells and triggers the release of pro-metastatic exosomes enriched in [[circ0000549]].³ These exosomes act as intercellular vehicles, transferring oncogenic signals to neighboring normal cells and promoting their conversion toward a malignant phenotype. Serum exosomes from gastric cancer patients with a history of high nitrite exposure carry the same [[circ0000549]]-driven signature, suggesting that environmental carcinogens establish a self-propagating EMT-driven ecosystem within the tumor microenvironment.

[[kirrel1|KIRREL1]] upregulation is strongly associated with poor progression-free, disease-specific, and overall survival in gastric cancer patients.² Functionally, KIRREL1 overexpression accelerates gastric cancer cell proliferation, migration, and invasion while simultaneously activating the EMT pathway. Conversely, KIRREL1 knockdown sensitizes gastric cancer cells to 5-fluorouracil chemotherapy, suggesting that EMT induction mediated by KIRREL1 contributes to therapeutic resistance.² This linkage between EMT activation and chemoresistance identifies KIRREL1-driven EMT as a critical barrier to systemic therapy.

## Development and wound healing

Beyond malignancy, EMT is indispensable in development, where transient and tightly regulated EMT events sculpt tissue architecture and enable cell migration during gastrulation, neural crest formation, and organ development.¹ During wound healing, EMT-like transitions in fibroblasts and epithelial-derived cells facilitate wound closure and matrix remodeling. Unlike cancer-associated EMT, these physiological transitions are reversible and context-dependent, underscoring the importance of distinguishing regulatory signals in pathological versus developmental EMT.

---

## Detection and therapeutic targeting via miRNA-CRISPR systems

Recent advances in synthetic biology have harnessed EMT-specific miRNA signatures as endogenous sensors for precise cell-state monitoring and intervention. miRNA-directed CRISPR systems leverage ribozyme-scaffolded, miRNA-sensing constructs that undergo polymerase-driven transcription only in the presence of EMT-specific miRNAs.¹ This platform enables selective elimination of cells undergoing mesenchymal transition, dynamic filtering of heterogeneous cell populations, and dynamic reprogramming of cell-state identities without external triggers. Such systems open new therapeutic avenues in cancer therapy by directly targeting cells exhibiting active EMT, while preserving normal epithelial cell populations. The integration of miRNA surveillance with CRISPR-based effectors also extends to regenerative medicine, where precise discrimination between physiological and pathological EMT enables selective promotion or suppression of cell-state transitions according to therapeutic context.¹
