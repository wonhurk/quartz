**FMS-Like Tyrosine Kinase 3 (FLT3)** is a receptor tyrosine kinase that undergoes oncogenic mutations in approximately 30% of acute myeloid leukemia (AML) cases, where these alterations are strongly associated with poor prognosis and high relapse risk.¹ The most clinically significant FLT3 variant is internal tandem duplication ([[flt3-itd|FLT3-ITD]]), which confers ligand-independent activation and drives leukemic cell survival through phosphorylation of critical downstream signaling molecules including [[stat5]], [[erk]], and [[akt1]]. Despite the emergence of targeted FLT3 inhibitors as important therapeutic agents, acquired resistance remains a major clinical challenge, frequently mediated by secondary tyrosine kinase domain mutations such as [[f691l|F691L]]. Recent discovery of [[nintedanib]]—a multi-kinase inhibitor previously approved for fibrotic lung disease—has identified a potent FLT3 inhibitor capable of suppressing FLT3 autophosphorylation and uniquely overcoming the F691L gatekeeper mutation.

---

## Mechanism

FLT3 is a member of the class III receptor tyrosine kinase family, functioning as a critical regulator of hematopoietic stem cell proliferation under normal circumstances. The oncogenic transformation of FLT3 occurs when the kinase domain undergoes internal tandem duplication, a chromosomal rearrangement that results in an in-frame duplication of exons encoding portions of the kinase domain and juxtamembrane region. This [[flt3-itd|FLT3-ITD]] mutation leads to ligand-independent constitutive activation of the receptor, driving uncontrolled autophosphorylation.¹ Once activated, FLT3-ITD phosphorylates downstream signaling molecules on tyrosine residues, particularly activating the [[stat5|STAT5]], [[erk|ERK]], and [[akt1|AKT1]] pathways, which collectively promote cell survival, proliferation, and prevention of apoptosis in leukemic blasts.

---

## Role in acute myeloid leukemia

FLT3 mutations are among the most frequent molecular alterations in AML, occurring in roughly one-third of newly diagnosed cases and conferring a particularly adverse prognosis.¹ Patients carrying FLT3-ITD mutations exhibit shorter overall survival, higher cumulative incidence of relapse, and reduced response to conventional chemotherapy agents. The presence of FLT3-ITD in AML is often accompanied by other genetic alterations that cooperate to maintain a leukemic stem cell phenotype. In FLT3-mutant AML, the [[erk|ERK]] and [[stat5|STAT5]] signaling axes become hyper-activated, promoting both cell cycle progression and suppression of differentiation, establishing the proliferative advantage characteristic of FLT3-driven leukemias.

---

## Resistance mechanisms

While FLT3 inhibitors have improved clinical outcomes in FLT3-ITD-positive AML, acquired resistance remains a substantial obstacle to sustained therapeutic benefit. Secondary mutations within the FLT3 tyrosine kinase domain represent a major mechanism of resistance, with the [[f691l|F691L]] gatekeeper mutation being particularly prevalent and clinically significant.¹ F691L substitutions alter the kinase domain architecture in a manner that reduces binding affinity for many conventional FLT3 inhibitors, thereby conferring resistance while maintaining the pathogenic activity of the FLT3-ITD allele. The emergence of F691L-harboring clones under selective pressure from FLT3 inhibitor therapy exemplifies the adaptive evolution of leukemic cells and highlights the pressing need for inhibitors capable of suppressing resistant variants.

---

## Nintedanib and breakthrough resistance management

[[Nintedanib]], an oral multi-kinase inhibitor previously approved for treatment of fibrotic pulmonary diseases, has emerged as a potent FLT3 inhibitor with remarkable activity against both FLT3-ITD-positive AML and resistant variants.¹ In cellular and biochemical assays, nintedanib demonstrated robust suppression of FLT3 autophosphorylation and downstream [[stat5|STAT5]], [[erk|ERK]], and [[akt1|AKT1]] signaling, leading to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis in FLT3-ITD-mutant cell lines and primary AML blasts. Notably, nintedanib retained efficacy against the [[f691l|F691L]] gatekeeper mutation both in vitro and in vivo, overcoming a resistance mechanism that renders many first-generation FLT3 inhibitors ineffective. This dual capacity—potent activity against FLT3-ITD-positive leukemias combined with the ability to suppress gatekeeper mutations—positions nintedanib as a valuable therapeutic agent for managing both treatment-naïve and inhibitor-resistant FLT3-driven AML.

---

## FLT3 mutations in minimally differentiated AML

FLT3 mutations occur with elevated frequency in [[aml-m0|minimally differentiated acute myeloid leukemia (AML-M0)]], a rare and aggressive AML subtype characterized by immature hematopoietic stem cell-like features and poor treatment outcomes.² Comprehensive multi-omics analyses of pediatric AML-M0 cases have revealed that signaling pathway mutations, including those affecting FLT3 and [[jak|JAK]], are present in the majority of cases and frequently co-occur with loss-of-function [[runx1|RUNX1]] alterations. When FLT3 mutations arise in the context of [[runx1|RUNX1]] disruption, they synergistically promote stem cell-like transcriptional programs and confer resistance to multiple chemotherapy agents, including cytarabine and anthracyclines. This molecular cooperation between FLT3 activation and RUNX1 loss exemplifies how multiple genetic alterations interact to establish both biological stemness and therapeutic resistance in AML-M0, underscoring the complexity of treating this aggressive disease subset.
