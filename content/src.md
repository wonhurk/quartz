**SRC** is a non-receptor protein tyrosine kinase serving as a critical hub protein in cellular signaling networks, functioning at the interface between neurovascular protection and developmental patterning. The kinase acts as a central interaction partner with the protein tyrosine phosphatase [[ptpn11|SHP2]], whose mutations can alter conformational dynamics and enhance SRC engagement, precipitating pathogenic signaling cascades.¹ In ischemic stroke, SRC functions as one of five key molecular targets through which [[nadph|NADPH]] mediates neuroprotection by preserving blood-brain barrier integrity and promoting angiogenesis.² Aberrant SRC-[[ptpn11|SHP2]] interactions driven by specific PTPN11 variants underlie the pathogenesis of Noonan syndrome, a developmental disorder with distinctive facial features, growth delay, and cardiac involvement.¹

---

## Mechanism

SRC operates as a non-receptor tyrosine kinase that phosphorylates substrates in response to upstream signals from diverse cellular sources. As a hub protein, SRC mediates critical interactions with multiple binding partners, including the adaptor protein [[grb2|GRB2]] and the phosphatase [[ptpn11|SHP2]]. The SHP2-SRC axis forms a central regulatory node in RAS/MAPK signaling cascades, where conformational changes in SHP2 directly modulate kinase-substrate interactions and phosphorylation efficiency. This interaction is particularly sensitive to point mutations in [[ptpn11|PTPN11]] that disrupt the phosphatase's normal structural equilibrium. Such mutations can shift SHP2 into an activated "open" conformation that preferentially strengthens SRC binding and subsequent activation of [[grb2|GRB2]] and downstream effectors, resulting in sustained RAS/MAPK pathway hyperactivation.¹

## Ischemic stroke neuroprotection

In ischemic stroke, SRC emerges as a central molecular target in [[nadph|NADPH]]-mediated neuroprotection. Network pharmacology analysis and molecular docking studies identified SRC alongside [[hif-1a|HIF-1α]], [[nlrp3|NLRP3]], [[casp3|CASP3]], and [[akt1|AKT1]] as five key targets through which NADPH exerts cytoprotective effects following acute cerebral ischemia.² In vivo models employing transient middle cerebral artery occlusion (tMCAO) demonstrated that NADPH administration at optimal doses substantially reduces infarct volume by approximately fifty percent, attenuates cerebral edema, and improves neurological functional recovery. Mechanistically, NADPH-mediated effects involving SRC contribute to blood-brain barrier preservation through upregulation of tight junction proteins, including a ~29% increase in zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1) expression.² The pathway simultaneously suppresses matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP9), a barrier-disrupting protease, and reduces caveolin-1 expression, collectively stabilizing endothelial tight junctions and limiting inflammatory infiltration. Beyond BBB preservation, NADPH enhances endothelial cell proliferation and angiogenic signaling, facilitating neovascularization and restoration of perfusion in the ischemic penumbra.

## Noonan syndrome pathogenesis

SRC plays a prominent role in the pathogenic mechanism of Noonan syndrome, a common autosomal dominant developmental disorder characterized by distinctive facial dysmorphism, short stature, and cardiac malformations. Heterozygous mutations in [[ptpn11|PTPN11]], encoding the protein tyrosine phosphatase [[ptpn11|SHP2]], constitute the most prevalent genetic cause of Noonan syndrome. The p.Asn308Ser variant exemplifies this mechanism: the mutation disrupts key hydrogen bonds within the SHP2 catalytic domain and remodels the protein-protein interaction network, shifting the phosphatase into an activated conformation.¹ This structural change specifically enhances interactions with hub proteins including SRC and [[grb2|GRB2]], resulting in sustained and amplified RAS/MAPK pathway hyperactivation.¹ Phosphoproteomic analysis revealed that this enhanced signaling induces extensive phosphorylation events, particularly enriched in nuclear and chromatin-associated proteins implicated in transcriptional regulation and developmental patterning, thereby underpinning the cardinal clinical features of the syndrome.
