**AKT** (protein kinase B/PKB) is a serine/threonine kinase functioning as a central hub for cell survival, proliferation, and metabolic homeostasis. Activated downstream of [[pi3k|PI3K]] signaling, AKT regulates critical nodes in cancer biology and hepatic lipid metabolism.¹ The kinase drives malignant transformation of gastric epithelial cells through exosomal-mediated intercellular communication and promotes hallmark cancer phenotypes including epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and cancer stemness. Beyond oncology, dysregulated AKT signaling contributes to [[nafld|nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)]] by aberrantly controlling the expression of genes governing lipogenesis, lipid oxidation, and VLDL secretion.

---

## Mechanism

AKT is activated by [[pi3k|phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase (PI3K)]] signaling in response to growth factor stimulation, establishing the PI3K/AKT axis as a master regulator of anabolic and anti-apoptotic programs. Once phosphorylated and translocated to the membrane, AKT phosphorylates a broad spectrum of substrates controlling protein synthesis, glucose metabolism, and mitochondrial dynamics. The pathway is subject to negative regulation by phosphatases including PTEN, which dephosphorylates and inactivates AKT, allowing cells to integrate survival signals with metabolic state and growth constraints.

## Gastric cancer

AKT plays a pivotal role in MNNG-induced gastric carcinogenesis, wherein malignantly transformed gastric epithelial cells secrete exosomes that propagate oncogenic signals to neighboring normal cells. Exosomal circular RNA circ0000549, markedly upregulated in MNNG-exposed GES-1 cells and serum exosomes from gastric cancer patients with high nitrite exposure, functions as a competing endogenous RNA to suppress miR-15b-5p, thereby derepressing [[kif1b|KIF1B]] and promoting malignant transformation.¹ Circ0000549 overexpression enhances EMT-related markers and cancer stemness properties, driving MNNG-induced gastric carcinogenesis in nude mouse xenograft models. The exosomal packaging of circ0000549 enables intercellular transfer of oncogenic signals, establishing a feed-forward loop wherein transformed cells continuously educate adjacent epithelium toward malignancy.

## Metabolic regulation and NAFLD

AKT controls hepatic lipogenesis through phosphorylation-dependent suppression of key catabolic and export pathways. In hepatocytes, AKT activation upregulates lipogenic enzymes [[acc|acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC)]] and [[scd1|stearoyl-CoA desaturase 1 (SCD1)]], promoting fatty acid synthesis and triglyceride accumulation. Conversely, AKT suppression downregulates [[ppar-alpha|peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor-alpha (PPARα)]] and carnitine palmitoyltransferase, impairing fatty acid oxidation and energy expenditure. Additionally, AKT inhibition reduces microsomal triglyceride transfer protein (MTTP) expression, curtailing VLDL assembly and hepatic lipid export.² Dysregulation of this AKT-dependent metabolic program—particularly its suppression by [[ctsg|cathepsin G (CTSG)]] in the setting of nutrient excess—drives hepatic steatosis, impaired glucose tolerance, and the pathogenic progression to [[nafld]]. In [[nash|nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH)]], elevated hepatic CTSG correlates with disease severity, and CTSG-driven suppression of AKT signaling exacerbates lipid deposition and metabolic dysfunction.
