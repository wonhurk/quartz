**Renal cell carcinoma (RCC)** is an advanced malignancy of the kidney characterized by marked resistance to current therapeutic combinations. The pathological basis of this resistance lies not in monolithic drug insensitivity, but in the failure of anti-angiogenic agents and immune checkpoint inhibitors to act in a spatiotemporally coordinated manner — they engage their molecular targets sequentially rather than simultaneously, preventing realization of their therapeutic synergy within the narrow vascular normalization window.¹ Two complementary resistance mechanisms now define the disease: defective vascular remodeling preventing T cell infiltration and, independently, upregulation of the [[usp20|USP20]]-[[gpx4|GPX4]] deubiquitinase axis, which enables cancer cells to evade ferroptosis-mediated cell death triggered by first-line [[sunitinib]] and [[sorafenib]].²

---

## Clinical challenge and disease burden

Advanced renal cell carcinoma represents one of the most therapeutically intractable solid tumors. While [[sunitinib]] and [[sorafenib]] remain standard-of-care tyrosine kinase inhibitors for metastatic disease, their efficacy is rapidly lost to acquired resistance. Combination regimens pairing these anti-angiogenic agents with immune checkpoint inhibitors—intended to normalize the tumor microvasculature while simultaneously releasing immunosuppression—fail to achieve coordinated action. The fundamental problem is temporal: anti-angiogenic signaling through [[vegf]]/[[tie2]] normalization and immune activation through checkpoint blockade operate on different kinetic timescales, and the therapeutic window in which both are optimally active narrows precipitously. As a result, the synergistic potential of these combinations is systematically unrealized.¹

---

## Vascular normalization and immune remodeling

The [[ring1|RING1]] multi-target inhibitor represents an emerging solution to the synchronization problem. Unlike sequential drug combinations, RING1 integrates three functional modules—VEGF/Tie2 targeting, TIGIT blockade, and immune costimulation—into a single molecular entity, enabling simultaneous vascular normalization and effector T cell infiltration.¹ Upon tumor accumulation, RING1 self-assembles into supramolecular nanonetworks that amplify target engagement and extend intratumoral retention, thereby sustaining coordinated pathway inhibition across the tumor microenvironment. In preclinical orthotopic models, RING1 enhanced vascular normalization 1.8-fold compared to standard clinical combination therapy, an increase sufficient to shift the immunological composition of the tumor. Specifically, RING1 reduced the abundance of immunosuppressive Tie2-expressing monocytes—a population that actively reinforces immune evasion—while simultaneously elevating interferon-gamma and granzyme B secretion by infiltrating T cells.¹ The net effect is superior tumor suppression and reduced metastatic burden even in disseminated disease models.

## Ferroptosis evasion and metabolic vulnerability

The second major resistance mechanism in TKI-resistant RCC involves evasion of ferroptosis through stabilization of [[gpx4|GPX4]], the central negative regulator of iron-dependent cell death. In tumors that have developed resistance to [[sunitinib]] and [[sorafenib]], upregulation of [[usp20|USP20]]—an ubiquitin-specific peptidase—enables deubiquitination of GPX4, preventing its proteasomal degradation and maintaining high cellular antioxidant capacity.² This adaptation is not tumor-type-specific; USP20 and GPX4 are co-overexpressed across both RCC and non-small cell lung cancer in patients with poor prognosis, implicating the axis as a general mechanism of TKI tolerance. Mechanistically, USP20 removes K48-linked polyubiquitin chains from GPX4, a modification normally targeted for destruction by the proteasome during ferroptosis induction. By preserving GPX4 levels, resistant cancer cells maintain glutathione peroxidase activity and continue to detoxify lipid hydroperoxides even under conditions of TKI exposure that would normally overwhelm antioxidant defenses in drug-sensitive cells.²

---

## Integrated therapeutic strategy

The distinct resistance mechanisms—impaired vascular normalization and ferroptosis evasion—present complementary opportunities for therapeutic intervention. Combining [[ring1|RING1]]-mediated vascular remodeling with [[usp20|USP20]] inhibition addresses both pathways simultaneously: vascular normalization restores immune infiltration and removes the hypoxic sanctuary that enables immunosuppression, while ferroptosis sensitization through USP20 inhibition resensitizes tumor cells to ferroptosis induction by [[sorafenib]] and other TKIs. Genetic ablation of USP20 in resistant xenografts markedly sensitizes them to ferroptosis, and pharmacological inhibition of USP20 demonstrably resensitizes resistant tumors to [[sorafenib]]-induced tumor growth suppression in vivo.² This convergent approach—restoring vascular immune homeostasis while simultaneously exploiting metabolic vulnerability to ferroptosis—represents a new paradigm for overcoming acquired TKI resistance in advanced RCC.
