**Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ)** is a nuclear receptor that functions as a master regulator of both metabolic homeostasis and neuroprotection, transducing ligand signals into transcriptional programs governing adipogenesis, lipid handling, and cell survival.¹ As a ligand-dependent transcription factor, PPARγ exhibits remarkable selectivity in response to different ligand classes: while classical agonists like rosiglitazone promote robust adipogenic gene expression, natural pentacyclic triterpenoids from [[rosa-canina]] act as selective antagonists that suppress lipid accumulation through distinct molecular mechanisms.¹ In the nervous system, PPARγ operates as a critical defender against white matter injury following [[subarachnoid-hemorrhage]], functioning through a conserved [[prdx1]]-dependent antioxidant axis that protects oligodendrocytes from apoptosis and promotes remyelination.²

---

## Mechanism

PPARγ binds ligands within a hydrophobic pocket formed by the ligand-binding domain, inducing conformational changes that govern cofactor recruitment and transcriptional activity. The binding of classical agonists stabilizes the C-terminal helices (H11–H12) and facilitates the formation of a functional AF-2 activation surface, enabling recruitment of the steroid receptor coactivator complex and TRAP220 to promoter regions.¹ In contrast, the natural antagonists [[pomolic-acid]] and [[hederagenin]] from [[rosa-canina]] adopt a distinctive binding mode that destabilizes these critical helices and physically disrupts the AF-2 coactivator interface, preventing rosiglitazone-induced transactivation without exerting intrinsic agonist activity.¹ At the cellular level, PPARγ-mediated transcription directly depends on the canonical PPAR response elements (PPREs) present in target gene promoters; selective antagonism thus permits differential modulation of downstream targets based on promoter architecture and cofactor availability.

---

## Metabolic regulation and natural antagonism

PPARγ plays a central role in adipogenesis and lipid metabolism, where its transcriptional output determines the balance between energy storage and mobilization. Classical agonists such as rosiglitazone promote the differentiation of 3T3-L1 preadipocytes into mature adipocytes and upregulate lipogenic genes encoding [[fatty-acid-synthase]] and [[acoyl-coa-synthase]] family members, resulting in robust triglyceride accumulation.¹ The natural triterpenoids [[pomolic-acid]] and [[hederagenin]] from [[rosa-canina]] antagonize this pathway through opposing mechanisms. Pomolic acid acts as a neutral antagonist that broadly represses adipogenic and lipogenic gene expression, preventing both TRAP220 recruitment and triglyceride accumulation in a manner independent of intrinsic agonist activity.¹ Hederagenin exhibits selective modulation, suppressing lipid-handling target genes involved in triglyceride synthesis while permitting basal expression of other PPARγ-responsive genes, thereby limiting lipid burden without wholesale silencing of the receptor.¹ These findings reveal that structurally related triterpenoids can exploit subtle differences in the ligand-binding pocket to achieve distinct regulatory outcomes, offering a mechanistic template for rational design of metabolic therapeutics.

## Neuroprotection and white matter injury

Beyond metabolic functions, PPARγ serves as a master regulator of oligodendrocyte survival and white matter integrity through activation of the PPAR​γ/[[prdx1]]/JNK signaling axis. Following [[subarachnoid-hemorrhage]], reactive oxygen species and inflammatory insults trigger apoptosis of mature oligodendrocytes and impair differentiation of oligodendrocyte precursor cells, leading to demyelination and persistent neurological dysfunction.² This pathophysiology is tightly controlled by the miRNA [[mir-27a-3p]], which is markedly upregulated in peripheral blood [[exosomes]] and white matter tissue after SAH and directly suppresses PPARγ expression.² When miR-27a-3p levels rise, PPARγ is downregulated, causing secondary loss of [[prdx1]]-dependent antioxidant signaling and activation of pro-apoptotic JNK phosphorylation cascades.² Conversely, suppression of miR-27a-3p via antagomir-loaded [[exosomes]] delivered intranasally restores PPARγ expression in oligodendrocytes, reactivates the [[prdx1]] antioxidant axis, and inhibits JNK-mediated apoptosis, thereby promoting oligodendrocyte survival and remyelination.² This pathway is sufficient to rescue electrophysiological function and neurological outcomes in post-hemorrhagic white matter injury models.

## Exosome-mediated therapeutic delivery

The discovery that [[exosomes]] derived from activated microglia (BV2 cells) can efficiently carry miRNA antagomirs and deliver them to oligodendrocytes via intranasal administration has opened a noninvasive route for PPARγ-axis modulation. When loaded with miR-27a-3p antagomirs, these [[exosomes]] cross the blood-brain barrier and target oligodendrocytes with high selectivity, reducing miR-27a-3p levels in white matter tissue and restoring PPARγ expression.² This approach not only suppresses oligodendrocyte apoptosis but also enhances the proliferation and differentiation of oligodendrocyte precursor cells, accelerating myelin repair and functional recovery.² The use of [[exosomes]] as vehicles for miRNA inhibition circumvents many drawbacks of systemic delivery, including off-target effects and degradation, making it a promising platform for translating PPARγ-modulating therapies to clinical subarachnoid hemorrhage.

## Natural pentacyclic triterpenoids as selective modulators

[[Rosa-canina]] (wild rose) contains a rich reservoir of bioactive pentacyclic triterpenoids with PPARγ-modulatory properties. The two characterized compounds, [[pomolic-acid]] and [[hederagenin]], exhibit a rare combination: direct binding confirmation via time-resolved fluorescence resonance energy transfer (TR-FRET) assays combined with selective repression of adipogenesis without intrinsic agonist activity.¹ Pomolic acid adopts a betulinic acid–like binding mode that destabilizes the ligand-binding domain's helical architecture, converting PPARγ into an antagonistic state that broadly suppresses rosiglitazone-induced transcription.¹ Hederagenin achieves lipid suppression through a more nuanced mechanism, selectively silencing the subset of PPARγ target genes involved in fatty-acid synthesis and triglyceride accumulation while preserving basal gene expression.¹ Both compounds reduce lipid accumulation in adipocyte cultures to a similar degree, yet their molecular footprints diverge significantly, suggesting that diverse dietary triterpenoids may offer distinct therapeutic windows for metabolic intervention.

---

## Therapeutic potential

PPARγ has emerged as a versatile target for both metabolic and neurological disorders. In the metabolic realm, the identification of natural antagonists that suppress lipid accumulation without agonist activity offers an alternative to classical thiazolidinediones, which promote fluid retention and weight gain through robust PPARγ agonism.¹ Natural triterpenoids represent a distinct pharmacological approach that may enhance compliance and reduce adverse effects in metabolic disease management. In the neurological context, the PPARγ/[[prdx1]]/JNK axis represents a tractable target for post-hemorrhagic white matter injury, where restoration of oligodendrocyte survival and myelin integrity directly correlates with improved motor and cognitive outcomes.² The use of [[exosomes]] to deliver miR-27a-3p antagomirs demonstrates that cellular and molecular insights can be rapidly translated into noninvasive therapeutic platforms, paving the way for bedside application in acute stroke and hemorrhage.
