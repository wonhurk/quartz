ROS generation has emerged as a potent strategy against antibiotic-resistant bacterial infections. PEGylated chitosan-functionalized bimetallic composite nanoparticles (Cu-Mn NPs) incorporating manganese dioxide into a copper-gallic acid complex synergistically induce bacterial cuproptosis-like death by generating abundant ROS that disrupt membrane integrity, interfere with the respiratory chain, and reduce virulence.¹⁶ In methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA)-infected pneumonia models, Cu-Mn NPs significantly improved survival rates and reduced inflammatory injury without systemic toxicity.¹⁶ The mucus-penetrating capabilities and lung-retention properties of these nanoparticles enable effective biofilm elimination and promote macrophage polarization toward the M1 phenotype.

In dental applications, hybrid peroxidase-like nanozymes composed of Prussian blue nanocube cores decorated with molybdenum disulfide shells and gold nanoparticles (PB@MoS₂@Au) integrate enhanced catalytic activity with 37.4% photothermal conversion efficiency.¹⁷ Combined treatment with low-concentration hydrogen peroxide (200 μM) and near-infrared irradiation achieved bactericidal rates exceeding 95% against seven representative oral bacterial strains and eliminated over 99.5% of Enterococcus faecalis biofilms from root canals within minutes.¹⁷ This approach addresses the limited efficacy of traditional disinfectants in treating dental caries, endodontic infections, and periodontitis.

## Wound healing and tissue regeneration

In chronic diabetic wounds characterized by excessive ROS accumulation and unresolved inflammation, ROS-scavenging hydrogels accelerate healing through multiple mechanisms. Injectable pH/ROS-dual-responsive antibacterial hydrogels encapsulating periodontal ligament stem cells (PDLSCs) enable sustained release of [[epigallocatechin-gallate|epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG)]] and tobramycin in response to pathological ROS levels.⁴ This design effectively scavenges intracellular ROS, reprograms macrophages toward pro-regenerative phenotypes, and enhances stem cell survival under oxidative stress, significantly accelerating wound closure and promoting angiogenesis while markedly reducing scar formation.⁴

Semiclosed cyclic hemiaminal-based multistimuli-responsive hydrogels synthesized via aldimine condensation of poly(ethylene glycol) diamines and polyformaldehyde degrade in response to acidic pH, ROS, and glutathione.⁸ These hydrogels release formaldehyde under weakly acidic conditions (pH 6.5) with 74% release rate, displaying significant antibacterial effects in acute bacterial-infected wounds, while simultaneously scavenging overexpressed ROS in chronic diabetic wounds to modulate the wound microenvironment and accelerate healing.⁸

## Bone regeneration

Excessive ROS and unresolved inflammation create a hostile microenvironment that compromises bone regeneration in critical-sized defects. Pomegranate-inspired bionic microgel/periosteum composites featuring hierarchical "membrane-seed" modular architecture integrate periosteum-mimetic PDA/PLLA membranes for ROS counteraction with conductive GelMA/PEDOT:PSS microgels for enhanced and prolonged ROS clearance.⁶ This system enables macrophage polarization toward reparative phenotypes, remarkable ROS clearance, and osteogenic differentiation, further enhanced under electrical stimulation.⁶ Transcriptomic analyses coupled with Seahorse metabolic measurements indicate that the composite remodels macrophage energy metabolism, with intercellular mitochondrial transfer observed as a potential contributory mechanism in macrophage-osteoblast coupling-based bone regeneration.⁶ In rat critical-sized mandibular defect models, implantation improved bone formation and defect bridging while moderating early inflammatory signals.

## Non-disease contexts

Beyond therapeutic applications, ROS scavenging plays protective roles in diverse physiological stress responses. In sepsis-induced [[acute-lung-injury|acute lung injury (ALI)]], polymyxin B-antioxidant nanoassemblies (PV NPs) co-assembled from ester-linked α-tocopherol-gallic acid conjugate and polymyxin B integrate antibacterial activity and endotoxin neutralization with broad-spectrum ROS scavenging while mitigating polymyxin B's dose-limiting nephrotoxicity.³ In murine models, PV NPs attenuated lung inflammation and oxidative stress, conferring 75% survival rate in cecal ligation-and-puncture models of polymicrobial sepsis, significantly outperforming either component alone.³

Sleep deprivation induces intestinal barrier dysfunction through ROS-mediated mechanisms. Nootkatone, a sesquiterpenoid from grapefruit, ameliorates sleep deprivation-induced colonic injury by activating the [[nrf2|Nrf2]] pathway, reducing ROS accumulation, and improving mitochondrial function in colonic tissues.¹⁵ This intervention upregulated epithelial barrier proteins, induced mucin production, suppressed proinflammatory cytokines, and restored gut microbial composition.¹⁵

In thermobiology, genome-wide CRISPR/Cas9 screening in Bombyx mori identified membrane-bound alkaline phosphatase-like gene (BmM-ALP) as a vital thermoregulator that triggers pleiotropic protection by decreasing ROS, suppressing apoptosis, and reprogramming mitochondrial metabolism via vitamin B1 phosphorylation.¹¹ BmM-ALP overexpression significantly enhanced thermal tolerance and prolonged survival under heat stress, establishing a regulatory axis linking energy homeostasis to thermal adaptation.¹¹

## Nanotechnology platforms

Advanced nanotechnologies leverage ROS-responsive chemistry for triggered drug release and integrate ROS-generating catalysts with immunomodulatory payloads. Near-infrared light-switchable prodrug nanosystems composed of rare earth nanoparticles and Ce-UiO-66-NH₂ enable controlled release of L-arginine and efficient production of reactive nitrogen species (RNS) through cascade enzymatic catalysis.¹ The 980-nm laser-responsive system exhibits excellent NIR-II fluorescence imaging and possesses Förster energy transfer-enhanced cascade enzymatic activity, with catalase- and oxidase-like activity converting O₂ into ROS that cleave ROS-sensitive thioketal bonds to release L-arginine for subsequent RNS generation.¹

Thermoresponsive chitosan nanocomposite-based double-network hydrogels capable of in situ gelation at physiological temperature form durable depots for sustained tumor microenvironment-responsive release.⁹ These systems integrate [[indocyanine-green|ICG]]-loaded gold nanorods within boronic acid-modified mesoporous silica, enabling synergistic photothermal and ROS-mediated tumor cell apoptosis, tumor-associated macrophage repolarization, and chitosan-modulated immune activation.⁹

## Antioxidant pathway regulation

Targeting endogenous antioxidant systems has emerged as a complementary strategy to modulate cellular ROS levels. [[trxr|Thioredoxin reductase (TrxR)]] serves as a critical therapeutic target owing to its overexpression in cancer cells, particularly [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|TNBC]].¹⁰ TrxR-targeted Pt(IV) prodrugs inhibit TrxR1 both in vitro and in vivo, inducing ferroptosis through glutathione depletion, lipid peroxidation accumulation, and [[gpx4|GPX4]] deactivation while triggering excessive ROS accumulation that activates endoplasmic reticulum stress-mediated [[immunogenic-cell-death|immunogenic cell death]].¹⁰

Copper chelation represents an orthogonal approach exploiting cancer cells' "copper addiction." Rationally designed Schiff base copper chelators incorporating triphenylphosphonium modifications disrupt redox homeostasis by depleting copper, leading to ROS-mediated mitochondrial damage and necroptosis with potent, nontoxic tumor suppression in vivo.¹³

The [[nrf2|Nrf2]] pathway serves as the master regulator of antioxidant responses across tissue types. Nootkatone activation of Nrf2 reduced ROS accumulation and improved mitochondrial function in sleep deprivation-induced gut barrier disruption, highlighting the pathway's central role in maintaining redox homeostasis under physiological stress.¹⁵
