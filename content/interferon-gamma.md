**Interferon gamma (IFN-γ)** is a pro-inflammatory cytokine primarily produced by activated CD8+ T lymphocytes and serves as a critical marker of robust antigen-specific and antitumor immune responses across multiple therapeutic contexts.¹ The cytokine functions as both a direct effector molecule and an immune orchestrator, stimulating downstream chemokine secretion — notably [[cxcl9|CXCL9]] — and activating macrophage-mediated antitumor responses essential for tumor control.² IFN-γ production indicates enhanced antitumor immunity, including CD8+ T cell infiltration, effector functions, and efficacy of immunotherapies such as checkpoint inhibitors, engineered CAR T cells, and T-cell receptor-modified cytotoxic lymphocytes, as well as protective responses to vaccination against infectious agents.³⁴

---

## Mechanism and cellular sources

IFN-γ is secreted predominantly by activated CD8+ T lymphocytes following antigen recognition, though natural killer cells and certain innate lymphoid populations also contribute under specific inflammatory conditions. Upon secretion, the cytokine binds to the interferon gamma receptor (IFNGR) on target cells, initiating JAK-STAT signaling cascades that drive transcriptional programs governing antimicrobial defense, antigen presentation, and immune cell recruitment. In the tumor microenvironment, IFN-γ promotes vascular normalization and reduces infiltration of immunosuppressive cell populations, thereby enhancing the efficacy of combination therapies.⁵ The cytokine also primes macrophages for enhanced phagocytosis and chemokine secretion, particularly under glucocorticoid-modulated conditions where C1Q+ macrophages exhibit IFN-γ-dependent [[cxcl9|CXCL9]] production that recruits additional effector T cells.⁶

## Cancer immunotherapy

IFN-γ production is a cardinal biomarker of successful cancer immunotherapy across multiple modalities. In chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cell engineering, lipid nanoparticle-mediated in vivo delivery of CAR-encoding mRNA yields a 2.9-fold increase in IFN-γ secretion and a corresponding 2.3-fold enhancement in tumor growth inhibition compared to benchmark formulations, underscoring the direct correlation between IFN-γ output and therapeutic efficacy.⁷ Similarly, cytotoxic T lymphocytes regenerated from induced pluripotent stem cells and engineered via genome editing to express tumor antigen-specific T-cell receptors at the endogenous TRAC locus produce IFN-γ in an antigen-specific manner and demonstrate robust cytotoxic activity against cognate target cells.⁸

Radiotherapy combined with adeno-associated virus delivery of inducible IL-12 generates a highly immunostimulatory tumor microenvironment, leading to local and systemic antitumor responses that proceed in an IFN-γ- and FAS-dependent manner capable of overcoming common immune-evasion mechanisms.⁹ In renal cell carcinoma, the multi-target peptide inhibitor RING1 — which simultaneously induces vascular normalization and immune checkpoint blockade — elevates IFN-γ and granzyme B secretion by 1.8-fold compared to clinical combination therapy, demonstrating superior tumor-suppressive and anti-metastatic efficacy.⁵

Ferroptosis-inducing nanoplatforms that combine redox-driven catalytic activation with localized xCT blockade increase CD8+ T-cell infiltration and IFN-γ production in colorectal cancer models, reflecting enhanced antitumor immune activity alongside ferroptotic pressure.¹⁰ The synergy between ferroptotic cell death and IFN-γ-mediated immune activation highlights the cytokine's role in coordinating metabolic and immunological tumor suppression.

## Vaccine responses

IFN-γ secretion by antigen-specific CD8+ T cells is a hallmark of protective immunity following vaccination against infectious pathogens. A circular RNA vaccine encoding a fusion antigen of factor H binding protein and Neisserial heparin-binding antigen elicited potent IFN-γ-secreting CD8+ T-cell responses alongside functional antibody production, providing complete protection against lethal Neisseria meningitidis serogroup B challenge and demonstrating cross-protection against prevalent strains.⁴ This response pattern establishes IFN-γ as a dual marker of both cellular and humoral vaccine efficacy.

In SARS-CoV-2 mRNA vaccination cohorts, prevaccine innate immune variability in the TLR3 type I interferon pathway predicts postvaccine antigen-specific T cell cytokine responses, with BNT162b2 recipients showing increased production of the antigen-specific T cell cytokines IL-2, IFN-γ, and IL-21 after spike protein stimulation.³ A common genetic polymorphism in TLR3 affects this association, highlighting the interplay between innate interferon pathways and adaptive IFN-γ production in vaccine-induced immunity.

## Inflammatory diseases

While IFN-γ is essential for protective immunity, its overproduction drives pathologic inflammation in several disease contexts. In [[inflammatory-bowel-disease|inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)]], elevated IFN-γ levels contribute to mucosal damage, and therapeutic suppression of IFN-γ signaling improves clinical outcomes. The benzofuran-2-carboxylic acid derivative compound 10j, a potent and selective [[ptpro|PTPRO]] inhibitor, suppresses IL-6 via NF-κB pathway modulation in macrophages and significantly attenuates disease severity in murine DSS-induced colitis by suppressing IFN-γ and IL-2 levels.¹¹ Further optimization yielded compound 30k with enhanced potency and superior in vivo efficacy, establishing phosphatase-targeted immunomodulation as a viable IBD therapeutic strategy.

In severe [[pards|pediatric acute respiratory distress syndrome (PARDS)]], high-dimensional multi-omics analysis reveals interferon-driven immune dysregulation as a central pathogenic feature. Pulmonary CD8+ T cells display an interferon-driven cytotoxic profile with exhaustion and apoptosis gene expression, while distinct macrophage subsets show high interferon-stimulated gene expression but suppressed IL-1 pathway activity, associated with impaired phagocytosis and M1-polarization.¹² Reduced pulmonary IL-1α/β coincides with elevated IFN-γ, and in vitro models demonstrate that IFN-γ priming suppresses TLR7-induced IL-1β production through transcriptional inhibition of downstream inflammatory pathways, recapitulating the immune signature observed in patients.

In [[chronic-graft-versus-host-disease|chronic graft-versus-host disease (cGVHD)]], donor regulatory T cell infusions that improve clinical symptoms and enable corticosteroid reduction are associated with increases in serum levels of IL-7 and IFN-γ over time, suggesting a complex immunoregulatory role for IFN-γ in balancing allogeneic immune responses.¹

## Therapeutic targeting

Modulation of IFN-γ signaling pathways represents an emerging therapeutic frontier. Targeted degradation of [[ptpn2|PTPN2]], a phosphatase that negatively regulates IFN-γ signaling, enhances cancer immunotherapy efficacy. The alkynylbenzene scaffold-based PROTAC degrader PD-305 exhibits subnanomolar degradation potency and nanomolar antiproliferation potency in IFN-γ-stimulated HT-29 cells, demonstrating excellent in vivo PTPN2 degradation and effective tumor suppression in murine models.¹³ This approach leverages IFN-γ-responsive cellular states to selectively amplify immunotherapeutic pressure within the tumor microenvironment.

Conversely, in glucocorticoid-rich tumor microenvironments such as adrenocortical carcinoma, IFN-γ stimulation of glucocorticoid-polarized C1Q+ CD163+ macrophages drives secretion of the T cell chemoattractant [[cxcl9|CXCL9]], with secretion levels exceeding those of classical M1 macrophages.⁶ In ACC-bearing mice, treatment with immune checkpoint inhibitors combined with glucocorticoid receptor blockade via mifepristone reduces tumoral CXCL9 expression, illustrating context-dependent modulation of IFN-γ effector functions.
