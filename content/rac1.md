**Rac1** is a small GTPase that functions as a central regulator of cytoskeletal dynamics, controlling membrane protrusion, lamellipodia extension, and cell migration.¹ In cancer, Rac1 exhibits context-dependent functions: as a driver of metastatic progression in [[estrogen-receptor|estrogen receptor-positive (ER+)]] breast cancer when activated through the [[p-rex1|P-Rex1]] signaling axis, and as a therapeutic asset for enhancing anti-leukemic immunity when engineered into [[car-t|CAR-T cells]] as a constitutively active variant.¹²

---

## Mechanism

Rac1 regulates cell migration through activation of downstream effectors that promote actin polymerization and membrane reorganization. The GTPase cycles between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state, a transition catalyzed by guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs) such as [[p-rex1|P-Rex1]].¹ Upon activation, Rac1 drives enhanced membrane ruffling, lamellipodia formation, and migratory capacity — phenotypic changes that enable cells to navigate tissue barriers and reach distant sites. The GTPase integrates upstream signaling cues from growth factors and intracellular stress pathways to coordinate cytoskeletal reorganization with cell locomotion.

## Breast cancer metastatic escape

In [[estrogen-receptor|ER+]] breast cancer, endocrine therapy paradoxically reprograms surviving tumor cells toward increased metastatic competency. Single-cell RNA sequencing of endocrine-resistant cells reveals selective upregulation of [[p-rex1|P-Rex1]], a guanine nucleotide exchange factor that hyperactivates Rac1 signaling.¹ This therapy-induced P-Rex1/Rac1 axis establishes a constitutive migratory phenotype, promoting dissemination from primary tumors. Clinically, elevated [[p-rex1|P-Rex1]] expression correlates with late-recurring [[estrogen-receptor|ER+]] breast cancers — slowly proliferating tumors that relapse years after initial therapy, defining a treatment-refractory disease state.¹ Intravital imaging confirms persistent Rac1 activation in endocrine-treated cells, mechanistically explaining their enhanced metastatic escape.

The therapeutic vulnerability of the P-Rex1/Rac1 axis has been demonstrated through small-molecule Rac1 inhibition. Both [[nsc23766|NSC23766]] and [[r-ketorolac|R-ketorolac]] directly suppress Rac1 activation, reducing migratory capacity in endocrine-resistant cells and blocking Rac1 signaling in vivo.¹ When combined with [[tamoxifen|tamoxifen]], these Rac1 inhibitors substantially reduced tumor burden in patient-derived xenograft models of endocrine-refractory disease, indicating a combinatorial strategy to prevent metastatic progression in late-recurring breast cancer.¹

## CAR-T cell engineering in acute myeloid leukemia

In contrast to its cancer-promoting role in breast cancer, Rac1 is therapeutically deployed to enhance [[car-t|CAR-T cell]] efficacy in [[acute-myeloid-leukemia|acute myeloid leukemia (AML)]]. The bone marrow microenvironment creates an immune-suppressive sanctuary that protects leukemia cells from T cell infiltration, representing a major barrier to cellular immunotherapy success.² To overcome this spatial exclusion, primary human T cells and [[car-t|CD33 CAR-T cells]] were engineered to express [[rac1v12|Rac1V12]], a constitutively active Rac1 variant locked in the GTP-bound state.² Expression of [[rac1v12|Rac1V12]] substantially increased T cell and CAR-T cell migration in vitro and, critically, promoted their retention and survival within the bone marrow microenvironment in vivo.²

[[car-t|CD33 CAR-T cells]] expressing [[rac1v12|Rac1V12]] demonstrated enhanced cytotoxicity against CD33+ leukemia cells in transwell migration-dependent killing assays, implicating augmented migratory competence in improved target recognition and destruction.² In xenograft models of AML, these engineered CAR-T cells achieved robust suppression of leukemia burden and significantly extended survival.² Mechanistically, [[rac1v12|Rac1V12]]-expressing CAR-T cells developed an enhanced immunological memory phenotype while simultaneously reducing tonic signaling — a combination that sustains T cell persistence and durable anti-tumor immunity in vivo.²
