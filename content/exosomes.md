**Exosomes** are endosome-derived extracellular vesicles ranging from 30 to 150 nm in diameter that serve as critical mediators of intercellular communication in health and disease. Their biogenesis is tightly regulated by purinergic signaling through P2X7 receptors, [[neutral-sphingomyelinase]] activation, and PI3K/AKT pathway modulation, with release controlled by glutathione-dependent redox mechanisms and MRP1-mediated efflux systems.¹ [[cd63|CD63-positive exosomes]] fulfilling MISEV 2023 criteria transport diverse molecular cargo including proteins such as [[bone-morphogenetic-protein-4|BMP4]] and [[ccl2|CCL2]], microRNAs like miR-27a-3p and miR-132, and circular RNAs including [[circ-rad18|circRAD18]] and [[circ0000549]], enabling the horizontal transfer of bioactive molecules between distant cell populations.²⁻⁴

---

## Biogenesis and molecular composition

Exosome formation is initiated when extracellular ATP acts as a danger signal activating P2X7 receptors on the cell surface, triggering a cascade that coordinates intracellular vesicle trafficking and membrane budding. In differentiated [[neutrophil-like-cells]], P2X7R agonist stimulation induces rapid, dose-dependent exosome release that correlates strongly with intracellular glutathione depletion and [[neutral-sphingomyelinase]] activation through the PI3K/AKT signaling axis.¹ The pathway proceeds through MRP1/ABCC1-mediated glutathione efflux, creating redox conditions that promote nSMase activity and ceramide-dependent intraluminal vesicle formation within multivesicular bodies. Pharmacological inhibition of nSMase abrogates exosome release entirely, confirming the central role of ceramide metabolism in membrane budding and cargo sorting.¹ The resulting vesicles display phosphatidylserine on their outer membrane surface, a signature lipid that distinguishes exosomes from other extracellular vesicle subtypes and provides a molecular handle for selective isolation.⁵

## Stem cell-derived therapeutic applications

Exosomes derived from mesenchymal stem cells and pluripotent stem cell derivatives have emerged as potent cell-free therapeutic agents for tissue regeneration and repair. In periodontal medicine, exosomes from periodontal ligament stem cells delivered via biodegradable piezoelectric fibrous membranes synergistically promote vascularized bone regeneration in periodontal defects by creating a microenvironment conducive to cell recruitment, proliferation, and osteogenic differentiation.² The piezoelectric properties of the carrier membrane, combined with sustained exosomal release, significantly enhance bone formation compared to membranes lacking either component. For diabetic wound healing, a distinct [[ccl2]]-expressing subpopulation of [[adipose-derived-stem-cells]] identified through single-cell RNA sequencing produces exosomes enriched in CCL2, TGFB1, and IL34 that function through the CCL2-ACKR1 signaling axis.³ These specialized exosomes reverse high-glucose-induced suppression of [[endothelial-cells|endothelial cell]] proliferation and tube formation, activating the PI3K/AKT/mTOR/HIF-1α pathway to promote angiogenesis, collagen deposition, and M2-macrophage polarization while reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine levels.³ In cardiovascular disease, exosomes from induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes engineered to overexpress Bcl-2 mitigate aortic valve calcification by re-establishing homeostasis in apoptotic pathways, upregulating the Bcl-2/Bax ratio and restoring miR-132 expression to inhibit abnormal calcium deposition in valve interstitial cells.¹⁰

## Cancer progression and treatment resistance

While exosomes mediate beneficial intercellular communication in regenerative contexts, tumor-derived and stromal exosomes drive cancer progression and confer resistance to standard therapies. Cancer-associated fibroblast-secreted exosomes deliver [[bone-morphogenetic-protein-4|BMP4]] to cervical cancer cells, activating the Nrf2-HO-1 antioxidant defense axis while simultaneously suppressing cuproptosis regulators, establishing a dual-pathway mechanism that renders tumor cells resistant to radiotherapy-induced stress.⁶ BMP4 functions as the master regulator within these exosomes, and its knockdown effectively restores radiosensitivity both in vitro and in vivo, demonstrating the pathological significance of this tumor-stroma communication axis.⁶ In glioblastoma, exosomal [[circ-rad18|circRAD18]] acts as a key mediator of intercellular signaling that promotes tumor cell proliferation, migration, and invasion through the miR-1231/LUC7L2 regulatory axis.⁷ Suppression of miR-1231 or overexpression of LUC7L2 enhances malignant cellular properties, while circRAD18 knockdown markedly inhibits tumor growth in subcutaneous xenograft models, establishing exosomal circular RNAs as functional regulators rather than merely passive biomarkers.⁷ Environmental carcinogen exposure further exploits exosomal pathways, as demonstrated by N-methyl-N'-nitro-N-nitrosoguanidine-induced malignant transformation of [[ges-1-cells]], where exosomal [[circ0000549]] from transformed cells promotes gastric cancer initiation in recipient normal cells through modulation of epithelial-mesenchymal transition and stemness-related properties.⁸

## Targeted neurological therapy

Engineering strategies enable exosomes to overcome the [[blood-brain-barrier]] and selectively target disease-specific neural cell populations for treatment of neurodegenerative disorders. The [[nexogflg-p1|NEXOGFLG-P1]] exosome platform demonstrates this approach through integration of three functional modules: a surficial glucose-regulated protein 94-targeting peptide N that confers blood-brain barrier permeability and substantia nigra neuron specificity, luminal α-synuclein-degrading peptide P1, and a cathepsin-B-cleavable GFLG linker connecting the exosomal skeleton protein to the therapeutic cargo.⁹ Following systemic administration in 1-methyl-4-phenyl-1,2,3,6-tetrahydropyridine-induced Parkinson's disease models, NEXOGFLG-P1 exosomes cross the blood-brain barrier, target diseased substantia nigra neurons, fuse with endosomes, and release P1 into the cytoplasm through cathepsin B-mediated GFLG cleavage, resulting in significant degradation of α-synuclein aggregates.⁹ For white matter injury following [[subarachnoid-hemorrhage]], BV2 microglia-derived exosomes loaded with [[mir-27a-3p-antagomir]] administered intranasally effectively target oligodendrocytes and mitigate injury by modulating the [[ppar-gamma|PPARγ]]/PRDX1/JNK pathway.⁴ Treatment with these exosomes reduces oligodendrocyte apoptosis, promotes proliferation and differentiation of oligodendrocyte precursor cells, and leads to improved neurological and electrophysiological recovery by restoring PPARγ expression and downregulating JNK-mediated cell death signaling.⁴

## Isolation and biomarker technologies

Clinical translation of exosome therapeutics requires high-efficiency, high-purity isolation methods that preserve structural and functional integrity. The [[tim4|TIM4]]@MGO magnetic bead system addresses this challenge through functionalization of magnetic graphene oxide with T cell immunoglobulin and mucin domain-containing protein 4, which specifically recognizes phosphatidylserine exposed on exosomal membranes via Ca²⁺-dependent binding.⁵ The large specific surface area of magnetic graphene oxide substantially increases TIM4 loading density, markedly enhancing binding capacity and recovery efficiency while enabling rapid isolation within 30 minutes.⁵ Crucially, EDTA-mediated chelation of Ca²⁺ induces charge reversal of the TIM4 domain, triggering highly reversible dissociation that allows efficient exosome release under mild physiological conditions, and the system retains over 84% initial isolation efficiency after three consecutive reuse cycles.⁵ Beyond isolation, exosomal circular RNAs serve as diagnostic biomarkers with clinical utility, as circRAD18 is markedly upregulated in glioblastoma patient exosomes,⁷ and circ0000549 is elevated in serum exosomes from gastric cancer patients with high nitrite exposure history,⁸ suggesting potential for non-invasive disease monitoring through liquid biopsy approaches.
