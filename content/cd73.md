**CD73** is an ectonucleotidase that catalyzes the hydrolysis of extracellular adenosine monophosphate (AMP) to adenosine, a potent immunosuppressive metabolite that promotes tumor immune evasion. Working in concert with [[cd39|CD39]] within the tumor microenvironment, CD73 functions as a critical axis of immune tolerance by suppressing [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cell]] activation and promoting [[regulatory-t-cells|regulatory T cell]] infiltration.¹ CD73 upregulation on cancer cells, particularly in [[egfr|EGFR]]-mutant and [[alk|ALK]]-rearranged [[non-small-cell-lung-cancer|non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC)]], is a spatial immune biomarker that predicts [[immune-checkpoint-inhibitors|immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)]] resistance more accurately than [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] expression alone.²

---

## Mechanism

CD73, operating downstream of [[cd39|CD39]], completes the sequential degradation of extracellular ATP to the immunosuppressive adenosine nucleoside. This [[adenosine|adenosine]]-mediated immunosuppression is executed through adenosine receptor signaling on T lymphocytes and myeloid cells, creating a dominant immunotolerant state within the tumor microenvironment.¹ The coordinated activity of the CD39/CD73 axis maintains an extracellular balance favoring immunosuppressive adenosine over pro-inflammatory ATP, effectively dampening anti-tumor immune responses. CD73 expression on cancer cells is particularly elevated in EGFR/ALK-driven oncogenic contexts, where it drives [[tumor-associated-macrophages|M2-polarized tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs)]] and aberrant angiogenesis.

---

## ICI resistance and spatial biomarkers

High CD73 expression on cancer cells confers resistance to [[immune-checkpoint-inhibitors|immune checkpoint inhibitors]], particularly in EGFR-mutant NSCLC, potentially through M2-TAM accumulation and HIF-1α/VEGF-A-driven vascular remodeling.¹ [[Spatial-proteomics|Spatial multiplexed proteomic profiling]] of pretreatment tumors reveals that CD73 upregulation is part of a predictive spatial composite signature that substantially outperforms single-marker approaches based on [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] alone in stratifying ICI responders from non-responders.² In mNSCLC, intratumoral CD8+ T cell density and the physical proximity between these tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes and cancer cells are critical for ICI efficacy; however, elevated CD73 on cancer cells disrupts this favorable microarchitecture by promoting immunosuppressive adenosine accumulation at the immune-cancer cell interface.¹

---

## Anti-CD73 monoclonal antibody therapy

Direct blockade of CD73 with monoclonal antibodies enhances anti-tumor immunity by multiple mechanisms. Anti-CD73 antibodies promote CD8+ T cell activation and residency within tumors, simultaneously shifting [[tumor-associated-macrophages|TAM]] phenotype toward a pro-inflammatory state and reducing [[regulatory-t-cells|Treg cell]] infiltration.³ The combination of anti-CD73 with [[anti-il-17-antibody|anti-IL-17 antibodies]] demonstrates enhanced efficacy in melanoma models, as [[il-17a|IL-17]] drives HIF-1α/VEGF-A metabolic reprogramming that sustains the immune-suppressive microenvironment—a limitation that anti-IL-17 antibodies overcome by attenuating this metabolic axis while anti-CD73 simultaneously blocks the downstream CD39/CD73/[[adenosine|adenosine]] pathway.³

---

## Brain metastases and nose-to-brain delivery

Melanoma brain metastases present a particular challenge for CD73-targeted immunotherapy due to blood-brain barrier impermeability and adenosine-mediated immune evasion within the brain microenvironment. A [[glycerol]]-based mucosal penetration enhancement platform was developed to achieve intranasal codelivery of anti-[[il-17a|IL-17]] and anti-CD73 antibodies directly to the brain. Glycerol reversibly opened nasal epithelial tight junctions, enhancing brain delivery of anti-IL-17 and anti-CD73 antibodies by 19.4-fold and 17.1-fold, respectively, while minimizing systemic exposure.³ This nose-to-brain strategy proved therapeutically superior to systemic administration, enabling robust CD8+ T cell activation within brain metastases and eliciting durable anti-tumor responses.

---

## Small-molecule inhibitors

Beyond monoclonal antibodies, nucleotide-based competitive inhibitors targeting CD73 have been developed as tool compounds for immunotherapy applications. [[psb-24379|PSB-24379]], an 8-(1-naphthylthio)-N6-(4-phenylbutyl)-AMP derivative, achieves potent CD73 inhibition with a Ki of 240 nM, concurrently inhibiting [[cd39|CD39]] (Ki 77.4 nM).⁴ PSB-24379 reduced ATP hydrolysis on melanoma and breast cancer cell membranes and partially reverted ATP-mediated suppression of T cell activation and proliferation in ATP-rich tumor microenvironments.⁴ These nucleotide-derived competitive inhibitors represent high-quality chemical scaffolds for exploring CD73 as a direct enzymatic target in immunotherapy development.
