**Calcium dysregulation** is a pathophysiological condition characterized by aberrant intracellular calcium homeostasis and dysregulated Ca²⁺ transport and accumulation within cellular compartments.¹ This dysregulation creates a critical intersection between [[oxidative-stress|oxidative stress]] and [[inflammation|inflammatory responses]], generating deleterious cascades across multiple disease contexts. In [[ischemic-stroke|ischemic stroke]], dysregulated calcium initiates a pathological amplification loop with [[reactive-oxygen-species|reactive oxygen species (ROS)]], exacerbating neuronal damage and brain inflammation.¹ In [[periodontitis|periodontal disease]], dysregulated intracellular Ca²⁺ transport between the [[endoplasmic-reticulum|endoplasmic reticulum (ER)]] and [[mitochondria|mitochondria]] drives both [[endoplasmic-reticulum-stress|ER stress]] and [[mitochondrial-dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]], converging to activate proinflammatory macrophage responses.² Therapeutic interventions targeting calcium dysregulation through chelation strategies — including [[egta|EGTA]] and [[polydopamine]]-based nanomaterials — have demonstrated efficacy in reducing cellular damage, restoring organellar homeostasis, and remodeling immune responses across disease models.

---

## Mechanism

Calcium dysregulation unfolds through multiple reinforcing pathways that amplify cellular injury. In ischemic environments, ATP depletion collapses Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase function, allowing calcium influx through receptor-operated channels and reverse-mode Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchange, causing intracellular calcium overload. This accumulated calcium is taken up by mitochondria, where it uncouples oxidative phosphorylation and generates excessive [[reactive-oxygen-species|ROS]]. In turn, ROS oxidizes cellular calcium-handling machinery and exacerbates dysregulation, forming a vicious Ca²⁺-ROS amplification loop that progressively amplifies cellular damage.¹

In chronic inflammatory diseases, a distinct but equally pathological mechanism operates through dysregulated ER-mitochondrial calcium crosstalk. The [[endoplasmic-reticulum|ER]] serves as the primary intracellular calcium store, while mitochondrial membrane potential drives calcium uptake into the matrix. When this intercellular communication becomes dysregulated, accumulated mitochondrial calcium impairs oxidative phosphorylation and triggers opening of the mitochondrial permeability transition pore, releasing calcium back into the cytoplasm and perpetuating organellar dysfunction.² This dysregulated calcium further drives macrophage proinflammatory activation: mitochondrial calcium overload triggers [[nlrp3|NLRP3]] inflammasome assembly and metabolic shifts favoring proinflammatory phenotypes, while calcium-dependent proteases amplify inflammatory signaling cascades. Importantly, this macrophage activation can be reversed through calcium chelation, indicating that dysregulated calcium is a central driver rather than a secondary consequence.²

## Calcium Dysregulation in Ischemic Stroke

[[ischemic-stroke|Ischemic stroke]] pathogenesis operates through a "calcium dysregulation-oxidative stress-inflammation" triple synergistic regulation mode, wherein these three processes mutually amplify neuronal damage.¹ During ischemia, oxygen and glucose deprivation collapses ATP-dependent calcium extrusion, allowing rapid cytoplasmic calcium accumulation. Reperfusion accelerates calcium influx further, driving ROS generation and triggering proinflammatory microglial activation. This cascade initiates neuronal death, neuroinflammation, and cerebral edema.

Therapeutic targeting of calcium dysregulation addresses this multifactorial pathogenesis. The multiaspect nanosheet platform [[s-e-ldh|S-E@LDH]] — a layered double hydroxide matrix integrating the calcium chelator [[egta|EGTA]] and the anti-inflammatory agent salvianic acid A (SAA) — effectively chelates intracellular Ca²⁺ while scavenging reactive oxygen and nitrogen species, thereby disrupting the Ca²⁺-ROS amplification loop.¹ In transient ischemia mouse models, [[s-e-ldh|S-E@LDH]] reduces neuronal damage, neuroinflammation, and cerebral edema while improving motor function recovery.¹ The spatiotemporally controlled release of SAA within the acidic post-ischemic microenvironment enables targeted reprogramming of microglia toward a protective M2 phenotype, contributing to restoration of brain homeostasis and neuroprotection.

## Calcium Dysregulation in Periodontal Tissue Regeneration

[[periodontitis|Periodontal tissue]] defects present a clinical challenge exacerbated by sustained proinflammatory responses driven by dysregulated calcium homeostasis. Transcriptomic analyses revealed that [[endoplasmic-reticulum-stress|ER stress]] and [[mitochondrial-dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]] are pivotal regulators of macrophage proinflammatory activation, linked through dysregulated intracellular Ca²⁺ transport between these organelles.² Targeting this ER-mitochondria calcium crosstalk offers a dual strategy to suppress inflammation while restoring regenerative capacity.

The osteoimmunomodulatory microneedle patch platform addresses this mechanism through dual-functional [[polydopamine]] nanoparticles (PDAB NPs) that leverage the metal ion-chelating properties of [[polydopamine]] to scavenge excessive intracellular Ca²⁺.² These nanoparticles are integrated with decellularized extracellular matrix within a microneedle architecture designed to overcome anatomical constraints and enable localized delivery to periodontal tissues. The microneedle patch restores both ER and mitochondrial homeostasis by reducing dysregulated calcium, while simultaneously remodeling macrophage polarization toward anti-inflammatory, pro-regenerative phenotypes.² This improved osteoimmune microenvironment further promotes osteogenic differentiation of bone mesenchymal stem cells and accelerates [[bmp9|BMP9]]-driven periodontal bone regeneration, supporting functional reconstruction of periodontal defects.

## Calcium Chelation Therapeutics

Calcium chelation has emerged as a cornerstone strategy for targeting dysregulation-driven pathology. [[egta|EGTA]], a well-established chelator, effectively reduces intracellular calcium accumulation and cellular damage in the acute phase following ischemic injury.¹ [[polydopamine]], a biocompatible polymer with inherent metal ion-chelating capacity, offers an additional platform for targeted calcium scavenging while simultaneously enabling the delivery of pro-regenerative molecules such as [[bmp9|bone morphogenetic protein 9 (BMP9)]].² These calcium-targeting therapeutics work synergistically with antioxidant and anti-inflammatory interventions to address the multifactorial nature of calcium dysregulation-driven disease.
