**Lipid nanoparticles (LNPs)** are a clinically validated nonviral RNA delivery platform composed of ionizable lipids, structural lipids, cholesterol, and polyethylene glycol–conjugated lipids that self-assemble into discrete spherical vehicles. Their central therapeutic limitation is inefficient endosomal escape—typically fewer than 3% of internalized RNA molecules reach the cytosol—a bottleneck that recent innovations in multiphasic core architecture and [[glycerol-monooleate|structured lipid design]] address by harnessing phase separation and topological mismatch.¹ Beyond passive delivery, LNPs exhibit active immunomodulatory properties, promoting dendritic cell maturation and sustained CD8+ T cell responses while enabling programmable cellular reprogramming via mRNA cargo.² LNPs have emerged as a versatile platform for regenerative medicine, oncology, and immunotherapy, with particular promise in sustaining therapeutic phenotypes in adoptively transferred cells and suppressing metastatic dissemination.

---

## Mechanism

Lipid nanoparticles mediate intracellular RNA delivery through a multi-step process initiated by cellular uptake via endocytosis. Conventional LNPs rely on pH-triggered protonation of ionizable lipids to destabilize endosomal membranes, allowing cargo release into the cytosol; however, this mechanism is rate-limiting and inefficient. Recent mechanistic advances reveal that the internal nanostructure of LNPs plays an active role in endosomal bypass. Multiphasic LNP designs, engineered through composition-dependent phase separation within the core, exploit topological mismatch arising from geometric and electrostatic packing asymmetry.¹ When [[glycerol-monooleate|glycerol monooleate (GMO)]]—a curvature-promoting monoglyceride—is incorporated as the base lipid alongside cationic and ionizable functional lipids, the resulting heterogeneous, multiphasic core architecture shows dramatically enhanced RNA cytosolic release. Cryo-electron microscopy and synchrotron small-angle X-ray scattering have demonstrated that increased internal heterogeneity directly correlates with transfection efficiency and that multiphasic LNPs deliver cargo via direct plasma membrane fusion, thereby bypassing endosomal entrapment entirely.¹

## Macrophage cell therapy

A transformative application of LNPs lies in the delivery of therapeutic mRNA to primary macrophages for sustained cellular reprogramming. An optimized [[il-4-mrna|IL-4 mRNA]]-loaded LNP formulation rapidly transfects human and murine macrophages, eliciting robust IL-4 secretion and driving a reparative phenotype characterized by anti-inflammatory and tissue-supportive properties.² In murine volumetric muscle loss models, adoptively transferred macrophages pre-treated with IL-4-LNPs maintained their reparative phenotype for at least one week despite the hostile inflammatory injury microenvironment. Importantly, these engineered macrophages did not merely act in isolation; instead, they promoted a reparative phenotype in endogenous macrophages and supported muscle repair outcomes including increased vascularization, enhanced fiber size distribution, and scaffold remodeling. Notably, T cell responses in the muscle or draining lymph nodes remained unaffected, indicating selective and tissue-directed immunomodulation. This strategy establishes a new paradigm for controlling macrophage cell therapies in regenerative medicine applications.

## Tumor targeting and biomimetic design

Conventional LNPs show limited intrinsic tumor tropism, a significant obstacle to their oncological application. Recent innovations overcome this limitation through biomimetic decoration with tumor-derived membrane components. LNPs are functionally integrated with cancer cell membranes by cloaking RNA-loaded nanoparticles with nanoghosts—membrane vesicles derived from [[4t1-cells|triple-negative breast cancer cells]].³ These cancer cell membrane-derived nanoghosts retain the native composition and orientation of tumor membrane proteins, which mediate homotypic binding to cancer cells and heterotypic recognition of cancer-associated fibroblasts. Biomimetic LNPs generated via ultrasound-assisted fusion of RNA-LNPs into nanoghost shells exhibit a multilamellar mRNA-LNP core enveloped by an intact nanoghost outer layer. While uncoated LNPs show negligible interaction with stromal cells, biomimetic LNPs display markedly enhanced affinity for cancer-associated fibroblasts, enabling efficient RNA delivery to the tumor microenvironment and revealing how membrane engineering can overcome the targeting limitations of conventional formulations.³

## Anti-metastasis and immunotherapy

LNPs deliver therapeutic inhibitors and immunogenic payloads capable of suppressing metastatic progression and activating potent anti-tumor immunity. Tumor cells transfer mitochondria bearing elevated levels of tumor-derived tRNA-derived small RNAs (tsRNAs) to endothelial cells, promoting vascular invasion and metastasis; LNPs encapsulating inhibitors of [[mt-5-tirna-34-glnttg-inhibitor|mt-5'tiRNA-34-GlnTTG]] effectively suppress vascular invasion and lung adenocarcinoma metastasis in vivo.⁴ On the immunogenic front, mRNA tumor nanovaccines expressing tumor antigens fused with angiotensin II promote type 1 conventional dendritic cell (cDC1) maturation and expansion when delivered by LNPs composed of optimized cationic lipids; these formulations elicit potent antigen-specific CD8+ T cell responses across multiple tumor models and effectively inhibit tumor growth.² The underlying mechanism involves LNP-mediated promotion of dendritic cell maturation and selective expansion of cDC1 subpopulations, which in turn present tumor antigens with enhanced efficiency to elicit sustained CD8+ T cell-mediated anti-tumor immunity. Notably, tumor protection is ablated in cDC1-deficient mice, confirming the necessity of this pathway for vaccine efficacy.²

---

## Clinical translation

Multiple LNP formulations have progressed from preclinical validation to clinical and pre-clinical therapeutic development, demonstrating translatable value across regenerative medicine and oncology. The validation of LNP-based IL-4 mRNA therapeutics in macrophage cell therapy, combined with proof-of-concept data for tsRNA inhibitor delivery and mRNA vaccination strategies, positions LNPs as a programmable platform capable of achieving durable phenotypic modulation and disease suppression in clinical settings.
