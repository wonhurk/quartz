**Neoadjuvant therapy (NAT)** is preoperative systemic treatment administered before surgical resection to reduce tumor burden, improve surgical outcomes, and enhance long-term survival across multiple malignancies, particularly [[breast-cancer|breast cancer]], [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)]], [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer]], and [[rectal-cancer|rectal cancer]].¹ In [[her2|HER2]]-positive and TNBC, pathologic complete response (pCR)—the complete histopathologic eradication of invasive tumor cells—serves as a robust surrogate for improved survival outcomes. However, emergence of treatment-resistant [[quiescent-persister-tumor-cells|quiescent persister tumor cells (PTCs)]] that evade chemotherapy through dormancy represents a critical clinical limitation, particularly in colorectal cancer where elevated PTC abundance correlates with poor long-term prognosis despite initial treatment response.² The [[cd155]]-[[cd96]] immunological axis, wherein PTCs express elevated [[cd155]] that suppresses [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cell]] anti-tumor immunity, has emerged as a therapeutic vulnerability addressable through checkpoint blockade and engineered cellular therapies.²³

---

## Mechanism and Surgical Rationale

Neoadjuvant therapy exploits the principle that preoperative systemic cytoreduction improves resectability, reduces micrometastatic burden, and enables surgeons to achieve negative surgical margins while preserving organ function in locally advanced or borderline-resectable tumors. The approach is particularly valuable in cancers where upfront surgery carries high morbidity or risks incomplete resection. Response assessment has historically relied on clinical and radiographic criteria, yet emerging molecular biomarkers now enable precise quantification of treatment efficacy and superior risk stratification beyond conventional pathology-based evaluation alone.

---

## Application in Breast Cancer

Patients with stage II/III [[her2|HER2]]-positive and [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)]] frequently receive neoadjuvant therapy as standard of care for locally advanced disease.¹ Pathologic complete response (pCR), defined as absence of invasive carcinoma in the breast and regional lymph nodes, correlates strongly with disease-free and overall survival and is recognized as a valid surrogate endpoint by regulatory agencies. Importantly, many patients who do not achieve pCR still experience durable long-term remissions, underscoring the heterogeneity of treatment response and the need for refined prognostic assessment beyond pathological endpoints alone.¹ 

Circulating tumor-DNA (ctDNA) [[minimal-residual-disease|minimal residual disease (MRD)]] assessment has emerged as a superior biomarker for risk stratification in breast cancer following neoadjuvant therapy.¹ Ultrasensitive detection of tumor-derived cell-free DNA identifies patients with submicroscopic residual disease, providing incremental prognostic information independent of pathological response status. Persistent ctDNA positivity after NAT indicates elevated relapse risk and may guide adjuvant therapy intensification or enhanced surveillance strategies, even in patients achieving complete pathologic response.¹

## Application in Colorectal Cancer

Neoadjuvant chemotherapy in [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer (CRC)]] has demonstrated efficacy in reducing tumor burden and improving resectability; however, its impact on long-term patient outcomes remains limited due to therapeutic resistance.² A critical determinant of treatment failure is the survival and proliferation of [[quiescent-persister-tumor-cells|quiescent persister tumor cells (PTCs)]], which persist through chemotherapy-induced dormancy while maintaining aggressive stem-like properties including enhanced self-renewal capacity.² Elevated abundance of PTCs correlates directly with poor long-term prognosis, even in patients exhibiting initial tumor response to cytotoxic chemotherapy.²

The PTC-driven resistance mechanism operates through the [[cd155]]-[[cd96]] immunological axis.²³ Surviving PTCs express elevated [[cd155]], a ligand for the inhibitory [[cd96]] checkpoint receptor on [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cells]], driving profound dysfunction of infiltrating tumor-specific lymphocytes.³ This [[cd155]]-[[cd96]] engagement causes CD8+ T cell exhaustion characterized by reduced effector function, impaired proliferation, and elevated expression of co-inhibitory receptors, thereby establishing an immunosuppressive microenvironment that shields PTCs from immune elimination.² Anti-[[cd96]] monoclonal antibody therapy effectively eliminates PTCs in preclinical models by restoring CD8+ T cell function and promoting memory-like phenotypes characterized by enhanced mitochondrial metabolism.² Engineered [[epcam|EpCAM]]-targeted CAR-T cells deficient in [[cd96]] expression demonstrate remarkable therapeutic potential against CRC by simultaneously targeting PTC surface adhesion molecules while preventing [[cd155]]-induced exhaustion.²

## Application in Rectal Cancer

Neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy is standard of care for locally advanced [[rectal-cancer|rectal cancer]] and achieves significant tumor downsizing in the majority of patients.⁴ Objective assessment of treatment response has traditionally relied on clinical and endoscopic evaluation; however, circulating tumor-DNA (ctDNA) assays measuring methylated [[bcat1]] and [[ikzf1]] now enable quantitative molecular evaluation of chemoradiotherapy response.⁴ These biomarkers detect rectal adenocarcinoma with 87% sensitivity in untreated patients and demonstrate robust reduction following neoadjuvant treatment in good responders.⁴ Tumor tissue from patients achieving good response contains methylated [[bcat1]]/[[ikzf1]] levels equivalent to normal tissue, providing objective molecular evidence of treatment efficacy.⁴ This liquid biopsy approach offers real-time kinetic monitoring of treatment dynamics and may identify poor responders at high relapse risk for adjuvant therapy intensification or alternative therapeutic strategies.

---

## Emerging Biomarker-Driven Strategies

The integration of molecular biomarkers into neoadjuvant therapy evaluation represents a paradigm shift toward precision oncology. Circulating tumor-DNA [[minimal-residual-disease|minimal residual disease (MRD)]] assessment utilizing ultrasensitive next-generation sequencing detects submicroscopic residual disease in breast cancer, providing superior risk stratification independent of traditional pathological endpoints.¹ Persistent ctDNA positivity after neoadjuvant therapy predicts elevated relapse risk and enables targeted adjuvant therapy intensification decisions.¹

In rectal cancer, methylated [[bcat1]]/[[ikzf1]] profiling provides objective, quantitative response assessment that reflects treatment efficacy and identifies candidates for adjuvant therapy or intensified surveillance.⁴ Unlike conventional imaging-based assessment, these liquid biopsy approaches offer multiple advantages: non-invasive serial sampling, quantitative kinetics enabling real-time response monitoring, and detection of chemotherapy-resistant micrometastatic disease. The incorporation of such biomarkers into treatment decision algorithms represents an emerging standard of care for precision management of patients receiving neoadjuvant therapy.
