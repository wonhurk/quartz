**CD8** is a surface glycoprotein marker that identifies cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs), a population of effector cells central to adaptive immunity and anti-tumor immune surveillance. These [[cd8|CD8+]] T cells are characterized by potent antigen-processing and cytotoxic killing capacities, enabling them to recognize and eliminate intracellular pathogens and malignant cells.¹ The absolute number and functional status of CD8+ T cells serve as pivotal indicators of immune competence across diverse clinical settings, from immune recovery assessment in sepsis to complications in allogeneic transplantation. Emerging evidence from neoantigen vaccine development has identified a specialized [[isg15|ISG15+]] CD8+ T cell subset possessing enhanced cytotoxic function and the capacity to orchestrate tertiary lymphoid structure formation within tumors, establishing CD8+ T cells as critical effector mediators in contemporary cancer immunotherapy.

---

## Mechanism and phenotype

CD8+ T cells develop in the thymus and circulate as resting naive cells until activated by antigen-presenting cells displaying cognate peptides on major histocompatibility complex class I molecules. Upon activation, these cells undergo rapid proliferation and acquire specialized functions including cytokine production and cytotoxic granule-mediated killing. The CD8 glycoprotein itself functions as a coreceptor stabilizing T cell receptor engagement with MHC class I, enhancing signal transduction and promoting T cell survival. Intriguingly, recent investigations have identified a discrete population of [[isg15|ISG15+]] CD8+ T cells with augmented antigen-processing capacity and elevated cytotoxic potential, representing a functionally distinct subset within the broader CD8+ T cell compartment.

## Immune recovery in sepsis

CD8+ T cell counts and absolute lymphocyte numbers serve as quantitative biomarkers of immune recovery in sepsis, a life-threatening condition characterized by systemic immunosuppression that predisposes patients to secondary infections and organ failure. In a pilot randomized clinical trial, selenium nanoparticle supplementation in sepsis patients was associated with significant restoration of CD8+ T cell counts by days 4, 7, and 10 post-enrollment, paralleled by increased total lymphocyte populations and elevated [[cd4|CD4+]] T cell numbers.² This immune reconstitution correlated with reduced circulating levels of pro-inflammatory cytokines including IL-6 and HMGB1, suggesting that restoring CD8+ T cell compartment size ameliorates the dysregulated inflammatory state underlying sepsis pathophysiology. The clinical benefit extended beyond immune metrics: selenium nanoparticle-supplemented patients demonstrated improved multiple organ function, lower systemic infection markers, and reduced intensive care unit and hospital mortality, establishing CD8+ T cell restoration as a mechanistic indicator of therapeutic efficacy.

## Modulation in hematopoietic cell transplantation

In allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation, CD8+ T cells mediate both graft-versus-tumor immunity and pathological graft-versus-host disease. [[graft-versus-host-disease|GVHD]] arises when donor-derived T cells, including CD8+ cells, recognize host tissues as foreign and mount destructive immune responses. The anti-CD4 antibody MAX.16H5 provides a selective immunomodulatory approach by ex vivo incubation of transplant grafts, significantly reducing both CD4+ and CD8+ T cell activation and proliferation without complete T cell depletion.³ This selective modulation shifts the developing T cell response toward an [[il-10|interleukin-10]]-dependent regulatory phenotype, resulting in impaired T cell receptor signaling that protects against GVHD manifestation. In murine transplantation models, a single incubation with anti-CD4 antibody proves equivalent to repeated administration of conventional systemic immunosuppressants in prolonging survival and reducing GVHD signs, while theoretically preserving beneficial graft-versus-tumor effects and reducing the risk of opportunistic infections and leukemic relapse.

## ISG15+ CD8+ T cells and tertiary lymphoid structures in cancer

Recent investigations into neoantigen-based cancer vaccines have revealed that specialized [[isg15|ISG15+]] CD8+ T cell populations emerge as dominant effector cells capable of orchestrating sophisticated anti-tumor immune architecture. In a spleen-targeted neoantigen mRNA vaccine ([[stnvac|STNvac]]) approach for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma, [[isg15|ISG15+]] CD8+ T cells displayed enhanced antigen-processing machinery and elevated cytotoxic granule-mediated killing compared to conventional CD8+ T cells.¹ These cells possess a unique capacity to promote tertiary lymphoid structure (TLS) formation within tumors through [[gzma|GZMA]]-F2R-mediated interactions with antigen-presenting cells, creating ectopic lymphoid microenvironments that sustain sustained anti-tumor T cell responses.¹ The spleen-targeted vaccine achieved remarkable therapeutic efficacy in orthotopic hepatocellular carcinoma models, with a high incidence of complete tumor regression and significantly improved survival, with [[isg15|ISG15+]] CD8+ T cell-driven tertiary lymphoid structure formation confirmed in both mouse models and human hepatocellular carcinoma patients. These findings position CD8+ T cells—particularly [[isg15|ISG15+]] subsets—as central executors of next-generation immunotherapeutic strategies targeting malignancy.
