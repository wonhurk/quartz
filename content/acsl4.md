**Long-chain Acyl-CoA Synthetase 4 (ACSL4)** is an essential enzyme that catalyzes the activation of long-chain fatty acids into acyl-CoA, a critical cofactor for cellular lipid remodeling and a master regulator of [[ferroptosis]], an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death distinct from apoptosis.¹ ACSL4 occupies a unique mechanistic position at the intersection of metabolic and cell-death pathways, where its phosphorylation by [[protein-kinase-c-beta-ii|Protein Kinase C Beta II (PKCβII)]] controls the remodeling of polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs) into cell membrane phospholipids, rendering cells acutely vulnerable to iron-catalyzed lipid peroxidation.¹ In castration-resistant prostate cancer, suppression of ACSL4-dependent ferroptosis drives resistance to the [[androgen-receptor|androgen receptor]] antagonist [[enzalutamide]], while restoration of ACSL4 activity re-sensitizes resistant cells to therapeutic intervention.¹ Beyond oncology, ACSL4 functions within the [[mtdh|metadherin (MTDH)]]/ACSL4/NF-κB signaling axis regulating intestinal inflammatory homeostasis, offering therapeutic potential in inflammatory bowel disease through ferroptosis modulation.²

---

## Mechanism of ferroptosis regulation

ACSL4 catalyzes the rate-limiting step of long-chain fatty acid activation, converting free PUFAs into acyl-CoA derivatives that are subsequently incorporated into membrane phospholipids through active remodeling pathways. This CoA-dependent acylation is essential for the biosynthesis of lipid species enriched in polyunsaturated moieties, which are particularly susceptible to iron-catalyzed oxidation and peroxidation. When [[ferroptosis]] is initiated by iron-dependent Fenton reactions, these PUFA-rich lipids become primary substrates for uncontrolled lipid peroxidation, ultimately leading to membrane rupture and cell death. The regulatory control of ACSL4 itself is mediated by [[protein-kinase-c-beta-ii|PKCβII]], which phosphorylates ACSL4 at specific serine residues and dramatically enhances its enzymatic activity, thereby priming cells for heightened ferroptotic sensitivity.¹ This phosphorylation-driven mechanism establishes ACSL4 as a critical metabolic node where extracellular signals and calcium-dependent kinase cascades can be transduced into lipid metabolic reprogramming that fundamentally determines ferroptosis susceptibility.

## Enzalutamide resistance in castration-resistant prostate cancer

In castration-resistant prostate cancer, the [[androgen-receptor|androgen receptor]] antagonist [[enzalutamide]] loses clinical efficacy through a mechanism involving active suppression of ACSL4-dependent ferroptosis. The key mechanistic driver involves downregulation of [[decorin]], a natural suppressor of prostate cancer progression, in [[enzalutamide]]-resistant cancer cells. Loss of decorin impairs the activation of [[protein-kinase-c-beta-ii|PKCβII]], preventing the critical phosphorylation and functional enhancement of ACSL4, thereby extinguishing ferroptotic cell death and enabling the emergence of drug resistance.¹ At the transcriptional level, [[androgen-receptor|androgen receptor]] nuclear translocation, sustained by residual AR signaling in CRPC cells despite [[enzalutamide]] exposure, actively suppresses [[decorin]] transcription, establishing a negative feedback loop that perpetuates ferroptosis evasion and treatment failure. Notably, this ferroptosis-based resistance mechanism operates independently of canonical apoptotic pathways, explaining why many CRPC cells retain functional apoptotic machinery while evading ferroptosis-based cell death.

## Therapeutic reactivation of ACSL4-dependent ferroptosis

Experimental restoration of [[decorin]] expression in [[enzalutamide]]-resistant prostate cancer cells successfully re-activates the [[decorin]]/[[protein-kinase-c-beta-ii|PKCβII]]/ACSL4 ferroptosis axis, restoring ACSL4-dependent lipid remodeling and re-sensitizing resistant cancer cells to [[enzalutamide]] both in vitro and in xenograft models.¹ Remarkably, [[decorin]] overexpression significantly inhibited tumor growth and metastatic dissemination of [[enzalutamide]]-resistant prostate cancer cells in a ferroptosis-dependent manner, with in vivo studies demonstrating durable tumor suppression and survival benefit.¹ This therapeutic rescue reveals that ACSL4-mediated ferroptosis reactivation may be broadly applicable across cancer types where drug resistance is driven by ferroptosis evasion, and highlights the potential for combination strategies pairing classical [[androgen-receptor|androgen receptor]] antagonists with ferroptosis inducers or direct ACSL4 pathway modulators to overcome acquired therapeutic resistance.

## ACSL4 and inflammatory bowel disease

Beyond cancer therapeutics, ACSL4 participates in a distinct regulatory axis controlling intestinal epithelial inflammation. The [[mtdh|metadherin (MTDH)]]/ACSL4/NF-κB signaling pathway has been identified as a central driver of pathogenic inflammation in ulcerative colitis, where dysregulation of this axis promotes pro-inflammatory gene transcription and compromises intestinal barrier integrity.² The therapeutic potential of ACSL4-targeting approaches in inflammatory bowel disease has been demonstrated through targeted delivery of [[dexamethasone]], a corticosteroid with potent anti-inflammatory properties, using ultrasound-targeted microbubble destruction combined with mesoporous silicon nanoparticle carriers.² This sophisticated delivery system effectively suppresses ACSL4 expression and downstream NF-κB signaling in intestinal epithelial cells, achieving significant disease amelioration in preclinical colitis models. The dual therapeutic role of ACSL4 — simultaneously offering pathways to overcome cancer drug resistance while providing targeted control of inflammatory intestinal disease — underscores its emerging importance as a versatile therapeutic target across distinct pathological contexts and disease modalities.
