**Acute bacterial-infected wounds** represent a significant clinical challenge characterized by bacterial contamination that impairs tissue repair and increases risk of systemic infection. Recent advances in biodegradable [[hydrogel]]-based wound dressings have enabled multifunctional treatment strategies that simultaneously address bacterial burden, excessive [[reactive-oxygen-species|reactive oxygen species (ROS)]], and tissue microenvironmental dysregulation. Two complementary hydrogel platforms have demonstrated particular promise: [[formaldehyde]]-releasing hemiaminal polymeric systems and peptide-based nanoparticle crosslinkers, both of which integrate stimulus-responsive degradation tailored to infected wound microenvironments.¹²

---

## Mechanism of hemiaminal hydrogels

The hemiaminal hydrogel platform (FP-hydrogel) is synthesized via one-pot aldimine condensation of poly(ethylene glycol) diamines and polyformaldehyde, generating stable semiclosed eight-membered cyclic hemiaminal structures within the hydrogel network.¹ This architecture is central to the system's multifunctional capacity: under weakly acidic conditions characteristic of bacterial-infected wounds (pH 6.5), the hemiaminal structures selectively degrade and release active [[formaldehyde]] at 74% efficiency, exerting significant antibacterial effects against wound pathogens while maintaining safer concentrations than commercial urotropine agents.¹ Beyond antimicrobial activity, the hemiaminal-type hydrogels simultaneously scavenge excessive [[reactive-oxygen-species|ROS]] generated during infection and the inflammatory response, a dual-action mechanism that addresses two critical aspects of infected wound pathophysiology.¹ The cyclic hemiaminal design also imparts remarkable mechanical properties — including self-healing capability, plasticity, and structural strength — essential for dressing functionality in dynamic wound environments.

---

## Peptide-nanoparticle crosslinked hydrogels

A complementary approach employs cationic peptide-based nanoparticles synthesized via dendritic polymerization through efficient click chemistry, which serve as multifunctional crosslinkers within a polyethylene glycol network.² These nanoparticles exhibit high stability independent of peptide sequence, coupled with readily modifiable surfaces that enable facile incorporation of bioactive moieties.² The resulting hydrogel provides inherent antibacterial activity from the cationic nanoparticles themselves while demonstrating superior mechanical stability, resistance to swelling, and rapid [[wound-healing|wound healing acceleration]].² Unlike conventional passive dressings, this platform actively eradicates bacteria, attenuates [[inflammation]], and promotes [[angiogenesis]] in full-thickness infected wound models.²

---

## Stimulus-responsive degradation and wound microenvironment modulation

Both hydrogel platforms are engineered to respond to the complex biochemical signals present in infected wounds. The hemiaminal hydrogels respond to multiple physiological stimuli — acidic pH, [[reactive-oxygen-species|ROS]], and [[glutathione|glutathione (GSH)]] — enabling spatially and temporally controlled release of active agents precisely when and where they are needed.¹ This stimulus-responsive design ensures that [[formaldehyde]] release is concentrated at sites of active infection rather than diffusing systemically, thereby improving both efficacy and safety. The peptide-nanoparticle hydrogels achieve stimulus-responsiveness through their tailorable crosslinker structure, allowing modulation of swelling kinetics and degradation rates to match specific wound microenvironments.²

---

## Extension to chronic diabetic wounds

Beyond acute bacterial infection, both hydrogel platforms demonstrate efficacy in chronic [[diabetic-wounds|diabetic wounds]], where the pathophysiology differs markedly. In diabetic wounds, [[formaldehyde]]-releasing hemiaminal hydrogels effectively scavenge the overexpressed [[reactive-oxygen-species|ROS]] that characterizes diabetic wound stasis, thereby improving the microenvironment for healing.¹ This extension of both platforms to chronic wound pathology underscores their versatility as advanced wound dressing materials capable of addressing the distinct immunometabolic dysregulation present across acute infectious and chronic metabolic wound disorders.

---

## Novel material design principles

The hemiaminal and peptide-nanoparticle platforms represent advances in rational biomaterial design. The hemiaminal approach leverages stable cyclic structures to confer both mechanical properties and controlled release capability from a single molecular motif — an example of elegant chemical design that minimizes complexity while maximizing function.¹ The peptide-based strategy demonstrates how dendritic polymerization principles, traditionally used in synthetic polymer chemistry, can be adapted to engineer stable peptide nanoparticles through covalent click chemistry, creating peptide-derived materials with tunable properties.² These innovations establish generalizable platforms for developing next-generation wound therapeutics addressing not only acute infection but also the complex microenvironmental failures that characterize both acute and chronic wound pathology.
