**Gasdermin D (GSDMD)** is a pore-forming executioner protein of inflammatory cell death pathways, most prominently in [[panoptosis]], whose cleavage converts it from an inactive cytoplasmic protein to an active membrane-disrupting effector. Upon proteolytic activation by inflammatory caspases or kinases, GSDMD N-terminal domains oligomerize to form lytic pores in the plasma membrane, triggering rapid cellular rupture and the release of danger-associated molecular patterns that amplify immunogenic responses.¹ The protein is transcriptionally regulated by the zinc finger transcription factor [[zfp148|ZFP148]] and proteolytically activated downstream of the [[nek2|NEK2]] kinase, making it a critical therapeutic target for inducing selective death in malignant cells while preserving normal cell viability.

---

## Mechanism

GSDMD is maintained in an inactive state by its inhibitory C-terminal domain, which masks the N-terminal pore-forming region. Upon cleavage — either by inflammatory caspase-1 and caspase-11 in canonical inflammasome pathways, or by upstream kinases including [[nek2|NEK2]] — the N-terminal domain is released and rapidly translocates to the plasma membrane where it oligomerizes to form aqueous pores.¹ These pores permit the efflux of potassium ions and influx of calcium, culminating in osmotic dysregulation and cell lysis. GSDMD pore formation is intimately coupled with [[panoptosis]], a unified inflammatory death pathway integrating apoptotic, necroptotic, and pyroptotic signals. The transcriptional regulation of GSDMD is driven by [[zfp148|ZFP148]], a chromatin-accessible transcription factor that directly binds and activates GSDMD promoter elements in the context of cellular stress.²

## EBV-positive lymphoma

GSDMD emerges as a critical therapeutic effector in [[ebv|EBV]]-positive [[non-hodgkin-lymphoma|non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL)]], where [[nek2|NEK2]] inhibition drives cleavage of the protein and selective elimination of lymphoma cells. [[nek2|NEK2]] kinase is overexpressed in EBV-infected lymphocytes and is activated by latency-associated proteins [[ebna1|EBNA1]], [[lmp1|LMP1]], and [[ebna2|EBNA2]], enabling lymphoma cell proliferation and survival.¹ Inhibition of NEK2 provokes EBV-specific inflammatory cell death characterized by rapid reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation and robust GSDMD cleavage in malignant cells, while normal lymphocytes remain spared — a selectivity that contrasts sharply with conventional chemotherapy.¹ Concurrent with GSDMD activation, [[nek2|NEK2]] inhibition downregulates the [[ebv|EBV]] oncoprotein [[lmp1|LMP1]] and the cellular oncogene [[c-myc|c-myc]], further attenuating lymphoma survival signals. Critically, GSDMD-mediated cell death overcomes multidrug resistance in EBV-positive NHL by suppressing the activity and expression of drug efflux pumps, particularly multidrug resistance-associated protein 1 (MRP1), thereby resensitizing resistant lymphomas to chemotherapeutic agents.¹

## Renal cancer stem cells

GSDMD functions as a core node in the [[panoptosis|PANoptosis]] pathway in renal cancer stem cells (RCSCs), where its upregulation mediates the transition from proliferation to immunogenic cell death. Treatment of RCSCs with the nanocomposite ARDAP@SPION-PEI — a polyethylenimine-coated superparamagnetic iron oxide nanoparticle loaded with the microtubule inhibitor 3-aroyl-1,4-diarylpyrrole (ARDAP) — triggers widespread chromatin accessibility changes and elevates [[panoptosis|PANoptosis]] pathway gene expression.² [[zfp148|ZFP148]], the predominant transcription factor enriched at chromatin-accessible sites, directly activates GSDMD and associated inflammasome components including [[ripk3|RIPK3]], [[casp1|caspase-1]], and [[il1b|interleukin-1β]].² GSDMD upregulation in this context suppresses RCSC proliferation, migration capacity, and tumorigenicity, while simultaneously inhibiting angiogenesis and preventing tumor establishment in vivo.² This therapeutic effect demonstrates that pharmacological manipulation of chromatin state can rewire gene expression toward GSDMD-driven PANoptosis, offering a strategy to overcome the intrinsic therapy resistance of cancer stem cells.
