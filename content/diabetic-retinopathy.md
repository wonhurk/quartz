**Diabetic retinopathy** (DR) is a progressive microvascular disease of the retina and a leading cause of vision loss in [[diabetes-mellitus|diabetes]], characterized by early vascular barrier breakdown and pericyte loss occurring well before extensive retinal damage manifests. Recent mechanistic investigations have identified [[ve-cadherin|vascular endothelial cadherin (VE-cadherin)]] Y685 phosphorylation as the critical initiating molecular event, triggered by hyperglycemia and [[ndpkb|nucleoside diphosphate kinase B (NDPKB)]] deficiency, which promotes endothelial barrier dysfunction and pericyte detachment through activation of the [[hexosamine-biosynthesis-pathway]] and upregulation of [[angiopoietin-2]].¹ A previously unrecognized pathogenic [[pttg1|pituitary tumor-transforming gene 1 (PTTG1)]]-enriched pericyte subpopulation becomes preferentially expanded in diabetic retinas and drives progressive cell loss through metabolic dysregulation and oxidative stress.² Emerging therapeutic strategies targeting VE-cadherin Y685 phosphorylation or [[pttg1|PTTG1]] silencing have demonstrated efficacy in restoring vascular integrity and preventing retinal dysfunction in experimental models of both overt diabetic and prediabetic disease, opening avenues for early intervention before extensive neuronal and vascular tissue damage occurs.

---

## Mechanism of barrier dysfunction

[[ve-cadherin|VE-cadherin]] comprises a critical structural component of endothelial adherens junctions and functions as a primary regulator of blood-retinal barrier integrity. Under hyperglycemic conditions, particularly when coincident with [[ndpkb|NDPKB]] deficiency, high glucose triggers Y685 phosphorylation of [[ve-cadherin|VE-cadherin]], causing internalization from the junctional membrane and consequent loss of barrier-supporting function.¹ This phosphorylation event activates the [[hexosamine-biosynthesis-pathway]], leading to increased protein O-linked β-N-acetyl glucosamine (O-GlcNAc) modification and subsequent upregulation of [[angiopoietin-2|angiopoietin 2 (Ang2)]].¹ The resulting elevation in [[angiopoietin-2|Ang2]] impairs endothelial tight junction stability and disrupts pericyte-endothelial contact through reduced adhesion signaling, thereby exacerbating loss of vascular integrity. Critically, the [[ve-cadherin|VE-cadherin]] Y685F mutation — which prevents phosphorylation at this residue — blocks these entire hyperglycemia-driven pathological cascades in vivo, demonstrating the central mechanistic role of this single posttranslational modification in retinopathy initiation.¹

## Pathogenic pericyte loss and metabolic dysfunction

Pericyte degeneration is an early and defining pathological hallmark of diabetic retinopathy, yet the intrinsic pericyte defects driving this loss have remained incompletely characterized. Single-cell RNA sequencing of diabetic retinas identified a distinct pericyte subpopulation defined by elevated [[pttg1|PTTG1]] expression that becomes pathologically enriched specifically in diabetic conditions.² These [[pttg1|PTTG1]]-high pericytes undergo accelerated dysfunction through metabolic dysregulation and heightened oxidative stress that destabilizes their structural integrity and impairs their capacity to maintain vascular support under hyperglycemic challenge. Functional studies demonstrate that silencing [[pttg1|PTTG1]] via CRISPR-Cas9 or small interfering RNA (siRNA) restores pericyte barrier-supporting function and metabolic homeostasis, reducing glycolytic flux dysregulation and substantially attenuating oxidative damage.² This [[pttg1|PTTG1]]-dependent metabolic reprogramming preserves pericyte survival and maintains vascular integrity even under sustained hyperglycemic stress, establishing [[pttg1|PTTG1]] dysregulation as a targetable vulnerability in diabetic retinal pericytes.

---

## Therapeutic targeting and vascular restoration

The discovery that [[ve-cadherin|VE-cadherin]] Y685F-knockin mice are protected from both overt diabetes- and prediabetes-induced vascular hyperpermeability establishes a clear therapeutic paradigm: blocking this single phosphorylation event prevents the molecular initiation of retinal vascular dysfunction.¹ In vivo protection correlates with reduced global protein O-GlcNAcylation, suppressed [[angiopoietin-2|Ang2]] induction, and critically, preservation of both neuronal and endothelial function.¹ Comprehensive retinal proteomics of protected animals revealed restoration of balanced neurovascular and mitochondrial signaling pathways, indicating that blocking [[ve-cadherin|VE-cadherin]] Y685 phosphorylation rescues multiple cellular systems compromised in diabetic disease.¹

For pericyte-directed therapy, [[pttg1|PTTG1]] silencing delivered via viral or pericyte-specific adeno-associated virus vectors substantially improves retinal vascular integrity in diabetic models.² A translational advancement employs spherical nucleic acid-based siPttg1 nanocarriers (sTDN-siPttg1), which provide a systemic delivery vehicle capable of substantially ameliorating retinal vascular dysfunction in vivo.² This nanoparticle approach enables sustained [[pttg1|PTTG1]] knockdown in the diabetic retina while maintaining acceptable tolerability.

---

## Early intervention in prediabetic disease

A clinically significant insight emerges from studies of [[ve-cadherin|VE-cadherin]] Y685F protection: vascular dysfunction initiation occurs not only in overt [[diabetes-mellitus|diabetes]] but also in [[prediabetes|prediabetic]] states through the identical molecular mechanism.¹ Early VE-cadherin Y685 phosphorylation and vascular hyperpermeability precede extensive pericyte loss in prediabetic retinas, suggesting that intervention at this early stage — before the irreversible transition to clinically apparent diabetic retinopathy — could prevent or substantially delay disease progression in at-risk populations.¹ This finding positions [[ve-cadherin|VE-cadherin]] Y685 phosphorylation as both a biomarker and tractable therapeutic target for primary prevention of vision-threatening complications in individuals with impaired glucose tolerance, offering an unprecedented opportunity for halting microvascular disease before extensive neuronal damage occurs.
