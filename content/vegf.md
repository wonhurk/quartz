**Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor (VEGF)** is a critical pro-angiogenic cytokine that promotes new blood vessel formation across both physiological and pathological contexts. It is primarily regulated by [[hif-1-alpha|hypoxia-inducible factor-1α (HIF-1α)]] and serves as a central mediator of angiogenesis in cancer, retinal degeneration, wound healing, and bone remodeling.¹ In the tumor microenvironment, VEGF-A expression is driven by [[il-17|IL-17]]-mediated HIF-1α signaling, contributing to immune evasion and metabolic immunosuppression.² Therapeutically, VEGF is targeted through multiple modalities including anti-VEGF antibodies, small-molecule inhibitors, and peptide-based platforms that modulate vascular normalization and enhance anti-tumor immunity.³

---

## Mechanism

VEGF exerts its pro-angiogenic effects by binding to VEGF receptors on endothelial cells, triggering downstream signaling cascades that promote endothelial cell proliferation, migration, and vascular permeability. Expression of VEGF-A is tightly regulated by the [[hif-1-alpha|HIF-1α]]/VEGF signaling pathway, particularly under hypoxic conditions.¹ In the tumor microenvironment, [[il-17|IL-17]] drives HIF-1α stabilization, which in turn upregulates VEGF-A transcription, creating an immunosuppressive niche that favors tumor progression.² The VEGF/Tie2 signaling axis regulates vascular maturation and integrity; inhibition of both pathways induces vascular normalization — a transient state characterized by improved vessel perfusion, reduced interstitial pressure, and enhanced immune cell infiltration.³

[[nadph|NADPH]] enhances VEGF expression by approximately 44.8% following cerebral ischemia through the HIF-1α/VEGF pathway, promoting angiogenesis and blood-brain barrier repair.¹ Conversely, fucoidan bioactivity suppresses VEGF-A-mediated angiogenesis in cancer contexts while simultaneously promoting M1 macrophage polarization.⁴

## Retinal disease and age-related macular degeneration

Intravitreal anti-VEGF injection represents the standard clinical treatment for choroidal neovascularization (CNV) in [[age-related-macular-degeneration|age-related macular degeneration (AMD)]], a leading cause of irreversible blindness. Current anti-VEGF monotherapy targets only late-stage CNV and is associated with incomplete response rates and potential adverse effects.⁵ To address these limitations, triple-pool-targeted nanotherapy combining the antiferroptosis agent nuciferine with antioxidant bilirubin (Nuci@PAM-BR) achieved iron homeostasis restoration and eradicated CNV through simultaneous antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antiangiogenic mechanisms.⁵ This platform reduced leakage area by over 80% and neovascular lesion burden by over 85%, offering a promising alternative to conventional anti-VEGF monotherapy for fundus neovascularization diseases.

## Cancer vascular normalization and immunotherapy

In advanced [[renal-cell-carcinoma|renal cell carcinoma (RCC)]] and other malignancies, VEGF inhibition serves a dual purpose: inducing vascular normalization and enhancing immune checkpoint blockade efficacy. The VEGF/Tie2 dual-targeting peptide inhibitor [[ring1|RING1]] simultaneously blocks VEGF and Tie2 receptors while inhibiting the TIGIT immune checkpoint, achieving spatiotemporally synchronized vascular normalization and immune activation within the transient normalization window.³ RING1 enhanced vascular normalization by 1.8-fold compared to clinical combination therapy, reduced immunosuppressive Tie2-expressing monocytes (TEMs), and elevated secretion of IFN-γ and granzyme B.³ Its cyclic structure incorporating D-amino acids confers enhanced in vivo stability, tumor-specific accumulation, and self-assembly into intratumoral nanonetworks that amplify target engagement.

In [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]], [[kcmf1|KCMF1]] overexpression correlates with poor prognosis and is functionally linked to VEGF and TGF-β signaling pathways that promote angiogenesis and immune suppression.⁶ Single-cell analysis identified KCMF1-positive malignant cells as hubs of intercellular communication within the tumor microenvironment, with positive correlation to M0 macrophage infiltration — the precursor population for tumor-associated macrophage (TAM) polarization.

In [[melanoma]] brain metastases, the metabolic regulator [[il-17|IL-17]] drives HIF-1α/VEGF-A signaling, establishing immunosuppression via the CD73/adenosine axis.² Nose-to-brain codelivery of anti-IL-17 and anti-CD73 antibodies using glycerol as a mucosal penetration enhancer enhanced brain delivery by 19.4- and 17.1-fold, respectively, and attenuated VEGF-A-mediated immune evasion, promoting CD8+ T cell activation and pro-inflammatory macrophage polarization.²

## Wound healing and tissue repair

VEGF plays an essential role in diabetic wound healing, where impaired angiogenesis contributes to chronic non-healing wounds. In type 2 diabetic wound models, combined application of a [[decellularized-dermal-scaffold|decellularized dermal scaffold (DDS)]] with [[photobiomodulation-therapy|photobiomodulation therapy (PBM)]] modulated angiogenic, inflammatory, and matrix remodeling responses.⁷ This combination significantly accelerated wound contraction, improved tissue tensile strength and structural organization, and enhanced angiogenesis compared to single-modality or untreated controls.⁷

In ischemic stroke, [[nadph|NADPH]] treatment at 7.5 mg/kg conferred neuroprotection by preserving blood-brain barrier (BBB) integrity and stimulating angiogenesis.¹ NADPH elevated VEGF expression by approximately 44.8% at day 7 post-ischemia through the HIF-1α/VEGF signaling pathway, promoting endothelial cell proliferation and functional recovery while reducing infarct volume by approximately 50%.¹

## Bone remodeling and osteogenesis-angiogenesis coupling

VEGF serves as a key angiogenic factor in the coupled osteogenesis-angiogenesis process essential for bone remodeling and repair, particularly in [[postmenopausal-osteoporosis|postmenopausal osteoporosis (PMOP)]].⁸ [[dendrobine|Dendrobine]], a natural alkaloid, promotes concurrent osteogenic differentiation of bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs) and angiogenic activity by inhibiting the Hippo signaling pathway.⁸ Western blotting demonstrated that dendrobine upregulated angiogenic factors including VEGF, MMP2, and MMP9 in coculture systems of BMSCs and human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs), enhancing tube formation capacity.⁸ In ovariectomized PMOP rat models, dendrobine treatment improved bone microarchitecture and suppressed apoptosis, highlighting the therapeutic potential of VEGF-mediated angiogenesis in skeletal health.

## Therapeutic platforms targeting VEGF

Multiple advanced drug delivery platforms have been engineered to suppress VEGF signaling. [[final|FINAL (Fucoidan-docetaxel Immunomodulatory Nanoparticles as an Antitumoral Lancer)]] is a surface-engineered nanoplatform targeting P-selectin-expressing cancer cells and TAMs in [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)]].⁴ Beyond targeting specificity, fucoidan surface modification provides intrinsic bioactivities that suppress VEGF-A-mediated angiogenesis, reduce reactive oxygen species, and promote M1 macrophage polarization.⁴ RNA-seq transcriptomic profiling demonstrated that FINAL drives synergistic immune activation pathways while repressing tumor progression signatures, doubling survival duration in TNBC models.

The supramolecular peptide inhibitor [[ring1|RING1]] integrates VEGF/Tie2-targeting and TIGIT-blocking modules within a single molecule, enabling simultaneous vascular normalization and immune checkpoint blockade with spatiotemporal synchronization.³ Upon tumor enrichment, RING1 self-assembles into extensive nanonetworks that amplify target engagement and prolong intratumoral retention, demonstrating superior tumor-suppressive and anti-metastatic efficacy with improved biosafety in orthotopic metastatic RCC models.³

Nuci@PAM-BR, a triple-pool-targeted nanotherapy, achieves VEGF suppression through combined antioxidant and antiferroptosis mechanisms, addressing the root causes of pathological angiogenesis in retinal degenerative diseases.⁵
