**Nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH)** is the progressive, inflammatory subtype of [[nafld|nonalcoholic fatty liver disease]] characterized by hepatic steatosis, hepatocellular inflammation, and lipid accumulation.¹ The disease arises from convergence of two dysregulated pathways: elevated hepatic [[ctsg|cathepsin G (CTSG)]] expression that drives lipid deposition through suppression of mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation, and activation of macrophages via the [[cd1d|CD1d]]-[[pira2|PIRA2]] signaling axis, which triggers innate immune responses and perpetuates hepatic inflammation.¹² Unlike simple steatosis, NASH progression involves coordinated lipogenic and immunologic dysfunction, positioning both CTSG and PIRA2 as central drivers of disease pathogenesis.

---

## Mechanism

NASH pathogenesis involves dual dysregulation of hepatic lipid metabolism and macrophage-driven inflammation. [[ctsg|CTSG]], a serine protease, is significantly elevated in NASH patient livers and its expression positively correlates with disease severity.² CTSG promotes hepatic lipid deposition by suppressing the [[akt|Akt signaling pathway]], thereby upregulating key lipogenic genes including [[acc|acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC)]] and [[scd1|stearoyl-CoA desaturase 1 (SCD1)]] while simultaneously downregulating genes involved in lipid oxidation ([[ppara|PPARα]] and LCAD) and lipid export via the microsomal triglyceride transfer protein (MTTP).² This metabolic reprogramming forces hepatocytes toward lipid accumulation and away from catabolism. Importantly, in vivo CTSG overexpression impairs glucose tolerance and elevates HOMA-IR, demonstrating systemic metabolic dysfunction.

In parallel, macrophage-intrinsic immune signaling drives hepatic inflammation. [[cd1d|CD1d]], functioning as a surface ligand, directly engages the immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motif (ITAM)-containing [[pira2|PIRA2]] receptor on macrophage surfaces, triggering FcRγ phosphorylation and downstream inflammatory cascade activation.¹ This CD1d-PIRA2 interaction occurs through the α1 and α2 domains of CD1d binding the D1 and D2 domains of PIRA2, establishing a molecular specificity in macrophage activation.¹ Studies in [[pira2]]-deficient mice demonstrate significantly reduced hepatic inflammation and lipid accumulation, confirming PIRA2 as a disease-critical node.¹ Further, macrophage-specific PIRA2 conditional knockout mice exhibit markedly diminished macrophage activation and reduced inflammatory cytokine production, establishing PIRA2's macrophage-intrinsic role in NASH progression.¹

---

## Disease severity and clinical features

NASH represents the progressive, symptomatic stage of nonalcoholic fatty liver disease and is distinguished from simple hepatic steatosis by the presence of hepatocellular inflammation and potential fibrosis progression. The elevation of [[ctsg|CTSG]] expression correlates directly with disease severity in NASH patients, making it a potential biomarker for risk stratification.² The dual dysfunction of lipid metabolism (CTSG-mediated accumulation) and immune homeostasis (CD1d-PIRA2 macrophage activation) creates a self-reinforcing inflammatory microenvironment where hepatocytes become lipid-laden while macrophages sustain chronic inflammation, driving progression toward fibrosis and cirrhosis.

---

## Therapeutic targets

The identification of [[ctsg|CTSG]] and [[pira2|PIRA2]] as discrete, tractable drivers of NASH pathogenesis offers multiple intervention points. Hepatic CTSG inhibition or suppression could reduce lipogenic gene expression and restore fatty acid oxidation, while simultaneously blocking [[cd1d|CD1d]]-[[pira2|PIRA2]] signaling via PIRA2 antagonism or neutralizing antibodies could attenuate macrophage activation and inflammatory cytokine production. In human macrophages, [[lilra2|LILRA2]] (the human ortholog of mouse PIRA2) blockade similarly suppresses CD1d-induced proinflammatory cytokine expression, indicating that the pathway is conserved and targetable in patients.¹ Elevated CD1d and LILRA2 protein levels in NAFLD patient livers suggest that this axis is pathologically active and amenable to immunotherapeutic intervention.
