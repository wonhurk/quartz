**Ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) injury** is a pathological condition characterized by cellular damage and dysfunction that occurs when blood flow is restored to previously ischemic tissue, paradoxically exacerbating rather than alleviating injury. The phenomenon affects multiple organs including the heart, brain, liver, and kidney, manifesting through complex interconnected mechanisms of excessive oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, aberrant macrophage polarization, endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress, and proinflammatory cell death pathways.¹⁻⁶ Recent discoveries have identified previously unrecognized organ-specific contributors including gut microbiota-derived [[dipeptidyl-peptidase-4|dipeptidyl peptidase 4 (BaDPP4)]] in cardiac I/R¹ and acute hyperglycemia-induced neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation in cerebral I/R.² NAD⁺-dependent sirtuins—particularly [[sirt1|SIRT1]] and [[sirt2|SIRT2]]—emerge as critical protective mediators across different organs, positioning NAD⁺ metabolism as a central therapeutic target.³⁻⁶

---

## Mechanism

The pathophysiology of I/R injury involves a cascade of cellular events initiated during the ischemic period and dramatically amplified upon reperfusion. Restoration of blood flow triggers a burst of reactive oxygen species (ROS) production that overwhelms endogenous antioxidant systems, leading to lipid peroxidation, protein oxidation, and DNA damage.⁴ Mitochondrial dysfunction plays a dual role, both as a source of excessive ROS generation and as a victim of oxidative damage, resulting in impaired energy production and release of pro-apoptotic factors.⁴ Simultaneously, ER stress accumulates through protein misfolding and calcium dysregulation, activating the unfolded protein response and driving inflammatory signaling cascades.⁶ Infiltrating immune cells—particularly neutrophils and macrophages—amplify tissue damage through release of proteolytic enzymes, cytokines, and formation of extracellular traps that occlude microvessels and prevent effective tissue perfusion even after macrovascular recanalization.²

## Cardiac I/R injury

A previously unrecognized gut-heart axis contributes substantially to myocardial I/R injury through selective enrichment of the gut bacterium Bacteroides acidifaciens following I/R-induced intestinal hypoxia and elevated luminal lactate.¹ This microbe produces a dipeptidyl peptidase 4 enzyme (BaDPP4) distinct from the host isozyme, which enters the circulation and degrades cardioprotective peptides including [[glucagon-like-peptide-1|glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1)]], thereby amplifying cardiomyocyte loss and cardiac dysfunction in [[acute-myocardial-infarction|acute myocardial infarction]] patients.¹ The correlation between fecal B. acidifaciens abundance, plasma BaDPP4 levels, and clinical markers of cardiac damage in human I/R patients establishes this microbial pathway as a validated therapeutic target.¹

Cardiac protection can be achieved through activation of [[sirt2|SIRT2]], a NAD⁺-dependent deacetylase that suppresses the NLRP3 inflammasome pathway.⁵ [[salvianolic-acid-b|Salvianolic acid B (SaB)]], a natural compound from Salvia miltiorrhiza, functions as a SIRT2 agonist binding specifically to the SER-263 residue, but its cardioprotective potential is limited by rapid metabolism and poor bioavailability.⁵ When formulated as [[sab-qd-cypate|organic quantum dot nanoparticles modified with the near-infrared dye cypate]], SaB accumulation in ischemic myocardium increases 3.2-fold, resulting in a 41% reduction in infarct area compared to 28% reduction achieved by mitochondrial-targeted CoQ10 nanoparticles.⁵ The cypate modification confers dual functionality, enabling real-time near-infrared imaging of ischemic borders and photothermal capabilities with temperature increases of 15.3°C under 808 nm laser irradiation.⁵

## Cerebral I/R injury

Acute hyperglycemia significantly exacerbates cerebral I/R injury through a mechanism that persists even after spontaneous glucose normalization within three hours.² The transient hyperglycemic state activates the receptor for advanced glycation end products (RAGE) and its downstream effector diaphanous-1 (DIAPH1), driving neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation that obstructs cerebral microvessels and produces futile recanalization—a phenomenon where macrovascular patency fails to restore tissue perfusion.² Pharmacological intervention with [[fps-zm1|FPS-ZM1]], a RAGE inhibitor, or [[dnase-i|deoxyribonuclease I (DNase I)]], which degrades NET components, significantly alleviates microvascular obstruction and reduces brain injury in middle cerebral artery occlusion/reperfusion models.²

[[Electroacupuncture]] at the GV20 and GV16 acupoints promotes functional recovery after cerebral I/R by orchestrating astrocyte phenotypic transformation from the neurotoxic A1 state to the repair-supporting A2 phenotype.³ This therapeutic effect is mediated through activation of [[sirt1|SIRT1]], which suppresses NF-κB phosphorylation and shifts inflammatory signaling toward an anti-inflammatory profile.³ Seven days of daily electroacupuncture treatment upregulates both SIRT1 protein levels and its deacetylase activity, reduces expression of the A1 marker C3 while increasing the A2 marker S100A10, and enhances synaptic protein expression accompanied by improved synaptic ultrastructure on transmission electron microscopy.³ Administration of the SIRT1 inhibitor Selisistat abolishes these benefits, confirming the SIRT1-dependent mechanism.³

## Hepatic I/R injury

The use of aged donor livers in transplantation is complicated by their heightened susceptibility to I/R injury, driven by NAD⁺ depletion and consequent SIRT1 dysfunction in aged hepatic macrophages.⁶ Following I/R, NAD⁺ levels decline markedly in aged macrophages, impairing SIRT1 deacetylase activity and leading to hyperacetylation of eukaryotic translation initiation factor 2α (eIF2α).⁶ This post-translational modification triggers ER stress accumulation, promotes pro-inflammatory macrophage polarization, and drives expression of inflammatory cytokines including TNF-α, iNOS, and IL-1β, culminating in exacerbated hepatic injury during the late phase of I/R.⁶ Supplementation with [[nmn|β-nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN)]], a NAD⁺ precursor, restores SIRT1 activity in aged hepatic macrophages both in vivo and in vitro, reduces eIF2α acetylation, alleviates ER stress, and suppresses pro-inflammatory polarization.⁶ This NAD⁺-SIRT1-eIF2α axis represents a promising therapeutic strategy to improve the viability of marginal liver grafts from elderly donors.⁶

## Renal I/R injury

Acute kidney injury (AKI) resulting from I/R involves overlapping pathological processes of oxidative stress overload, aberrant macrophage polarization, mitochondrial dysfunction, and renal tubular epithelial cell apoptosis, presenting a therapeutic challenge due to the absence of effective clinical drugs.⁴ A multifunctional nanoplatform based on RGD-peptide-functionalized mesoporous hollow cerium dioxide nanospheres loaded with quercetin (RGD-CeO₂@Que) addresses this complexity through multiple coordinated mechanisms.⁴ The RGD peptide enables targeted accumulation in injured kidneys by binding to integrin αvβ3 receptors that are overexpressed under oxidative stress, while the cerium dioxide core functions as a catalytic antioxidant that alleviates oxidative stress through activation of the Nrf2/HO-1/GPX4/SOD1 pathway.⁴ The quercetin payload promotes mitophagy in renal tubular epithelial cells, facilitating clearance of damaged mitochondria and reducing apoptotic cell death.⁴ In vivo studies demonstrate that this integrated nanoplatform improves renal function indices, ameliorates pathological damage, and reduces inflammatory infiltration in murine AKI models.⁴

## Therapeutic approaches

Emerging therapeutic strategies for I/R injury span traditional modalities, natural compound activation of protective pathways, microbiota-targeted interventions, and advanced nanomedicine platforms. The convergence of multiple studies on sirtuins—particularly SIRT1 and SIRT2—as protective mediators across cardiac, cerebral, and hepatic I/R injury establishes NAD⁺ metabolism as a unifying therapeutic target.³⁵⁶ [[Nmn|NMN]] supplementation restores NAD⁺ levels in aged tissues and reactivates sirtuin-dependent deacetylation pathways, offering a pharmacologically tractable approach to counteract age-related vulnerability to I/R injury.⁶ Natural sirtuin agonists including [[salvianolic-acid-b|salvianolic acid B]] demonstrate organ-protective effects when formulated to overcome bioavailability limitations through nanoparticle encapsulation.⁵

The gut-heart axis presents a novel intervention point through targeting of microbial-derived [[dipeptidyl-peptidase-4|DPP4]], with the microbial DPP4-specific inhibitor daurisoline mitigating cardiac dysfunction by preventing degradation of cardioprotective peptides.¹ In cerebral I/R complicated by acute hyperglycemia, disruption of the [[acute-hyperglycemia|RAGE/DIAPH1]] axis with [[fps-zm1|FPS-ZM1]] or enzymatic degradation of neutrophil extracellular traps with [[dnase-i|DNase I]] prevents microvascular obstruction and futile recanalization.² Advanced nanoplatform approaches integrate multiple therapeutic mechanisms—antioxidant activity, targeted drug delivery, macrophage polarization modulation, and mitophagy promotion—within a single construct guided to sites of injury through receptor-mediated targeting, exemplified by the RGD-CeO₂@Que system for renal protection.⁴
