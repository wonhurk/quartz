**Interleukin-15 (IL-15)** is a pleiotropic cytokine that serves as a critical immunomodulatory agent for the activation and expansion of [[cytokine-induced-killer-cells|cytokine-induced killer (CIK) cells]], which combine the functional properties of both [[t-cells|T cells]] and [[natural-killer-cells|natural killer (NK) cells]].¹ The IL-15-activated CIK cell approach addresses a significant unmet clinical need in post-transplantation disease control, as demonstrated by the first-in-human study showing durable remission and immune reconstitution in patients with high-risk leukemia undergoing [[allogeneic-hct|allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation (HCT)]].¹ IL-15's role in enhancing both T cell and NK cell proliferation and cytotoxic function establishes it as a cornerstone agent for adoptive cellular immunotherapy.

---

## Mechanism of CIK Cell Activation

IL-15 activates [[cytokine-induced-killer-cells|CIK cells]] through stimulation of T cell receptor signaling pathways and natural killer cell receptors, generating immune effector cells with uniquely enhanced cytotoxic capacity.¹ The resulting IL-15-activated CIK population exhibits hybrid immune functionality, expressing both [[t-cells|T cell]] markers (CD3+) and [[natural-killer-cells|NK cell]] markers, allowing them to recognize and eliminate malignant cells through multiple activation pathways. This dual-receptor expression paradigm enables CIK cells to maintain broad tumor recognition capabilities while simultaneously engaging both CD4+ and CD8+ T cell lineages and NK-mediated cytotoxicity, creating a versatile cellular therapy platform amenable to allogeneic transfer without the immunogenicity constraints of single-lineage cell therapies.

---

## Clinical Application in Allogeneic Hematopoietic Cell Transplantation

Allogeneic [[allogeneic-hct|HCT]] remains the only curative option for high-risk leukemia, yet patients face substantial risk of early relapse and treatment-related mortality. The first-in-human, disease burden-guided study of donor-derived allogeneic IL-15-activated [[cytokine-induced-killer-cells|CIK cells]] demonstrated clinical feasibility, safety, and efficacy for post-transplant disease control.¹ IL-15-activated CIK cells were successfully expanded from donor tissue and infused into leukemia patients following allogeneic HCT, achieving durable remission alongside serotherapy-associated immune reconstitution.¹ This landmark trial established IL-15-activated cellular therapy as a viable strategy for relapse prevention, combining disease surveillance with targeted immune intervention while simultaneously promoting immune reconstitution and reducing both relapse risk and late infectious complications commonly observed after transplantation.

---

## Emerging delivery platforms for immunomodulatory cytokines

Beyond direct cellular therapy, emerging [[influenza-a-virus|influenza virus]]-based vector systems are being developed as platforms for the delivery of immunomodulatory cytokines.² Live-attenuated [[influenza-a-virus|influenza A virus]] vectors engineered to express and secrete therapeutic proteins represent a novel approach to harness cytokine-mediated immune activation with integrated viral immunogenicity, offering replicative capacity in interferon-competent cellular systems while maintaining genetic stability across passage.² Such versatile delivery platforms could potentially extend the therapeutic reach of IL-15 and related immunomodulatory cytokines to mucosal and systemic immunization contexts, providing complementary strategies to conventional adoptive cell therapy approaches.
