**Photothermal therapy (PTT)** is a light-based therapeutic technology that leverages the conversion of near-infrared (NIR) irradiation into localized heat to achieve direct tissue destruction and activate immunomodulatory responses. The approach simultaneously induces tumor cell death through thermal damage and triggers [[immunogenic-cell-death|immunogenic cell death (ICD)]], mobilizing adaptive immune responses against cancer and metabolic disorders.¹ [[indocyanine-green|Indocyanine green (ICG)]] remains the only U.S. Food and Drug Administration-approved photothermal agent, though its clinical application is constrained by poor stability, short blood half-life, and lack of tumor targeting—limitations now addressed through advanced [[nanoparticle|nanocarrier systems]] such as biosynthetic [[gas-vesicles|gas vesicles (GVs)]] that enable tumor-specific delivery and remote acoustic triggering.²

---

## Mechanism

PTT operates by delivering focused NIR light (typically 700–1100 nm) to tissue, where photothermal agents absorb photons and dissipate the energy as heat through nonradiative pathways. Temperatures exceeding 50°C within the tumor microenvironment cause direct protein denaturation, mitochondrial dysfunction, and membrane rupture, inducing rapid cancer cell apoptosis and necrosis. Beyond thermal ablation, the heat generated during PTT activates damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) and promotes the release of tumor-associated antigens, recruiting dendritic cells and tumor-infiltrating [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T lymphocytes]] to establish systemic antitumor immunity.¹ Modern PTT platforms exploit heterostructured materials such as [[bi2se3-bise|Bi₂Se₃@BiSe nanosheets]], which utilize efficient charge carrier separation at interfacial contacts to simultaneously activate photothermal conversion and generate photodynamic reactive oxygen species, achieving dual thermal and oxidative cell death simultaneously.³

## Cancer applications

PTT has demonstrated clinical promise across multiple malignancies, particularly where conventional chemotherapy encounters resistance. In [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]], platycodin-D-loaded iron nanocomposites (PIF NCs) integrate PTT with [[ferroptosis]] and photodynamic therapy, disrupting cellular redox equilibrium by depleting glutathione and downregulating protective enzymes such as [[gpx4|GPX4]] and [[slc7a11|SLC7A11]], thereby triggering ferroptotic cell death concurrent with thermal ablation.⁴ In [[bladder-cancer|murine bladder cancer models]], acoustic delivery of ICG-conjugated biosynthetic gas vesicles achieves tumor temperatures exceeding 60°C upon laser irradiation, resulting in complete tumor regression and prolonged survival without systemic toxicity.² The acoustic-triggered cavitation of gas vesicles provides spatiotemporal control, enhancing intratumoral drug concentration and penetration while minimizing off-target heating.

## Immunotherapy integration and synergy

PTT-based nanoplatforms increasingly serve as immunomodulatory agents when combined with checkpoint inhibitors. Heat-induced [[immunogenic-cell-death|immunogenic cell death]] primes dendritic cells and enhances [[mhc-ii|MHC-II]] presentation on tumor-associated macrophages, sensitizing tumor cells to [[anti-pd-l1-antibody|anti-PD-L1 blockade]].⁴ Triple-modal heterostructures such as [[bi2se3-bise|Bi₂Se₃@BiSe]] simultaneously polarize M0 macrophages toward the tumor-suppressive M1 phenotype through direct immunomodulation, amplifying the cytotoxic T cell response beyond thermal effects alone.³ Additionally, cascade-responsive nanoplatforms coupling PTT with [[cuproptosis]] and hydrogen sulfide gas therapy (H₂S-GT) synchronously release copper ions and sulfide in the acidic tumor microenvironment, inducing mitochondrial copper accumulation through ATP7A downregulation whilst generating reactive oxygen species, and simultaneously reprogram immunosuppressive macrophages via vascular normalization.⁵ Combined PTT and anti-PD-L1 therapy in HCC models establishes prolonged immunological memory, overcoming adaptive resistance limitations inherent to single-modality approaches.

## Obesity management and metabolic applications

Beyond oncology, PTT has emerged as a metabolic intervention exploiting the unique hypoxic microenvironment of dysfunctional adipose tissue in obesity. Hypoxia-activated [[cbh-o|phototheranostic probes (CBH-O)]] enable targeted [[photoacoustic-imaging|photoacoustic imaging]] of visceral adipose tissue whilst generating photothermal conversion upon hypoxic activation, inducing white adipose tissue browning through thermogenic stimulation and enhancing lipolysis.¹ In obese mice, photothermal modulation of visceral adipose tissue reduced body weight by 15.6%, adipose tissue mass by 65%, and serum cholesterol, triglyceride, and insulin by 49.1%, 30.3%, and 75.7% respectively, whilst suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, MCP-1) and elevating anti-inflammatory IL-10.¹ This targeted modulation of the adipose tissue microenvironment addresses metabolic dysfunction without systemic toxicity, providing a novel approach to obesity-associated complications including insulin resistance and chronic inflammation.

## Multimodal integration and spatiotemporal control

Contemporary PTT platforms are increasingly architected as structurally simple yet inherently multifunctional nanocomposites that activate multiple complementary cell death pathways simultaneously. Heterostructured nanomaterials integrate photothermal, [[photodynamic-therapy|photodynamic]], and ferroptotic functionalities within a single synthetic entity, obviating the need for cocktail-loaded platforms that suffer from preparation complexity, batch heterogeneity, and potential off-target effects.³ Cascade-responsive designs further refine therapeutic precision through tumor microenvironment-responsive release mechanisms: dendritic mesoporous organosilica shells release cuproptosis-inducing copper ions and hydrogen sulfide gas selectively in acidified endosomal compartments, ensuring minimal systemic exposure and maximal intracellular efficacy.⁵ Spatially controlled activation of multiple death pathways—thermal ablation, oxidative stress, ferroptotic lipid peroxidation, and mitochondrial copper toxicity—dramatically reduces the probability of therapeutic escape and monotherapy resistance.

---

## Novel delivery and next-generation agents

Biosynthetic gas vesicles represent a major innovation in PTT delivery, providing uniform nanostructure (~200 nm), high ICG loading capacity (58%), colloidal stability, and trimodal imaging capabilities encompassing ultrasound, near-infrared fluorescence, and photoacoustic imaging for real-time visualization.² Acoustic cavitation of GVs enables remote triggering, transforming inert nanoparticles into active therapeutics upon ultrasound application whilst preserving systemic safety. Supramolecular coordination nanocomposites—exemplified by iron-based platforms loaded with natural bioactive compounds such as platycodin-D—leverage designer self-assembly to achieve tunable pharmacokinetics and tumor microenvironment-responsive release whilst maintaining exceptional biocompatibility.⁴ Emerging heterostructured materials with engineered interfacial charge transfer properties offer intrinsic multifunctionality without additional chemical conjugation, reducing synthetic complexity and improving reproducibility for clinical translation.³
