**Insulin** is a pancreatic protein hormone essential for regulating glucose metabolism and maintaining metabolic homeostasis across diverse physiological contexts. Secreted by pancreatic β-cells, insulin mediates cellular glucose uptake, suppresses hepatic glucose production, and coordinates lipid and protein metabolism through activation of the insulin receptor (IR) signaling cascade.¹ Its therapeutic landscape spans innovative delivery technologies, engineered analogs with enhanced receptor binding profiles, [[automated-insulin-delivery|automated dosing systems]], and combination strategies that reduce insulin requirements in both type 1 and type 2 diabetes.

---

## Mechanism of action

Insulin exerts its metabolic effects by binding to the insulin receptor, which exists in two isoforms (IR-A and IR-B) distributed across metabolic tissues. Receptor engagement triggers autophosphorylation and activation of downstream signaling cascades, including the PI3K-AKT pathway governing glucose uptake and the MAPK pathway regulating cell growth.² In pathological states such as [[type-2-diabetes|type 2 diabetes]], insulin signaling becomes impaired through mechanisms including chronic inflammation and receptor desensitization. The traditional Chinese herbal formula [[xiehuang-san|Xiehuang San (XHS)]] restores insulin signaling by targeting the CLCF1-STAT3 pathway, demonstrating therapeutic efficacy through modulation of upstream inflammatory mediators.¹ Similarly, [[metformin|metformin]] enhances insulin sensitivity by suppressing hepatic gluconeogenesis and activating AMP-activated protein kinase.

## Diabetes management applications

Insulin replacement therapy remains the cornerstone of [[diabetes-mellitus|type 1 diabetes]] treatment, where autoimmune destruction of β-cells eliminates endogenous production. In type 2 diabetes with progressive β-cell failure, exogenous insulin becomes necessary when oral agents no longer maintain glycemic control. [[automated-insulin-delivery|Automated insulin delivery systems]] have transformed clinical practice by coupling continuous glucose monitoring with algorithm-driven insulin dosing. The Control-IQ+ system enables simplified meal bolus strategies in adults with insulin-treated type 2 diabetes, maintaining glycemic targets while reducing dosing complexity.³ 

Adjunct pharmacotherapy can substantially reduce insulin dose requirements. In adults with type 1 diabetes and obesity using automated delivery systems, weekly semaglutide 1 mg produces dose reductions proportional to weight loss, addressing both glycemic control and obesity simultaneously.⁴ This dual benefit reflects semaglutide's complementary mechanisms: enhanced insulin secretion, suppressed glucagon release, and delayed gastric emptying that collectively reduce prandial insulin demand.

## Autoimmune biomarkers and disease pathogenesis

Anti-insulin autoantibodies serve as critical biomarkers in type 1 diabetes, with both [[immunoglobulin-m|IgM]] and IgG isotypes detectable prior to clinical onset. The in utero environment profoundly influences autoantibody patterns and disease trajectory. NOD mouse embryos gestated in C57BL/6 females exhibit approximately 50% reduced diabetes incidence despite equivalent insulitis severity, accompanied by significantly elevated IgM titers against insulin, DNA, and GAD65.⁵ These NOD/B6 offspring display altered IgG subclass distributions—including elevated IgG1 and IgG2c—along with increased CD4⁺CD25⁺ regulatory T cells that may confer partial protection. The data demonstrate that maternal factors during fetal development imprint both B-cell and T-cell populations, shaping autoantibody repertoires and modulating diabetes penetrance independently of genetic background.

## Novel delivery technologies

Conventional subcutaneous insulin injection faces limitations including patient discomfort, inconsistent absorption, and slow pharmacokinetics. [[bubble-microneedles|Separable bubble microneedles (BMNs)]] address these constraints through tip-confined payload loading and controlled tip-baseplate separation engineered via finite element analysis. Insulin-loaded BMNs enable injection-free sublingual delivery with greater than 80% payload release within 10 minutes, generating rapid and sustained glucose lowering in diabetic rats comparable to subcutaneous injection.⁶ The measured transverse separation forces (4.17 N) and axial failure loads (0.28 N) exceed sublingual mucosa penetration thresholds while preserving structural integrity during insertion. This transmucosal route bypasses hepatic first-pass metabolism and achieves rapid systemic availability, positioning microneedle platforms as viable alternatives to conventional injection for acute glycemic control.

## Engineered insulin analogs

Rational protein engineering has produced insulin variants with enhanced receptor binding profiles and expanded biological activity. [[1ins|[GluB10, D-HisB24, GlyB31, TyrB32]-insulin (1Ins)]] represents a dual insulin receptor and [[igf-1r|IGF-1 receptor (IGF-1R)]] agonist engineered through minimal substitutions that preserve IR binding while achieving approximately 1000-fold enhanced IGF-1R affinity—approaching native IGF-1 potency.² Cryo-electron microscopy structures reveal how these targeted mutations enable effective engagement of both receptor architectures despite divergent binding site geometries. In neuronal cells, 1Ins robustly activates both IR and IGF-1R pathways, promotes survival, and exceeds native ligands in neuroprotective assays, reflecting coordinated signaling through pathways governing glucose metabolism, growth, and neuronal maintenance. Phosphoproteomic profiling confirms dual pathway activation and identifies signaling targets specific to 1Ins. In vivo, the analog regulates glucose effectively in mice and rats while conferring neuroprotective benefits relevant to diabetic complications and neurodegenerative contexts.

## Obesity and metabolic dysfunction

Hyperinsulinemia frequently accompanies obesity and contributes to metabolic syndrome through sustained activation of anabolic pathways. Photothermal modulation of visceral adipose tissue using hypoxia-activated phototheranostic probes induces white adipose tissue browning, enhances lipolysis, and alleviates inflammation, leading to 75.7% reduction in serum insulin levels alongside 15.6% body weight loss and 65% adipose tissue mass reduction.⁷ The approach exploits the enhanced hypoxic microenvironment characteristic of expanded adipose tissue, enabling targeted photoacoustic imaging and localized photothermal conversion. Resultant metabolic improvements include reduced cholesterol (49.1%), triglycerides (30.3%), and pro-inflammatory markers (IL-6, MCP-1), with increased anti-inflammatory IL-10. This tissue-targeted strategy addresses root pathophysiology rather than systemic pharmacotherapy, offering a complementary approach to obesity-associated insulin resistance.
