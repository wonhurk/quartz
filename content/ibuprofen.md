**Ibuprofen** is a widely used non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) approved for over-the-counter use in [[pain]] and [[fever]] management, with a maximum recommended daily dose of 1,200 mg for non-prescription settings.¹ The therapeutic efficacy of ibuprofen is substantially influenced by its formulation, with innovative [[ir-sr-bi-layer-tablet|immediate-release/sustained-release (IR/SR) bi-layer formulations]] achieving superior pharmacokinetic profiles compared to conventional immediate-release tablets. Emerging computational screening reveals an unexpected therapeutic opportunity: ibuprofen may enhance anti-tumor activity in lung cancer by overcoming resistance mechanisms to third-generation EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors, pointing toward potential repurposing beyond conventional pain and fever management.

---

## Formulation and Pharmacokinetics

Ibuprofen's clinical efficacy is fundamentally determined by formulation-specific differences in drug release profiles and absorption kinetics.¹ Systematic pharmacokinetic comparisons of four ibuprofen formulations — immediate-release (IR) 400 mg tablets, IR 200 mg tablets, lysinate salt equivalent to 400 mg ibuprofen, and a novel bi-layer tablet integrating immediate- and sustained-release components — revealed substantial differences in therapeutic duration. Plasma concentration thresholds of 6.8 μg/mL and 10.1 μg/mL are used clinically to assess the onset and duration of analgesic and antipyretic action. The [[ir-sr-bi-layer-tablet|IR/SR 400 mg bi-layer tablet]] maintained plasma concentrations above these therapeutic thresholds for eight hours, extending the duration of clinical action by 2–3.5 hours compared to conventional immediate-release formulations.¹

Virtual pharmacokinetic modeling identified optimized dosing regimens that sustain therapeutic efficacy while remaining within OTC safety limits. IR 200 mg tablets administered six times daily and the [[ir-sr-bi-layer-tablet|IR/SR 400 mg bi-layer formulation]] administered three times daily both maintained therapeutic plasma concentrations throughout a 24-hour period without surpassing the maximum recommended daily dose of 1,200 mg.¹ The reduced dosing frequency and prolonged efficacy profile of the IR/SR formulation support its suitability for providing continuous pain and fever relief with improved patient adherence.

## Pain and Fever Management

Ibuprofen has been a cornerstone of [[pain]] management in over-the-counter settings for decades, owing to its rapid onset of action and favorable safety profile at approved doses.¹ The drug is equally effective for [[fever]] reduction, with the same therapeutic concentration thresholds governing both analgesic and antipyretic responses. The development of formulation-optimized regimens extends the duration of symptom relief, allowing patients to maintain therapeutic plasma concentrations with fewer dosing occasions throughout the day, thereby improving medication adherence and clinical outcomes.

---

## Emerging Anticancer Applications

Beyond its established analgesic and antipyretic roles, computational screening using the Connectivity Map (CMap) identified ibuprofen as a candidate compound with potential anti-tumor activity against lung cancer.² Patient-derived malignant effusion organoids from lung cancer patients demonstrated that ibuprofen could enhance the efficacy of osimertinib, a third-generation EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor, and overcome resistance mechanisms in organoid and cellular models.² Transcriptomic analysis of resistant tumors revealed that key signal pathways — including extracellular matrix remodeling, cell cycle dysregulation, and angiogenesis — mediate resistance to EGFR-targeted therapy. Ibuprofen's ability to suppress these resistance pathways suggests a novel approach to repositioning an established analgesic as a precision oncology agent, particularly in settings where conventional EGFR inhibition has failed, though further clinical validation is required.
