**The aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR)** is a nuclear receptor functioning as a ligand-activated transcription factor that regulates immune homeostasis and suppresses pro-inflammatory responses. Its central mechanism is the inhibition of [[nf-kb]] and [[mapk]] signaling pathways, which coordinate the expression of major inflammatory mediators. AhR is activated by endogenous tryptophan-derived metabolites, particularly [[indole-3-propionic-acid|indole-3-propionic acid (IPA)]], a gut microbiota-produced metabolite that serves as a direct ligand for the receptor.¹ Upon activation, AhR acts as a transcriptional repressor of pro-inflammatory genes in [[astrocytes]], the primary drivers of neuroinflammation, suppressing the expression of [[il-6]], [[il-1b]], inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS), [[cox-2]], and chemokines including [[ccl2]], [[cxcl2]], and [[cxcl10]]. In models of [[spinal-cord-injury]], AhR activation via IPA treatment attenuates neuroinflammation, reduces glial scarring, preserves neuronal tissue, and promotes motor function recovery, establishing AhR as a therapeutic target for secondary neurodegeneration.

---

## Mechanism

AhR exerts its anti-inflammatory effects by serving as a molecular checkpoint on two major signaling cascades: the canonical [[nf-kb]] pathway and mitogen-activated protein kinases ([[mapk]]). When activated by its endogenous ligand [[indole-3-propionic-acid|IPA]], AhR translocates to the nucleus and suppresses the transcription of pro-inflammatory genes, effectively silencing production of [[il-6]], [[il-1b]], iNOS, [[cox-2]], and chemokines.¹ This ligand-dependent inhibition is most pronounced in [[astrocytes]], which are central regulators of neuroinflammatory cascades. The AhR-IPA axis thus represents a microbiota-dependent mechanism for maintaining immune tolerance and preventing excessive inflammatory responses that would otherwise exacerbate tissue injury.

## Spinal Cord Injury and Neuroprotection

[[Spinal-cord-injury|Spinal cord injury]] triggers persistent neuroinflammation characterized by aberrant [[astrocytes|astrocyte]] activation and progressive secondary neurodegeneration. AhR activation via [[indole-3-propionic-acid|IPA]] suppresses this pathological cascade across both in vitro and in vivo models.¹ In mouse [[spinal-cord-injury]] models, IPA-mediated AhR activation produces robust neuroprotective effects: glial scar formation is significantly reduced, neuronal survival is substantially enhanced, and long-term motor function is markedly improved as measured by Basso Mouse Scale scores, inclined plane test performance, and gait coordination metrics.¹ Magnetic resonance imaging and histopathological analyses further confirm reduced lesion volume and preserved tissue integrity following AhR activation. These findings identify AhR as a critical therapeutic target for mitigating secondary injury cascades and promoting functional recovery after [[spinal-cord-injury]].

## Microbiota-Immune Axis

The discovery of AhR activation by gut microbiota-derived [[indole-3-propionic-acid|IPA]] reveals a previously unknown axis through which commensal metabolism directly regulates systemic immune homeostasis. Rather than relying solely on host-intrinsic mechanisms, the immune system has evolved to sense microbial tryptophan metabolism through xenobiotic receptors like AhR. This microbiota-host dialogue is mediated by tryptophan-derived metabolites that cross the intestinal barrier and activate AhR in both local and systemic immune compartments. The robustness of this signaling axis suggests that disruption of the gut microbiota — through dysbiosis, broad-spectrum antibiotics, or dietary deficiency in tryptophan — may impair AhR-dependent immune homeostasis and increase susceptibility to inflammatory diseases including neuroinflammatory conditions.

---

## Xenobiotic Sensing and Detoxification

AhR functions as a master xenobiotic sensor that integrates signals from both exogenous environmental compounds and endogenous metabolites. The receptor's capacity to detect tryptophan-derived ligands reflects its dual role as both a detoxification coordinator and an immune tolerance enforcer. This function allows AhR to coordinate transcriptional programs that establish immune tolerance at barrier tissues and maintain tissue-resident immune homeostasis. The structural plasticity of the AhR ligand-binding domain accommodates diverse chemical classes — from dietary compounds such as polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons to microbially-produced tryptophan metabolites — making it a hub for translating environmental and microbial signals into coherent immune responses.
