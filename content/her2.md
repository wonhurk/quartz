**Human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)**, encoded by the ERBB2 gene, is a transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinase that functions as a clinically significant biomarker driving tumorigenesis and progression across multiple cancer types. Unlike other members of the epidermal growth factor receptor family, HER2 has no known ligand and is activated through homodimerization or heterodimerization with other ERBB receptors, triggering downstream signaling cascades that promote cell proliferation, survival, and metastasis.¹ HER2 overexpression or activating mutations define distinct molecular subtypes in breast cancer, [[gastric-adenocarcinoma|gastric and gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma]], and non-small-cell lung cancer, each requiring tailored therapeutic strategies.²

---

## Gastric and gastroesophageal junction cancer

In HER2-positive gastric or gastroesophageal junction carcinoma, combination therapy with pembrolizumab plus trastuzumab and platinum-based chemotherapy received FDA approval in 2021 (accelerated) and 2025 (regular) based on the pivotal KEYNOTE-811 trial.² This randomized, multiregional study demonstrated superior overall response rates in the pembrolizumab arm (74.4% versus 51.9%, P = 0.00006) and a median overall survival of 20.0 months compared to 16.8 months with placebo plus trastuzumab and chemotherapy (hazard ratio 0.80, 95% CI 0.67–0.94; P = 0.004).² Exploratory subgroup analyses revealed that treatment benefit was primarily driven by the PD-L1 combined positive score ≥1 population, which comprised 85% of enrolled patients, whereas patients with PD-L1 CPS <1 showed no improvement with pembrolizumab (HR 1.10, 95% CI 0.72–1.68).² 

By contrast, HER2-negative [[gastric-adenocarcinoma|gastric adenocarcinoma]] is managed with chemotherapy-based regimens, with ongoing efforts to optimize [[neoadjuvant-therapy|neoadjuvant]] combinations. A multicenter real-world cohort study of 1,283 patients with locally advanced HER2-negative gastric cancer compared docetaxel, oxaliplatin, and S-1 (DOS) to the standard oxaliplatin and S-1 (SOX) regimen.³ DOS achieved a higher major pathological response rate (25.7% versus 17.8%; OR 1.59, 95% CI 1.14–2.23) and reduced progression risk (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.59–0.93) without increasing severe postoperative complications, particularly in patients aged ≥65 years and those with cT2-3 or cN0-1 disease.³

## Breast cancer

HER2-positive breast cancer patients undergoing [[neoadjuvant-therapy|neoadjuvant therapy]] benefit from emerging diagnostic advances that enable more precise risk stratification beyond traditional pathologic complete response assessment. Circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) assessment for [[minimal-residual-disease|minimal residual disease]] using ultrasensitive detection platforms provides additional or superior prognostic information, particularly for patients who do not achieve pathologic complete response but may still have favorable long-term outcomes.⁴ This molecular profiling approach complements tissue-based evaluation and may guide post-neoadjuvant treatment decisions in stage II/III HER2-positive and triple-negative breast cancer.⁴

## Non-small-cell lung cancer

For HER2-mutant non-small-cell lung cancer, [[zongertinib]] represents the first targeted treatment option approved for first-line use.⁵ This oral, irreversible tyrosine kinase inhibitor selectively inhibits HER2 while sparing wild-type epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR), thereby minimizing the dermatologic and gastrointestinal toxic effects typically associated with pan-ERBB inhibitors.⁵ The selectivity profile of [[zongertinib]] addresses a longstanding therapeutic gap for patients whose tumors harbor HER2 exon 20 insertions or other activating mutations, providing a mechanism-based alternative to non-selective chemotherapy.⁵

## Diagnostic innovation

Current immunohistochemistry-based HER2 evaluation suffers from labor intensity, interpretive variability, and frequent need for confirmatory fluorescence in situ hybridization. A proximity-driven, spatially matched [[3d-dna-aptasensor|3D DNA aptasensor (3DDA)]] addresses these limitations by integrating tetrahedral framework nucleic acids functionalized with cholesterol for membrane anchoring, overcoming electrostatic repulsion between negatively charged aptamers and the cell membrane.⁶ The aptamer is structurally integrated to enable optimal spatial presentation for membrane-bound HER2 recognition, and an on-membrane catalytic hairpin assembly-based isothermal amplification strategy achieves significant signal enhancement beyond the "one-signal-per-protein" model of conventional chromogenic immunohistochemistry.⁶ This platform effectively distinguishes HER2-positive cancer cells in living specimens and clinical tissue samples.⁶

Complementary dual-mode immunoassays have been developed using [[copper-doped-carbon-dots|copper-doped carbon dots]] and [[cu-ceo2-nanozymes|Cu-CeO2 nanozymes]] as bifunctional signal probes, generating strong electrochemical and colorimetric signals to reduce false-positive results.⁷ Y-shaped zwitterionic biopeptides resist nonspecific adsorption of interfering substances, while [[cof-au-nanoparticles|COF-Au nanoparticles]] serve as sensing substrates with high electrochemically active surface area, improving detection sensitivity and reliability for early clinical diagnosis and prognosis evaluation.⁷

## Novel therapeutics

Antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) have transformed HER2-targeted therapy, yet efficacy in solid tumors remains limited by poor penetration due to large molecular size (~150 kDa) and the binding-site barrier created by strong bivalent IgG-antigen interactions. The Antibody Fragment-Cleavable PEGylated Drug Conjugate ([[afcdc|AFCDC]]) platform resolves this trade-off through site-specific dual conjugation: genetically encoded bifunctional noncanonical amino acids attach cytotoxic payloads, while MMP2-cleavable PEG chains extend systemic half-life and are cleaved by tumor proteases to yield smaller fragment-payload conjugates with improved tissue penetration.⁸ In HER2-positive NCI-N87 xenografts, [[afcdc|AFCDC]] demonstrated enhanced intratumoral penetration, pharmacokinetics, and activity compared to T-DXd under the evaluated regimen.⁸

Targeted protein degradation represents an orthogonal approach to HER2 inhibition. The modular [[uptab|UPTAB (Ultrahigh-affinity Protein pairs fused to Targeting Binders)]] platform leverages orthogonal ultrahigh-affinity Im/CL protein pairs to assemble complexes between lysosomal trafficking receptor-binding modules and protein of interest-binding modules.⁹ Type-III [[uptab|UPTAB]] configurations enable simultaneous degradation of EGFR, c-MET, and HER2 in cancer models, while degrader-drug conjugates (DDCs) retain degradation capacity alongside substantially enhanced anti-proliferative activity through site-specific conjugation of cytotoxic payloads such as MMAE.⁹ This platform addresses the historical ineffectiveness of traditional targeted protein degradation strategies against membrane proteins, which constitute over 60% of drug targets.⁹
