**Prostate cancer (PCa)** is the second most common malignancy in men worldwide, characterized by diverse molecular subtypes and a propensity to develop treatment resistance.¹ While [[radical-prostatectomy]] effectively controls localized disease, it frequently causes urinary incontinence and erectile dysfunction, driving interest in tissue-sparing alternatives.¹ In advanced castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC), loss of [[decorin]] suppresses [[acsl4]]-dependent lipid remodeling and ferroptosis, conferring resistance to [[enzalutamide]] and other androgen receptor inhibitors.² Therapeutic strategies now span focal ablation techniques such as [[ire|irreversible electroporation]], epigenetic modulation via selective HDAC inhibitors, and immunometabolic interventions targeting dipeptidyl peptidases in aggressive neuroendocrine subtypes.

---

## Focal Therapy and Immunomodulation

[[ire|Irreversible electroporation (IRE)]] has emerged as a non-thermal focal ablation technique that preserves neurovascular structures while inducing immunogenic cell death in localized prostate cancer. Unlike radical surgery, IRE delivers microsecond-duration electrical pulses that permeabilize cell membranes without thermal damage to surrounding tissues, minimizing treatment-related morbidity.¹ The ablation process releases damage-associated molecular patterns that activate dendritic cells and prime systemic antitumor immunity, offering the potential for both local control and distant immune surveillance.¹ First-in-human single-cell RNA sequencing and T-cell receptor profiling have begun to map the temporal dynamics of immune remodeling following IRE, revealing clonal expansion of tumor-reactive T cells and shifts in the myeloid compartment that may predict durable responses.¹

## Resistance Mechanisms in Castration-Resistant Disease

Enzalutamide resistance in CRPC is mechanistically linked to diminished expression of [[decorin]], a proteoglycan that normally promotes [[acsl4]]-dependent lipid remodeling and ferroptosis.² In enzalutamide-resistant LNCaP cells, decorin downregulation correlates with ferroptosis evasion and sustained proliferation despite androgen receptor blockade.² Decorin activates calcium-dependent protein kinase C beta II (PKCβII), which phosphorylates [[acsl4|ACSL4]] to drive the incorporation of polyunsaturated fatty acids into membrane phospholipids, rendering cells vulnerable to lipid peroxidation.² Restoring decorin expression re-sensitizes resistant cells to enzalutamide, inhibits tumor growth, and suppresses metastasis in vivo through a ferroptosis-dependent mechanism.² Conversely, enzalutamide-mediated androgen receptor nuclear translocation negatively regulates decorin transcription, establishing a feed-forward loop that sustains resistance.² Integrative analysis of clinical specimens reveals that decorin is significantly reduced in high-grade PCa and CRPC tissues, and correlates with poor survival outcomes, positioning it as both a predictive biomarker and a therapeutic target.²

## Epigenetic Targeting with HDAC Inhibitors

Histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibitors offer a complementary approach to androgen-targeted therapy by reversing transcriptional repression and inducing apoptosis across androgen receptor-dependent and independent prostate cancer models. Biphenyl-substituted uracil-based hydroxamic acids (UBHAs) represent a next-generation scaffold achieving nanomolar potency against HDAC6 with reduced activity toward class I isoforms, addressing the toxicity and limited selectivity of earlier pan-HDAC inhibitors.³ [[compound-14a|Compound 14a]] and its analog 14b reduce cancer cell viability at submicromolar concentrations while sparing noncancerous cells, demonstrating a favorable therapeutic window.³ In AR-positive prostate cancer models, 14a enhances histone H3K9 and α-tubulin acetylation, upregulates the cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor p21, and downregulates the antiapoptotic protein Bcl-2.³ The compounds induce cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis in both leukemia and prostate cancer lines, modulating apoptosis-related microRNAs and validating the uracil-based scaffold as a versatile platform for HDAC-targeted drug discovery.³

## Aggressive Neuroendocrine Subtypes

[[scnc|Small cell neuroendocrine prostate cancer (SCNC)]] is a lethal subset of prostate cancer characterized by neuroendocrine differentiation, rapid progression, and limited response to conventional androgen deprivation therapy.⁴ Talabostat, an oral small-molecule inhibitor of dipeptidyl peptidases DPP4, DPP8, and DPP9, triggers inflammasome activation and induces the proinflammatory cytokines interleukin-18 (IL-18) and IL-1β, bridging innate and adaptive immunity.⁴ By alerting and priming immune effector cells, talabostat creates a permissive microenvironment for checkpoint blockade, prompting a phase 2 clinical trial combining talabostat with pembrolizumab in patients with SCNC.⁴ This immunometabolic strategy exploits the inflammasome as a central hub linking metabolic stress to immune activation, offering hope for a disease with few effective treatment options.⁴

## Diagnostic Advances

[[prostate-specific-antigen|Prostate-specific antigen (PSA)]] testing remains the cornerstone of prostate cancer screening, yet its limited specificity produces frequent false positives and unnecessary biopsies. [[prostate-specific-membrane-antigen|Prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)]], a cell-surface glycoprotein highly overexpressed in prostate cancer, can be detected in urine and provides superior diagnostic specificity.⁵ A multiplexed [[electrochemical-aptasensor]] platform based on three-working-electrode screen-printed technology and modified with three-dimensional reduced graphene oxide (3D-rGO) and gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) achieves ultrasensitive detection of urinary PSMA with a detection limit of 5 pg/mL and a linear range spanning 15–2000 pg/mL.⁵ The nanostructured interface enhances electron transfer, stabilizes the DNA aptamer probe, and amplifies electrochemical signals, while the three-electrode configuration enables simultaneous triplicate measurements from a single sample, improving analytical precision.⁵ This non-invasive aptasensor approach addresses the shortcomings of serum PSA testing and holds promise for early detection and risk stratification in prostate cancer.⁵
