**Lupus nephritis (LN)** is a severe kidney manifestation of [[systemic-lupus-erythematosus|systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)]], particularly in childhood, characterized by immune dysregulation and rapid progression to renal impairment or end-stage renal disease (ESRD). At the core of LN pathogenesis lies dysregulation of the [[baff|B-cell activating factor (BAFF)]] pathway, which normally maintains immune homeostasis by controlling B-cell survival and innate immune cell function.¹ A key therapeutic insight has emerged: targeted BAFF-pathway inhibition in pediatric LN rebalances the immune system by expanding regulatory B cells (Bregs) while simultaneously suppressing type 1 innate lymphoid cells (ILC1s), suggesting an immunomodulatory strategy distinct from conventional immunosuppression.¹

---

## Mechanism

BAFF is a critical controller of B-cell survival and activation, and dysregulation of the BAFF pathway contributes directly to LN pathogenesis by tilting the balance between pro-inflammatory and regulatory immune populations.¹ In healthy individuals, BAFF signaling maintains a crucial equilibrium: adequate levels support B-cell maturation and survival, while suppressing excessive innate lymphoid activation. In lupus nephritis, this balance breaks down, with BAFF dysregulation driving unchecked B-cell proliferation and heightened ILC1-mediated inflammation. The disease is further characterized by immune complex deposition in the glomerular basement membrane, where anti-double-stranded DNA antibodies and anti-histone antibodies accumulate, triggering complement activation and progressive glomerular injury.

Therapeutic targeting of the BAFF pathway reverses this pathological state. BAFF-pathway inhibition not only expands regulatory B cells—populations that actively suppress inflammation through production of anti-inflammatory cytokines—but also suppresses type 1 innate lymphoid cells, cells whose interferon-γ production amplifies renal inflammation.¹ This dual mechanism represents an advancement in immunomodulatory therapy, offering a more targeted approach than broad immunosuppression.

---

## Clinical manifestations and progression

Lupus nephritis ranks among the most severe manifestations of childhood systemic lupus erythematosus (cSLE), with a propensity for rapid progression to renal impairment or ESRD if left untreated.² Early renal involvement typically presents as asymptomatic hematuria and proteinuria, often detected on routine screening. However, the disease frequently escalates, leading to declining glomerular filtration rate, hypertension, and progressive kidney dysfunction. Without early intervention, many pediatric patients face chronic kidney disease or transplantation. The heterogeneity of disease presentation—ranging from mild urinary abnormalities to rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis—underscores the critical need for sensitive, early diagnostic methods and precise disease activity assessment to guide therapeutic decisions.

---

## Diagnostic challenges and conventional approaches

Conventional diagnosis of lupus nephritis has long relied on a combination of serological markers—such as anti-double-stranded DNA antibodies and complement levels—and urinary biomarkers including proteinuria and cellular casts. However, these traditional markers exhibit limited sensitivity and specificity, particularly in distinguishing LN from other forms of SLE and in accurately assessing disease activity over time.² The diagnostic gold standard remains [[renal-biopsy|renal biopsy]], which provides definitive histopathological classification of lupus nephritis according to the World Health Organization classification system. Yet renal biopsy is invasive, carries procedural risks, and is unsuitable for serial monitoring to track disease progression or treatment response, creating a significant clinical gap between diagnostic precision and feasibility for longitudinal patient management.

---

## Surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy: non-invasive diagnosis and activity assessment

[[sers|Surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy (SERS)]] has emerged as a transformative non-invasive analytical platform for lupus nephritis diagnosis and disease monitoring. SERS works by detecting the unique metabolic fingerprints—characteristic patterns of small molecules, proteins, and lipids—present in serum and urine of LN patients. A high-throughput SERS microspot array platform established for dual-modal analysis simultaneously profiled both serum and urine metabolic signatures in healthy controls, SLE patients without kidney involvement, and LN patients, revealing distinct molecular patterns exclusive to lupus nephritis.²

The dual-modal fusion approach—combining serum and urine SERS fingerprints—demonstrated significantly superior diagnostic performance compared to single-modal analysis alone, achieving notably enhanced sensitivity, specificity, and overall accuracy for both LN diagnosis and disease activity assessment.² Beyond diagnostic utility, dual-modal SERS profiling identified key biomolecular alterations associated with immune activation, inflammatory responses, and renal injury at the molecular level, providing mechanistic insights into the pathophysiology of childhood SLE and LN.² This non-invasive method is particularly suited for serial monitoring, enabling longitudinal assessment of disease activity and treatment response without the procedural burden and risk inherent to repeated biopsies.

---

## Therapeutic strategies and immunomodulation

BAFF-pathway inhibition has emerged as a rational immunomodulatory approach specifically tailored to address the underlying immune dysregulation in pediatric lupus nephritis. By targeting BAFF signaling, therapies can reprogram the immune balance: expanding protective regulatory B cells while constraining type 1 innate lymphoid cell populations and their pro-inflammatory mediators.¹ This mechanism contrasts with conventional broad-spectrum immunosuppressants and represents a disease-mechanism-driven approach with potential for improved efficacy and reduced systemic toxicity. Clinical translation of BAFF-pathway-targeted therapies into pediatric LN populations is underway, with the dual immune modulation pathway offering promise for halting progression and preserving renal function.
