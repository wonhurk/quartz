**Copper** is a critical micronutrient that occupies a paradoxical position in cancer biology and therapeutics, functioning both as an essential metabolic cofactor for tumor growth and as the trigger for a newly characterized form of regulated cell death termed cuproptosis. Cancer cells exhibit copper addiction, requiring bioavailable copper ions for proliferation, angiogenesis, and survival—a dependency exploitable through [[copper-chelators]] that deplete intracellular copper pools.¹ Conversely, targeted delivery of copper to tumor cells induces cuproptosis, a mitochondrial damage pathway mechanistically distinct from [[apoptosis]] and [[ferroptosis]], which overcomes chemotherapy resistance and ablates cancer stemness.² This dual nature positions copper as both a therapeutic target and a therapeutic agent across oncology, immunotherapy, and fibrotic diseases.

---

## Mechanism

Cancer cells display aberrant copper metabolism, accumulating the metal to support oncogenic signaling, mitochondrial respiration, and neovascularization. This copper addiction creates a therapeutic vulnerability: depletion of bioavailable copper through chelation disrupts redox homeostasis, generating reactive oxygen species (ROS) and triggering mitochondrial dysfunction.¹ Cuproptosis, by contrast, is initiated when excess intracellular copper directly binds lipoylated mitochondrial proteins, causing protein aggregation, mitochondrial membrane rupture, and necroptotic cell death.² Schiff base copper chelators such as [[compound-2d]]—engineered with triphenylphosphonium (TPP) moieties for mitochondrial targeting—induce this pathway by disrupting copper buffering systems, generating ROS-mediated damage, and precipitating necroptosis independently of traditional death receptor pathways.¹ The lethality of cuproptosis is amplified when Wnt/β-catenin signaling, which confers intrinsic tolerance, is suppressed through endoplasmic reticulum stress and calcium overload.²

## Cancer applications

Copper-based therapeutics have demonstrated efficacy across multiple malignancies, particularly those resistant to conventional chemotherapy. In [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)]], twinborn metallic polymer nanoparticles co-delivering copper and cisplatin synergize cuproptosis with DNA-damaging chemotherapy, achieving superior tumor suppression in orthotopic mouse models compared to monotherapies.² The system acts as a type II immunogenic cell death inducer, reversing cuproptosis tolerance by inhibiting the Wnt/β-catenin pathway through p53-mediated upregulation of RARRES3.² 

In [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer (CRC)]], a purpurin-copper coordinated nanoplatform coated with d-α-tocopheryl polyethylene glycol succinate (TPGS) exploits tumor cell macropinocytosis to enhance intracellular accumulation.⁵ The platform integrates cuproptosis with photodynamic therapy and metabolic intervention: purpurin inhibits glutaminase to reprogram glutamine metabolism, while copper ions synergize with chlorin e6-generated ROS to ablate [[cancer-stemness|cancer stemness]] and convert immunologically cold tumors to T-cell-inflamed phenotypes.⁵ This tripartite strategy established durable immunological memory, achieving 100% survival in tumor-rechallenged mice at 90 days.⁵

## Immunotherapy enhancement

Cuproptosis induces immunogenic cell death, releasing damage-associated molecular patterns that activate dendritic cell maturation and prime [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cell]] responses.² Copper-driven metabolic reprogramming further enhances antitumor immunity: glutaminase inhibition by purpurin-copper conjugates improves dendritic cell function, initiating robust T-cell priming and infiltration.⁵ The conversion of cold tumors to hot phenotypes through cuproptosis-mediated stemness ablation amplifies the efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade, establishing a metabolic-immunological co-regulation paradigm with high translational potential.⁵ Suppression of the immunosuppressive Wnt/β-catenin pathway through ER stress-triggered calcium overload further augments this effect, positioning copper-based nanotherapeutics as potent chemo-immunotherapy platforms.²

## Fibrotic diseases

Beyond oncology, cuproptosis regulates fibroblast heterogeneity in fibrotic pathologies. Single-cell RNA sequencing of endometrial tissues revealed that elevated cuproptosis-related gene activity defines a distinct fibroblast subpopulation with profibrotic transcriptional features.⁴ The copper-dependent fibrogenesis modulator [[aebp1|AEBP1]] emerged as a hub gene linking cuproptosis to β-catenin pathway activation and extracellular matrix remodeling.⁴ Treatment with [[tetrathiomolybdate]] (TTM), a copper chelator, attenuated fibrosis progression in a mouse model of [[endometriosis]], while in vitro copper-elesclomol treatment increased AEBP1 expression and fibrotic marker upregulation—effects reversed by FDX1 or AEBP1 knockdown.⁴ This positions copper chelation as a therapeutic strategy for targeting copper-dependent fibrotic processes in reproductive and potentially other organ systems.

## Manufacturing considerations

Trace catalytic copper poses a significant challenge in biopharmaceutical manufacturing. During routine automated buffer exchange into phosphate-buffered saline at physiological pH, copper leached from disposable chromatography columns coordinates to antibody hinge regions, driving highly localized Fenton-like oxidative cleavage and protein fragmentation.³ Complex antibody architectures such as 2+1 CrossMabs exhibit significantly higher susceptibility than standard IgGs, likely due to steric crowding enhancing metal-binding site accessibility.³ Mitigation requires [[ethylenediaminetetraacetic-acid|EDTA]]-based column conditioning protocols to eliminate the leachable catalyst, highlighting a critical but often-overlooked source of chemically induced protein instability in developability assessments.³

---

## Therapeutic agents

[[compound-2d|Compound 2d]], a rationally designed Schiff base copper chelator bearing triphenylphosphonium, demonstrates potent nontoxic tumor suppression in vivo through mitochondrial-targeted copper depletion and necroptosis induction.¹ Purpurin-copper coordinated nanoparticles delivered via TPGS-mediated macropinocytosis combine cuproptosis with photodynamic therapy, metabolic reprogramming, and stemness ablation.⁵ Twinborn metallic polymers incorporating both copper and platinum (IV) provide on-demand release of dual metal ions for synergistic cuproptosis-chemotherapy while suppressing Wnt tolerance pathways.² [[tetrathiomolybdate]], a clinical-stage copper chelator, targets both oncogenic copper addiction and copper-dependent fibrotic progression.⁴
