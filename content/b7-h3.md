**B7-H3** is an immune checkpoint protein overexpressed on the cell surface of many solid tumors, functioning as both a prognostic biomarker and a promising target for CAR T cell immunotherapy. Elevated B7-H3 expression correlates with worse overall survival and increased molecular tumor burden across multiple malignancies, including pancreatic cancer, neuroblastoma, and glioblastoma.¹ The protein has become the focus of clinical development efforts centered on chimeric antigen receptor T cell therapy, with both murine-derived monoclonal antibodies and newly engineered fully human alternatives advancing through clinical trials and preclinical studies.

---

## Tumor expression and prognostic significance

B7-H3 is significantly overexpressed on the cell surface of diverse solid tumors, establishing it as a broadly applicable immunotherapy target.¹ In metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC), plasma-derived extracellular vesicles bearing B7-H3 have emerged as a valuable liquid biopsy biomarker. High levels of B7-H3-containing extracellular vesicles associate with worse overall survival and positively correlate with markers of molecular tumor burden, including prostate-specific antigen and serum alkaline phosphatase.² B7-H3 was identified alongside three other cell-surface targets—[[psma|PSMA]], Trop-2, and STEAP1—in a comprehensive proteomics analysis of plasma extracellular vesicles from patients receiving 177Lu-PSMA-617 therapy, establishing it as part of a multiplex biomarker panel for treatment response and patient stratification.

---

## CAR T cell immunotherapy

B7-H3-targeting CAR T cell therapy has advanced rapidly through clinical development, building on two murine-derived monoclonal antibody scaffolds. The [[376-96]] and [[mga271]] antibodies have been incorporated into CAR T cells that are now in phase 1/2 clinical trials for B7-H3-expressing solid tumors.¹ However, the reliance on non-human monoclonal antibody sequences poses a critical therapeutic challenge: immune recognition of the foreign murine variable domains can provoke host immune responses, leading to rejection of the therapeutic CAR T cells themselves and limiting therapeutic durability and efficacy.¹

---

## Fully human B7-H3 CAR T cell engineering

To overcome the immunogenicity risks inherent in murine-derived CAR T therapeutics, fully human B7-H3-specific single-chain variable fragments (scFvs) have been engineered using in vitro phage display technology.¹ The lead candidate from this engineering effort, designated [[y111|Y111]], demonstrates markedly superior antitumor activity compared with the established murine-based CAR T cells. In pancreatic cancer, neuroblastoma, and glioblastoma xenograft models, Y111 CAR T cells achieve complete tumor responses and rejection with significant survival benefits.¹ The superior efficacy of the fully human Y111 platform positions it as a promising advance in B7-H3-directed CAR T therapy for solid tumors.

---

## Expression in lymphoid malignancies

B7-H3 expression patterns correlate with distinct modes of extranodal invasion in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, reflecting the underlying organotropism and developmental trajectories of malignant B cell migration.³ In mesoderm-derived extranodal sites—representing a particularly aggressive mode of disease spread—B7-H3 is significantly upregulated on malignant B cells. Patients with mesoderm-originating extranodal invasion exhibit inferior progression-free and overall survival compared with those whose lymphoma invades ectoderm- or endoderm-derived tissues, highlighting the clinical relevance of B7-H3 expression patterns in lymphoid malignancies.³
