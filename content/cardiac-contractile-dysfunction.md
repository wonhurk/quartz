**Cardiac contractile dysfunction** is an impairment of myocardial contractility arising from distinct pathophysiological mechanisms, characterized by reduced myofilament calcium sensitivity and partial sarcomere disassembly.¹ Two major etiological pathways drive this condition: multi-targeted [[vandetanib\|tyrosine kinase inhibitors]] that directly degrade sarcomeric proteins through selective proteolysis, and [[mash\|metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH)]] that disrupts cardiac metabolism through systemic lipid and amino acid dysregulation.² The defect represents true excitation-contraction uncoupling, as contractile failure occurs despite preserved intracellular calcium dynamics and intact calcium-handling machinery, indicating the fundamental lesion resides in the mechanical competence of the myofilament itself.

---

## Mechanism

Cardiac contractile dysfunction results from disruption of the sarcomeric protein complex that underpins myocardial force generation. In the context of drug-induced injury, the cardiac troponin system, which regulates actin-myosin interaction and force transmission, undergoes dramatic degradation. Multi-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as [[vandetanib]] precipitate selective proteolysis of core sarcomeric proteins: [[cardiac-troponin-t\|cTnT]] protein levels decline by 42.3% and [[myomesin\|MYOM1]] by 32.7%, accompanied by partial disassembly of the sarcomeric lattice.¹ This geometric disruption compromises the organized arrangement necessary for efficient force generation.

A critical mechanistic insight distinguishes this form of contractile dysfunction: it is not driven by dysregulation of intracellular calcium dynamics. Calcium imaging demonstrates preserved sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium release and intact expression of key calcium-handling proteins, ruling out a calcium-dependent mechanism. Instead, the fundamental defect lies in myofilament calcium sensitivity—the intrinsic ability of the contractile apparatus to generate force in response to a given intracellular calcium concentration. This calcium-independent contractile failure represents true excitation-contraction uncoupling, distinguishing drug-induced contractile dysfunction from traditional calcium-handling cardiomyopathies.

---

## TKI-induced contractile dysfunction

[[vandetanib]] is a multi-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor widely used in the long-term treatment of medullary thyroid carcinoma. However, chronic vandetanib exposure precipitates severe myocardial contractile dysfunction through selective degradation of the sarcomeric proteome—the structural foundation of the myocardium.¹ Rather than triggering broad-based cardiomyocyte death, vandetanib preferentially targets core contractile proteins for proteolytic destruction, rendering the myocardium mechanically incompetent even when calcium and bioenergetic support remain adequate.

This mechanism explains the disconnect between preserved calcium dynamics and profound contractile failure: without intact [[cardiac-troponin-t\|cTnT]] and [[myomesin\|MYOM1]], the myofilament cannot translate calcium-induced conformational changes into coordinated actin-myosin cross-bridge cycling. The partial disassembly of the sarcomere exacerbates this defect, as the geometric organization necessary for force transmission becomes corrupted. This pathophysiological sequence indicates that therapeutic intervention must directly address myofilament degradation rather than attempting to compensate for lost contractile capacity.

## Metabolic dysfunction and cardiac disease

A parallel pathophysiological mechanism emerges in the context of [[mash\|metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH)]]. Western diet-induced MASH triggers a cascade of metabolic disturbances that extend far beyond the liver to compromise cardiac function.² The initial insult involves altered lipid metabolism and dysregulated mitochondrial function within hepatocytes, but the downstream consequence—elevated circulating and tissue ceramides and deoxy-ceramides—creates a systemic environment hostile to myocardial contractility.

The accumulation of elevated sphingolipids in cardiac tissue directly impairs contractile function and increases cardiovascular disease risk. This occurs through disruption of amino acid metabolism, particularly branched-chain amino acid homeostasis and nutrient transporter networks that normally sustain myocardial protein synthesis and oxidative metabolism. Chronic [[mash\|MASH]] thus establishes a cardio-metabolic crosstalk mechanism: hepatic lipid dysfunction and hepatocyte-derived dysregulation of ceramide synthesis drive systemic metabolic poisoning that culminates in myocardial contractility loss through overlapping pathways of metabolic reprogramming rather than direct protein degradation.

## Therapeutic approaches

[[calpain\|Calpain]] inhibition has emerged as a potential therapeutic strategy to preserve myofilament integrity and prevent [[vandetanib]]-induced contractile dysfunction.¹ By blocking the cysteine protease [[calpain]], which mediates proteolysis of sarcomeric proteins, calpain inhibitors may arrest or reverse the cascade of myofilament degradation initiated by drug exposure. This approach directly targets the root mechanism—selective protein degradation—rather than attempting to compensate for structural myofilament loss.

For metabolic-driven contractile dysfunction, therapeutic strategies must address the upstream cardio-metabolic dysfunction. Understanding the precise role of elevated sphingolipids and disrupted amino acid metabolism in driving myocardial contractility loss may reveal interventions that normalize hepatic lipid metabolism, reduce circulating ceramide burden, or directly restore cardiac amino acid handling and mitochondrial function. Such approaches would target systemic metabolic pathology underlying [[mash\|MASH]]-associated cardiac disease, potentially preventing progression before sarcomeric protein loss becomes irreversible.
