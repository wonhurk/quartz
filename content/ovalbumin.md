**Ovalbumin** is a model antigen protein widely employed in cancer immunotherapy research to generate robust tumor-specific immune responses. Beyond its classical use in basic immunological studies, ovalbumin serves as a functional antigenic component in cutting-edge immunotherapy platforms, including [[lnp|lipid nanoparticle]]-delivered mRNA vaccines and [[nanomedicine|nanoparticle-based]] immunotherapies. When co-delivered with immune-remodeling mRNAs encoding transcription factors such as interferon regulatory factor 8 (IRF8) or NF-κB-inducing kinase (NIK), ovalbumin mRNA elicits approximately tenfold amplification of antigen-specific [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cell]] responses, sustaining long-term immunological memory and preventing tumor growth in vaccinated mice.¹ In nanoparticle-based approaches, ovalbumin-conjugated lanthanide nanoparticles enhance the antigen cross-presentation capability of [[tam|tumor-associated macrophages]] in glioblastoma, overcoming immunosuppression and activating tumor-infiltrating CD8+ T cells through photo-controllable mechanisms.²

---

## Mechanism

Ovalbumin functions as a well-characterized model antigen whose immunogenicity depends on cellular uptake and processing into peptide epitopes presented on major histocompatibility complex (MHC) molecules. When formulated in [[lnp|lipid nanoparticles]] alongside immune-remodeling mRNAs (IR-mRNAs) encoding IRF8 or NIK, ovalbumin mRNA is translocated into [[apcs|antigen-presenting cells]], where it is translated and processed for cross-presentation to [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cells]].¹ The co-delivered IR-mRNAs activate conventional dendritic cells and other innate immune cells, amplifying the antigen-specific response approximately tenfold and establishing durable immunological memory that protects against tumor rechallenge.

In nanoparticle-based delivery, ovalbumin serves as a coupled antigenic cargo on lanthanide nanoparticles engineered with [[tam|tumor-associated macrophage]]-targeting peptides. These nanoparticles specifically localize to M2-like immunosuppressive macrophages within tumors, where a photo-controllable cysteine protease inhibitor is released upon near-infrared laser irradiation. This controlled release enhances antigen cross-presentation by modulating protease activity, enabling more efficient peptide presentation to tumor-infiltrating CD8+ T cells and overcoming the antigen-presentation deficiency that typically characterizes M2-like macrophages.²

## mRNA vaccine applications

[[lnp|Lipid nanoparticle]]-mediated delivery of ovalbumin mRNA demonstrates potent therapeutic efficacy when combined with immune-remodeling mRNA adjuvants. In syngeneic mouse tumor models, co-administration of ovalbumin mRNA with IR-mRNAs encoding IRF8 or NIK elicited approximately tenfold increases in antigen-specific [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cell]] responses compared to either agent alone, with sustained long-term memory phenotypes persisting months after vaccination.¹ This immunological memory proved functionally relevant, as vaccinated mice effectively rejected tumor rechallenge and exhibited significantly delayed tumor growth kinetics. Both intratumoral and intravenous [[lnp|lipid nanoparticle]] administration achieved durable antitumor responses, indicating that systemic delivery of ovalbumin mRNA-IR-mRNA combinations can establish protective adaptive immunity across multiple anatomical compartments and tumor types. Additionally, ovalbumin-based mRNA vaccines demonstrated synergy with humoral immune pathways, as evidenced by studies combining ovalbumin mRNA with hemagglutinin mRNA, which enhanced cellular responses approximately 15-fold and antibody responses approximately fivefold.¹

## Glioblastoma immunotherapy

Ovalbumin-conjugated lanthanide nanoparticles (designated NPs-RuIn-M) represent a precision immunotherapy strategy specifically designed to overcome the profound immunosuppression characteristic of glioblastoma. The formulation couples ovalbumin to nanoparticles bearing [[tam|tumor-associated macrophage]]-targeting peptides and a photo-controllable cysteine protease inhibitor.² Delivery into orthotopic glioblastoma is achieved through focused ultrasound-mediated blood-brain barrier opening, enabling precise nanoparticle accumulation within the tumor microenvironment. Near-infrared-IIb imaging (1500–1700 nm wavelength) localizes the NPs-RuIn-M to M2-like macrophages, while subsequent 808 nm laser irradiation triggers release of the cysteine protease inhibitor, suppressing protease activity and significantly enhancing the capacity of these immunosuppressive cells to cross-present the ovalbumin antigen.² By converting immunosuppressive M2-like macrophages into competent antigen-presenting cells, this approach activates robust ovalbumin-specific [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cell]] responses directly within the glioblastoma microenvironment, effectively overcoming a major barrier to effective immunotherapy in this lethal malignancy.
