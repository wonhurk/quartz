**Nitric oxide (NO)** is a small gaseous signaling molecule whose therapeutic utility depends critically on context-specific bioavailability. In diabetic wounds, controlled exogenous NO delivery—coupled with oxygen generation—activates tissue repair pathways, promoting angiogenesis, macrophage reprogramming, and bacterial clearance.¹ Conversely, in acute ischemic stroke, excess NO production by neuronal nitric oxide synthase ([[nnos]]) drives excitotoxic neuronal death; selective blockade of the [[psd95|PSD95]]/[[nnos|nNOS]] protein-protein interaction reduces pathological NO generation and confers neuroprotection.² These opposing therapeutic strategies—NO delivery versus NO inhibition—exemplify how molecular mechanism-driven drug design can exploit the same signaling axis to address distinct disease pathologies.

---

## Mechanism

Nitric oxide mediates its biological effects through diverse protein targets and enzymatic pathways depending on cellular context. In the vasculature and immune compartment, NO activates soluble guanylate cyclase, driving cGMP-dependent signaling that promotes vasodilation, endothelial proliferation, and immune cell differentiation. During wound healing, exogenous NO delivery enhances neuro-vascular coupling by stimulating endothelial cell proliferation and angiogenic gene expression, whilst simultaneously polarizing tissue-resident macrophages toward the pro-regenerative M2 phenotype through cGMP-independent pathways.¹ In contrast, during acute neuronal injury, excessive NO synthesis by [[nnos|neuronal NOS]] generates high local NO concentrations that overactivate the [[psd95|PSD95]]-bound NMDA receptor, leading to catastrophic calcium influx and mitochondrial dysfunction. The [[psd95|PSD95]]/[[nnos|nNOS]] protein-protein interaction is essential for anchoring nNOS to the postsynaptic density; disrupting this scaffolding complex directly impairs NO-mediated excitotoxicity without globally suppressing NOS activity.²

## Diabetic wound healing

Diabetic wounds exist in a pathologically hostile microenvironment characterized by impaired angiogenesis, bacterial colonization, neuropathy, and dysregulated immune function that collectively arrests the normal healing cascade. Engineered cascade nanozyme hydrogel dressings have emerged as a sophisticated strategy to address these multifactorial defects through spatially controlled NO and oxygen release. The hydrogel matrix—composed of dopamine-modified gelatin and quaternary ammonium-lipoic acid-modified chitosan—provides excellent tissue adhesion and inherent antimicrobial capability, whilst incorporating Cu/Mg-tannic acid nanozymes loaded with the NO donor [[bnn6|BNN6]] as the functional core.¹ Upon near-infrared irradiation, this composite system undergoes bifurcated activation: the nanozymes trigger sustained [[bnn6|BNN6]]-derived NO release whilst simultaneously catalyzing decomposition of endogenous hydrogen peroxide to generate oxygen. In diabetic wound models, the simultaneous delivery of NO and O₂ demonstrated robust antibacterial activity, shifted macrophage populations toward the healing-associated M2 phenotype, and enhanced both endothelial sprouting and neurite outgrowth.¹ The synergistic neuro-vascular coupling achieved through this dual-release strategy significantly accelerated the healing of infected diabetic wounds, eradicating bacterial bioburden whilst modulating the local immune microenvironment to promote angiogenic and neurogenic regeneration.

## Ischemic stroke and neuronal excitotoxicity

The pathological role of NO in acute ischemic stroke stands in stark contrast to its beneficial effects in chronic wound repair. Ischemia triggers massive upregulation of [[nnos|neuronal NOS]], flooding the synaptic cleft with NO that hyperactivates NMDA receptors anchored to the postsynaptic density via the [[psd95|PSD95]] scaffolding complex. This NO-driven excitotoxicity precipitates uncontrolled calcium entry, mitochondrial depolarization, and neuronal apoptosis within minutes, expanding the infarct lesion and determining clinical outcome. Selective disruption of the [[psd95|PSD95]]/[[nnos|nNOS]] protein-protein interaction therefore represents a mechanistically precise neuroprotective strategy that decouples pathological synaptic NO signaling from the broader physiological roles of NOS in vascular regulation and inflammation. Such an approach preserves the protective effects of endothelial NOS whilst eliminating the excitotoxic contribution of neuronal NOS.²

## Novel therapeutic agents

The small-molecule inhibitor 4-dimethylaminophenol represents an ultralow molecular weight compound (137 Da) that directly targets the guanylate kinase-like domain of [[psd95|PSD95]], disrupting its previously unknown interaction with [[nnos|nNOS]].² This scaffold-disrupting approach proved superior to direct NOS inhibition in preclinical stroke models, reducing NO overproduction and neuronal excitotoxicity whilst preserving vascular homeostasis. Critically, 4-dimethylaminophenol possesses established clinical safety as a cyanide antidote, along with blood-brain barrier permeability enabling central nervous system access—attributes that position it as a translatable lead for ischemic stroke therapy. The [[cascade-nanozyme-hydrogel-dressing|cascade nanozyme hydrogel dressing]] represents the complementary technological advance for wound repair, engineering multilayer functionality—tissue adhesion, antimicrobial activity, NO delivery, and oxygen generation—into a single bioresponsive platform.¹
