**Osimertinib** is a third-generation epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor (EGFR-TKI) that selectively targets EGFR-activating mutations and the T790M resistance mutation in [[lung-adenocarcinoma]] and other forms of [[NSCLC|non-small cell lung cancer]].⁵ The drug has demonstrated transformative efficacy across multiple clinical settings, including as first-line therapy in metastatic disease, as second-line treatment following progression on earlier-generation EGFR-TKIs, and as consolidation therapy in unresectable stage III disease.⁵⁶ In the phase III LAURA study, osimertinib significantly improved progression-free survival (PFS) compared to placebo in patients with unresectable stage III [[egfr-mutated-adenocarcinoma|EGFR-mutated]] NSCLC who had not progressed after definitive chemoradiotherapy, with a hazard ratio of 0.16 (95% CI: 0.10–0.24; p<0.001).⁵

---

## Clinical applications

Osimertinib is administered across a spectrum of clinical scenarios in EGFR-mutated [[lung-cancer|lung cancer]]. In the stage III unresectable setting, the LAURA trial established osimertinib as consolidation therapy following definitive chemoradiotherapy, demonstrating marked PFS benefit in patients whose disease had not progressed during or after CRT.⁵ The China cohort of LAURA confirmed consistent efficacy in this geographic population, reinforcing osimertinib's role as a standard of care post-chemoradiotherapy.⁵

Beyond the curative-intent setting, osimertinib is frequently deployed as second-line therapy in patients who have progressed on first- or second-generation EGFR-TKIs such as gefitinib or afatinib.⁶ Notably, osimertinib can be administered empirically in patients with unknown T790M mutation status following progression on earlier-generation TKIs, particularly when rebiopsy is not feasible due to tumor inaccessibility or insufficient tissue for molecular testing.⁶ Retrospective data support the viability of this empirical approach, enabling treatment continuation in patients who would otherwise face therapeutic delay.⁶

## Resistance mechanisms

Acquired resistance to osimertinib represents a major clinical obstacle, with the C797S mutation emerging as the predominant mechanism of escape.² This mutation confers resistance by altering the drug-binding pocket of the EGFR kinase domain, rendering osimertinib ineffective and leaving patients without approved targeted alternatives.²

Beyond on-target resistance, multiple off-target pathways contribute to osimertinib resistance. The circular RNA-encoded protein RIPK1-98, transcribed from circRIPK1, modulates [[cdk2|cyclin-dependent kinase 2 (CDK2)]]-dependent cell-cycle regulation, driving tumor proliferation in osimertinib-resistant cells and serving as a biomarker for cell-cycle progression.¹ [[patient-derived-organoid|Patient-derived organoid]] models of osimertinib-resistant disease have identified extracellular matrix remodeling, cell cycle dysregulation, and angiogenesis as key resistance-associated pathways.⁴

Immune evasion also plays a critical role in resistance. EGFR-TKI-resistant cell lines established through long-term osimertinib exposure exhibit distinct immunomodulatory gene expression profiles, with afatinib-resistant cells displaying the most pronounced immunosuppressive transcriptional signatures.³ These signatures include elevated [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] expression, minimal changes in MHC class I levels, downregulation of shared immune-modulated gene sets, and a cytokine profile favoring immunosuppression (lower IL-6, CXCL10, IFN-γ, CCL22; higher IL-8).³ Inhibition of IRE1α signaling partially reverses this immunosuppressive transcriptional signature, suggesting a potential therapeutic avenue to restore immune recognition.³

---

## Overcoming resistance

Fourth-generation EGFR-TKIs are in clinical development to overcome C797S-mediated resistance. JIN-A02, a mutant-selective fourth-generation inhibitor, has demonstrated activity against C797S-positive tumors and exhibits enhanced intracranial activity, addressing a key site of osimertinib failure in NSCLC.²

[[patient-derived-organoid|Patient-derived organoid]] platforms have emerged as powerful tools for dissecting resistance mechanisms and guiding tailored therapy. Organoids derived from malignant effusions of osimertinib-resistant patients recapitulate resistance phenotypes with high fidelity, enabling functional drug screening and mechanistic interrogation.⁴ Connectivity Map screening in these models identified ibuprofen as a candidate agent that enhances osimertinib anti-tumor activity and overcomes resistance, validated in both PDO and TKI-resistant cell line models.⁴ This finding offers a novel combination strategy and highlights the translational potential of organoid-based precision therapy.⁴
