**Plasminogen Activator Inhibitor-1 (PAI-1)** is a serine protease inhibitor that suppresses [[fibrinolysis]] and promotes blood clot stabilization, thereby driving [[thrombosis|thrombotic pathology]]. By inactivating [[rt-pa|tissue plasminogen activator (tPA)]] and urokinase plasminogen activator (uPA), PAI-1 prevents the breakdown of fibrin networks and stabilizes formed clots.¹ Circulating PAI-1 levels increase substantially with age and are elevated across multiple age-related disease states, including [[thrombosis]], [[cardiovascular-disease|cardiovascular disease]], [[obesity]], [[diabetes]], [[cancer]], and [[alzheimers-disease|Alzheimer disease]], establishing it as a key molecular link between aging and thrombotic risk.¹ Genetic and pharmacological reduction of PAI-1 provides significant protective effects, including decreased cardiovascular morbidity and extended lifespan in experimental models, validating it as a validated therapeutic target for age-related thrombotic disorders.¹

---

## Mechanism

PAI-1 functions as a rapid inhibitor of the fibrinolytic system by directly inactivating [[rt-pa|tissue plasminogen activator (tPA)]] and urokinase plasminogen activator (uPA).¹ Through formation of stable inhibitor-protease complexes, PAI-1 blocks the conversion of plasminogen to plasmin, the key enzyme responsible for fibrin degradation. This inhibition of fibrinolysis results in reduced clot dissolution and extended clot persistence in the circulation and at injury sites. The net effect is a shift toward a prothrombotic state in which newly formed clots remain stable and resistant to natural fibrinolytic clearance.

---

## Thrombosis and vascular disease

Elevated PAI-1 is a major driver of [[thrombosis|thrombotic disease]] and cardiovascular complications. In models of venous thromboembolism induced by inferior vena cava (IVC) ligation, PAI-1 knockdown using [[sipai-1|siRNA-lipid nanoparticles (siPAI-1)]] significantly reduces thrombus burden and improves survival in both young and aged mice.¹ The therapeutic benefit is particularly pronounced in aged animals, where elevated baseline PAI-1 creates a permissive environment for clot formation and poor outcomes. Furthermore, genetic partial PAI-1 deficiency has been shown to decrease cardiovascular morbidity in experimental models, suggesting that therapeutic reduction of PAI-1 levels could provide broad cardioprotective benefits.¹

---

## Age-related disease associations

Circulating PAI-1 concentrations increase progressively with aging and contribute to the pathophysiology of multiple age-associated disorders beyond [[thrombosis]] alone.¹ In [[obesity|diet-induced obesity]], hepatic PAI-1 messenger RNA expression rises more than ten-fold compared to healthy mice, with circulating PAI-1 levels exhibiting exponential correlation with body weight.¹ This metabolic dysregulation links PAI-1 to systemic insulin resistance, inflammation, and [[diabetes|diabetic complications]]. In [[cancer]], elevated PAI-1 within the tumor microenvironment promotes epithelial-mesenchymal transition and facilitates metastatic dissemination through suppression of fibrinolysis-dependent matrix remodeling. In [[alzheimers-disease|Alzheimer disease]], elevated PAI-1 impairs proteolytic clearance of amyloid-beta aggregates and promotes cerebrovascular dysfunction, accelerating neurodegeneration. Across these disease contexts, PAI-1 emerges as a shared molecular driver of age-related pathology, amenable to common therapeutic targeting strategies.

---

## RNA therapeutic approaches

[[sipai-1|siRNA-encapsulated lipid nanoparticles (siPAI-1)]] represent a novel RNA therapeutic strategy for sustained PAI-1 reduction. A single intravenous dose of siPAI-1 achieves approximately 90% knockdown of circulating plasma PAI-1, with therapeutic effects persisting for at least ten days post-administration and without overt systemic toxicity.¹ This extended duration of action contrasts with traditional small-molecule or antibody-based approaches and enables PAI-1 regulation even in disease contexts where conventional therapeutics are ineffective. In diet-induced obese mice, siPAI-1 achieves approximately 70% knockdown of circulating PAI-1, demonstrating efficacy across metabolic disease states.¹ In aged mice with supraphysiologic baseline PAI-1 levels, siPAI-1 normalizes circulating concentrations, effectively reversing age-associated elevation.¹ The RNA therapeutic approach thus offers a generalizable method to address PAI-1 overexpression across thrombotic, metabolic, and age-related disease phenotypes.

## Fibrin-targeted delivery systems

Recent advances in targeted drug delivery have integrated PAI-1 modulation with enhanced thrombolytic activity. [[rt-pa|Recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (rt-PA)]] encapsulated in hollow chitosan microcapsules coated with fibrin-targeting CREKA peptides (rt-PA@CSMs-CREKA) achieves superior thrombus targeting and penetration compared to free tPA.² These self-assembling microcapsules exhibit high drug loading capacity, favorable biocompatibility, and prolonged circulation time, enabling site-specific accumulation at thrombi. Notably, rt-PA@CSMs-CREKA not only enhances fibrinolytic activity but simultaneously downregulates key procoagulant factors, including factor Xa and PAI-1.² This dual mechanism — enhanced fibrinolysis coupled with suppression of fibrinolytic inhibitors — synergistically improves thrombus dissolution and reduces the systemic hypercoagulable state, establishing a paradigm for combination-based thrombolytic therapy.
