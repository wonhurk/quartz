**Histone Deacetylase 6 (HDAC6)** is a cytoplasmic class IIb histone deacetylase that regulates gene transcription and protein function through the removal of acetyl groups from lysine residues on histone and non-histone targets, with dysregulation implicated in cancer progression, DNA damage repair, and pain signaling disorders.¹ Spatially segregated from nuclear class I HDAC isoforms by its cytoplasmic localization, HDAC6 interacts with DNA repair proteins [[ddb2|DDB2]] and [[dek|DEK]] to coordinate damage response in cancer cells, while simultaneously operating in the central amygdala to regulate nociceptive signaling and the integration of pain with affective comorbidities in disorders such as [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]].²³ Selective HDAC6 inhibitors including [[wt-161|WT-161]] and [[acy-738|ACY-738]] have emerged as promising therapeutics, achieving nanomolar potency with minimal off-target effects against class I HDACs.

---

## Mechanism and substrates

HDAC6 functions as a deacetylase that catalyzes the removal of acetyl groups from lysine residues on target proteins, thereby modulating protein function through alterations in charge, protein-protein interactions, and cellular localization.¹ The enzyme achieves substrate specificity through its cytoplasmic localization, which spatially segregates it from nuclear class I HDACs and enables selective deacetylation of non-histone proteins including α-tubulin, heat shock protein 90, and other cytoplasmic regulatory molecules.¹ HDAC6's multifaceted roles in neuronal plasticity and inflammatory signaling distinguish it functionally from its nuclear counterparts, establishing the enzyme as a key regulator of cytoplasmic protein homeostasis and cell signaling cascades.

---

## Cancer applications

HDAC6 dysregulation promotes cancer development across multiple malignancies through altered transcription and protein stability. In acute myeloid leukemia cell models such as U937 cells, selective HDAC6 inhibition induces robust pro-apoptotic responses characterized by cell-cycle arrest, enhanced apoptosis, and increased acetylation of both histone H3K9 and α-tubulin, accompanied by downregulation of [[bcl2|anti-apoptotic Bcl-2]] and modulation of pro-survival microRNAs.¹ In [[prostate-cancer|prostate cancer]], selective HDAC6 inhibitors suppress proliferation of both androgen receptor-positive and androgen receptor-negative cell populations while upregulating the cyclin-dependent kinase inhibitor p21 and suppressing [[bcl2|Bcl-2]] expression, offering a therapeutic approach independent of hormone receptor status.¹

In [[melanoma]], HDAC6 coordinates DNA damage sensing and repair through its physical interaction with DNA repair proteins [[ddb2|DDB2]] and [[dek|DEK]].² Pharmacological inhibition of HDAC6 with the selective inhibitor [[wt-161|WT-161]] disrupts these protein-protein interactions and suppresses expression of genes within the [[dna-damage-repair-pathway|DNA damage repair pathway]], resulting in accumulation of irradiation-induced DNA damage and enhanced tumor regression.² By depleting DNA repair capacity, HDAC6 inhibition establishes the enzyme as a predictive biomarker for radiation response in melanoma, with WT-161 substantially enhancing radiosensitivity while reducing adverse effects of conventional radiotherapy.

---

## Pain and irritable bowel syndrome

Beyond cancer, HDAC6 plays a critical regulatory role in pain pathophysiology, particularly through its expression in the central amygdala where it modulates nociceptive signaling and integrates pain perception with emotional processing.³ The enzyme is dynamically upregulated during pro-nociceptive signaling from the parabrachial nucleus to the central amygdala, while being downregulated during anti-nociceptive signaling from the basolateral amygdala, establishing HDAC6 as a molecular switch that links acute pain perception to affective state.³ In [[irritable-bowel-syndrome|irritable bowel syndrome (IBS)]], a functional gastrointestinal disorder characterized by visceral hypersensitivity and prominent affective symptoms, HDAC6 expression in the central amygdala coordinates the pathological integration of visceral pain and negative emotional states that define the disorder's significant comorbidities.³

Intra-central amygdala infusion of the selective HDAC6 inhibitor [[acy-738|ACY-738]] alleviates both visceral pain and anxiety-like behaviors in IBS-like animal models, providing proof-of-concept for HDAC6 targeting in pain disorders.³ The therapeutic benefits of ACY-738 are accompanied by reduced phosphorylation of intracellular signaling kinases p38 MAPK and ERK1/2, alongside attenuation of synaptic and neuroimmune alterations within the central amygdala that characterize the disease state.³ These findings position HDAC6 as a therapeutic target for pain-related disorders, with selective inhibitors offering improved safety profiles compared to earlier generations of non-selective HDAC inhibitors that were limited by broad off-target effects and systemic toxicity.

---

## Selective inhibitor development

The clinical limitations of first-generation, non-selective HDAC inhibitors—including modest efficacy in solid tumors and dose-limiting systemic toxicity—have driven development of isoform-selective HDAC6 inhibitors with enhanced therapeutic windows.¹ Biphenyl-substituted uracil-based hydroxamic acids (UBHAs) represent a structurally refined class of HDAC6 inhibitors engineered to achieve nanomolar potency while maintaining minimal off-target activity against class I HDAC isoforms.¹ Systematic chemical modification, including variation of cap group and linker chemistry, identified that para-substituted phenyl moieties and four-carbon linkers substantially enhance both potency and selectivity, with lead compounds [[compound-14a]] and [[compound-14b]] reducing cancer cell viability at submicromolar concentrations while preserving noncancerous cell survival.¹

[[WT-161]] and [[acy-738|ACY-738]] exemplify clinical-stage selective HDAC6 inhibitors now validating disease-specific therapeutic applications, demonstrating efficacy in melanoma radiosensitization and pain-related disorders, respectively.²³ The increased selectivity of these agents affords substantially improved therapeutic margins by minimizing the off-target inhibition of class I HDACs that previously limited the utility of pan-HDAC inhibitors, thereby enabling more precise modulation of HDAC6-dependent pathways across oncologic and neurological disease contexts. As drug development progresses, selective HDAC6 inhibitors are positioned to expand the clinical arsenal for both cancer therapy and pain management with improved tolerability.
