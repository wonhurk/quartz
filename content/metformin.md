**Metformin** is a first-in-class antidiabetic biguanide widely used in [[type-2-diabetes-mellitus|type 2 diabetes mellitus]] management that exhibits pleiotropic therapeutic effects across multiple disease contexts through mitochondrial targeting and metabolic reprogramming. The drug exerts its primary molecular effects by targeting the [[atp5i|ATP synthase subunit e (ATP5I)]] and inhibiting mitochondrial complex I, leading to disrupted oxidative phosphorylation, altered mitochondrial cristae morphology, and compensatory metabolic shifts that underlie both its antidiabetic and anticancer properties.¹ Beyond glucose homeostasis, metformin has emerged as a versatile therapeutic agent with applications in cancer ferroptosis induction, HIV silencing, [[endometriosis]] therapy, and sepsis-induced organ injury, enabled by advanced tissue-specific delivery platforms including [[hlipo|LEPR-targeted liposomes]] and mesenchymal stem cell-loaded microsphere systems.²⁻⁴

---

## Mechanism of action

Metformin's molecular mechanism centers on its interaction with [[atp5i|ATP5I]], the subunit e of [[f1f0-atp-synthase|F₁F₀-ATP synthase]], which maintains the stability of ATP synthase dimers crucial for cristae morphology.¹ Binding studies demonstrate that ATP5I directly interacts with biguanide analogues in vitro, and genetic inactivation of ATP5I by CRISPR-Cas9 in pancreatic cancer cells recapitulates the complete phenotype of metformin treatment, including mitochondrial morphology alterations, reduction of the NAD⁺/NADH ratio, inhibition of oxidative phosphorylation, and compensatory upregulation of glycolysis.¹ Metformin disrupts F₁F₀-ATP synthase oligomerization, leading to accumulation of vestigial assembly intermediates in pancreatic and osteosarcoma cancer cells — a phenotype mirrored by ATP5I knockout.¹ Genome-wide CRISPR screening in NALM-6 lymphoma cells revealed that metformin-treated cells exhibit genetic interaction profiles similar to those of the ATP synthase inhibitor oligomycin rather than the complex I inhibitor rotenone, providing unbiased support for ATP5I as the functionally relevant primary target.¹

## Diabetes and metabolic disorders

In [[type-2-diabetes-mellitus|type 2 diabetes]], metformin alleviates hyperglycemia through AMPK activation and hepatic gluconeogenesis suppression downstream of mitochondrial complex I inhibition. Advanced oral delivery systems have been developed to overcome metformin's pharmacokinetic limitations. One such platform employs N-acetylgalactosamine-modified pillar[6]arene encapsulated in mucoadhesive interpolymer complex micropatches, achieving 66.31% oral bioavailability through reversible tight junction opening, P-glycoprotein efflux inhibition, and activation of clathrin- and caveolae-mediated endocytosis.³ In high-fat diet/streptozotocin-induced diabetic mice, this formulation outperformed conventional metformin in improving glycemic control and [[insulin]] sensitivity.³

## Cancer therapy

Metformin exhibits antiproliferative effects across multiple malignancies, including pancreatic cancer, osteosarcoma, and acute lymphoblastic leukemia, mediated by [[atp5i|ATP5I]]-dependent metabolic disruption.¹ Cancer cells with ATP5I knockout exhibit resistance to metformin's antiproliferative effects, while reintroduction of ATP5I fully restores drug sensitivity, confirming the target-dependence of therapeutic activity.¹ Beyond cytostasis, metformin has emerged as a ferroptosis enhancer. The [[ml-mlip|ML-MLip]] platform co-delivers lactate oxidase and metformin in cancer cell membrane-biomimetic liposomes, leveraging the tumor microenvironment for synergistic ferroptosis induction.⁴ Lactate oxidase catalyzes abundant intratumoral lactate — a consequence of the Warburg effect — to generate H₂O₂, while metformin simultaneously alleviates tumor hypoxia by inhibiting mitochondrial complex I, ensuring sufficient oxygen for lactate oxidase activity.⁴ Crucially, metformin suppresses [[slc7a11|SLC7A11]]-mediated glutathione synthesis and disables [[gpx4|GPX4]]-mediated lipid peroxide detoxification, rendering cells vulnerable to iron-dependent lipid peroxidation.⁴ Soybean phosphatidylcholine within the liposomal carrier provides polyunsaturated lipids as peroxidation substrates, overcoming endogenous lipid shortages.⁴ This strategy depletes lactate and triggers ferroptosis-mediated immunogenic cell death, releasing tumor antigens and damage-associated molecular patterns that reverse the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment.⁴

## HIV silencing and ART-free remission

Multiomic analysis of analytical treatment interruption cohorts identified [[ddit4|DDIT4]] and [[znf254|ZNF254]] as transcriptional regulators associated with delayed HIV rebound after antiretroviral therapy cessation.⁵ DDIT4, an mTOR inhibitor, and ZNF254, a zinc finger transcription factor, suppress HIV expression in both in vitro and in vivo models.⁵ Remarkably, metformin induces DDIT4 expression in primary CD4⁺ T cells and suppresses HIV reactivation in cells from ART-suppressed people with HIV, suggesting this widely available antidiabetic drug could be repurposed to silence latent HIV reservoirs and enable ART-free remission strategies.⁵⁻⁶ The discovery that an affordable, FDA-approved diabetes medication exhibits HIV-silencing activity positions metformin as a immediately translatable intervention for cure research.⁶

## Endometriosis and inflammatory diseases

The leptin receptor ([[lepr|LEPR]]) is strongly associated with [[endometriosis]] independent of age, menstrual cycle phase, pelvic pain, infertility history, or disease stage, enabling LEPR-targeted drug delivery to ectopic lesions.² The [[hlipo|HLipo platform]], a liposomal carrier modified with the HY7 peptide for LEPR targeting, achieves highly specific accumulation at ectopic endometrial lesions in mouse models, with LEPR blockade or mutation abolishing lesion-specific delivery.² Metformin-loaded HLipo (Met@HLipo) exhibits markedly superior antiendometriotic efficacy compared to free metformin without detectable toxicity, mechanistically inducing autophagy-mediated degradation of [[estrogen-receptor-beta|estrogen receptor β (ERβ)]], thereby directly targeting a central driver of endometriosis pathogenesis.² In sepsis-induced liver injury, bioengineered hybrid spheroids integrating mesenchymal stem cells and metformin-loaded [[plga|poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid)]] microspheres ([[mscargo|MSCargo]]) exhibit synergistic antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and immunomodulatory activities.⁷ MSCargo significantly enhances hepatocyte viability and protects against oxidative stress and inflammatory cytokine damage, while post-transplantation via the hepatic portal vein remarkably reduces sepsis-associated organ dysfunction across liver, kidney, and lung through modulation of TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathways and enhancement of hepatic regeneration.⁷

---

## Advanced delivery systems

Multiple delivery platforms have been engineered to overcome metformin's pharmacokinetic limitations and enable tissue-specific targeting. The [[hlipo|LEPR-targeted HLipo system]] achieves receptor-mediated endocytosis at endometriotic lesions through HY7 peptide modification.² Cancer cell membrane-biomimetic liposomes ([[ml-mlip|ML-MLip]]) provide immune-evasive delivery with prolonged circulation and preferential tumor accumulation.⁴ Mesenchymal stem cell-microsphere hybrid spheroids ([[mscargo|MSCargo]]) integrate controlled metformin release with stem cell homing to inflamed tissues, enabling dual-mechanism therapeutic effects in sepsis.⁷ Oral micropatch formulations employing mucoadhesive interpolymer complexes achieve enhanced intestinal absorption through multiple uptake pathways, demonstrating that intelligent delivery design can transform metformin's therapeutic window across diverse disease contexts.³
