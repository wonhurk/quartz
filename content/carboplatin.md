**Carboplatin** is a platinum-based chemotherapy agent employed primarily in the treatment of [[nsclc|non-small cell lung cancer]], particularly in squamous histologies. It mediates cytotoxicity through DNA crosslinking via platinum-DNA adducts, triggering apoptotic cell death. In contemporary clinical practice, carboplatin functions as a backbone agent within multimodal perioperative and advanced-stage regimens incorporating [[paclitaxel|taxanes]] and [[serplulimab|immunotherapy]] to enhance anti-tumor efficacy.¹

---

## Mechanism

Carboplatin exerts its anti-cancer activity by forming covalent intrastrand and interstrand DNA crosslinks with cellular nucleophilic sites, disrupting transcription and replication and ultimately triggering apoptosis. Compared to its precursor cisplatin, carboplatin demonstrates improved tolerability with reduced nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity, making it preferable in many patient populations. Tumor sensitivity to carboplatin-induced DNA damage correlates with the integrity of cellular DNA repair pathways; malignancies with deficient mismatch repair or homologous recombination machinery often exhibit heightened responsiveness.

## Squamous NSCLC Perioperative Treatment

Squamous non-small cell lung cancer represents a distinct NSCLC subtype with unique biological and clinical characteristics requiring tailored therapeutic approaches. A phase II trial evaluated the feasibility and efficacy of a simplified perioperative regimen combining carboplatin with a [[paclitaxel|taxane]] ([[paclitaxel]] or [[nab-paclitaxel]]) and the PD-L1 inhibitor [[serplulimab]] administered over four cycles in patients with resectable stage II-IIIA squamous NSCLC.¹ This multimodal strategy leverages carboplatin's DNA-damaging activity alongside taxane-induced mitotic disruption and [[serplulimab|immune checkpoint inhibition]], directly addressing the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment characteristic of squamous cancers. The combination aims to improve pathological response rates and long-term survival outcomes in this historically aggressive disease subtype.

---

## Taxane Combinations

Carboplatin is conventionally paired with [[paclitaxel|taxane agents]], which disrupt microtubule dynamics and induce mitotic arrest through distinct molecular mechanisms. Both [[paclitaxel]] and [[nab-paclitaxel]] — the albumin-bound formulation — have been successfully integrated into carboplatin-based regimens across perioperative and advanced-stage NSCLC settings. The synergistic interaction between platinum-induced DNA crosslinking and taxane-mediated microtubule destabilization produces enhanced apoptotic induction exceeding either agent administered alone, establishing carboplatin-taxane doublets as foundational chemotherapy backbones. These combinations are further augmented with immunotherapeutic agents such as [[serplulimab]] to maximize anti-tumor responses and overcome immunological tolerance.

## Resistance and Advanced Delivery Systems

Intrinsic and acquired chemotherapy resistance fundamentally limits the efficacy of conventional carboplatin administration. Recent advances in drug delivery have employed programmable, temporally coordinated multi-drug release to overcome these barriers. A modular microgel platform, inspired by LEGO brick assembly, enables independent programming of release kinetics for distinct drug classes from a single injectable formulation — utilizing gelatin methacrylate microspheres for hydrophilic agents and alginate microspheres for hydrophobic compounds.² Sequential delivery of carboplatin followed by [[rsl3|RSL3]], a ferroptosis inducer, overcame chemotherapy resistance in preclinical models and extended mouse survival by ten days compared to conventional monolithic hydrogels.² This approach exploits distinct cell death pathways sequentially rather than simultaneously, representing a paradigm shift in how multi-agent combination therapies are engineered. The modular architecture enables future customization for additional carboplatin-based regimens, addressing a fundamental limitation of conventional formulations: the inability to achieve independent, programmable drug release from a single therapeutic depot.
