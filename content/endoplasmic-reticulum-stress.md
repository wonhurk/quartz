**Endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS)** is a pathological condition characterized by the accumulation of unfolded or misfolded proteins in the endoplasmic reticulum lumen and dysregulation of cellular [[calcium-dysregulation|calcium homeostasis]], triggering the unfolded protein response (UPR) and serving as a critical regulator of immune activation, cell death, and therapeutic resistance across diverse disease contexts. The UPR is orchestrated by three canonical ER transmembrane sensors — IRE1α, PERK, and ATF6 — which detect protein-folding defects and initiate adaptive transcriptional programs.¹ When ER homeostasis cannot be restored, sustained ERS activates cell death pathways including apoptosis, ferroptosis, and immunogenic cell death, positioning ERS modulation as a therapeutic strategy in cancer, regenerative medicine, and transplantation.²³

---

## Mechanism

Endoplasmic reticulum stress is initiated when the protein-folding capacity of the ER is overwhelmed by increased demand or impaired machinery, triggering the UPR through sequential activation of IRE1α, PERK, and ATF6 pathways. The PERK arm phosphorylates [[eif2-alpha|eukaryotic translation initiation factor 2α (eIF2α)]], attenuating global protein synthesis while paradoxically upregulating stress-responsive transcription factors including ATF4 and CHOP.⁴ In aged hepatic macrophages, eIF2α undergoes aberrant hyperacetylation due to declining NAD⁺ levels and consequent loss of Sirt1 deacetylase activity, driving pro-inflammatory polarization through sustained expression of TNF-α, iNOS, and IL-1β.⁴ The ER and mitochondria form intimate contact sites that facilitate bidirectional [[calcium-dysregulation|calcium flux]], and when dysregulated, this crosstalk mutually reinforces ER stress and mitochondrial dysfunction, creating a self-amplifying cycle of organellar damage.¹ Excessive Ca²⁺ release from the ER into the cytosol disrupts mitochondrial respiration, generates reactive oxygen species (ROS), and suppresses survival pathways including Wnt/β-catenin signaling, thereby sensitizing cells to cuproptosis and other regulated cell death modalities.³

## Cancer immunotherapy

Therapeutic induction of ERS has emerged as a potent strategy to elicit immunogenic cell death in multiple cancer types, converting immunologically "cold" tumors into targets for adaptive immunity. In triple-negative breast cancer, TrxR-targeted Pt(IV) prodrugs simultaneously inhibit thioredoxin reductase and induce excessive ROS accumulation, triggering ER stress that activates the UPR and culminates in immunogenic cell death characterized by surface calreticulin exposure, ATP secretion, and HMGB1 release.² These damage-associated molecular patterns recruit dendritic cells and activate tumor-infiltrating T cells, establishing a durable antitumor immune response. Similarly, in glioblastoma multiforme, tumor-treating fields induce immunogenic cell death through sphingolipid-metabolism-associated pathway-mediated ER stress, as revealed by single-cell RNA sequencing analysis.⁵ Complementing this physical therapy with ATP-responsive hydrogel adjuvants that spatiotemporally release tumor antigens and CpG oligonucleotides amplifies myeloid cell activation and sustains long-term immune surveillance.⁵ The convergence of ERS-driven immunogenic cell death with cuproptosis represents another therapeutic frontier; metallic polymer nanoparticles co-delivering copper and cisplatin induce ER stress-triggered Ca²⁺ overload that suppresses Wnt/β-catenin signaling, reversing cuproptosis tolerance and enabling type II immunogenic cell death for enhanced chemo-immunotherapy efficacy in triple-negative breast cancer models.³

## Therapeutic resistance

Paradoxically, chronic activation of the UPR can confer immune evasion and therapeutic resistance in certain cancer contexts. In non-small cell lung cancer cells with acquired resistance to EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors, prolonged exposure to gefitinib, afatinib, or osimertinib drives differential dependency on EGFR downstream pathways and UPR elements.⁶ Afatinib-resistant cells exhibit the most pronounced immunosuppressive transcriptional signature, characterized by elevated PD-L1 expression, minimal MHC class I alterations, and a cytokine profile favoring immunosuppression with reduced IL-6, CXCL10, IFN-γ, and CCL22 but elevated IL-8.⁶ This immunosuppressive-like phenotype is partially mediated by IRE1α signaling, as its pharmacological inhibition reverses the transcriptional program and restores immunogenic features.⁶ In hepatocellular carcinoma associated with chronic hepatitis B virus infection, integrated multi-omics analysis identified [[ssr2|SSR2]] and [[ube2d3|UBE2D3]] as core stemness biomarkers linked to ER stress pathways, with high SSR2 expression and low UBE2D3 expression correlating with poor clinical outcomes and therapeutic resistance.⁷ These findings underscore the dual role of ERS in cancer — as both a therapeutic vulnerability when acutely induced and a resistance mechanism when chronically activated.

## Non-cancer diseases

Beyond oncology, aberrant ER stress drives pathological inflammation and tissue injury in multiple organ systems. In [[periodontitis|periodontitis]], ERS has been identified as a pivotal regulator driving macrophage pro-inflammatory activation, with transcriptomic analyses revealing that ER stress and mitochondrial dysfunction are mutually reinforced via dysregulated intracellular Ca²⁺ transport.¹ Polydopamine nanoparticles designed to chelate excessive calcium ions restore ER and mitochondrial homeostasis, remodel macrophage polarization from M1 to M2 phenotype, and mitigate periodontal inflammation while promoting bone regeneration.¹ In liver transplantation, the use of aged donor organs is limited by heightened susceptibility to ischemia-reperfusion injury, a process exacerbated by ER stress-driven pro-inflammatory macrophage activation during the late injury phase.⁴ In aged hepatic macrophages subjected to ischemia-reperfusion, NAD⁺ depletion reduces Sirt1 activity, leading to eIF2α hyperacetylation, aggravated ER stress, and sustained expression of TNF-α, iNOS, and IL-1β.⁴ These findings position ER stress modulation as a therapeutic strategy to improve marginal liver graft viability and expand the donor pool.

---

## Therapeutic modulation strategies

Restoration of ER homeostasis through targeted interventions demonstrates efficacy across regenerative medicine and cancer contexts. [[nmn|β-Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN)]] supplementation restores NAD⁺ levels in aged hepatic macrophages, reactivates Sirt1 deacetylase activity, reduces eIF2α acetylation, alleviates ER stress, and suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokine expression, thereby mitigating liver ischemia-reperfusion injury and improving transplantation outcomes.⁴ For anatomically constrained tissues such as periodontal defects, osteoimmunomodulatory microneedle patches integrated with decellularized extracellular matrix enable localized delivery of dual-functional polydopamine nanoparticles that simultaneously scavenge excessive calcium ions and release osteogenic peptide BMP9, restoring ER-mitochondrial crosstalk while promoting bone regeneration.¹ In cancer therapy, ROS-responsive polymer nanoparticles co-encapsulating copper and platinum compounds provide on-demand release triggered by the tumor microenvironment, inducing ER stress-mediated immunogenic cell death while suppressing Wnt/β-catenin-driven survival signaling.³ Conversely, inhibition of IRE1α signaling in therapy-resistant lung cancers reverses immunosuppressive transcriptional programs associated with chronic UPR activation, suggesting that context-dependent UPR pathway targeting may restore immunotherapy sensitivity.⁶
