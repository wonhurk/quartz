**Ruxolitinib** is a selective JAK1/JAK2 inhibitor and the first FDA-approved Janus kinase inhibitor for [[myelofibrosis]], representing a paradigm shift in the treatment of myeloproliferative neoplasms.¹ It suppresses JAK-STAT signaling, the core oncogenic pathway in JAK2 V617F-driven myeloproliferative disorders, thereby controlling hematocrit levels, reducing splenomegaly, and ameliorating constitutional symptoms.² The drug has now extended beyond myelofibrosis to demonstrate therapeutic benefit in [[polycythaemia-vera|polycythaemia vera (PV)]] patients refractory or intolerant to [[hydroxyurea]], and more recently in steroid-refractory [[chronic-graft-versus-host-disease|chronic graft-versus-host disease (cGVHD)]], where it exhibits corticosteroid-sparing properties.

---

## Mechanism

Ruxolitinib works by competitively inhibiting the catalytic activity of JAK1 and JAK2 kinases, blocking the downstream phosphorylation of signal transducers and activators of transcription ([[stat]] proteins). In [[myeloproliferative-neoplasms|myeloproliferative neoplasms]] driven by [[jak2]] V617F mutations, this inhibition directly suppresses the pathological proliferation of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells. The drug does not directly target the mutant allele but instead interrupts the constitutively active signaling that drives clonal expansion, effectively allowing residual normal hematopoiesis to recover in the context of a suppressed malignant clone.

---

## Myelofibrosis

Ruxolitinib established its clinical efficacy in primary myelofibrosis and post-PV/ET myelofibrosis, becoming the first JAK inhibitor to achieve regulatory approval for these indications.¹ Its approval preceded [[fedratinib]], the second JAK inhibitor licensed for myelofibrosis, and has become the reference standard for JAK inhibitor-based therapy in this disease. The drug effectively reduces splenomegaly, a hallmark of symptomatic myelofibrosis, while also controlling constitutional symptoms including fatigue, night sweats, and fever. Its JAK-selective mechanism allows sustained suppression of JAK-STAT signaling while maintaining adequate normal hematopoiesis, a balance critical for long-term tolerability.

---

## Polycythaemia vera

Ruxolitinib has demonstrated substantial efficacy in [[polycythaemia-vera|polycythaemia vera (PV)]] patients resistant to or intolerant of [[hydroxyurea]], a first-line cytoreductive agent. The drug effectively controls hematocrit levels, reduces expanded red blood cell mass, and ameliorates PV-associated symptoms including pruritus and thrombotic risk.² In high-risk PV patients, ruxolitinib achieves pronounced hematocrit reduction of approximately −6.3%, sustaining disease control beyond what cytoreductive therapy alone can provide.² Furthermore, ruxolitinib reduces leukocyte counts and platelet numbers, addressing the multilineage cytopenias often present in advanced PV.

---

## Real-world efficacy and JAK-inhibitor-naive advantage

The prospective, non-interventional PaVe study assessed ruxolitinib effectiveness in 433 German patients with PV in a real-world setting, validating the efficacy observed in controlled trials.² Among these patients, 38% were JAK-inhibitor-naive and 62% had prior exposure to JAK inhibitors. Notably, JAK-inhibitor-naive patients demonstrated substantially greater reductions in hematocrit and leukocyte counts compared to JAK-inhibitor-pretreated patients: naive cohorts achieved −6.2% versus −1.6% hematocrit reduction and −4.5 versus −1.4 ×10³/μl leukocyte reduction, respectively.² High-risk patients similarly showed more pronounced responses than low-risk stratification, with −6.3% versus −5.3% hematocrit reductions. Platelet reduction was likewise more marked in naive patients (−80.5 ×10³/μl versus −42.6 ×10³/μl), suggesting that prior JAK inhibitor exposure may attenuate subsequent therapeutic response, possibly through selection of cells with altered JAK signaling dependencies or reduced drug sensitivity.

---

## Steroid-refractory chronic graft-versus-host disease

Beyond myeloproliferative disorders, ruxolitinib exhibits significant immunomodulatory activity in steroid-refractory cGVHD, particularly when combined with donor-derived regulatory T cell ([[treg|Treg]]) infusions. In phase 1/2 trials of Treg therapy for steroid-dependent or steroid-refractory cGVHD, ruxolitinib dose reduction was achieved in 83% of treated patients, concurrent with corticosteroid tapering in 58% and discontinuation of calcineurin inhibitors in 75%.³ This corticosteroid-sparing property reduces immunosuppressive toxicity and infection risk in transplant recipients while maintaining disease control. The Treg-ruxolitinib synergy reflects both the drug's JAK-dependent inhibition of pathological T-effector function and the enhanced suppressive capacity of Treg cells when provided in sufficient numbers, establishing ruxolitinib as a versatile agent across hematologic and immunologic diseases.
