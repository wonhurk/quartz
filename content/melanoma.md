**Melanoma** is a malignant tumor originating from melanin-producing melanocytes, presenting primarily in cutaneous and uveal forms and posing significant global health threats.¹ The disease is predominantly driven by [[brafv600e|BRAFV600E]] mutations, though rare non-V600E/K BRAF variants occur in approximately 1.8% of cases with comparable survival outcomes but reduced response rates to targeted therapy.² Current therapeutic strategies span multiple modalities including immunotherapy with checkpoint inhibitors such as [[pembrolizumab]], targeted protein degradation, [[ferroptosis]] induction, and radiotherapy sensitization, with combination approaches addressing the critical challenge of immune evasion mediated by [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] upregulation.¹ ³

---

## Genetic drivers and molecular mechanisms

The oncogenic landscape of melanoma is dominated by activating BRAF mutations, with [[brafv600e|BRAFV600E]] representing the most prevalent alteration.² Rare BRAF mutations encompassing codon 600 and adjacent positions occur at low frequency but retain functional significance, displaying variable degrees of structural destabilization that promote constitutive kinase activation.² Melanomas harboring these rare variants show overall survival and progression-free survival comparable to V600E/K-mutant disease following BRAF/MEK inhibitor therapy, though objective response rates are modestly lower (48% versus 66%).² The BRAFV600E signaling axis is further sustained by isoprenylcysteine carboxyl methyltransferase (ICMT), which catalyzes post-translational methylation of prenylated CAAX proteins including [[inpp5e|INPP5E]].⁴ ICMT-mediated membrane targeting of INPP5E supports tumor proliferation and invasion, and genetic or pharmacologic ICMT inhibition with UCM-1336 suppresses growth in xenograft models and BRAF-inhibitor-resistant cell lines.⁴

## Immunotherapy strategies

Melanoma has emerged as a paradigm for cancer immunotherapy, with diverse vaccine and checkpoint modulation approaches under investigation. [[peptide-cancer-vaccine|Peptide-based vaccines]] targeting HLA-A2-restricted epitopes such as [[melan-a27|Melan-A27]] have been formulated using freeze-dried liposome platforms incorporating [[qs21|QS21]] adjuvant and cobalt porphyrin-phospholipid (CoPoP) to enhance epitope immunogenicity.⁵ These multiplex formulations maintain structural integrity through mannitol stabilization and induce robust tumor inhibition in murine models.⁵ Complementary glycolipid-based strategies employ [[gm3-based-conjugate-vaccine|ganglioside GM3 conjugate vaccines]] with lipid A mimetics as self-adjuvanting carriers, achieving IgG titers more than twice those of conventional GM3-KLH/alum formulations when combined with EcMPLA and QS21.⁶

Beyond direct vaccination, modulation of the tumor microenvironment via ectonucleotidase inhibition represents a novel axis for immune enhancement. The CD39 inhibitor [[psb-24379|PSB-24379]] (8-(1-naphthylthio)-N6-(4-phenylbutyl)-AMP) blocks ATP hydrolysis in melanoma cell membranes with a Ki of 77.4 nM, partially reversing ATP-mediated suppression of T cell activation and proliferation in adenosine-rich tumor microenvironments.⁷ BET protein inhibition with [[jq1|JQ1]] broadly enhances melanoma immunogenicity through pleiotropic effects on interferon signaling, antigen presentation machinery, and immune checkpoint regulation.⁸ JQ1 treatment upregulates HLA class I components and [[melan-a27|MART-1]] expression while simultaneously suppressing [[pd-l1|PD-L1]], IDO1, and HLA class II, resulting in markedly improved tumor recognition by autologous MART-1-specific and neoantigen-specific CD8+ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes.⁸

## Targeted protein degradation and radiotherapy

Proteolysis-targeting chimeras (PROTACs) offer tumor-selective protein degradation with reduced off-target toxicity. The HSP90-dependent bioorthogonal PROTAC prodrug system (HBPROTAC) achieves spatial and temporal control through tetrazine-conjugated HSP90 inhibitors (Tz-PU) that accumulate in tumors and activate trans-cyclooctene-caged PROTAC prodrugs via inverse electron demand Diels-Alder reactions.⁹ This platform enables tumor-specific degradation of BRD4 and Bcl-xL in melanoma xenografts, with HSP90 inhibition synergistically enhancing degradation efficiency.⁹ Complementary PROTAC strategies targeting deubiquitinase USP7 reveal distinct cellular consequences of protein degradation versus enzymatic inhibition, with degraders modulating cancer-specific protein networks without affecting cell growth, while prolonged inhibitor treatment induces off-target proteomic and metabolic dysregulation.¹⁰

For uveal melanoma, radiotherapy remains a primary modality, and its efficacy can be enhanced through histone deacetylase 6 (HDAC6) inhibition. The selective HDAC6 inhibitor [[wt-161|WT-161]] acts as a potent radiosensitizer by disrupting HDAC6 interactions with DNA damage repair proteins DDB2 and DEK, suppressing repair pathway gene expression and promoting accumulation of irradiation-induced DNA damage.¹¹ This approach establishes HDAC6 as both a predictive biomarker for radiation response and a therapeutic target for expanding radiotherapy utility in uveal melanoma.¹¹

## Ferroptosis induction and immune checkpoint modulation

Although melanoma cells exhibit sensitivity to [[ferroptosis]], the iron-dependent cell death pathway paradoxically induces [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] upregulation that facilitates immune evasion.¹ [[neferine|Neferine]], a natural alkaloid, reverses this immune suppression by simultaneously inducing ferroptosis and inhibiting PD-L1 expression.¹ The [[nef-mof-c|Nef@MOF@C]] delivery system leverages coordination networks between neferine's phenolic hydroxyl groups and iron within metal-organic frameworks, enabling pH-responsive disassembly in acidic tumor microenvironments with sustained release of both neferine and Fe³⁺.¹ This dual release mechanism provides stable energy for ferroptosis while suppressing PD-L1 upregulation, thereby enhancing T cell-mediated immune surveillance.¹ Coating the nanoformulation with homologous cell membranes further enhances targeting specificity, effectively inhibiting both primary and lung metastatic melanoma in murine models.¹

## Metastatic disease and precision approaches

Lung metastasis represents a critical clinical challenge in advanced melanoma, addressable through engineered extracellular vesicle platforms. Tumor cell-derived medium vesicles (TMVs) modulated with cationic DOTAP lipids exhibit altered internal charges and tissue tropism, achieving lung-selective accumulation as confirmed by positron emission tomography imaging.¹² The TMVs-DOTAP/[[sipd-l1|siPD-L1]] system enables lung-targeted delivery of siRNA that effectively silences [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] in melanoma lung metastases, enhancing tumor immune responses through mechanisms mediated by protein corona composition.¹²

Predictive modeling is advancing personalized immunotherapy selection through computational analysis of T cell receptor-neoantigen interactions. The lightweight masked language model TcrLM, pretrained on large-scale TCR CDR3 sequence datasets, achieves robust zero-shot generalization in predicting peptide-TCR binding specificity and effectively captures biochemical properties and positional preferences of amino acids.¹³ In an exploratory melanoma cohort, TcrLM-predicted TCR-neoantigen binding scores correlate with immunotherapy response and clinical outcomes, highlighting potential for advancing personalized medicine.¹³ Real-world evidence from stage IIB-IIC cutaneous melanoma patients treated in community oncology settings demonstrates concordance with [[keynote-716|KEYNOTE-716]] trial control arm estimates for recurrence-free and distant metastasis-free survival, validating the external generalizability of clinical trial endpoints.¹⁴

## Novel agents

Silsesquioxane nanozymes represent a metal-free organocatalytic platform capable of intracellular prodrug activation. These ultrasmall, water-stable octa-amino silsesquioxanes exhibit aldolase-like activity and enable stimuli-responsive catalysis through reversible supramolecular aggregation.¹⁵ Their high biocompatibility and efficient cellular internalization permit retro-aldol activation of doxorubicin prodrugs within melanoma cells, resulting in selective cytotoxicity.¹⁵
