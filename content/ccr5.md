**C-C Chemokine Receptor 5 (CCR5)** is a seven-transmembrane G protein-coupled receptor that serves dual roles as a critical [[hiv|HIV]] entry co-receptor and a regulator of neuroinflammatory responses in the central nervous system. The strong genetic association between loss-of-function [[ccr5-delta32|CCR5-Δ32/Δ32]] mutations and natural resistance to HIV infection has established CCR5 as a validated antiviral target, motivating both small-molecule antagonists and novel gene therapy approaches. Beyond infectious disease, CCR5-driven microglial activation contributes to secondary brain injury following [[subarachnoid-hemorrhage|subarachnoid hemorrhage]], making it an emerging target for neuroprotective intervention.

---

## Mechanism

CCR5 is expressed on the surface of [[cd4-t-cells|CD4+ T cells]], [[macrophages]], and [[dendritic-cells|dendritic cells]], where it serves as a primary binding site for the chemokine ligands RANTES, MIP-1α, and MIP-1β. Upon ligand engagement, the receptor undergoes conformational changes that activate downstream signaling cascades regulating immune cell migration and activation. In the context of viral infection, CCR5 functions as an essential co-receptor alongside [[cd4|CD4]] for viral envelope fusion and cell entry. The epidemiological observation that individuals homozygous for the [[ccr5-delta32|CCR5-Δ32 deletion]] — a 32-base pair frameshift mutation resulting in a nonfunctional receptor — remain persistently seronegative despite repeated exposure to [[hiv|HIV]] established CCR5 as a rate-limiting determinant of viral infectivity.¹ In microglia and other [[myeloid-cells|myeloid cells]], CCR5 signaling drives pro-inflammatory activation through recruitment of inflammatory gene transcription programs, making it a key upstream regulator of neuroinflammatory responses.

---

## HIV Entry and Antiviral Strategies

[[Hiv|HIV]] utilizes CCR5 as its primary co-receptor for cell entry in macrophage-tropic and early-stage infection, alongside the initial binding interaction with [[cd4|CD4]]. The strong correlation between [[ccr5-delta32|CCR5-Δ32/Δ32 genotype]] and durable resistance to infection has motivated the development of CCR5-blocking therapeutics. The monoclonal antibody [[leronlimab]] binds to CCR5 with high affinity and prevents [[hiv|HIV]] and [[shiv|SHIV]] envelope glycoprotein interaction with the receptor, thereby blocking viral access to target cells. Importantly, therapeutic resistance mutations that permit viral breakthrough are less common against CCR5-targeted agents than against reverse transcriptase or integrase inhibitors, suggesting a higher barrier to resistance.

---

## Gene Therapy and Long-term Viral Suppression

[[Aav|Adeno-associated virus]] vectors represent a promising vehicle for delivering sustained CCR5 blockade in [[hiv]]-infected individuals. In a pivotal study, [[aav]] vectors engineered to express the [[leronlimab|CCR5-blocking antibody leronlimab]] were administered to [[shiv]]-infected rhesus macaques to achieve durable suppression of viral replication.¹ The approach successfully generated detectable plasma leronlimab concentrations and maintained full CCR5 receptor occupancy on blood [[cd4-t-cells|CD4+ T cells]] for more than one year in animals that did not mount robust antidrug antibody responses. Of the nine macaques producing sufficient leronlimab to achieve complete receptor occupancy, six demonstrated stringent or partial control of [[shiv]] viremia over the long term.¹ Notably, even in animals that initially developed antidrug antibody responses leading to rapid plasma leronlimab clearance, spontaneous transgene reemergence occurred approximately one year later, with CCR5 receptor occupancy persisting indefinitely without subsequent antibody rebound.¹ In the three macaques with uncontrolled [[shiv]] viremia despite monotherapy, short-term administration of exogenous [[leronlimab]] restored complete viral suppression in two animals and achieved a 100-fold reduction in the third, indicating that pharmacological reinforcement of the CCR5 blockade threshold can overcome replication-competent viral strains. These findings suggest that [[aav]]-mediated CCR5 gene therapy represents a pathway toward a functional viral cure, contingent upon maintaining threshold levels of receptor occupancy.

## Neuroinflammation and Microglial Activation

Beyond its role in [[hiv|HIV]] pathogenesis, CCR5 serves as an upstream regulator of pro-inflammatory microglial responses in the setting of acute brain injury. Following [[subarachnoid-hemorrhage|subarachnoid hemorrhage]], [[microglia|microglial cells]] undergo rapid activation that contributes significantly to secondary neuronal injury, and CCR5 expression is dramatically upregulated in microglia residing in cortical and perilesional brain regions.² The [[microglia|microglial]] activation phenotype triggered by CCR5 signaling involves the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines including interleukin-6 and TNF-α, which directly damage neurons and promote neuronal apoptosis. The CCR5 antagonist [[maraviroc]], originally developed as an [[hiv]] entry inhibitor, can traverse the blood-brain barrier when delivered intranasally and effectively blocks CCR5-mediated microglial activation. In experimental models of [[subarachnoid-hemorrhage|subarachnoid hemorrhage]], intranasal [[maraviroc]] treatment successfully reoriented [[microglia]] from pro-inflammatory to anti-inflammatory phenotypes and reduced the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines.² Mechanistically, [[maraviroc]] prevented oxyhemoglobin-induced microglial polarization, thereby indirectly protecting neurons from microglial-dependent apoptosis.²

---

## Neuroprotection and Clinical Outcomes

The therapeutic efficacy of CCR5 antagonism in [[subarachnoid-hemorrhage|subarachnoid hemorrhage]] extends across both structural and functional neuroclinical measures. Intranasal [[maraviroc]] administration significantly attenuated brain edema and suppressed neuronal apoptosis in perilesional tissue, with improvements sustained through both acute and chronic recovery phases following [[subarachnoid-hemorrhage|subarachnoid hemorrhage]].² Early neurological assessments revealed significant improvements in sensorimotor function, and these gains persisted over long-term follow-up, suggesting sustained neuroprotection rather than temporary symptom modification.² The modest side effect profile of [[maraviroc]] — well-established from its use as an [[hiv]] therapeutic — combined with its brain-penetrant properties and capacity to modulate the [[microglia|microglial]] inflammatory response, makes it an attractive candidate for translation to [[subarachnoid-hemorrhage|subarachnoid hemorrhage]] patients experiencing secondary brain injury. These preclinical findings establish CCR5 inhibition as a multimodal neuroprotective strategy addressing both the inflammatory cascade and downstream neuronal damage.
