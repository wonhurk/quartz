**Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG (LGG)** is a naturally acid-tolerant probiotic bacterium engineered as a dual-function therapeutic agent for both mucosal inflammatory diseases and solid tumors. Its therapeutic activity operates through two distinct mechanisms: restoration of intestinal epithelial integrity by promoting [[goblet-cells|goblet cell]] expansion and mucus secretion via the [[cgrp]]-[[ramp1]] signaling axis, and direct anti-tumor immunity through mitochondrial-dependent apoptosis, immune cell polarization, and competitive inhibition of tumor-promoting microbiota.¹² The organism's intrinsic preference for acidic environments mirrors the hostile pH of tumor microenvironments, enabling both preferential colonization and sustained therapeutic delivery when formulated as bioengineered nanoconjugates.

---

## Mechanism

LGG exhibits remarkable acid-environment tropism, demonstrating strong growth and adhesion in acidic conditions that characterize both tumor microenvironments and inflamed intestinal tissue.² This acidophilic preference is coupled with the organism's capacity for deep tissue penetration and production of microbial metabolites that directly acidify the local milieu, thereby amplifying cytotoxic effects against sensitive cell populations. The bacterium secretes bioactive compounds that induce mitochondrial dysfunction and caspase-mediated apoptosis in target tumor cells, while simultaneously triggering innate immune responses through [[macrophage]] M1 polarization and [[dendritic-cell]] maturation — shifts that promote anti-tumor T cell infiltration.² LGG further suppresses tumor growth through competitive exclusion of pro-tumoral microbiota, thereby reshaping the tumor microenvironment's bacterial ecology toward a therapeutically favorable composition.¹

---

## Ulcerative colitis applications

In [[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis]], LGG acts as a scaffold for targeted mucosal restoration through a multi-layer delivery system. When formulated within the [[leg-pda|LEG@PDA]] nanocomposite — LGG backpacked with emodin-loaded lactoferrin nanoparticles and coated with polydopamine — the system achieves colon-specific delivery via M-cell-mediated transcytosis.¹ Upon arrival at colonic patches, LGG orchestrates enhanced mucus secretion from [[goblet-cells|goblet cells]] through the [[cgrp]]-[[ramp1]] signaling axis, simultaneously expanding the goblet cell population to establish a durable mucin-secreting cellular reservoir.¹ Concurrently, the lactoferrin and emodin components suppress ferroptosis in intestinal epithelial cells by modulating iron homeostasis, stabilizing mitochondria, and activating the glutathione-[[gpx4|GPX4]] antioxidant axis.¹ This integrated approach restores mucosal barrier integrity while preventing disease relapse in murine ulcerative colitis models.

---

## Cancer applications

LGG exhibits potent anti-tumor activity across multiple cancer models through complementary mechanisms of direct cytotoxicity, immune orchestration, and microbiota remodeling. In breast cancer and melanoma contexts, LGG's acidification of the tumor microenvironment coupled with mitochondrial-targeting metabolites drives apoptosis in malignant cells.² The bacterium simultaneously activates M1-polarized [[macrophage|macrophages]] and mature [[dendritic-cell|dendritic cells]], orchestrating downstream CD8+ T cell recruitment and sustained anti-tumor immunosurveillance.² By competitively inhibiting tumor-promoting bacteria, LGG further suppresses pro-inflammatory myeloid cell differentiation, tilting the immune balance toward anti-tumor immunity.² These properties render LGG particularly amenable to bioengineering for enhanced efficacy in chemotherapy-resistant cancers.

---

## Bioengineered delivery systems

To maximize therapeutic impact, LGG has been conjugated with chemotherapeutic nanoparticles and integrated into smart delivery matrices. Paclitaxel-loaded poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) nanoparticles attached directly to LGG (PTX-NPs-LGG) enable simultaneous bacteriotherapy and targeted chemotherapy, with the bacterium's inherent tropism directing the cytotoxic payload toward acidic tumor tissue.² Integration into thermo-sensitive hydrogels permits peritumoral injection with sustained, localized release over extended periods, preventing systemic toxicity while maintaining high local drug concentrations.² Complementarily, the [[leg-pda|LEG@PDA]] system for ulcerative colitis employs polydopamine surface coating to provide gastric protection and pH-responsive dissolution in the colon, while lactoferrin nanoparticle carriers enable epithelial targeting via specific receptor-mediated transcytosis.¹ These bioengineered platforms represent a practical advance in combining live bacterial immunotherapy with conventional chemotherapy or small-molecule therapeutics.
