**Cardiac troponin I (cTnI)** is a structural cardiac protein biomarker that serves as the gold-standard indicator for myocardial injury and acute myocardial infarction. Upon myocardial necrosis, cTnI is rapidly released into the bloodstream in detectable concentrations, enabling early diagnosis, dynamic monitoring, and prognostic evaluation across both centralized laboratory and point-of-care diagnostic settings.¹ Recent advances in immunosensing technologies have transformed cTnI detection through innovations in DNA-engineered surface immobilization strategies and plasmonic signal amplification mechanisms, achieving ultrasensitive quantification with limits of detection in the sub-picogram range.

---

## Mechanism and clinical significance

cTnI is a regulatory protein component of the thin filament within the cardiac sarcomere, where it modulates the interaction between actin and myosin during the cardiac contraction cycle. During myocardial injury—whether from acute myocardial infarction, myocarditis, heart failure, or septic cardiomyopathy—the integrity of the sarcolemmal membrane is compromised, and cTnI leaks into the interstitial fluid and systemic circulation. The temporal kinetics of cTnI release make it a uniquely valuable clinical marker: elevation begins within 2-4 hours of myocardial injury, peaks at 24-48 hours, and remains detectable for 7-14 days, enabling both acute event detection and retrospective diagnosis when patients present late.¹ This extended window of detection, combined with superior cardiac specificity compared to earlier markers, has established cTnI as the preferred biomarker for myocardial infarction diagnosis in current clinical guidelines.

---

## DNA-engineered immunosensing platform

A transformative advance in cTnI quantification is the DNA-engineered immunosensing platform (DEISP), which employs DNA duplex-based capture probe immobilization to overcome limitations inherent in conventional protein-based attachment strategies.¹ The [[deisp|DEISP approach]] firmly anchors capture antibodies to the sensing interface via DNA duplexes while preserving their antigen-binding sites, yielding exceptional biocompatibility and detection performance. When applied to cTnI detection, the platform achieved a limit of detection of 2.35 pg/mL with a linear dynamic range spanning 7 orders of magnitude, substantially exceeding the clinical decision thresholds required for myocardial infarction diagnosis.¹ In clinical validation studies encompassing serum and urine samples, DEISP demonstrated 91% diagnostic accuracy for myocardial infarction assessment with a total assay time of 90 minutes, highlighting its utility for rapid clinical decision-making.¹

---

## Point-of-care lateral flow immunoassays

Beyond centralized laboratory testing, lateral flow immunoassay platforms have been engineered to enable rapid, decentralized cTnI measurement at the patient bedside or in resource-limited clinical settings. Hierarchical mesochannel-confined nanozyme reactors—synthesized through spatially controlled assembly of polydopamine scaffolds and platinum nanozymes within magnetically core-shelled mesoporous silica—have been developed to dramatically enhance colorimetric signal amplification in visual lateral flow assays.² These nanoreactors function through a combination of electrostatic adsorption, high-specific-surface-area interception, and mesoporous confinement catalysis, achieving 125-fold enhancement in signal brightness compared to conventional gold nanoparticles of equivalent size.² The resulting point-of-care platform enables both qualitative visual detection and quantitative semi-quantitative analysis of cTnI, allowing clinicians to rapidly assess cardiac injury at the point of patient presentation.

Clinical validation of nanozyme-enhanced lateral flow immunoassays was conducted using 234 serum samples from centralized clinical laboratories and 34 samples collected directly from emergency departments, demonstrating detection limits 125-fold lower than conventional gold nanoparticle-labeled assays while maintaining quantitative performance comparable to or superior to standard laboratory-based instruments.² The platform provides rapid, stable, and user-friendly diagnostics suitable for home healthcare monitoring and emergency department triage, fundamentally expanding access to cardiac injury assessment.

---

## Multiplex cardiac biomarker detection

Modern immunoassay platforms have evolved toward simultaneous multiplex quantification of cTnI alongside complementary cardiac and systemic injury biomarkers, enabling more comprehensive clinical risk stratification.² Nanozyme-enhanced lateral flow formats can detect cTnI in parallel with markers such as alpha-fetoprotein, kidney injury biomarkers, and inflammatory cytokines, allowing differential diagnosis between acute myocardial infarction and other causes of elevated troponin including sepsis, acute kidney disease, myocarditis, or pulmonary embolism.² This integrated multiplex approach represents a key advancement in point-of-care diagnostic capability, enabling rapid comprehensive assessment at the time of patient presentation to emergency departments or primary care settings.
