**Complement C3** is the central proteolytic hub of the [[alternative-complement-pathway|alternative complement pathway]], activated during innate immune responses to initiate a proteolytic cascade that amplifies inflammatory signaling. In [[iga-nephropathy|IgA nephropathy (IgAN)]], the most common primary glomerulonephritis worldwide, C3 plays a mechanistically dual role: its activation by mesangial IgA deposition drives pathogenic glomerular inflammation and kidney injury, yet simultaneously C3 mediates protective clearance of circulating IgA immune complexes through macrophage-dependent phagocytosis.¹ The pathway is regulated by [[cfh|complement factor H (CFH)]], the principal negative regulator of C3 activation, whose supplementation provides superior therapeutic outcomes compared to complete C3 inhibition by selectively dampening pathogenic deposition while preserving beneficial circulating C3 levels.¹

---

## Mechanism

C3 activation proceeds through cleavage of the complement protein into two functional fragments: C3a, a potent chemotactic peptide that recruits inflammatory cells, and C3b, an opsonin that deposits on pathogen surfaces and self-tissues. Within the alternative pathway, C3 amplification is controlled by several regulators, including complement factor H, which acts as a cofactor for factor I-mediated degradation of C3b and prevents formation of the C3 convertase.¹ The pathway becomes dysregulated in IgAN when mesangial deposition of IgA initiates complement activation, leading to sustained C3b accumulation in the glomerular compartment and recruitment of inflammatory macrophages via complement receptors. This dual participation — both as an inflammatory driver and as a tagging molecule for immune complex clearance — creates a therapeutic paradox central to understanding IgAN pathophysiology.

---

## IgA nephropathy and glomerular inflammation

IgAN is initiated by abnormal IgA1 glycosylation in the bone marrow, resulting in circulating IgA-dominant immune complexes that deposit in the mesangium and activate the alternative pathway.¹ C3 cleavage and C3b deposition within glomeruli trigger intracellular signaling through complement receptors, upregulating pro-inflammatory cytokine expression and promoting macrophage-mediated glomerular damage. Complete renal dysfunction and proteinuria in IgAN are tightly linked to the extent of mesangial C3 deposition, making C3 a key biomarker of active disease. The paradox, however, emerges when examining the role of C3-mediated immune complex clearance: while mesangial C3 deposition is pathogenic, circulating C3 is essential for efficient removal of circulating IgA immune complexes by the reticuloendothelial system before they accumulate in the kidney.

---

## The C3 deficiency paradox

Complete C3 knockout experiments in murine IgAN models revealed an unexpected therapeutic liability: ablating C3 entirely prevented glomerular C3b deposition and mesangial C3 complement activation, yet paradoxically increased glomerular IgA deposition and macrophage infiltration.¹ This counterintuitive finding demonstrates that despite contributing to inflammation, C3 also mediates a protective function—the opsonization and clearance of circulating IgA immune complexes via C3b-dependent macrophage phagocytosis. When C3 is completely absent, circulating immune complexes accumulate and eventually deposit in the glomerulus in greater quantities, overwhelming the kidney's filtration tolerance. This dual-role mechanism explains why brute-force C3 inhibition is inferior to selective pathway regulation: blocking only the pathogenic deposition pathway while maintaining systemic immune complex clearance is therapeutically superior.

---

## Therapeutic regulation via complement factor H

Supplementation of [[cfh|complement factor H]] rather than complete C3 knockout provides the optimal therapeutic strategy for IgAN by selectively attenuating pathogenic C3 activation while preserving circulating C3-mediated immune complex clearance.¹ CFH-supplemented mice showed reduced glomerular C3 deposition and mesangial IgA accumulation, alongside decreased inflammatory cytokine expression and reduced macrophage infiltration. Proteinuria was ameliorated, and glomerular morphology was preserved. Crucially, CFH supplementation maintained elevated circulating C3 levels, enabling continued opsonization and [[macrophage]]-mediated phagocytosis of IgA immune complexes in the bloodstream. Clinical evidence supports this mechanism: IgAN patients with naturally elevated plasma CFH levels exhibited reduced mesangial C3 and IgA deposition alongside sustained circulating C3, confirming that pathway-selective regulation rather than complete ablation is the physiologically superior approach.

---

## Broader complement roles and immune homeostasis

Beyond IgAN, C3 activation is essential for opsonization of pathogens, activation of terminal complement components, and enhancement of adaptive immune responses. C3 deficiency globally impairs bacterial clearance and vaccine responsiveness, illustrating its critical role in host defense. The protective immune complex clearance function of C3 extends beyond IgAN to other immune complex-mediated diseases, where dysregulation of C3 can similarly result in pathological deposition. This broader role underscores why complete complement inhibition carries significant immunological costs and why pathway-selective modulation represents a more balanced therapeutic approach across glomerulonephritis and other complement-driven diseases.

---

## Complement inhibitors and modulators

[[compstatin]] and related C3-targeted complement inhibitors achieve C3 blockade through direct inhibition of C3 convertase or C3 itself, providing an alternative mechanism to pathway regulation.¹ In preclinical models examining anti-PEG immunity, C3 blockade with compstatin attenuated complement-mediated hypersensitivity reactions, demonstrating utility in managing immunotoxicity. However, complete C3 blockade carries the same paradoxical liability evident in genetic C3 deficiency: loss of immune complex clearance capacity. Pathway-selective approaches, such as CFH supplementation or factor B inhibition, offer a more nuanced alternative by dampening pathogenic deposition while preserving beneficial circulating complement activity.
