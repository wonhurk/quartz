**Mantle cell lymphoma (MCL)** is an aggressive B-cell lymphoma arising from the transformed cells of the [[mantle-zone]] and predominantly affecting older adults, characterized historically by limited treatment options in relapsed or refractory disease and a dismal prognosis prior to the modern targeted therapy era.¹ The disease is driven by the t(11;14) translocation resulting in [[cyclin-d1|cyclin D1]] overexpression and aberrant [[cdk|cyclin-dependent kinase]] signaling. Recent advances in [[car-t-cell-therapy|CAR-T cell therapy]] and [[btk-inhibitor|Bruton tyrosine kinase inhibition]] have fundamentally transformed MCL management, delivering unprecedented response rates and survival improvements across both elderly and younger patient populations, from front-line therapy through multiply-resistant disease.

---

## Molecular Background

Mantle cell lymphoma arises from B lymphocytes at the mantle zone of lymph node follicles and is pathologically defined by the t(11;14)(q13;q32) chromosomal translocation, which places the CYCLIN D1 oncogene under control of the immunoglobulin heavy chain enhancer and drives constitutive proliferation.¹ The disease exhibits additional molecular alterations in tumor suppressor pathways including [[tp53]] and [[rb1]], conferring aggressive biology and chemoresistance. Historically, MCL carried a median overall survival of 3–5 years from diagnosis, with very limited salvage options for patients experiencing relapse or chemotherapy refractoriness.

---

## Relapsed/Refractory Disease and CAR-T Therapy

For patients with relapsed or refractory MCL, therapeutic options were historically restricted and prognosis remained dismal. [[Brexucabtagene autoleucel (brexu-cel)]], a second-generation [[car-t-cell-therapy|CAR-T cell therapy]] targeting the B-cell antigen [[cd19]], has emerged as a transformative treatment in this setting. A large real-world registry analysis of 233 elderly patients aged ≥70 years (median age 74.6 years) with relapsed/refractory MCL demonstrated marked efficacy: at day +100, the best overall response rate was 91%, comprising 78% complete remission and 13% partial remission.¹ Remarkably, 62% of these heavily pretreated patients had prior exposure to [[btk-inhibitor|BTK inhibitors]], demonstrating the drug's utility in multiply-resistant disease and patients who have exhausted conventional targeted approaches.¹

One-year follow-up data from the same cohort revealed an overall survival of 74% and progression-free survival of 62%, with a nonrelapse mortality of only 13%, indicating that disease control rather than treatment toxicity was the primary determinant of outcome.¹ Notably, patients aged greater than 75 years achieved survival outcomes comparable to those aged 70–75 years, suggesting that chronological age alone is not a contraindication to CAR-T cell therapy in MCL. In multivariable analysis, baseline [[ecog-performance-status|ECOG performance status]] ≥2 emerged as the strongest independent predictor of inferior overall survival (hazard ratio 4.50) and progression-free survival (hazard ratio 3.10), whereas age at infusion was not independently associated with outcomes—underscoring the prognostic importance of functional status rather than age in this population.¹

Brexu-cel is accompanied by substantial immune-mediated toxicity reflecting its potent cytotoxic mechanism. At 30 days post-infusion, the cumulative incidence of any-grade [[cytokine-release-syndrome|cytokine release syndrome (CRS)]] was 80%, with grade ≥3 severity in 9% of patients.¹ Immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) occurred in 57% at any grade, with grade ≥3 toxicity in 22%.¹ These toxicities require careful patient selection, institutional expertise in CAR-T toxicity management, and involvement of intensive care capabilities, but are manageable in appropriately selected elderly patients.

---

## First-Line Treatment and ASCT Integration

For younger patients aged 18–65 years with newly diagnosed MCL, the TRIANGLE trial established that adding [[ibrutinib]] to standard first-line immunochemotherapy significantly improves failure-free survival compared to chemotherapy alone.² The integration of [[autologous-stem-cell-transplantation|autologous stem-cell transplantation (ASCT)]] with ibrutinib-containing induction regimens further enhanced both failure-free and overall survival outcomes, as demonstrated in the 4.5-year follow-up analysis of the TRIANGLE trial.² This combined approach leverages the anti-BTK activity of ibrutinib with the cytoreductive intensity of ASCT, delivering durable remission in fit younger patients and substantially extending survival horizons compared to historical controls.
