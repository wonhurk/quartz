**Cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2)** is a pro-inflammatory mediator expressed in reactive astrocytes, whose upregulation is a critical driver of secondary neurodegeneration following spinal cord injury. Expression of COX-2 is markedly elevated in TNF-α-stimulated astrocytes and in vivo SCI models, exacerbating neuroinflammation through activation of downstream [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] and [[mapk|MAPK]] signaling pathways.¹ This expression can be suppressed by [[indole-3-propionic-acid|indole-3-propionic acid (IPA)]], a gut microbiota-derived tryptophan metabolite that acts through [[ahr|aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR)]] activation, resulting in reduced glial scar formation, enhanced neuronal survival, and improved long-term motor function recovery in experimental SCI models.

---

## Mechanism and expression in spinal cord injury

COX-2 is not constitutively expressed in the normal spinal cord but becomes significantly upregulated within reactive astrocytes following traumatic SCI. This expression is induced by TNF-α, a key pro-inflammatory cytokine released during the acute inflammatory phase of secondary injury. The elevation of COX-2 in astrocytes drives the synthesis of prostaglandin E₂ and other pro-inflammatory eicosanoids, perpetuating a self-amplifying cycle of neuroinflammation that compromises neuronal survival and impedes functional recovery.¹ COX-2-mediated inflammatory signaling occurs through classical transcription factor activation, particularly the [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] and [[mapk|MAPK]] pathways, which amplify the production of additional pro-inflammatory mediators including IL-6, IL-1β, iNOS, and chemokines such as CCL2 and CXCL2.

---

## Therapeutic targeting via aryl hydrocarbon receptor activation

[[Indole-3-propionic-acid|Indole-3-propionic acid (IPA)]] effectively suppresses COX-2 expression in both in vitro TNF-α-stimulated astrocyte cultures and in vivo mouse SCI models by activating the [[ahr|aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR)]], a ligand-activated transcription factor with pleiotropic anti-inflammatory properties. AhR activation by IPA leads to inhibition of the [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] and [[mapk|MAPK]] signaling cascades, preventing nuclear translocation of pro-inflammatory transcription factors and suppressing the expression of COX-2 alongside other astrocytic inflammatory mediators.¹ The functional consequences of this COX-2 suppression are substantial: IPA-treated SCI mice demonstrate markedly reduced lesion volume and glial scar formation, preserved neuronal tissue integrity, enhanced motor neuron survival, and significantly improved long-term functional recovery as measured by increased Basso Mouse Scale (BMS) scores and restored gait coordination.¹ This demonstrates that COX-2 suppression through the AhR/[[nf-kb|NF-κB]]/[[mapk|MAPK]] axis represents a viable therapeutic strategy for mitigating the secondary neurodegeneration cascade in traumatic SCI.
