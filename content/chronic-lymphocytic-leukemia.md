**Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia/Small Lymphocytic Lymphoma (CLL/SLL)** is a CD5+ CD19+ B-cell malignancy characterized by progressive accumulation of mature lymphocytes in blood, bone marrow, lymph nodes, and spleen. The treatment paradigm has undergone profound transformation with the introduction of [[bruton-tyrosine-kinase-inhibitors|Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK) inhibitors]]—including [[zanubrutinib]], [[acalabrutinib]], and [[ibrutinib]]—which have become the standard first-line approach, displacing traditional chemoimmunotherapy regimens.¹ The disease is further complicated by its propensity to undergo [[richter-transformation|Richter transformation]] to aggressive [[diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma|diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL)]], and by the emergence of BTK inhibitor-resistant clones that persist despite therapeutic intervention, preventing the achievement of complete remissions and undetectable minimal residual disease.²

---

## Clinical Presentation and Prognostic Factors

CLL/SLL arises from malignant transformation of CD5+ CD19+ B lymphocytes, which progressively accumulate in the peripheral blood, bone marrow, and lymphoid tissues. The disease is driven by aberrant B-cell receptor signaling and constitutive activation of [[bruton-tyrosine-kinase|Bruton tyrosine kinase (BTK)]], a critical hub in lymphocyte survival pathways. Stratification of patients according to genetic markers—particularly [[tp53]] mutations and deletion of chromosome 17p (del(17p))—defines disease prognosis and guides treatment selection in newly diagnosed disease.¹ These high-risk features necessitate more intensive therapeutic interventions than chemotherapy alone, informing whether patients should receive BTK inhibitor monotherapy or combination strategies.

## BTK Inhibitor Therapy and Standard of Care

The advent of BTK inhibitors has revolutionized CLL/SLL management by selectively targeting the malignant B-cell survival signal while preserving normal immune function. In treatment-naive patients without del(17p), [[zanubrutinib]] monotherapy demonstrated superior progression-free survival compared with bendamustine-rituximab, achieving an investigator-assessed 3-year PFS of 84.3%.¹ Both [[acalabrutinib]] and [[ibrutinib]] have similarly demonstrated efficacy as first-line agents, substantially improving disease control and tolerability compared to conventional chemotherapy-based regimens.¹ These agents now represent the preferred standard of care across diverse CLL/SLL patient populations, fundamentally altering the therapeutic landscape of this historically chemotherapy-dependent disease.

## Combination Therapy Approaches

While BTK inhibitor monotherapy provides substantial benefit, combination regimens integrating [[venetoclax]], a selective BCL-2 inhibitor that promotes apoptosis of malignant cells, achieve deeper disease suppression. [[Acalabrutinib]] combined with [[venetoclax]], with or without the anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody [[obinutuzumab]], achieved a 3-year PFS of 78.9% in treatment-naive patients without del(17p) or [[tp53]] mutations.¹ These fixed-duration combination approaches represent the contemporary standard for eligible patients and offer durable suppression of disease burden through synergistic targeting of both BTK-driven survival signals and apoptotic resistance mechanisms, extending progression-free intervals substantially beyond earlier chemoimmunotherapy regimens.

## Richter Transformation and Salvage Therapy

A significant and often devastating clinical complication of CLL/SLL is [[richter-transformation|Richter transformation]], an aggressive clonal evolution to [[diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma|diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL)]] that develops in a proportion of patients during disease progression.³ This transformed lymphoma is characteristically chemorefractory and yields poor outcomes with conventional chemoimmunotherapy approaches. [[Mosunetuzumab]], a bispecific CD20/CD3 T-cell-engaging antibody that bridges malignant B cells to endogenous cytotoxic T lymphocytes, has emerged as an effective salvage option.³ In a cohort of 20 patients with relapsed/refractory Richter transformation, mosunetuzumab achieved an overall response rate of 40% with a complete response rate of 20%, with complete responses proving durable—some lasting more than 20 months without further therapy—and enabling progression to allogeneic stem cell transplantation in selected patients.³ The safety profile was favorable, with cytokine release syndrome occurring in 65% of patients predominantly at grade 1 or 2 severity during initial treatment cycles and no adverse events necessitating treatment discontinuation.

## Resistance and Minimal Residual Disease

Despite the clinical efficacy of [[ibrutinib]] and other BTK inhibitors, monotherapy fails to induce complete remissions or achieve undetectable minimal residual disease (MRD) even with extended therapy.² This limitation represents a fundamental clinical challenge, as persistent residual disease may serve as a reservoir for subsequent relapse and evolution of treatment-resistant clones. Recent investigations employing single-cell RNA sequencing have begun characterizing the phenotypic heterogeneity within individual patients, revealing distinct populations of [[ibrutinib]]-sensitive and -resistant CLL cells with differing transcriptional profiles.² Concurrently, the tumor microenvironment is characterized by immunosuppressive T-cell populations that likely contribute to immune evasion and therapeutic resistance.² These observations suggest that future therapeutic advances will require strategies addressing both the intrinsic biology of the malignant clone and the pathologic immune context to achieve deeper disease control and MRD negativity, potentially combining BTK inhibitors with agents targeting residual disease mechanisms.
