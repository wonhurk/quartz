**Interleukin-8 (IL-8)**, also designated CXCL8, is a potent pro-inflammatory chemokine central to both innate immune responses and tumor immunosuppression. Its expression is driven upstream by [[kras]] mutations through [[mapk]] signaling cascades, particularly in [[pancreatic-cancer|pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)]].¹ The canonical mechanism of IL-8 action involves binding to its cognate receptor [[cxcr2|CXCR2]], which orchestrates recruitment of [[tumor-associated-neutrophils|tumor-associated neutrophils (TANs)]] and promotes formation of neutrophil extracellular traps ([[nets|NETs]]), thereby creating an immunosuppressive microenvironment resistant to checkpoint immunotherapy. Beyond cancer, IL-8 sustains chronic inflammation in bacterial infections, exemplified by persistent IL-8 secretion from lung epithelial cells during chronic [[pseudomonas-aeruginosa|Pseudomonas aeruginosa]] colonization in [[cystic-fibrosis|cystic fibrosis (CF)]].

---

## Mechanism

IL-8 expression in cancer cells is tightly coupled to [[kras]]-driven MAPK signaling. In [[pancreatic-cancer|PDAC]], oncogenic KRAS mutations constitutively activate downstream MAPK effectors, leading to sustained IL-8 transcription and secretion.¹ Once secreted, IL-8 acts as a direct ligand for [[cxcr2|CXCR2]], a seven-transmembrane G-protein-coupled receptor expressed on neutrophils and other myeloid cells. The IL-8/CXCR2 interaction triggers intracellular signaling cascades that promote chemotaxis, cellular activation, and the release of extracellular trap material, amplifying local inflammation. In the tumor microenvironment, this axis coordinates the recruitment of [[tumor-associated-neutrophils|TANs]] that express immunosuppressive molecules and impair [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cell]] function, thereby limiting anti-tumor immunity.

## KRAS-driven pancreatic cancer

IL-8 has emerged as a central mediator of therapeutic resistance in [[pancreatic-cancer|PDAC]], where [[kras]] mutations are near-ubiquitous. Tumors with mutant KRAS (KrasWT) show markedly elevated IL-8 expression compared to wild-type controls, and this elevation correlates with recruitment of [[tumor-associated-neutrophils|TANs]] via [[cxcr2|CXCR2]] signaling.¹ The combination of anti-[[pd-1]] antibodies and [[mek]] inhibitors drives initial anti-tumor responses in KrasWT tumors by expanding memory [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cells]] and enhancing interferon-gamma production.¹ However, in larger, more hypoxic tumors, NETosis becomes hyperactive—a phenomenon driven by the IL-8/[[cxcr2|CXCR2]] axis—and TANs migrate to necrotic tumor areas, creating isolated immunosuppressive niches that exclude CD8+ T cells and limit therapeutic efficacy.¹

---

## Immunotherapy resistance and CXCR2 blockade

The IL-8/[[cxcr2|CXCR2]] signaling axis is a critical driver of resistance to both checkpoint inhibitors and MAPK-targeted therapies in [[pancreatic-cancer|PDAC]]. Single-agent anti-[[pd-1]] antibodies are ineffective due to IL-8-mediated immunosuppression in the tumor microenvironment, a problem compounded by [[mek]] inhibitor resistance when hypoxia-induced NETosis dominates.¹ Blockade of [[cxcr2|CXCR2]] with anti-[[cxcr2]] antibodies overcomes this resistance by preventing TAN infiltration into hypoxic and necrotic tumor compartments, allowing CD8+ T cells to re-establish control.¹ A molecular signature based on NETosis-MAPK signaling interactions independently predicts poor survival in human [[pancreatic-cancer|PDAC]] patients, highlighting the clinical relevance of this axis. Additionally, in diabetes-associated [[pancreatic-cancer|PDAC]], metabolic reprogramming via SREBP2-dependent cholesterol biosynthesis exacerbates NETosis through the same CXCL1/[[cxcr2|CXCR2]] pathway (which shares functional similarity with IL-8/[[cxcr2|CXCR2]]), further establishing the [[cxcr2|CXCR2]]-neutrophil-NET triad as a therapeutic vulnerability.²

## Chronic bacterial infection and cystic fibrosis

Beyond cancer, IL-8 plays a critical role in sustaining the inflammatory pathology of chronic [[pseudomonas-aeruginosa|Pseudomonas aeruginosa]] infection in [[cystic-fibrosis|cystic fibrosis]] lung disease. During long-term colonization, [[pseudomonas-aeruginosa|P. aeruginosa]] isolates downregulate canonical virulence factors—including toxins and exoproteases—and adopt a biofilm-associated, mucoid phenotype as an adaptation strategy. Despite this apparent immune evasion, excessive airway inflammation persists, a paradox resolved by the discovery of novel bacterial immune mediators. Comparative proteomic analysis of clinical [[pseudomonas-aeruginosa|P. aeruginosa]] CF isolates revealed that a subset of strains induce robust IL-8 secretion from 3D lung epithelial cell cultures despite minimal production of classically recognized virulence factors.³ Notably, [[dksa|DksA]], a bacterial transcription factor previously uncharacterized as an immunomodulatory mediator, was confirmed to drive IL-8 induction in this system. The sustained IL-8 secretion perpetuates airway inflammation independently of traditional virulence-based immunity, maintaining recruitment of neutrophils and prolonging tissue damage even as the organism reduces its classical pathogenic strategies. This mechanism explains the clinical observation that older [[cystic-fibrosis|CF]] patients with chronic [[pseudomonas-aeruginosa|P. aeruginosa]] colonization experience progressive lung decline despite minimal changes in bacterial load.
