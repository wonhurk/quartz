**Dexamethasone** is a synthetic glucocorticoid with potent anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory properties, functioning through glucocorticoid receptor-mediated suppression of pro-inflammatory gene expression and immune cell activation. As a core component of the KRd-b regimen combining belantamab mafodotin, [[carfilzomib]], [[lenalidomide]], and dexamethasone, it achieves exceptional clinical outcomes in relapsed or refractory [[multiple-myeloma|multiple myeloma (RRMM)]], with an 89.5% overall response rate and a durable 24-month progression-free survival of 74.3%.¹ Beyond malignancy, dexamethasone is being explored for [[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis]] through targeted nanoparticle delivery systems that modulate ferroptosis-related pathways including [[acsl4|ACSL4]], [[mtdh|MTDH]], and [[nfkb|NF-κB]], underscoring its therapeutic versatility across diverse disease contexts.

---

## Mechanism

Dexamethasone exerts its immunomodulatory effects through high-affinity binding to the glucocorticoid receptor, which translocates to the nucleus upon ligand binding and suppresses transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokine genes. The drug potently inhibits [[nfkb|NF-κB]] signaling, a master regulator of inflammatory gene expression, and reduces immune cell activation and proliferation. In the context of multiple myeloma, these anti-inflammatory properties synergize with proteasome inhibitors and immunomodulatory drugs to overcome intrinsic and acquired therapeutic resistance. Additionally, in inflammatory contexts such as ulcerative colitis, dexamethasone can suppress ferroptosis — an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death — by inhibiting the [[acsl4|ACSL4]]-mediated lipid peroxidation pathway, thereby protecting epithelial cells from oxidative damage.

## Multiple myeloma applications

Dexamethasone plays a critical role in the KRd-b regimen, a contemporary combination engineered to overcome resistance in relapsed or refractory disease. A phase 1/2 trial evaluated this four-drug combination in 19 patients with RRMM who had received at least one prior therapy line.¹ The regimen combined belantamab mafodotin (an anti-BCMA antibody-drug conjugate), [[carfilzomib]] (a next-generation proteasome inhibitor), [[lenalidomide]] (an immunomodulatory drug), and dexamethasone. The combination achieved an overall response rate of 89.5%, with 78.9% of patients achieving very good partial response or better; notably, minimal residual disease negativity was accomplished in all complete responders. At a median follow-up of 19.3 months, the 24-month progression-free survival rate was 74.3% and overall survival was 85.1%, demonstrating durable disease control and extended progression-free intervals.¹

Dexamethasone-containing regimens maintain manageable tolerability despite their potency. Keratopathy, an ocular toxicity associated with belantamab mafodotin, occurred in 94.7% of patients but was predominantly grade 1 to 2 in severity and reversible, with no patients requiring permanent discontinuation.¹ This favorable safety profile, combined with the deep and sustained responses observed, establishes the KRd-b regimen as a viable option for patients with RRMM seeking alternatives to conventional triple-drug combinations.

## Inflammatory bowel disease exploration

Emerging evidence demonstrates that dexamethasone suppresses ferroptosis in [[ulcerative-colitis|ulcerative colitis]] through inhibition of the [[mtdh|MTDH]]/[[acsl4|ACSL4]]/[[nfkb|NF-κB]] pathway. [[acsl4|ACSL4]], an acyl-CoA synthetase that activates long-chain polyunsaturated fatty acids for incorporation into phospholipids, is a critical node in ferroptosis regulation; dexamethasone-mediated suppression of ACSL4 reduces lipid peroxidation and protects intestinal epithelial cells from ferroptotic death. Concurrently, inhibition of the [[nfkb|NF-κB]] pathway by dexamethasone reduces production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, addressing both the oxidative stress and innate immune hyperactivation characteristic of IBD. However, the clinical utility of systemic dexamethasone in ulcerative colitis is constrained by poor colonic bioavailability and glucocorticoid-related systemic adverse effects, necessitating innovations in drug delivery.

---

## Targeted nanoparticle delivery

Hollow mesoporous silica nanoparticles (HMSN) have been engineered to encapsulate dexamethasone (HMSN@DEX) for enhanced colonic tissue penetration in inflammatory bowel disease. When combined with ultrasound-targeted microbubble destruction (UTMD) — a technique employing acoustic pressure to reversibly disrupt the intestinal epithelial barrier and release nanoparticle cargo at inflammatory sites — the HMSN@DEX system achieves synergistic anti-inflammatory effects.² This dual-targeting approach maximizes dexamethasone concentration at diseased tissue while minimizing systemic glucocorticoid exposure, thereby reducing off-target toxicity. The UTMD-assisted HMSN@DEX delivery inhibits ferroptosis through coordinated suppression of the [[mtdh|MTDH]]/[[acsl4|ACSL4]]/[[nfkb|NF-κB]] axis, demonstrating that rational nanoparticle engineering can expand the therapeutic applications of this classical glucocorticoid and provide patients with IBD targeted alternatives to conventional systemic immunosuppression.²
