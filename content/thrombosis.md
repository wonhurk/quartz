**Thrombosis** is the pathological formation of blood clots within blood vessels, representing a leading cause of stroke, myocardial infarction, and other cardiovascular and cerebrovascular complications. The condition arises from a complex interplay of hemodynamic forces, impaired fibrinolysis, and inherited or acquired prothrombotic states. [[pai-1|Plasminogen activator inhibitor 1 (PAI-1)]], the primary physiological inhibitor of fibrinolysis, serves as the central molecular brake preventing clot dissolution—and its dysregulation is fundamentally tied to thrombotic disease pathogenesis, age-related thrombotic risk, and multiple systemic diseases including diabetes, cancer, and neurodegenerative illness.¹

---

## Mechanism

Thrombosis results when blood coagulation overwhelms the fibrinolytic system, which normally dissolves clots via tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) and urokinase plasminogen activator (uPA). [[pai-1|PAI-1]] inhibits both plasminogen activators, thereby suppressing fibrinolysis and promoting blood clot stabilization.¹ Circulating PAI-1 levels increase progressively with age, explaining the steep rise in thrombotic risk observed in older populations and the enhanced thrombotic susceptibility associated with age-related diseases.¹ PAI-1 excess contributes not only to thrombotic disease but also to diet-induced obesity, diabetes mellitus, and neurodegenerative pathology including [[alzheimers-disease|Alzheimer disease]].¹ Conversely, partial PAI-1 deficiency confers cardioprotection and longevity, establishing PAI-1 as both a disease driver and an actionable therapeutic target.

## Hemodynamic determinants

Thrombosis within complicated atherosclerotic plaques is strongly influenced by local hemodynamic forces. [[wall-shear-stress|Wall shear stress (WSS)]] and [[oscillatory-shear-index|oscillatory shear index (OSI)]]—measures of fluid mechanical stress on endothelial surfaces—are independently associated with the development of [[complicated-carotid-artery-plaques|complicated carotid artery plaques]] characterized by intra-plaque hemorrhage, thin fibrous cap, and superimposed thrombi.² These hemodynamic alterations impair endothelial integrity and predispose to thrombotic occlusion, particularly in the carotid circulation where high stroke risk accompanies plaque rupture and thrombus formation.² Individual variation in carotid artery geometry creates localized zones of disturbed flow, establishing microenvironments permissive for both plaque destabilization and thrombosis.

## Antiphospholipid syndrome

[[antiphospholipid-syndrome|Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS)]] is a major acquired cause of thrombosis, characterized by the presence of persistently positive antiphospholipid antibodies (aPL) including [[lupus-anticoagulant|lupus anticoagulant]], anti-cardiolipin, and anti-β₂-glycoprotein I antibodies.³ These autoantibodies activate endothelial cells and promote a prothrombotic state, leading to both venous and arterial thrombosis in approximately 50% of APS patients. Despite standard anticoagulation with vitamin K antagonists, thrombotic APS remains poorly controlled: recurrent thrombosis occurred in 34–40% of patients over a decade, with rates comparable across patients with single-, dual-, and triple-positive aPL antibody profiles.³ Notably, the presence of [[lupus-anticoagulant|lupus anticoagulant]] conferred significantly higher recurrence risk compared to other aPL specificities, highlighting antibody heterogeneity in clinical outcomes.³ The high recurrence rate despite conventional anticoagulation underscores the need for intensified therapeutic strategies and emerging molecular interventions.

## PAI-1 silencing as therapeutic approach

Recent advances in RNA therapeutics have opened new avenues for reducing thrombotic burden by directly targeting [[pai-1|PAI-1]]. Small interfering RNA (siRNA) delivered via lipid nanoparticles (siPAI-1) achieves sustained, robust knockdown of circulating PAI-1 without overt toxicity. A single dose of siPAI-1 produced 90% plasma PAI-1 knockdown lasting ten days, with efficacy maintained across both young and aged mice.¹ In preclinical models of thrombosis—specifically inferior vena cava ligation—siPAI-1 treatment substantially reduced thrombus weight and, critically, extended survival in aged mice four days post-ligation.¹ In diet-induced obese mice, which exhibit supraphysiologic PAI-1 elevation exceeding tenfold that of healthy controls, siPAI-1 achieved 70% circulating PAI-1 knockdown and normalized pathological levels in aged animals.¹ This molecular therapeutic approach offers promise for translating PAI-1 suppression into clinical benefit across age-related thrombotic disease, obesity-associated prothrombotic states, and age-related comorbidities.
