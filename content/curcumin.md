**Curcumin** is a polyphenolic natural product with potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antibacterial properties that has been extensively leveraged in advanced therapeutic delivery systems to overcome its inherent bioavailability limitations. The compound exhibits robust reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavenging capacity and direct microbicidal activity against pathogenic bacteria, making it therapeutically valuable across multiple disease contexts.¹² However, its hydrophobic nature and poor systemic absorption have historically limited clinical efficacy. Recent innovations in polysaccharide-based encapsulation and transdermal delivery have transformed curcumin into a highly targeted, sustained-release therapeutic agent for chronic inflammatory and infectious diseases.

---

## Mechanism

Curcumin exerts its therapeutic effects through multiple, complementary molecular pathways. Its antioxidant activity derives from direct ROS scavenging, neutralizing free radicals generated during inflammatory responses and oxidative stress.¹ The compound simultaneously suppresses pro-inflammatory signaling cascades by modulating key transcription factors and cytokine production, providing dual anti-inflammatory action at both the molecular and cellular levels.

Against pathogenic bacteria, curcumin demonstrates potent antibacterial activity through membrane disruption and inhibition of bacterial virulence factors. When evaluated against [[propionibacterium-acnes]], the primary causative agent of acne vulgaris, free curcumin shows moderate efficacy; however, its incorporation into advanced delivery platforms dramatically amplifies antimicrobial potency.² The compound's multiple therapeutic mechanisms — antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial — make it particularly effective for conditions driven by infectious and inflammatory processes operating simultaneously.

## Ulcerative colitis treatment

The therapeutic application of curcumin in [[ulcerative-colitis]] has been revolutionized through dual-crosslinked polysaccharide microsphere technology. A protein-polysaccharide composite system was engineered using [[andrias-davidianus-collagen]] (ADC) and its xylose-glycated derivative (ADCXy) as the primary curcumin-loading matrix, which was then crosslinked with [[sodium-alginate]] and chelated with calcium ions to form a stable primary hydrogel network.¹ The system was further functionalized with [[chitosan]], a cationic polysaccharide conferring colon-specific enzymatic degradation, mucosal adhesion, and pH-responsive controlled release characteristics. This dual-crosslinked architecture (ADCXy-Cur-SA-CS) exhibited enhanced cellular antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity compared to non-glycated variants, maintained high stability in simulated gastric conditions to protect the cargo during upper gastrointestinal transit, and enabled controlled curcumin release specifically in the colonic pH environment where it is most needed.¹ In vivo studies demonstrated that this formulation alleviated ulcerative colitis through a multi-mechanism strategy encompassing direct antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects, restoration of the colon mucosal barrier, and beneficial modulation of the gut microbiota ecosystem.

## Acne treatment and photothermal synergy

Curcumin-loaded transdermal delivery systems have emerged as highly effective platforms for treating [[acne-vulgaris]], particularly when combined with photothermal therapy. A polysaccharide-based soluble microneedle system was developed incorporating curcumin-loaded mesoporous [[polydopamine]] nanoparticles (Cur-MPDA) into a backing matrix composed of [[hyaluronic-acid]] and [[fucoidan]] (FPS).² The resulting multifunctional microneedle (Cur-MPDA + Cur@HA/FPS MNs) exhibited rapid tip dissolution upon insertion (within 2 minutes), demonstrating excellent mechanical strength and biocompatibility. When evaluated against [[propionibacterium-acnes]], the system achieved 70.9% antibacterial activity in vitro.² Remarkably, when combined with near-infrared (NIR) photothermal irradiation, the same microneedle system achieved 99.4% bacterial killing efficiency and restored skin thickness to near-normal levels, indicating both resolution of acne inflammation and restoration of skin barrier integrity.² The photothermal enhancement is attributed to the light-absorbing properties of [[polydopamine]], which generates localized heat that synergistically amplifies curcumin's antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory effects while simultaneously improving its release kinetics from the microneedle matrix.

## Polysaccharide delivery innovations

The success of advanced curcumin therapeutics hinges on polysaccharide-based encapsulation and targeting strategies that address the compound's inherent limitations in bioavailability and specificity. Dual-crosslinked microspheres employing [[sodium-alginate]], [[chitosan]], and collagen provide pH-responsive, sustained-release platforms with tunable release kinetics for gastrointestinal applications.¹ Transdermal microneedle systems utilize [[hyaluronic-acid]] and [[fucoidan]] as soluble backing matrices, enabling rapid transdermal penetration while maintaining mechanical integrity during insertion.² The integration of mesoporous [[polydopamine]] nanoparticles further enhances therapeutic efficacy by providing photothermal functionality and improving curcumin loading capacity. These polysaccharide carriers not only stabilize curcumin during storage and transit but also enable organ-specific targeting — colonic delivery for inflammatory bowel disease, transdermal for localized skin infections — transforming a historically problematic natural product into a clinically viable precision therapeutic.
