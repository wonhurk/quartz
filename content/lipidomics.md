**Lipidomics** is the comprehensive analysis of cellular and tissue lipid composition and metabolism, enabling quantitative characterization of thousands of diverse lipid species and their bioactive metabolites.¹ Modern lipidomics platforms leverage ion mobility mass spectrometry with direct infusion methodology, eliminating liquid chromatography and enabling quantification of over 1300 lipid species from a single sample in less than 5 minutes.¹ This technological advance permits seamless integration with complementary [[proteomics]] and [[metabolomics]] analyses from a single injection, dramatically reducing analysis time and costs while enabling large-scale studies of lipid metabolism across diverse human populations and disease contexts.

---

## Platform and technology

Lipidomics has undergone rapid technological advancement through adoption of ion mobility mass spectrometry combined with direct infusion mass spectrometry. The single-injection multi-omics analysis by direct infusion (SMAD) platform represents a major breakthrough, enabling simultaneous quantification of over 9000 metabolite m/z features, more than 1300 proteins, and comprehensive lipid species from the same sample in under 5 minutes without liquid chromatography separation.¹ The platform integrates self-developed software tools with high-resolution ion mobility detection, permitting rapid yet comprehensive characterization of the lipidome alongside the proteome and metabolome. This approach has been validated across diverse biological systems—cultured mammalian cells, tissue samples, and high-throughput screening formats including 96-well plates—demonstrating its utility in large-scale functional genomics pipelines.¹

---

## Drug discovery and cellular screening

The efficiency and speed of modern lipidomics platforms have transformed their application in drug discovery and functional screening. High-throughput lipidomic profiling conducted in mammalian cells within 96-well plate formats enables rapid assessment of drug effects on lipid metabolism alongside concurrent changes in proteome and metabolome composition from a single injection.¹ This multi-omics readout facilitates discovery of mechanistic relationships between proteomic alterations and metabolomic shifts during drug treatment or transitions in cellular state. Applications have included characterization of lipid metabolism remodeling during macrophage polarization and cellular senescence, where changes in lipid composition directly correlate with shifts in protein abundance and metabolite pools in response to biological stimuli.¹

## Population diversity and geographic variation

Large-scale lipidomic studies have uncovered profound ethnicity- and geography-associated variations in lipid metabolism across human populations with significant implications for metabolic health and disease susceptibility. Comprehensive multiomics profiling of 322 healthy individuals spanning European, East Asian, and South Asian ancestry across multiple continents identified ethnicity-associated molecular features directly linked to host metabolism, autoimmune disease risk, and drug metabolism pathways.² Specific genetic variants and gene expression differences showed strong association with lipid metabolism and immune regulation, with distinct patterns emerging across ancestry groups.² Geography itself profoundly influences lipid-mediated metabolic aging: East Asians demonstrated lower biological age in their ancestral regions, whereas individuals of European ancestry exhibited lower biological age in North America than in Europe.² These findings establish lipidomics as a critical tool for understanding population-level differences in lipid metabolism as a fundamental determinant of metabolic health and biological aging across diverse geographic and ancestral contexts.

## Lipid mediators in disease

Lipidomics has revealed the critical role of [[arachidonic acid]] (AA) metabolism as a driver of pathologic inflammation in cancer and other diseases. [[Arachidonic acid]], a polyunsaturated fatty acid abundant in cell membranes, is liberated from membrane phospholipids by [[phospholipase-a2]] (PLA2) and subsequently metabolized into bioactive [[eicosanoids]] that regulate vascular tone, systemic inflammation, and immune cell activation.³ With lipidomics advances, AA metabolism has emerged as a multifaceted determinant of tumor microenvironment remodeling in [[lung-adenocarcinoma]] and other malignancies.³ Lipidomic characterization of AA-related biomarkers through spatial and single-cell transcriptomics has identified eicosanoid signaling cascades as key drivers of tumor-associated inflammation and immune evasion, establishing lipid metabolism as an emerging therapeutic axis in lung adenocarcinoma and conditions where inflammation and immune dysregulation play central pathogenic roles.³
