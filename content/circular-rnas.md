**Circular RNAs (circRNAs)** are closed-loop RNA structures generated through backsplicing of pre-mRNA that encode functional protein products with biological activities mechanistically distinct from their parental genes.¹ These molecules represent previously unexplored genetic elements whose protein-encoding capacity is orchestrated by [[rpb1|RNA polymerase II subunit A (RPB1)]], the master regulator of circRNA biogenesis. Unlike linear mRNA, circRNAs form covalently closed circles through a regulated backsplicing process, conferring enhanced stability and enabling discovery of novel protein isoforms with therapeutic potential across oncology and infectious disease prevention.

---

## Mechanism

CircRNA generation proceeds through backsplicing, in which the 3′ splice site of one exon ligates to the 5′ splice site of an upstream exon, creating a closed-loop structure that circumvents linear mRNA processing pathways.¹ [[rpb1|RPB1]], the largest subunit of RNA polymerase II, is critical for orchestrating the transcriptional conditions necessary for circRNA-encoded protein translation. These closed-loop structures are translated through mechanisms distinct from canonical linear mRNA, yielding protein products that often diverge functionally and structurally from their parental gene counterparts. The discovery of circRNA-encoded proteins has been enabled by integrated multi-omics approaches applied to human tissue specimens, revealing a previously uncharacterized stratum of proteome complexity in both normal physiology and disease states.

---

## Cancer applications

Circularly-encoded proteins have emerged as potent drivers of malignant progression in [[lung-adenocarcinoma]]. The protein RIPK1-98, generated from backsplicing of circRIPK1, was identified as functionally distinct from its parental gene product [[ripk1|receptor-interacting serine/threonine kinase 1 (RIPK1)]].¹ RIPK1-98 modulates [[cdk2|cyclin-dependent kinase 2 (CDK2)]]-dependent cell-cycle regulation, facilitating tumor proliferation across cellular and animal models of lung cancer. This circRNA-encoded protein correlates with distinct pathological stages of lung adenocarcinoma in patient tissues, establishing it as a disease biomarker and prognostic indicator of cell-cycle progression.

The therapeutic targeting of RIPK1-98 represents a promising strategy to overcome resistance to front-line treatments such as osimertinib, which commonly emerges through alternative signaling pathway activation in advanced disease. Notably, since RIPK1-98 is not generated from the canonical RIPK1 locus, selective inhibition can be achieved through targeting the unique structural epitopes of the circRNA-derived protein, potentially minimizing off-target toxicity to the parental kinase and enabling a precision therapeutic approach.

## Vaccine applications

Beyond oncology, circRNAs are being developed as innovative vaccine platforms to enhance immunogenicity and overcome limitations inherent to conventional nucleic acid vaccine modalities. When compared systematically with nucleoside-modified mRNA and self-amplifying RNA (saRNA), circRNA vaccines demonstrate distinct immunological properties and overcome strain-specific immunogenicity constraints of traditional mRNA platforms.² Seasonal [[influenza]] vaccination has emerged as a focal application domain. Conventional mRNA vaccines targeting the influenza B virus have demonstrated suboptimal efficacy in clinical trials, a limitation attributed to structural features of the influenza B hemagglutinin antigen that reduce immunogenic presentation on standard mRNA scaffolds.

CircRNA vaccine candidates administered at low doses (0.1 μg) achieved complete protection against homologous viral challenge in preclinical models, surpassing the immunogenicity of conventional quadrivalent inactivated vaccines (QIV, 2 μg) and demonstrating superior efficacy to nucleoside-modified mRNA at equivalent doses.² Furthermore, circRNA-based platforms enabled substantial dose-sparing benefits while maintaining durable antibody responses over extended follow-up periods spanning weeks. The ability to induce robust humoral immunity across multiple influenza subtypes—particularly overcoming the documented immunogenicity deficiency of conventional mRNA platforms against influenza B virus—positions circRNA vaccines as a transformative approach to seasonal influenza control and potentially other respiratory viral threats.
