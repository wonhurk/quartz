**Acute pancreatitis (AP)** is a major gastrointestinal emergency characterized by sudden-onset pancreatic inflammation and a significant cause of hospital admission, with an overall mortality rate of approximately 3%. Biliary obstruction represents the leading etiology, accounting for 30–55% of cases, particularly in elderly and frail patients who face elevated risk of recurrence and related complications.¹ Currently, no specific pharmacological treatments exist for acute pancreatitis, representing a substantial therapeutic gap. Emerging approaches target soluble epoxide hydrolase ([[seh|sEH]]) to redirect the inflammatory response, with derivatives from the Mediterranean herb [[myrtus-communis|Myrtus communis]] showing promise in preclinical models.

---

## Epidemiology and etiology

Acute pancreatitis ranks among the most common causes of gastrointestinal admission across healthcare systems. The disease presents with sudden onset of abdominal pain, elevated pancreatic enzymes, and variable severity ranging from mild self-limiting inflammation to necrotizing disease with multiorgan failure. Biliary stone obstruction of the pancreatic duct predominates as the leading etiology, particularly in elderly populations and those with cholecystolithiasis. Patients with acute biliary pancreatitis who are elderly or considered frail frequently remain unfit for definitive surgical management via [[cholecystectomy]], leaving them at persistent risk of recurrent biliary events following initial recovery.¹

---

## Inflammatory mechanisms and therapeutic targeting

The pathophysiology of acute pancreatitis involves activation of pancreatic acinar cells, release of digestive enzymes, and a cascade of pro-inflammatory signaling leading to tissue injury and systemic inflammation. Soluble epoxide hydrolase ([[seh|sEH]]) catalyzes the hydrolysis of anti-inflammatory epoxyeicosatrienoic acids (EETs) into inactive dihydroxyeicosatrienoic acids (DHETs), thereby suppressing endogenous anti-inflammatory signaling pathways. Inhibition of [[seh|sEH]] preserves EET levels while reducing DHET accumulation, simultaneously elevating anti-inflammatory eicosanoids and suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokines including [[tnf-alpha|TNF-α]] and [[il-6|IL-6]].²

---

## Novel pharmacological approaches

Myrtucommulone B, a natural compound from the Mediterranean shrub [[myrtus-communis|Myrtus communis]], was identified as a lead structure for developing [[seh|sEH]] inhibitors. Synthetic derivatives, notably the analog (+)-34, demonstrated potent inhibition of [[seh|sEH]] with low toxicity and robust anti-inflammatory and analgesic effects in vitro and in vivo.² In murine models of acute pancreatitis, (+)-34 provided significant protection from pancreatic inflammation and injury. Its mechanism involves elevating anti-inflammatory EETs while suppressing pro-inflammatory DHETs, thereby reducing systemic cytokine production and pancreatic damage. These findings establish a foundation for developing [[seh|sEH]] inhibitors derived from natural products as novel pharmacological agents for acute pancreatitis.

---

## Management of high-risk populations

For elderly and frail patients who experience acute biliary pancreatitis but are unsuitable surgical candidates for [[cholecystectomy]], standard management relies on supportive care and management of complications. Prophylactic [[endoscopic-sphincterotomy|endoscopic sphincterotomy]] is under clinical evaluation as a preventive strategy to reduce mortality and hospital readmissions for recurrent biliary events in this vulnerable population.¹ The endoscopic intervention aims to permanently reduce biliary pressure gradients, thereby preventing future obstruction of the pancreatic duct. However, the efficacy of this approach in reducing mortality and recurrent complications requires further formal clinical evaluation through randomized controlled trials.
