**Graft-versus-host disease (GVHD)** is a major immunological complication following allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation wherein donor-derived T lymphocytes recognize recipient tissues as foreign and mount a destructive immune attack. Although standard treatment with systemic immunosuppressants can suppress GVHD, this approach carries severe toxicities including opportunistic infections and tumor relapse in patients with hematologic malignancies.¹ Emerging tolerance-inducing strategies such as [[max-16h5|MAX.16H5]] anti-[[cd4]] antibody modulation and [[vla-4-agonist|VLA-4 agonists]] offer selective suppression of donor T-cell activation while preserving protective immunity and enhancing hematopoietic reconstitution.

---

## Mechanism and pathophysiology

GVHD arises from the recognition of recipient tissues as alloantigen by donor T cells generated during transplantation. [[cd4|CD4+]] and [[cd8|CD8+]] T lymphocytes in the donor graft recognize polymorphic HLA and minor histocompatibility antigens on recipient cells, triggering T-cell receptor signaling and subsequent activation, proliferation, and tissue infiltration. The severity and manifestation of GVHD depend on both acute and chronic phases, with acute GVHD typically appearing within the first 100 days post-transplantation affecting skin, gastrointestinal tract, and liver. Current standard of care relies on systemic calcineurin inhibitors and corticosteroids, which nonspecifically suppress immune function and expose patients to serious complications including severe infections and malignancy recurrence.¹

---

## Anti-CD4 antibody modulation

A breakthrough approach exploits short-term ex vivo treatment of donor hematopoietic cell grafts with the [[max-16h5|MAX.16H5 anti-CD4 antibody]], which selectively modulates T-cell properties without systemic immunosuppression. Mechanistically, MAX.16H5 engagement impairs T-cell receptor signaling and promotes a shift toward an [[il10|interleukin-10]]-dependent regulatory phenotype in [[cd4|CD4+]] and [[cd8|CD8+]] T cells.¹ This epigenetic reorientation significantly reduces the activation and proliferation capacity of donor T cells while maintaining their functional anti-pathogen and anti-leukemic activities. A single ex vivo incubation of hematopoietic transplants with MAX.16H5 prior to infusion provides durable GVHD prevention in preclinical models, achieving survival extension and reduction of GVHD manifestations comparable to repeated systemic immunosuppressant administration.¹

---

## VLA-4 agonist-mediated engraftment enhancement

Complementary to GVHD prevention, [[vla-4-agonist|VLA-4 agonists]] represent an orthogonal strategy that directly promotes hematopoietic stem cell engraftment and early immune reconstitution following allogeneic transplantation. VLA-4 (very late antigen-4) agonists activate [[erk|extracellular signal-regulated kinase 1/2]] phosphorylation, thereby enhancing hematopoietic stem cell self-renewal capacity and lymphoid progenitor differentiation.² The enhanced immune reconstitution mediated by VLA-4 agonists proceeds without exacerbating acute GVHD, with reconstituted immune cells demonstrating potent antiviral activity.² This mechanism contrasts with immunosuppressants, which delay both GVHD onset and protective immunity, by instead supporting the development of functional immune reconstitution while maintaining [[cd4|CD4+]] and [[cd8|CD8+]] populations tolerant to recipient tissues.

---

## Transplant outcomes and conditioning strategies

The success of allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation in managing [[graft-failure|graft failure]] — a life-threatening complication in which donor cells fail to establish durable hematopoietic reconstitution — depends critically on conditioning regimen intensity. In children with inborn errors of immunity requiring second allogeneic transplantation for primary graft failure, myeloablative and reduced-toxicity conditioning regimens yielded significantly improved event-free survival (74%) compared to nonmyeloablative approaches (50%).³ Five-year overall survival following second transplantation reached 78%, with event-free survival of 69%, though these outcomes were adversely affected by the use of cord blood stem cell sources, which independently predicted lower event-free survival.³ Grade 2–4 acute GVHD occurred in approximately 27% of second transplant recipients, underscoring that GVHD remains a significant risk even in salvage transplantation scenarios.
