**Breast cancer** is a clinically and molecularly heterogeneous malignancy comprising multiple subtypes—including invasive lobular carcinoma, HER2-positive, [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|triple-negative]], and estrogen receptor-positive disease—each defined by distinct oncogenic drivers and therapeutic vulnerabilities.¹⁻³ The tumor microenvironment, particularly the extracellular matrix-cancer-associated fibroblast-integrin signaling axis, orchestrates growth, invasion, and resistance to therapy through collagen remodeling, focal adhesion dynamics, and mechanical reprogramming.¹⁴ Emerging therapeutic strategies exploit vulnerabilities in matrix biology, immune checkpoints including [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] and [[cd39|CD39]], and metabolic dependencies, while next-generation biomarkers—circulating tumor DNA, glycosylated PD-L1 on extracellular vesicles, and single-cell transcriptomic signatures—enable precision risk stratification and treatment selection.³⁹¹⁰

---

## Subtypes and molecular architecture

Invasive lobular carcinoma accounts for 15% of breast cancers and is characterized by loss of E-cadherin (CDH1), which creates a dependency on collagen-integrin signaling for survival and metastatic competence.¹ Genome-wide CRISPR screens revealed that CDH1-deficient cells require ITGAV and ITGB5 integrins, which activate MYC, NF-κB, and AP-1 transcriptional programs in response to fibrillar collagen.¹ HER2-positive and [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|triple-negative breast cancer]] subtypes display distinct therapeutic responses to [[neoadjuvant-therapy|neoadjuvant therapy]], with pathologic complete response rates influenced by residual circulating tumor DNA burden.³ Estrogen receptor-positive disease, the most common subtype, frequently develops endocrine therapy resistance through upregulation of P-Rex1 and activation of Rac1-driven motility programs, enabling late recurrence and metastatic escape.¹²

---

## Tumor microenvironment and matrix remodeling

The breast cancer stroma forms a self-reinforcing barrier through reciprocal crosstalk between activated cancer-associated fibroblasts and dense extracellular matrix, impeding drug penetration and promoting therapeutic resistance.⁴ In invasive lobular carcinoma, lysyl oxidase enzymes cross-link collagen fibers, creating aligned fibrillar networks that sustain integrin-MYC signaling; inhibition of lysyl oxidase with the clinical agent [[pxs-5505|PXS-5505]] remodels collagen architecture, halts tumor expansion, and prevents metastatic seeding in patient-derived xenograft models without systemic toxicity.¹ Dual-function nanoparticles such as [[pfd-tk-cy|PFD-TK-Cy]]—which combine biochemical suppression of fibroblast activation via modified [[pirfenidone|pirfenidone]] with physical matrix softening via mild photothermia—disrupt the CAF-ECM vicious cycle, reduce solid stress, and enhance intratumoral delivery of liposomal chemotherapy in stroma-rich orthotopic models.⁴

Focal adhesion dynamics are governed by talin2-β-integrin interactions, which promote tumor cell migration and invasion. [[peptide-2|Peptide-2]], a structure-based inhibitor identified by virtual screening, exhibits nanomolar binding affinity for talin2 (Kd = 8.05 nM), suppresses FAK phosphorylation at Y397 and Y576, and demonstrates strong in vivo antitumor activity in MDA-MB-231 xenografts without significant toxicity.⁵

## Resistance mechanisms

Radiation therapy induces polyploid giant cancer cells—a therapy-resistant subpopulation exhibiting multinucleation, genetic instability, and stem-like features—that adopt a mechanically dormant yet invasive phenotype.² Nanomechanical profiling reveals that these cells undergo cortical actin remodeling and nuclear enlargement, resulting in biomechanical stiffening coupled with suppressed proliferation and sustained expression of invasion markers, representing a latent driver of recurrence.² This mechanical dormancy constitutes a mechanobiological hallmark of radiation resistance in breast cancer.

Endocrine therapy resistance in estrogen receptor-positive disease involves transcriptional reprogramming mediated by the P-Rex1/Rac1 signaling axis.¹² Single-cell RNA sequencing of slow-growing resistant cells reveals upregulation of P-Rex1, a Rac1 signaling component that drives motility and facilitates metastatic escape years after primary treatment.¹² Intravital imaging confirms active Rac1 signaling following endocrine therapy, and dual pharmacologic inhibition with [[r-ketorolac|R-ketorolac]] or NSC23766 combined with [[tamoxifen|tamoxifen]] reduces tumor burden in drug-refractory patient-derived xenograft models.¹²

High-fat diet-induced alterations in [[gut-microbiota|gut microbiota]] composition reshape microbial metabolite profiles, promoting cancer stemness through Streptococcus mutans-mediated leucine accumulation, linking dietary obesity to breast cancer progression.¹³

## Immune evasion and checkpoint targeting

Glycosylated [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] on small extracellular vesicles regulates tumor immune suppression and predicts response to immune checkpoint blockade, yet remains undetectable by conventional immunohistochemistry due to glycan-mediated epitope shielding—a critical "detection blind spot."¹⁰ Proximity-amplified biosensors incorporating dual-aptamer recognition and hybridization chain reaction achieve ultrasensitive detection (limit of detection 52 particles/mL) of glycosylated PD-L1-bearing vesicles, enabling biomarker-guided immunotherapy stratification.¹⁰

The ectonucleotidase [[cd39|CD39]] hydrolyzes extracellular ATP to generate immunosuppressive adenosine, dampening T cell activation in the tumor microenvironment.¹¹ Nucleotide-derived competitive inhibitors based on 8-butylthio-AMP, including [[psb-24379|PSB-24379]] (8-(1-naphthylthio)-N⁶-(4-phenylbutyl)-AMP; Ki = 77.4 nM), reduce ATP hydrolysis in melanoma and breast cancer cell membranes and partially reverse ATP-mediated suppression of T cell proliferation, representing high-quality tool compounds for cancer immunotherapy.¹¹

## Biomarker innovation

Circulating tumor DNA minimal residual disease assessment following [[neoadjuvant-therapy|neoadjuvant therapy]] provides superior risk stratification beyond pathologic response in HER2-positive and [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|triple-negative breast cancer]], identifying non-responders with occult systemic disease who may benefit from escalated adjuvant therapy.³ Single-cell RNA sequencing of primary tumors and [[axillary-lymph-node-metastasis|axillary lymph node metastases]] reveals metastasis-specific expansion of monocytes, pre-B cells, and CD34+ hematopoietic stem cells, alongside 2421 differentially expressed genes in epithelial compartments; Mendelian randomization prioritizes 12 genes whose genetically predicted expression associates with breast cancer risk, nominating causal mediators of lymphatic dissemination.¹⁴

Proximity-driven, spatially matched 3D DNA aptasensors overcome limitations of immunohistochemistry and fluorescence in situ hybridization for HER2 assessment.⁸ Tetrahedral framework nucleic acids functionalized with cholesterol anchor to cell membranes and present HER2-binding aptamers with tunable spatial orientation; on-membrane catalytic hairpin assembly amplifies signal beyond conventional "one-signal-per-protein" chromogenic detection, enabling precise biomarker quantification in living cells and clinical specimens.⁸

## Novel therapeutic agents

Bis-triazole-linked benzenesulfonamides selectively inhibit carbonic anhydrase IX and XII—enzymes overexpressed in hypoxic tumor regions that regulate pH homeostasis and drive therapeutic resistance.⁷ Lead compounds 2g and 4g exhibit nanomolar potency (hCA IX Ki = 25.1 and 28.1 nM; hCA XII Ki = 35.2 and 11.6 nM) with reduced off-target activity against hCA I/II, and significantly enhance antiproliferative effects of azacitidine and [[sorafenib|sorafenib]] under normoxic and hypoxic conditions in breast and pancreatic cancer models.⁷

Mannose-conjugated mesoporous silica nanocarriers resolve the efficiency-toxicity trade-off inherent in polyethylenimine-functionalized delivery systems.⁶ Mannose conjugation maintains high drug loading efficiency and pH-responsive release kinetics while substantially reducing cytotoxicity and enhancing selective uptake in epithelial and immune cell populations via carbohydrate-binding receptor-mediated endocytosis.⁶
