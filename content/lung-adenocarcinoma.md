title: "Lung Adenocarcinoma"
---
Immunotherapy resistance in LUAD patients reflects profound heterogeneity in tumor immune infiltration, with distinct microenvironmental phenotypes determining clinical outcomes. Integrated bulk and single-cell transcriptomic analyses across 31 datasets have identified elevated infiltration of ZNF683+ CD8+ T cells as the crucial immune subset enriched in immunotherapy responders, with ZNF683 expression serving as a robust indicator of treatment responsiveness.³ A ZNF683+ CD8+ T cell-derived risk score ([[znfrs|ZNFRS]]), constructed using an optimized StepCox[forward] + Ridge algorithm combination screened from 296 candidates, demonstrates superior prognostic capability for patient stratification.³

High-ZNFRS tumors exhibit a "cold" tumor microenvironment characterized by reduced immune infiltration, immunotherapy resistance, and enhanced [[spp1|SPP1]] signaling.³ SPP1, also known as osteopontin, drives immunosuppressive macrophage polarization and impairs CD8+ T cell effector function. In vivo experiments demonstrate that anti-SPP1 treatment suppresses tumor growth, restores CD8+ T cell cytotoxic activity, inhibits M2-like macrophage polarization, and significantly enhances the efficacy of anti-[[pd-1|PD-1]] therapy in previously resistant models.³ These findings establish SPP1 as a critical mediator of immunotherapy resistance and a tractable therapeutic target for combination immunotherapy strategies.

## Metastasis and vascular invasion

Vascular invasion is associated with metastasis and poor prognosis in LUAD patients, and recent mechanistic studies have revealed an unexpected role for intercellular mitochondrial transfer in this process. Tumor cells exhibit abnormal mitochondrial accumulation in adjacent vascular endothelial cells, driven by the transfer of mitochondria enriched in specific mitochondrial tRNA-derived small RNAs (tsRNAs).⁴ The mitochondrial tsRNA mt-5'tiRNA-34-GlnTTG, highly expressed in tumor cell mitochondria, is transferred to endothelial cells through both contact-dependent and contact-independent mechanisms.⁴

Once inside endothelial cells, mt-5'tiRNA-34-GlnTTG binds to the far upstream element-binding protein 3 (FUBP3) and facilitates its translocation, ultimately enhancing ribosomal assembly efficiency and translation rates.⁴ This molecular cascade leads to increased endothelial cell proliferation and migration, thereby promoting LUAD vascular invasion and metastatic dissemination.⁴ The mechanism represents a novel paradigm in tumor-endothelial crosstalk mediated by organellar RNA transfer rather than canonical signaling pathways.

## Tumor microenvironment

The LUAD tumor microenvironment is shaped by metabolic and spatial organizational features that regulate inflammation and immune activation. [[arachidonic-acid|Arachidonic acid]], a membrane-abundant polyunsaturated fatty acid liberated from phospholipids by phospholipase A2 (PLA2), is metabolized into bioactive eicosanoids that modulate vascular tone and inflammation within the tumor microenvironment.⁵ Single-cell and spatial transcriptomic analyses reveal that arachidonic acid metabolism serves as a key driver and potential therapeutic axis, with lipidomics advances illuminating its multifaceted roles across diverse cellular compartments.⁵

Spatial transcriptomic profiling has further identified critical epithelial-myeloid cellular crosstalk that stratifies LUAD patients by [[immune-checkpoint-inhibitors|immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)]] responsiveness.² Stratification by consensus meta-programs reveals ICI-MP-high and ICI-MP-low subgroups, with the former exhibiting significant enrichment of the E3-IFI6 epithelial subpopulation characterized by high expression of interferon-responsive and antigen presentation genes.² Spatially, E3-IFI6 cells are positioned adjacent to Ma-MHC myeloid cells, and both subpopulations co-enrich in a specific tumor niche where they interact via the APP-CD74 signaling axis.² Functional validation confirms that IFI6 overexpression in LUAD cell lines promotes proinflammatory macrophage activation, establishing a mechanistic link between epithelial cell state and myeloid cell phenotype.²

## Therapeutic strategies

Multiple therapeutic approaches have emerged targeting the distinct molecular vulnerabilities identified in LUAD. For tumors driven by circRNA-encoded drivers, RIPK1-98 inhibition represents a strategy to overcome resistance to [[osimertinib]] and other targeted therapies.¹ For immunotherapy-resistant tumors exhibiting high ZNFRS and SPP1-driven immunosuppression, combination therapy with anti-SPP1 antibodies and anti-PD-1 checkpoint blockade restores immune effector function and suppresses tumor growth in preclinical models.³

For metastatic disease driven by mitochondrial tsRNA transfer, lipid nanoparticle (LNP)-encapsulated inhibitors of mt-5'tiRNA-34-GlnTTG have demonstrated clinical translational potential in animal models, effectively suppressing lung cancer vascular invasion and metastatic spread.⁴ This approach represents a novel class of RNA-targeted therapeutics addressing the organellar RNA transfer mechanism. The spatial characterization of epithelial-myeloid crosstalk via APP-CD74 signaling further suggests that IFI6 modulation or APP-CD74 axis disruption may enhance immunotherapy efficacy in patients with rare actionable mutations.²
