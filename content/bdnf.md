**Brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)** is a neurotrophic protein essential for [[neuroprotection]], neuroinflammation regulation, and neuronal survival in the central nervous system. As a critical mediator of neuronal health and dysfunction, BDNF exerts pleiotropic effects including promotion of oligodendrocyte maturation, myelin formation, and suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokines. In the context of neurological injuries and pain disorders, BDNF dysregulation represents a key therapeutic target—reduced circulating levels correlate with increased pain severity in [[temporomandibular-disorder|temporomandibular disorder]], while elevation of brain BDNF drives recovery of motor and cognitive function following cerebral injury.¹ Therapeutic delivery of BDNF is particularly challenging in the central nervous system due to poor blood-brain barrier penetration; emerging strategies including stem cell-derived exosomes and intranasal lipid nanoparticles now enable direct CNS targeting and selective mRNA expression in the brain.²³

---

## Mechanism

BDNF operates as a potent modulator of neuronal survival and inflammatory homeostasis within the central nervous system. In response to injury or disease, BDNF signaling suppresses the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines including tumor necrosis factor-α and interleukin-6, simultaneously activating neuroprotective pathways that promote oligodendrocyte lineage maturation and differentiation. Elevation of brain BDNF, in particular, drives the upregulation of [[myelin-basic-protein|myelin basic protein]] and oligodendrocyte lineage transcription factors, restoring the myelination capacity necessary for functional neural recovery. Beyond its roles in myelination, BDNF stabilizes neuronal populations under pathological stress by activating anti-inflammatory and neuronal survival signals while simultaneously reducing markers of neuroinflammation.² The neurotrophic actions of BDNF extend to microglial and astrocytic regulation, rebalancing the inflammatory environment to favor neuroprotective over pro-inflammatory transcriptional programs.

## Pain pathophysiology and temporomandibular disorder

Dysregulation of circulating BDNF represents a biological signature of pain-related [[temporomandibular-disorder|temporomandibular disorder (TMD)]]. In genetically identical twins discordant for TMD pain, peripheral BDNF levels are significantly reduced in affected individuals compared to asymptomatic co-twins, with reductions correlating directly with greater pain severity, functional symptom burden, and mechanical sensitivity.¹ This within-pair comparison isolates environmental and epigenetic factors from genetic influences, demonstrating that BDNF dysregulation reflects acquired biological alterations associated with chronic orofacial pain. The selective reduction in peripheral BDNF occurs alongside elevations in pro-inflammatory markers and oxidative stress indices, suggesting that pain-related BDNF depletion may impair neuroprotective and anti-inflammatory responses central to pain chronification. Restoration of BDNF signaling therefore represents a potential therapeutic avenue for pain modulation, though peripheral elevation may require addressing central mechanisms that maintain pain perception.

## Cerebral palsy and motor-cognitive recovery

In moderate-to-severe cerebral palsy, stem cell-derived exosomal delivery provides a means to elevate brain BDNF and achieve profound functional recovery. Treatment with human umbilical cord mesenchymal stem cell-derived exosomes ([[hucmsc-exos]]) significantly restores both motor coordination and cognitive function in preclinical models of cerebral palsy, with the therapeutic benefit mechanistically linked to BDNF-driven oligodendrocyte maturation and remyelination.² The exosomes, upon intravenous administration, demonstrate sustained retention within the brain and promote robust remyelination through marked increases in [[myelin-basic-protein|myelin basic protein]] expression and oligodendrocyte differentiation. Concurrently, exosomal BDNF elevation attenuates persistent neuroinflammatory responses, reducing activation of microglial markers such as ionized calcium-binding adapter molecule 1 while suppressing the production of tumor necrosis factor-α and interleukin-6. This dual-modality mechanism—combining remyelination with anti-inflammatory neuroprotection—establishes stem cell-derived exosomes as a promising regenerative therapy for the motor and cognitive impairments that define severe cerebral palsy.

## Traumatic brain injury and intranasal delivery

Direct brain delivery of BDNF via intranasal lipid nanoparticles represents a noninvasive approach to treating repetitive mild traumatic brain injury. [[inbt-lnp|Intranasal brain-targeting lipid nanoparticles (INBT LNPs)]] designed with optimized surface charge efficiently traverse the olfactory and trigeminal nerve pathways to bypass the blood-brain barrier and enable selective mRNA expression in the brain.³ Co-delivery of mRNAs encoding BDNF and [[il-10|interleukin-10]] using this intranasal platform significantly reduces neuroinflammation, inhibits neuronal death, and improves cognitive function in repetitive mild traumatic brain injury models.³ The synergistic action of BDNF and interleukin-10 amplifies neuroprotective signaling while simultaneously dampening microglial activation and pro-inflammatory cytokine cascades, addressing both neuronal loss and inflammatory pathology simultaneously. This intranasal mRNA-lipid nanoparticle strategy represents a patient-compliant, noninvasive delivery platform enabling repeated dosing for chronic neurological conditions while minimizing peripheral off-target effects.

---

## Blood-brain barrier penetration strategies

A primary challenge limiting BDNF therapeutics is the impermeability of the blood-brain barrier to macromolecular proteins, necessitating innovative delivery approaches. Stem cell-derived exosomes naturally cross the blood-brain barrier and achieve sustained brain retention, enabling prolonged BDNF elevation and sustained neuroprotective effects.² Intranasal administration via lipid nanoparticles alternatively exploits olfactory and trigeminal nerve pathways to bypass the barrier entirely, directing mRNA cargo directly to the brain while minimizing systemic exposure.³ Optimization of lipid nanoparticle surface charge and ionizable lipid composition is critical for brain-specific transfection efficiency; positively charged intranasal formulations demonstrate superior brain accumulation and selective mRNA expression. These complementary delivery strategies—exosome-mediated and intranasal nanoparticle-based—expand the therapeutic utility of BDNF beyond peripheral applications and enable direct CNS-targeted interventions for acute and chronic neurological injuries.
