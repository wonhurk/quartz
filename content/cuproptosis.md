**Cuproptosis** is a copper-dependent form of regulated cell death mechanistically distinct from [[apoptosis]] and [[ferroptosis]], characterized by intracellular [[copper-ions|copper]] accumulation within mitochondria leading to proteotoxic stress and metabolic collapse. The pathway is initiated when excess copper binds to lipoylated components of the tricarboxylic acid cycle, causing aggregation of lipoylated proteins and loss of iron-sulfur cluster biosynthesis.¹ Central to this mechanism is [[fdx1|ferredoxin 1 (FDX1)]], which reduces Cu²⁺ to the more toxic Cu⁺ form, and dihydrolipoamide S-acetyltransferase (DLAT), whose copper-induced oligomerization triggers mitochondrial dysfunction and cell death.²

---

## Mechanism

Cuproptosis proceeds through coordinated disruption of mitochondrial metabolism and proteostasis. Following cellular uptake, copper ions preferentially accumulate in mitochondria where FDX1-mediated reduction generates highly reactive Cu⁺ species.³ This copper overload directly inhibits iron-sulfur cluster proteins essential for electron transport chain function and lipoic acid synthesis, precipitating bioenergetic crisis.² The physical interaction between Cu⁺ and lipoylated proteins—particularly DLAT—induces aberrant protein oligomerization that overwhelms mitochondrial quality control systems, culminating in organelle dysfunction and cell death.⁴ Concurrently, copper-catalyzed reactive oxygen species generation triggers lipid peroxidation and oxidative damage, amplifying the cytotoxic cascade.⁵

---

## Resistance mechanisms

Multiple cellular adaptations confer intrinsic tolerance to cuproptosis induction. Aberrant activation of the [[wnt-beta-catenin-pathway|Wnt/β-catenin pathway]] represents a critical resistance mechanism, requiring targeted suppression for effective therapy.¹ The copper efflux transporter ATP7A actively extrudes intracellular copper, maintaining sublethal concentrations and preventing mitochondrial accumulation—a process reversible through ATP7A downregulation by [[hydrogen-sulfide|hydrogen sulfide]] donors.⁶ Additionally, elevated intracellular glutathione rapidly sequesters free copper ions, neutralizing their cytotoxic potential before mitochondrial targeting can occur.⁶ These resistance pathways necessitate combination strategies that simultaneously deliver copper and disable cellular defense mechanisms.

---

## Cancer applications

Cuproptosis has demonstrated therapeutic efficacy across multiple malignancies resistant to conventional treatments. In [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer]], purpurin-copper coordinated nanoplatforms synergistically combine cuproptosis induction with photodynamic therapy using [[chlorin-e6|chlorin e6 (Ce6)]], achieving complete tumor eradication and durable immunological memory in preclinical models.⁴ The [[tpgs|TPGS]]-mediated enhancement of macropinocytosis critically amplifies intratumoral nanoparticle accumulation, overcoming the limited drug delivery that hampers many nanotherapeutics.⁴ In [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|triple-negative breast cancer]], twinborn metallic polymer nanoparticles codelivering platinum and copper reverse cuproptosis tolerance by suppressing Wnt signaling through p53-mediated upregulation of RARRES3, while simultaneously inducing type II immunogenic cell death.¹ This dual mechanism converts immunologically cold tumors to T-cell-inflamed phenotypes, establishing a foundation for durable antitumor immunity.

---

## Immunotherapy synergy

Cuproptosis-induced cell death exhibits potent immunostimulatory properties that synergize with checkpoint blockade and innate immune activation. The release of damage-associated molecular patterns triggers dendritic cell maturation and T-cell priming, initiating adaptive immune responses capable of controlling distant metastases.⁴ Critically, cuproptosis can be spatiotemporally coordinated with [[sting|STING pathway]] activation through engineered bacteria-driven biohybrid systems that selectively accumulate in hypoxic tumor regions and release cyclic di-adenosine monophosphate upon laser irradiation.⁵ STING activation amplifies cuproptosis efficacy by disrupting tumor cell glycolysis, simultaneously enhancing copper-mediated cell death and reversing the immunosuppressive microenvironment.⁵ Furthermore, metabolic reprogramming through glutaminase inhibition augments dendritic cell function while depleting the biosynthetic resources cancer stem cells require for survival and immune evasion.⁴

---

## Non-cancer applications

Beyond oncology, cuproptosis demonstrates therapeutic potential across diverse pathological contexts. In multidrug-resistant bacterial infections, copper-coordinated nanoassemblies induce cuproptosis-like death in [[mrsa|methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus]] by triggering lethal intracellular copper overload, inhibiting iron-sulfur cluster proteins, and disrupting the tricarboxylic acid cycle.² This antibacterial mechanism proves particularly valuable in diabetic foot ulcers and keratitis, where concurrent anti-inflammatory drug release and copper-mediated tissue regeneration accelerate wound closure.² In endometriosis-associated fibrosis, single-cell profiling identified a cuproptosis-vulnerable fibroblast subpopulation characterized by elevated [[aebp1|AEBP1]] expression, and treatment with the copper chelator tetrathiomolybdate reduced fibrotic lesion burden in preclinical models.³ These findings suggest cuproptosis modulation as a strategy for fibrotic diseases resistant to existing therapies.

---

## Delivery platforms

Engineered nanocarrier systems have proven essential for achieving therapeutically relevant intracellular copper concentrations while minimizing systemic toxicity. Cascade-responsive platforms incorporating Cu₂O cores with glutathione-degradable dendritic mesoporous organosilica shells enable synchronized copper and [[hydrogen-sulfide|hydrogen sulfide]] release, with triphenylphosphine functionalization directing mitochondrial accumulation and stabilizing the labile Cu⁺ oxidation state.⁶ Hyaluronic acid surface modification facilitates tumor targeting through CD44-mediated endocytosis, while near-infrared photothermal properties enable spatiotemporal control of drug release.⁶ Biohybrid approaches leverage genetically engineered bacteria as living delivery vehicles, exploiting their innate hypoxic tropism to achieve precise tumor localization impossible with passive nanoparticles.⁵ The [[cu-sf|Cu-SF]] nanoparticle functionalization of such bacterial carriers through synthetic biology approaches represents a paradigm shift toward programmable, autonomously targeted therapeutics.
