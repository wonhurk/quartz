**Thioredoxin reductase (TrxR)** is a redox-active enzyme essential for maintaining cellular antioxidant homeostasis through the recycling of [[thioredoxin]] and the regulation of intracellular [[glutathione]] pools. TrxR catalyzes the NADPH-dependent reduction of oxidized thioredoxin, enabling the latter to serve as an electron donor for biosynthetic and antioxidant reactions throughout the cell. In cancer cells, particularly [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)]], TrxR is frequently overexpressed, rendering it a therapeutic vulnerability. Targeting TrxR through small-molecule inhibitors and metal-based compounds triggers a convergent cell death program integrating [[ferroptosis]], endoplasmic reticulum stress, and immunogenic cell death, offering a potent strategy to overcome platinum drug resistance.¹

---

## Cellular function and redox regulation

TrxR maintains the intracellular antioxidant network by catalyzing the NADPH-dependent reduction of oxidized thioredoxin back to its active dithiol form. Recycled thioredoxin then serves as a cofactor for thioredoxin peroxidases and other reductases, supporting the detoxification of hydrogen peroxide and organic hydroperoxides. Beyond thioredoxin recycling, TrxR also participates in the regulation of [[glutathione]]-dependent antioxidant systems, ensuring that cells maintain an appropriate redox state for biosynthesis and homeostatic signaling. The tight coupling of TrxR to both the thioredoxin and glutathione systems makes it a central hub in cellular redox balance, particularly critical in cancer cells where metabolic demands and oncogenic signaling amplify dependence on antioxidant capacity.¹

---

## TrxR inhibition and ferroptosis induction

The therapeutic potential of TrxR targeting stems from its essential role in supporting the antioxidant machinery protecting cells from iron-catalyzed lipid peroxidation. When TrxR is inhibited by dual-targeted compounds such as platinum(IV) prodrugs, intracellular glutathione is rapidly depleted. This depletion cascades to inactivate [[gpx4|glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4)]], the central brake on [[ferroptosis]]. The resulting accumulation of lipid hydroperoxides triggers ferroptosis through uncontrolled lipid peroxidation in the cell membrane. Simultaneously, TrxR inhibition causes excessive accumulation of intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS), which amplifies the ferroptotic signal and triggers endoplasmic reticulum stress through protein misfolding and proteotoxic signaling.¹

---

## Immunogenic cell death and chemoimmunotherapy

Beyond ferroptosis, TrxR inhibition activates a broader cell death program characterized by immunogenic cell death. The combination of ferroptotic triggers and ER stress-induced apoptotic pathways in TrxR-inhibited cells leads to the exposure of danger-associated molecular patterns, rendering dying cells immunogenic. This immunogenic phenotype is particularly valuable in combination with anti-cancer immunity, where the cell death signature primes dendritic cells and promotes infiltration of anti-tumor effector cells. The convergent nature of TrxR-directed cell death — integrating ferroptosis, ROS stress, ER dysfunction, and immunogenic apoptosis — establishes TrxR as a rational target for chemoimmunotherapy in malignancies driven by redox scavenging capacity.¹

---

## Triple-negative breast cancer and platinum resistance

In [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)]], TrxR is characteristically overexpressed, and this upregulation drives dependence on redox scavenging for survival. Platinum-based chemotherapy such as [[cisplatin]] induces DNA damage but may be circumvented in resistant cells through elevated antioxidant capacity and thioredoxin-mediated detoxification. Dual-targeting Pt(IV) prodrugs designed to simultaneously inhibit TrxR and induce DNA crosslinks demonstrate remarkable activity against both cisplatin-sensitive and cisplatin-resistant TNBC cells. Complex 6b, a lead Pt(IV) compound, effectively inhibits TrxR1 in vitro and in vivo, triggering ferroptosis through glutathione depletion and GPX4 inactivation while concurrently inducing endoplasmic reticulum stress and immunogenic cell death. This multi-modal mechanism enables the compound to overcome the anti-apoptotic and antioxidant adaptations that allow TNBC cells to escape platinum therapy.¹

---

## Ovarian cancer and mitochondrial targeting

TrxR inhibition also shows promise in [[ovarian-cancer|ovarian cancer]], particularly in platinum-resistant disease. The heterobimetallic Pd(II)/Sn(II) η³-allyl complex 2i, bearing N-heterocyclic carbene ligands, preferentially targets TrxR and mitochondrial function across cisplatin-sensitive, cisplatin-resistant, and high-grade serous ovarian cancer cell lines. Critically, compound 2i retained potent activity in patient-derived tumor organoids including platinum-resistant models, while remaining inactive in non-malignant fallopian tube epithelial organoids, establishing a favorable therapeutic window. The mechanistic basis involves early mitochondrial dysfunction with cytochrome c release and loss of mitochondrial membrane potential, followed by downstream DNA damage through a mitochondria-driven apoptotic pathway distinct from classical platinum-induced mechanisms.²
