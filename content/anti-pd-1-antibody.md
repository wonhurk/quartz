**Anti-PD-1 antibodies** are immune checkpoint inhibitors that restore antitumor immunity by blocking the programmed death-1 (PD-1) receptor on T cells, thereby unleashing CD8+ T cell-mediated cytotoxic activity against malignant cells.¹ While single-agent anti-PD-1 therapy produces durable responses in select patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), microsatellite-unstable [[metastatic-colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer]], and other malignancies, most patients exhibit primary or acquired resistance driven by immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) features.²³ Resistance mechanisms span epigenetic dysregulation via [[cbx4|CBX4]]-mediated retrotransposon silencing, immune cell recruitment defects including PKC𝜄-driven macrophage infiltration and KRAS-dependent IL-8/CXCR2-mediated neutrophil accumulation, and metabolic reprogramming through HIF-1α/VEGF-driven hypoxia.

---

## Mechanism

Anti-PD-1 antibodies function by disrupting the PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitory axis that normally dampens T cell responses in peripheral tissues. Upon binding PD-1 on activated T cells, these antibodies prevent engagement with its ligands PD-L1 and PD-L2 expressed on tumor cells and antigen-presenting cells, thereby sustaining effector CD8+ T cell proliferation, cytokine production, and cytotoxic granule release.⁴ In responsive tumors, anti-PD-1 treatment increases infiltration of dysfunctional CD8+ T cell populations transitioning toward effector phenotypes, elevates interferon-γ production, and expands memory T cell compartments.¹⁴ However, clinical benefit requires a pre-existing "hot" TME with baseline immune infiltration, and the majority of solid tumors — particularly [[pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma|pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)]] and triple-negative breast cancer — exhibit "cold" immunosuppressive microenvironments that resist single-agent PD-1 blockade.¹⁵

---

## Resistance mechanisms

Resistance to anti-PD-1 therapy arises through multifaceted TME alterations that prevent or counteract CD8+ T cell activation. Epigenetic silencing orchestrated by [[cbx4|chromobox 4 (CBX4)]] represents a critical resistance pathway: CBX4 deposits H3K9me3 and H3K27me3 repressive marks on endogenous retrotransposons such as RLTR4-Mm-int, suppressing their expression in both tumor cells and tumor-associated macrophages.² Loss of CBX4 derepresses these retroelements, activating cytosolic RNA-sensing pathways and triggering robust type I interferon responses that convert the TME to an inflamed state permissive for anti-PD-1 efficacy. High CBX4 expression in nonresponders correlates with immunosuppressive macrophage accumulation and diminished CD8+ T cell infiltration.²

In NSCLC, protein kinase C𝜄 (PKC𝜄) drives anti-PD-1 resistance through transcriptional activation of CCL7 via Yap1-dependent signaling, recruiting tumor-associated macrophages and fostering an immunosuppressive niche with depleted CD8+ T cells.⁶ High PKC𝜄 expression marks tumors refractory to PD-1 blockade, while low PKC𝜄 expression predicts sensitivity. Similarly, the [[spp1|SPP1]] pathway generates a "cold" TME characterized by reduced immune infiltration and M2-like macrophage polarization; anti-SPP1 treatment restores CD8+ T cell effector function and synergizes with anti-PD-1 therapy in preclinical models.³

In KRAS-mutant tumors, particularly PDAC, oncogenic KRAS upregulates IL-8 via MAPK pathway activation, which in turn recruits CXCR2+ tumor-associated neutrophils (TANs) that promote immunosuppression through NETosis (neutrophil extracellular trap formation) in hypoxic and necrotic tumor regions.¹ This neutrophil-mediated barrier prevents CD8+ T cell infiltration and confers resistance to both single-agent anti-PD-1 and combination regimens with MEK inhibitors in larger, established tumors.

## Combination strategies

Combination approaches targeting upstream oncogenic drivers and downstream immune checkpoints have demonstrated synergistic efficacy in resistant tumor types. In KRAS-mutant PDAC, dual MEK1/2 and PD-1 inhibition overcomes single-agent resistance by suppressing IL-8 upregulation, enhancing memory CD8+ T cell recruitment, and increasing interferon-γ production, though only in early-stage tumors without extensive hypoxic/necrotic cores.¹ Addition of CXCR2 blockade to this doublet therapy prevents TAN infiltration into hypoxic regions and overcomes NETosis-mediated resistance even in larger tumors, providing a three-pronged strategy that targets KRAS effector signaling, checkpoint inhibition, and myeloid immunosuppression simultaneously.¹

In microsatellite-unstable/mismatch repair-deficient [[metastatic-colorectal-cancer|metastatic colorectal cancer (mCRC)]], where 15–50% of patients experience primary or secondary resistance to immune checkpoint inhibitors, the addition of anti-CTLA-4 antibodies to anti-PD-1 therapy improves outcomes, particularly in patients stratified by an 8-gene transcriptomic signature and mucinous component status.⁷ Patients with "StromalHIGH - ProliferationLOW" transcriptomic cluster A tumors exhibit the shortest progression-free survival on single-agent therapy but derive greater relative benefit from dual checkpoint blockade.⁷

Pharmacological inhibition of PKC𝜄 with auranofin reduces tumor-associated macrophage accumulation and promotes CD8+ T cell infiltration by disrupting the PKC𝜄-Yap1-CCL7 axis, and combination therapy with auranofin and anti-PD-1 triggers robust CD8+ T cell-dependent antitumor responses in NSCLC models resistant to single-agent PD-1 blockade.⁶

## Biomarkers and patient selection

Predictive biomarkers for anti-PD-1 responsiveness extend beyond PD-L1 expression and tumor mutational burden to include functional immune infiltration signatures. Elevated infiltration of ZNF683+ CD8+ T cells — a tissue-resident memory subset — distinguishes responders from nonresponders across lung and colorectal cancers.³ ZNF683 expression serves as a robust indicator of immunotherapy responsiveness, and a ZNF683+ CD8+ T cell-related risk score (ZNFRS) constructed through machine learning demonstrates superior prognostic capability for lung adenocarcinoma patients.³ High-ZNFRS tumors exhibit a "cold" TME with reduced immune infiltration, elevated [[spp1|SPP1]] signaling, and resistance to immunotherapy, whereas low-ZNFRS tumors show favorable immune contexture and durable responses.³

Low PKC𝜄 expression similarly predicts anti-PD-1 sensitivity in NSCLC, as these tumors exhibit reduced macrophage infiltration and intact CD8+ T cell responses.⁶ Conversely, high PKC𝜄 expression correlates with resistance and warrants combination strategies targeting macrophage recruitment. In PDAC, a risk score based on NETosis-MAPK signaling interaction is significantly associated with poorer survival and identifies patients likely to benefit from triple-combination therapy with MEK, PD-1, and CXCR2 inhibitors.¹

## Novel immunomodulatory partners

Beyond conventional checkpoint and targeted therapy combinations, metabolic-immune modulators have emerged as potent partners for anti-PD-1 antibodies by inducing immunogenic cell death (ICD) and remodeling the TME. In triple-negative breast cancer, rosmarinic acid/selenium nanoparticles (RA-Se@M) coated with homologous cancer cell membranes deliver both direct cytotoxic and immunomodulatory effects.⁵ RA-Se@M triggers robust ICD characterized by calreticulin exposure, HMGB1 release, and dendritic cell maturation, and when combined with anti-PD-1 therapy promotes profound intratumoral infiltration of cytotoxic T cells, achieving superior therapeutic outcomes in syngeneic models.⁵

In hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), pH-responsive [[zns-encapsulated-gelatin-microspheres|gelatin microspheres encapsulating zinc sulfide (ZnS@GMs)]] deliver hydrogen sulfide gas and Zn²⁺ ions to reverse post-embolization hypoxia-driven resistance.⁸ ZnS@GMs suppress the HIF-1α/VEGF angiogenic axis and glycolytic metabolism through dual mechanisms: hydrogen sulfide disrupts mitochondrial respiration and destabilizes HIF-1α, while Zn²⁺ inhibits hexokinase and lactate dehydrogenase, depleting ATP and biosynthetic intermediates.⁸ Furthermore, ZnS@GMs induce ICD, triggering dendritic cell maturation, cytotoxic T cell infiltration, and macrophage repolarization, thereby enhancing anti-PD-1 efficacy and establishing a metabolic-immune therapeutic paradigm for overcoming transarterial embolization resistance in HCC.⁸
