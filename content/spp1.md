**SPP1** (secreted phosphoprotein-1, also known as osteopontin) is a [[msr1|macrophage scavenger receptor-1 (MSR1)]]-dependent signaling molecule that drives immune suppression and pathological inflammation in the tumor microenvironment. Elevated SPP1 signaling characterizes immunotherapy-resistant non-small cell lung cancer, where it suppresses [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cell]] infiltration and promotes [[m2-macrophages|M2-like macrophage]] polarization, establishing a cold tumor phenotype refractory to immune checkpoint blockade.¹ Therapeutically, anti-SPP1 treatment restores CD8+ T cell effector function and significantly enhances [[anti-pd-1-antibody|anti-PD-1]] efficacy, while pathway inhibition simultaneously reduces macrophage-driven inflammation and suppresses fibrosis development following tissue injury.²

---

## Mechanism

SPP1 functions as a critical downstream effector in the [[msr1|MSR1]]/[[pi3k|PI3K]]/[[akt|AKT]]/SPP1 macrophage activation cascade, coupling macrophage sensing of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) to sustained inflammatory activation and migration.² The pathway is initiated when tissue-derived DAMPs engage [[msr1|MSR1]], triggering phosphoinositide 3-kinase and AKT-dependent phosphorylation events that culminate in SPP1 secretion and downstream pro-inflammatory signaling. This sensing axis is particularly active in response to tissue injury, where [[msr1|MSR1]]-mediated danger perception drives excessive macrophage hypermigration and aberrant activation. SPP1 secretion is therefore rate-limiting for the transition from injury-responsive macrophage infiltration to pathological inflammation and fibrosis-associated remodeling.

---

## Immunotherapy resistance in NSCLC

Elevated SPP1 signaling is a hallmark of immunotherapy-resistant non-small cell lung cancer, enriched in tumors with suppressed [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cell]] infiltration and a cold tumor microenvironment.¹ High-SPP1 tumors exhibit reduced immune infiltration and resistance to [[anti-pd-1-antibody|anti-PD-1]] checkpoint inhibitors, establishing SPP1 as both a mechanistic driver and a predictive biomarker of immunotherapy failure. Mechanistically, SPP1-driven [[m2-macrophages|M2-like macrophage]] polarization dampens anti-tumor immunity by suppressing CD8+ T cell effector function, while simultaneously promoting immunosuppressive [[tumor-microenvironment|tumor microenvironment]] remodeling. Targeting SPP1 pharmacologically reverses this immunosuppressive phenotype: anti-SPP1 treatment suppresses tumor growth, restores CD8+ T cell activation and infiltration, inhibits M2-like differentiation, and synergizes with [[anti-pd-1-antibody|anti-PD-1]] therapy to achieve durable tumor control.¹

---

## Fibrosis and macrophage-mediated inflammation

Beyond oncology, SPP1 drives pathological inflammation and tissue fibrosis in the context of injury. Macrophage sensing of tissue injury via the [[msr1|MSR1]]/PI3K/AKT/SPP1 pathway is necessary for early macrophage aggregation and hyperactivation at sites of tissue damage.² Inhibiting this pathway through pharmacological or biomaterial-based approaches—such as polyinosinic acid (Poly I) delivery systems that downregulate [[msr1|MSR1]] signaling—suppresses early macrophage activation and prevents downstream fibroblast activation and excessive extracellular matrix deposition.² The therapeutic benefit persists for weeks following injury, suggesting that early pathway masking establishes durable protection against fibrosis-associated complications. These findings position SPP1 pathway inhibition as an upstream modulator of immune and fibrotic responses, addressable through multiple therapeutic modalities.

---

## Therapeutic strategies

Anti-SPP1 monoclonal antibodies represent a direct therapeutic approach in immunotherapy-resistant NSCLC, combining tumor growth suppression with checkpoint inhibitor sensitization.¹ Upstream pathway inhibition targeting [[msr1|MSR1]], [[pi3k|PI3K]], or [[akt|AKT]] similarly attenuates SPP1 secretion and reduces both tumor-associated immune suppression and injury-induced macrophage activation.² Biomaterial-based delivery of DAMP-blocking agents such as polyinosinic acid offers localized sustained inhibition of [[msr1|MSR1]]-dependent macrophage activation, providing tissue-local protection against fibrosis without systemic immunosuppression. The dual applicability of SPP1 pathway inhibition—simultaneously restoring anti-tumor immunity and reducing pathological inflammation—makes it a uniquely attractive target across cancer and non-cancer inflammatory diseases.
