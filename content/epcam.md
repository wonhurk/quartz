**Epithelial cell adhesion molecule (EpCAM)** is a cell-surface glycoprotein overexpressed on epithelial tumors and [[circulating-tumor-cells|circulating tumor cells (CTCs)]], serving dual roles as both a therapeutic target for engineered [[car-t-cells|CAR-T cell]] immunotherapy and a diagnostic biomarker for patient stratification in advanced cancer. The protein is particularly valuable in identifying [[persister-tumor-cells|therapy-resistant persister tumor cells]] that survive [[neoadjuvant-chemotherapy|neoadjuvant chemotherapy]] and orchestrate immunosuppressive tumor microenvironments.¹ When detected on CTCs in combination with [[psma|PSMA]] and other surface antigens, EpCAM enables comprehensive circulating tumor cell profiling for precision oncology applications.²

---

## Mechanism and Expression

EpCAM is constitutively expressed on normal epithelial cells but becomes substantially overexpressed on transformed cells across multiple cancer types, including [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer]] and [[metastatic-prostate-cancer|metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer]]. The protein accumulates on both primary tumors and circulating tumor cell populations, making it an ideal targeting antigen for cellular immunotherapy. In [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer]], EpCAM+ [[persister-tumor-cells|persister tumor cells]] display aggressive stem-like traits and suppress anti-tumor immunity through recruitment of exhausted CD8+ T cells expressing the co-inhibitory receptor [[cd96|CD96]].¹

---

## CAR-T Cell Immunotherapy

EpCAM-targeted [[chimeric-antigen-receptor|chimeric antigen receptor T cells]] represent a transformative immunotherapeutic strategy for eliminating therapy-resistant tumor populations in [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer]]. Engineering CAR-T cells with [[cd96|CD96]] deficiency — eliminating a co-inhibitory receptor that ordinarily promotes T cell exhaustion — dramatically enhances their capacity to recognize and eliminate EpCAM+ [[persister-tumor-cells|persister tumor cells]].¹ [[cd96]]-deficient CAR-T cells redirect CD8+ T cell differentiation toward memory-like phenotypes characterized by enhanced mitochondrial bioenergetics, enabling robust targeting of quiescent, [[neoadjuvant-chemotherapy|chemotherapy]]-resistant populations that otherwise perpetuate metastatic disease and recurrence.

---

## Circulating Tumor Cell Biomarker

EpCAM+ [[circulating-tumor-cells|circulating tumor cell]] enumeration has emerged as a critical prognostic and predictive biomarker in metastatic cancer. In [[metastatic-prostate-cancer|metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer]], EpCAM+ CTC populations — particularly when co-expressing [[psma|PSMA]] — associate with significantly worse progression-free and overall survival.² Multi-marker CTC profiling combining EpCAM with [[psma|PSMA]], B7-H3, Trop-2, and STEAP1 enables comprehensive patient stratification and precision treatment selection. High EpCAM+ circulating tumor cell abundance directly correlates with increased molecular tumor burden on PSMA-PET imaging, elevated serum PSA, and elevated alkaline phosphatase, establishing EpCAM enumeration as a valuable component of [[liquid-biopsy|liquid biopsy]] for non-invasive outcome prediction and treatment response assessment.

---

## Dual Utility in Precision Oncology

The complementary roles of EpCAM as both a therapeutic target and diagnostic biomarker underscore its importance in modern cancer medicine. In the therapeutic context, EpCAM-directed CAR-T cell therapy addresses a critical clinical challenge: the persistence of quiescent, [[neoadjuvant-chemotherapy|chemotherapy]]-resistant populations that establish immunosuppressive microenvironments and drive metastatic spread. In the diagnostic context, EpCAM detection on [[circulating-tumor-cells|circulating tumor cells]] — integrated with multi-marker [[extracellular-vesicles|extracellular vesicle]] proteomics — enables robust patient stratification and outcome prediction, supporting the integration of [[liquid-biopsy|liquid biopsy]] into biomarker-driven cancer trials for improved patient selection and therapeutic monitoring.
