**SN-38** is a potent topoisomerase I (TOP1) inhibitor and the bioactive metabolite of the camptothecin derivative [[irinotecan]]. It induces cytotoxicity through TOP1-mediated DNA damage and represents the active payload in the anti-[[trop2]] antibody-drug conjugate [[immu132]], which has emerged as a targeted therapy for advanced [[colorectal-cancer]] resistant to conventional chemotherapy.¹ Clinically, SN-38 undergoes hepatic glucuronidation via [[ugt1a1]] and [[ugt1a3]], rendering it vulnerable to drug-drug interactions with UGT inhibitors such as [[fedratinib]].² Beyond its direct cytotoxic mechanism, SN-38 delivered via [[immu132]] suppresses the [[perk]]-eIF2α-ATF4 axis of the unfolded protein response, disrupting tumor adaptive survival and enhancing efficacy when combined with PERK pathway inhibitors.

---

## Mechanism of action

SN-38 exerts its cytotoxic effects by stabilizing the TOP1–DNA cleavage complex, preventing DNA religation and triggering irreversible DNA damage during replication. As a topoisomerase poison, it converts TOP1 from an essential enzyme into a DNA-damaging agent, inducing cell death across chemotherapy-sensitive and resistant malignancies.¹ The drug's potency derives from its high binding affinity for the TOP1–DNA intermediate and its ability to collapse replication forks, forcing cells into apoptosis or other regulated death pathways.

---

## Colorectal cancer therapy via IMMU132

SN-38 is clinically deployed as the cytotoxic warhead of [[immu132]], a site-specifically conjugated antibody-drug conjugate that targets the tumor-associated antigen [[trop2]]. In advanced [[colorectal-cancer]], [[immu132]] mediates two complementary mechanisms: direct TOP1-driven DNA damage and concurrent suppression of the PERK-eIF2α-ATF4 adaptive survival axis.¹ This dual targeting disrupts a critical resistance mechanism whereby tumor cells upregulate the unfolded protein response to survive therapy-induced endoplasmic reticulum stress. The PERK pathway suppression mediated by SN-38 delivery also attenuates the oncogenic [[wnt-beta-catenin]] signaling axis, further impairing tumor growth.

---

## Metabolism and drug-drug interaction liability

SN-38 is metabolized in human hepatocytes through glucuronidation, catalyzed primarily by [[ugt1a1]] and [[ugt1a3]].² The drug exhibits high susceptibility to inhibition by fedratinib, a JAK/STAT inhibitor used in myelofibrosis, which exhibits mixed-type inhibition of SN-38 glucuronidation with a Ki,u of 0.58 ± 0.13 μM.² This competitive interaction significantly increases plasma exposure to SN-38 when co-administered, raising the risk of hepatotoxicity and cumulative bone marrow toxicity. Clinicians must carefully evaluate polypharmacy scenarios in patients receiving [[immu132]]-based regimens, particularly in elderly or immunocompromised populations where hepatic function may be compromised.

---

## Combination therapy and resistance mechanisms

Preclinical [[colorectal-cancer]] models demonstrate robust synergy between [[immu132]]-delivered SN-38 and the selective [[perk]] inhibitor GSK2606414.¹ The mechanistic basis for this synergy involves enhanced suppression of ER stress and concurrent blockade of the [[wnt-beta-catenin]] oncogenic pathway. PERK inhibition alone does not fully phenocopy the effects of SN-38 delivery; rather, the combination achieves multi-axis pathway suppression — simultaneously disabling DNA repair stress responses, adaptive UPR signaling, and Wnt-driven proliferation. This rational combination strategy exploits the inherent vulnerability of ADC-resistant tumors that rely on stress adaptation mechanisms to survive initial therapy. Future clinical translation of [[immu132]] plus [[perk]] inhibitors may establish a new standard-of-care approach for [[colorectal-cancer]] patients with limited options.
