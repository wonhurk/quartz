**CD163** is a scavenger receptor expressed on monocytes and macrophages that serves as both a mechanistic mediator and diagnostic biomarker in occupational and immune-mediated lung diseases. Its elevation in body fluids reflects macrophage activation and inflammatory burden, making it a potential indicator of disease progression in [[silicosis]] and other pathological states.¹ CD163 is implicated in the pathogenic cascade of [[pulmonary-fibrosis|pulmonary fibrosis]] induced by [[nano-silica|nano-SiO₂]] exposure, where it mediates macrophage-driven inflammatory responses to nanomaterial deposition.

---

## Mechanism in pulmonary inflammation

[[Silicosis]], a progressive occupational lung disease caused by silica dust inhalation, triggers aberrant macrophage activation and chronic inflammation in the lower respiratory tract. CD163 expression on alveolar and infiltrating macrophages amplifies this response to [[nano-silica|nano-SiO₂]] particles, facilitating innate immune signaling and the subsequent recruitment of fibrogenic factors that promote [[pulmonary-fibrosis|pulmonary fibrosis]].¹ The protein's role as a scavenger receptor enables recognition of damage-associated molecular patterns released during particle-induced cellular injury, sustaining a maladaptive inflammatory milieu that favors the transition from acute lung inflammation to progressive fibrotic remodeling.

---

## Diagnostic and biomarker potential

CD163's utility extends beyond mechanistic understanding to clinical application as a disease biomarker. In silicosis patients, CD163 levels—both circulating and accumulated in the lung—reflect disease activity and the extent of ongoing pulmonary inflammation.¹ More broadly, urinary soluble CD163 has been evaluated as a potential biomarker of disease activity in autoimmune glomerulonephritis, including [[immunoglobulin-a-nephropathy|IgA nephropathy]], where telitacicept-based immunosuppression significantly decreased urinary CD163 levels alongside improvements in proteinuria reduction.² This multidisease biomarker utility suggests that CD163 mobilization and shedding may be a general indicator of macrophage activation across distinct pathological contexts.

---

## Clinical validation approaches

Characterization of CD163's role in silicosis has combined clinical analysis of occupationally exposed patients with in vitro validation models employing [[nano-silica|hydrophilic nano-SiO₂]] exposure systems, enabling mechanistic elucidation of how the protein drives disease pathology.¹ This integrated approach—pairing patient-derived evidence with controlled experimental models—has strengthened confidence in CD163 as both a mechanistic target and a prognostic/diagnostic biomarker for [[silicosis|silica-related lung disease]].
