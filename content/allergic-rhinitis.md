**Allergic Rhinitis** is a chronic inflammatory disease of the nasal cavity driven by immune-mediated responses to environmental allergens, characterized by nasal congestion, rhinorrhea, and pruritus that significantly compromise quality of life. The pathophysiology involves both local mucosal mast cell and eosinophil activation, alongside systemic endothelial dysfunction extending beyond the nasal epithelium.¹ Current management faces two critical challenges: suboptimal nasal deposition of conventional intranasal corticosteroids, and the absence of objective systemic biomarkers for disease diagnosis and phenotyping.²

---

## Mechanism

Allergic rhinitis is initiated when inhaled allergens engage [[ige]]-sensitized mast cells in the nasal mucosa, triggering the release of [[histamine]] and other inflammatory mediators. Dendritic cells present allergen epitopes to T cells, promoting a Th2-skewed immune response characterized by eosinophil recruitment and infiltration. Eosinophils undergo activation and degranulation, releasing cationic granule proteins and amplifying local inflammation. The condition extends beyond local nasal pathology; systemic endothelial activation occurs in pediatric allergic rhinitis, reflected by elevated serum [[endocan]], which indicates vascular permeability and endothelial dysfunction.² This systemic inflammatory component underscores that allergic rhinitis involves coordinated local and systemic immune remodeling.

---

## Drug delivery and quaternized chitosan

Intranasal [[mometasone-furoate]] and other corticosteroids are first-line therapies for allergic rhinitis but suffer from poor nasal deposition and rapid mucociliary clearance, limiting therapeutic efficacy. Conventional chitosan-based delivery systems rely on pH-dependent protonation for mucoadhesion, a mechanism that fails at the near-neutral pH of the nasal cavity. [[qcs-mf-lip|Quaternized chitosan-coated liposomes (QCS-MF-Lip)]] overcome this limitation by incorporating permanently cationic chitosan that maintains strong electrostatic interactions with mucin independent of pH.¹ The optimized formulation demonstrated a 5.26-fold higher mucin-binding affinity and approximately 4.7-fold greater mucosal retention compared with unmodified liposomes and commercial Nasonex®. This enhanced mucoadhesion enables establishment of a sustained local drug depot, prolonging corticosteroid residence time within the nasal mucosa. In an allergic rhinitis rat model, QCS-MF-Lip significantly reduced serum [[histamine]] levels and displayed superior anti-inflammatory effects compared to conventional sprays, suggesting a transformative strategy for improving intranasal corticosteroid bioavailability and therapeutic outcome.¹

## Diagnostic biomarkers: endocan and eosinophil-derived neurotoxin

Traditional allergic rhinitis diagnosis relies on clinical presentation and allergen testing, with peripheral blood eosinophil counts as an indirect inflammatory marker. These conventional measures lack diagnostic specificity and fail to capture systemic inflammatory heterogeneity. Serum [[endocan]] and [[edn|eosinophil-derived neurotoxin (EDN)]] emerged as objective biomarkers superior to eosinophil counts in pediatric allergic rhinitis. Both markers achieved high diagnostic accuracy by receiver operating characteristic curve analysis—endocan with an area under the curve of 0.931 and EDN with 0.929, compared to only 0.871 for absolute eosinophil counts.² [[endocan|Endocan]] directly reflects systemic endothelial activation, whereas [[edn|EDN]] reflects eosinophil degranulation and allergic inflammatory activity. These biomarkers demonstrated strong intercorrelation (r = 0.88), indicating coordinated endothelial and eosinophilic responses in allergic disease.² Both showed significant positive associations with total [[ige]] and eosinophil counts, confirming biological relevance to allergic pathophysiology. Notably, neither biomarker correlated with disease severity assessed by symptom control scores, suggesting they reflect allergic immune status rather than disease intensity.
