**CTNNB1** encodes β-catenin, a multifunctional armadillo-repeat protein that orchestrates both cell-cell adhesion and transcriptional regulation through the [[wnt-pathway|Wnt signaling pathway]].¹ As a structural component of [[adherens-junction|adherens junctions]], β-catenin links [[cdh1|E-cadherin]] to the actin cytoskeleton, while as a soluble signaling protein, it acts as a coactivator for [[tcf-lef|TCF/LEF transcription factors]] to regulate developmental and proliferative gene expression. Network toxicology analysis has identified CTNNB1 as a disease-specific hub gene for [[recurrent-pregnancy-loss|recurrent pregnancy loss (RPL)]], where it serves as a direct molecular target for endocrine-disrupting plastic additives including [[dehp|DEHP]], [[mehp|MEHP]], and [[tphp|TPHP]], with molecular docking confirming direct binding.² In bone biology, CTNNB1 participates in the [[trxr1|TrxR1]]/ROS-scavenging anti-oxidation cascade that promotes osteogenic differentiation under oxidative stress, positioning it as a regulatory hub for both reproductive and skeletal health.

---

## Mechanism

CTNNB1 expression is tightly controlled at multiple levels, with its protein stability serving as the critical regulatory node in the canonical [[wnt-pathway|Wnt pathway]]. In the resting state, cytoplasmic β-catenin is rapidly phosphorylated by the [[gsk3-beta|GSK3β]] kinase within the [[axin|APC/AXIN/GSK3β]] destruction complex, marking it for ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation; this prevents nuclear accumulation and transcriptional activation.¹ Upon Wnt ligand binding to Frizzled receptors and co-receptor LRP, GSK3β is phosphorylated and inactivated, allowing β-catenin to escape degradation, accumulate in the cytoplasm, and translocate to the nucleus where it displaces corepressors from [[tcf-lef|TCF/LEF]] family proteins to activate downstream proliferation-associated target genes.

In structural contexts, β-catenin localizes to [[adherens-junction|adherens junctions]] via binding to the cytoplasmic tail of [[cdh1|E-cadherin]], where it connects the cadherin-catenin complex to the actin cytoskeleton through [[alpha-catenin|α-catenin]]. This adhesion-dependent localization competes with nuclear import pathways, thereby regulating the balance between transcriptional signaling and cell-cell adhesion function. Post-translational modifications including phosphorylation, ubiquitination, and redox-dependent modifications modulate both CTNNB1 stability and subcellular localization, allowing dynamic switching between adhesion and signaling roles in response to developmental and environmental cues.

## Reproductive Toxicology and Risk Stratification

CTNNB1 is a primary molecular target for endocrine-disrupting chemical contaminants abundant in consumer plastics and personal care products. Systematic molecular docking analysis confirmed direct binding of the phthalate plasticizer [[dehp|DEHP]] and its metabolite [[mehp|MEHP]], as well as the organophosphate ester [[tphp|TPHP]], to CTNNB1, establishing it as a toxicologically relevant target for plastic additive exposure.² Network toxicology integrating protein-protein interaction analysis, gene ontology enrichment, and multi-omics data identified CTNNB1 and [[bcl2|BCL2]] as disease-specific hub genes for [[recurrent-pregnancy-loss|recurrent pregnancy loss]], distinguished from [[esr1|ESR1]]/[[mmp9|MMP9]] hubs in [[pcos|polycystic ovary syndrome]] and [[il6|IL6]]/[[nfkb1|NFKB1]] hubs in [[endometriosis]].² Mechanistically, plastic additive-induced dysregulation of CTNNB1 converges on shared pathogenic mechanisms including [[pi3k-akt|PI3K-AKT]] signaling dysregulation and microRNA-mediated post-transcriptional silencing, which impair trophoblast differentiation and placental angiogenesis in the context of [[recurrent-pregnancy-loss|RPL]].

Diagnostic and prognostic nomograms constructed by integrating CTNNB1 with co-identified hub gene [[bcl2|BCL2]] achieved high discriminative accuracy, with area-under-curve (AUC) values of 0.72–0.94 across cross-validation cohorts.² These findings position CTNNB1 as a clinically actionable biomarker for reproductive disorder risk stratification and suggest that environmental exposure assessment combined with CTNNB1 quantification may guide targeted prevention and early clinical intervention strategies in at-risk pregnancies.

## Bone Biology and Oxidative Stress

Beyond reproductive pathology, CTNNB1/β-catenin functions as a key regulator of osteogenic differentiation and bone homeostasis, particularly under conditions of elevated oxidative stress. In [[diabetic-periodontitis|diabetic periodontitis]]—a complication characterized by hyperglycemia-induced reactive oxygen species overproduction and accelerated alveolar bone loss—the [[trxr1|TrxR1]]/ROS/β-catenin anti-oxidation cascade represents an essential regulatory axis.¹ Emerging nanotherapeutic platforms such as selenium-albumin corona nanoparticles can activate this cascade through direct ROS scavenging, thereby suppressing the oxidative stress-mediated inhibition of β-catenin and restoring its osteogenic signaling function in periodontal tissues.¹ By reducing intracellular ROS, such interventions simultaneously promote macrophage polarization toward an anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype while enhancing osteoblast differentiation and mineralization, suggesting that [[trxr1|TrxR1]]-mediated restoration of CTNNB1 function offers a novel therapeutic paradigm for managing bone loss in the diabetic microenvironment.

## Cross-disease Network Architecture

The identification of CTNNB1 as a convergent hub gene across [[recurrent-pregnancy-loss|recurrent pregnancy loss]]—while distinct from [[pcos|PCOS]]-associated [[esr1|ESR1]]/[[mmp9|MMP9]] and [[endometriosis]]-associated [[il6|IL6]]/[[nfkb1|NFKB1]] hubs—underscores a fundamental principle in environmental toxicology: disease specificity emerges from the tissue context and cofactor landscape within which a hub gene operates, rather than from unique target identity alone.² All three reproductive disorders share upstream convergence on [[nfkb|NF-κB]] activation and [[pi3k-akt|PI3K-AKT]] dysregulation, suggesting that CTNNB1-directed therapies targeting its protein stability through [[gsk3-beta|GSK3β]] modulation, ubiquitin-mediated degradation, or redox-dependent post-translational modification may have broad applicability across reproductive pathologies exacerbated by plastic additive exposure. This network perspective also highlights potential therapeutic opportunities for multi-targeted interventions that simultaneously address CTNNB1-centered hubs alongside tissue-specific cofactors in each reproductive disorder.
