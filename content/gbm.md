**Glioblastoma** (GBM) is the most prevalent and aggressive primary malignant brain tumor of the central nervous system, comprising 15% of all primary CNS tumors and 50% of malignant primary CNS tumors worldwide.¹ Despite surgical resection, nearly universal recurrence occurs, driven by profound tumoral heterogeneity that has rendered frontline therapies ineffective for two decades.¹ The blood-brain barrier (BBB) constitutes a critical obstacle to therapeutic delivery, necessitating innovative transport mechanisms to achieve intratumoral drug penetration.² Both the neoplastic compartment and tumor-associated macrophages express actionable molecular targets, while radiotherapy resistance mediated by methionine addiction pathways limits durable disease control.³

---

## Background

Glioblastoma exhibits extensive molecular and cellular heterogeneity that underlies treatment failure across conventional modalities. Multiomic analysis of patient-derived cell lines has identified protumorigenic markers expressed on putative brain tumor-initiating cells, which perpetuate tumorigenesis even after primary treatment.¹ The tumor microenvironment is dominated by M2-like tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) that suppress CD8+ T cell activation through insufficient antigen cross-presentation, creating an immunosuppressive niche.⁴ Circulating exosomal cargo, particularly circular RNAs, serves both as mediators of disease progression and as diagnostic biomarkers reflecting the molecular state of the tumor.⁵

---

## Blood-brain barrier penetration

The BBB severely constrains therapeutic efficacy in GBM, but recent polymer engineering has enabled active transcytosis mechanisms. Amphiphilic [[zwitterionic-nanodiscs|zwitterionic polymer-coated nanodiscs]] (zNDs) undergo a three-stage morphogenetic process when co-assembled with phospholipids: polymer insertion into liposomal membranes, membrane disruption, and reassembly into ultrasmall protein-free nanodiscs approximately 15.5 nm in diameter.² These zNDs cross the BBB via choline transporter-mediated transcytosis and penetrate deeply into tumor parenchyma through clathrin-mediated endocytosis, followed by endo-lysosomal escape.² When loaded with [[honokiol]]—a natural compound that disassembles three-dimensional tumor spheroids—the nanodisc platform extended median survival by 2.5-fold in orthotopic GBM models without systemic toxicity.²

## Immunotherapy strategies

### CAR T cell therapy

The urokinase plasminogen activator receptor ([[upar|uPAR]]) emerged from multiomic screening as a protumorigenic marker of brain tumor-initiating cells and a dual-compartment therapeutic target.¹ Genetic disruption of uPAR impairs protumorigenic characteristics both in vitro and in vivo, validating its biological role in tumorigenesis.¹ [[car-t-cells|uPAR-specific chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cells]] demonstrated potent antitumor activity in patient-derived xenograft models of recurrent GBM.¹ Critically, uPAR is expressed not only on GBM cells but also on tumor-associated macrophages, enabling CAR T cells to simultaneously target the neoplastic population and immunosuppressive microenvironmental cells.¹

### Immunogenic cell death and tumor-treating fields

[[tumor-treating-fields|Tumor-treating fields]] (TTF)—an FDA-approved physical modality for GBM—trigger immunogenic cell death through sphingolipid metabolism-associated endoplasmic reticulum stress at single-cell resolution.⁶ An ATP-responsive hydrogel adjuvant designed to synergize with TTF enables spatiotemporally controlled release of tumor antigens and CpG oligonucleotides, reactivating myeloid cell interaction networks and augmenting sustained antitumor immunity.⁶ In GBM-bearing rats, the hydrogel-complemented TTF therapy effectively suppressed tumor growth, outperforming TTF monotherapy which merely delayed progression.⁶

### Macrophage reprogramming

M2-like TAMs represent the most abundant immune population in GBM but exhibit impaired antigen cross-presentation that limits CD8+ T cell priming.⁴ A photo-controllable nanotherapeutic platform utilizing lanthanide nanoparticles coupled with M2-targeting peptides delivers Ru-caged cysteine protease inhibitors after focused ultrasound-mediated BBB opening.⁴ Near-infrared-IIb imaging (1500–1700 nm) enables precise localization of M2-like TAMs, and subsequent 808 nm laser irradiation triggers upconversion emission that releases cysteine protease inhibitors.⁴ This inhibition enhances antigen cross-presentation capacity, directly promoting CD8+ T cell activation and remodeling the immunosuppressive microenvironment.⁴

## Targeted molecular therapies

### Bevacizumab and miRNA signaling

The anti-VEGF monoclonal antibody [[bevacizumab]] induces apoptosis in GBM cells through upregulation of microRNA-4695-5p, which directly targets and inhibits PKMYT1—a kinase that normally suppresses apoptotic signaling.⁷ This pathway increases the pro-apoptotic marker Bax while decreasing the anti-apoptotic protein Bcl-2, resulting in dose-dependent apoptosis in U87MG and U251 cell lines.⁷ Inhibition of miR-4695-5p or overexpression of PKMYT1 rescues cells from bevacizumab-induced death, confirming the mechanistic link.⁷

### β3-adrenergic receptor pathway

The [[beta-3-adrenergic-receptor|β3-adrenergic receptor]] (β3-AR), a G protein-coupled receptor subclass, plays a pivotal role in regulating oxidative stress within the GBM microenvironment.⁸ A BBB-permeable dual-responsive probe enables simultaneous visualization of β3-AR expression and real-time reversible monitoring of surrounding redox status, providing direct evidence for redox adaptation under stress.⁸ High-throughput screening identified [[baicalin]]—a natural flavonoid—as a potent β3-AR inhibitor that induces glutathione depletion and triggers oxidative stress-mediated apoptosis via the Gαi/o-ERK-Nrf2-GCLc signaling pathway in U251 cells.⁸ In orthotopic models, the probe penetrated the brain and enabled dual-channel imaging of β3-AR overexpression and redox imbalance for glioma discrimination and therapeutic monitoring.⁸

## Radioresistance mechanisms

PRMT5-mediated symmetric dimethylation of YAP at arginine 124 (R124me2s) represents a critical driver of radioresistance and methionine addiction in GBM.³ This post-translational modification protects YAP from inhibitory phosphorylation by LATS kinases at Ser127, facilitating nuclear translocation and enhancing transcriptional activity.³ Methionine promotes YAP activation through this PRMT5-dependent mechanism, and YAP subsequently transcriptionally upregulates both PRMT5 and the methionine transporters SLC3A2 and SLC7A5, creating a feedforward loop that drives methionine addiction and tumorigenicity.³ Radiation activates the PRMT5-YAP-SLC3A2/7A5 axis to promote GBM cell survival, and inhibition of this signaling pathway in combination with radiotherapy impairs intracranial xenograft growth with significant survival extensions.³

## Biomarkers

Circular RNA [[circ-rad18|circRAD18]] is markedly upregulated in GBM tissue samples and cell lines, where it promotes proliferation, migration, and invasion via the miR-1231/LUC7L2 regulatory axis.⁵ Exosomes—key mediators of intercellular communication—carry circRAD18 and may regulate disease progression across the tumor microenvironment.⁵ Knockdown of circRAD18 markedly inhibits GBM cell proliferation, migration, and invasion both in vitro and in vivo, while suppression of miR-1231 or overexpression of LUC7L2 rescues these phenotypes.⁵ Exosomal circRAD18 has demonstrated potential as a diagnostic biomarker for GBM.⁵

## Novel agents

### Metal-free nanozymes

Ultrasmall octa-amino silsesquioxane nanostructures synthesized via one-step sol-gel chemistry function as metal-free nanozymes with aldolase-like organocatalytic activity in aqueous environments.⁹ These nanostructures exhibit stimuli-responsive modulation of catalysis through reversible supramolecular aggregation and disaggregation.⁹ Their structural simplicity, water stability, and biocompatibility enable efficient cellular internalization, permitting their use as intracellular prodrug activators.⁹ In human glioblastoma and metastatic melanoma cells, the nanozymes catalyze retro-aldol activation of doxorubicin prodrugs, resulting in selective cytotoxicity.⁹
