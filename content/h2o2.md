**Hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂)** is a reactive oxygen species that serves as both a key biomarker of [[oxidative-stress|oxidative stress]] in pathological processes including [[cancer]], [[inflammation]], and neurodegenerative diseases, and as a functional substrate in advanced nanomaterial-based therapies.¹ Unlike conventional therapeutics that act through single molecular targets, H₂O₂ occupies a dual role in precision nanomedicine: its elevated concentrations in diseased tissues enable tumor-selective imaging and biosensing, while its chemical reactivity powers novel therapeutic platforms including self-propelled nanomotors, oxygen-generating [[cu-mg-tannic-acid-nanozymes|catalase-like nanozymes]], and peroxidase-mimicking antimicrobial systems.²³⁴ This multifaceted functionality positions H₂O₂ as a central node connecting diagnostic detection with site-specific therapeutic activation.

---

## Biosensing and imaging

The development of H₂O₂-responsive biosensing systems has addressed a long-standing challenge in redox biology: the real-time visualization of oxidative stress in living systems. The [[a-bo-crispr|A-BO-CRISPR]] platform represents a breakthrough in this domain, employing a sterically-hindered DNA activator caged with 4-bromomethylphenylboronic acid pinacol ester that initially blocks [[cas12a|Cas12a]] trans-cleavage activity.¹ Upon encountering endogenous H₂O₂, the boronate ester undergoes selective hydrolysis, restoring activator-crRNA hybridization and triggering amplified fluorescent signal generation through Cas12a-mediated collateral cleavage of a single-stranded DNA reporter. This turn-on strategy achieves a detection limit of 0.64 μM and responds within minutes, enabling real-time monitoring of H₂O₂ fluxes in living cells and tumor-bearing mice with high selectivity and robust stability in complex biological environments.¹

In the context of neurodegenerative disease biomarking, H₂O₂ serves as an enzymatic product of monoamine oxidase B metabolism, enabling dual-signal ratiometric detection systems. Carbon dots integrated with [[fe-co-cu-ldh|Fe/Co/Cu layered double hydroxide]] nanosheets leverage the peroxidase-mimicking activity of the nanosheets to catalyze H₂O₂-mediated oxidation of o-phenylenediamine, producing 2,3-diaminophenazine with characteristic optical signals at 570 nm fluorescence and 430 nm absorption.⁵ This approach achieves detection limits as low as 0.00067 U/L for MAO-B activity, substantially enhancing sensitivity for early diagnosis of neurodegenerative disorders.

## Nanomotor-driven antimicrobial therapy

H₂O₂ serves as a propulsion fuel for self-propelled [[platinum-mesoporous-silica-nanomotors|Janus platinum-mesoporous silica nanoparticles]], addressing the clinical challenge of biofilm eradication in endodontic infections.² These multifunctional nanomotors combine H₂O₂-triggered autonomous movement with biofilm matrix degradation through ficin protease enzymatic drilling and pH-dependent controlled release of chlorhexidine. At concentrations ten times lower than conventional commercial formulations, H₂O₂-activated nanomotors carrying chlorhexidine exhibited strong antimicrobial effects against saliva-derived and root canal biofilms, whereas chlorhexidine alone showed minimal impact.² The nanomotors effectively penetrated dentinal microtubules and eradicated bacterial biofilms residing within them, demonstrating efficacy in clinically relevant oral environments where anatomical complexity and biofilm resilience typically limit treatment outcomes.

## Catalase-like oxygen generation for tissue repair

In diabetic wound healing, where impaired angiogenesis, neuropathy, and hypoxia converge to disrupt normal repair processes, [[cu-mg-tannic-acid-nanozymes|Cu/Mg-tannic acid nanozymes]] exploit endogenous H₂O₂ as a substrate for sustained oxygen generation.³ These catalase-like nanozymes decompose H₂O₂ through near-infrared-triggered catalysis, simultaneously releasing nitric oxide from co-loaded BNN6 donors to enhance neuro-vascular coupling. The cascade nanozyme hydrogel integrating dopamine-modified gelatin, quaternary ammonium-lipoic acid-modified chitosan, and PTA-Cu-Mg/BNN6 nanozymes achieved synergistic effects in infected diabetic wounds: eradicating bacteria, modulating immune microenvironment toward M2 macrophage polarization, promoting endothelial cell proliferation and angiogenic gene expression, and facilitating neurite outgrowth.³ This approach transforms the pathological accumulation of H₂O₂ in chronic wounds from a marker of oxidative damage into a functional therapeutic resource.

## Peroxidase-mimicking antibacterial systems

Peroxidase-like nanozymes catalyze H₂O₂-mediated oxidation reactions to generate reactive oxygen species for antimicrobial treatment of oral diseases including dental caries, endodontic infections, and periodontitis. The hybrid nanozyme PB@MoS₂@Au, consisting of a Prussian blue nanocube core decorated with molybdenum disulfide shell and functionalized with gold nanoparticles, integrates enhanced peroxidase-like activity with photothermal conversion efficiency of 37.4% within the mild-hyperthermia window.⁴ Combined treatment with low-concentration H₂O₂ (200 μM) and near-infrared irradiation exhibited potent antibacterial efficacy against seven representative oral bacterial strains including Streptococcus species, achieving bactericidal rates as high as 95% and eradicating over 90% of bacteria within biofilms.⁴ In an ex vivo tooth infection model, this synergistic approach eliminated over 99.5% of Enterococcus faecalis biofilms from root canals within minutes, substantially outperforming traditional disinfectants that often necessitate repeated interventions due to incomplete bacterial clearance.

## Novel detection platforms

The [[fe-co-cu-ldh|Fe/Co/Cu layered double hydroxide]] nanosheet system represents a dual-signal approach integrating ratiometric fluorescence and colorimetric readouts for H₂O₂ quantification via enzymatic generation.⁵ When monoamine oxidase B catalyzes oxidative deamination of phenethylamine, the resulting H₂O₂ enables Fe/Co/Cu-LDH nanosheets to oxidize chromogenic substrates, producing detectable signals across two linear ranges (0.003-0.08 U/L and 0.08-10 U/L for fluorescence; 0.03-10 U/L for colorimetry).⁵ This multiplexed detection strategy enhances reliability in complex biological matrices including saliva and cerebrospinal fluid, where single-signal methods may suffer from interference.
