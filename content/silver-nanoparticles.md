**Silver nanoparticles (AgNPs)** are nanoscale metallic particles that function as versatile therapeutic agents across wound healing, ophthalmology, sensing, and aquaculture applications. These particles exert broad-spectrum antimicrobial and antiparasitic effects primarily through sustained silver ion release and photothermal/photoelectric mechanisms.¹ Recent advances emphasize green synthesis approaches using plant extracts such as [[neem|neem (Azadirachta indica)]], which produce stable, spherical nanoparticles with excellent biocompatibility and low environmental burden.² Silver nanoparticles are increasingly integrated into sophisticated composite materials and multifunctional platforms—including [[polydopamine]]-based dressings, ionogel matrices, and nanocomposites with bioactive compounds—to achieve enhanced therapeutic efficacy for complex clinical challenges ranging from infected wounds to bacterial keratitis.³

---

## Synthesis and characterization

Green synthesis of AgNPs from neem leaf extract yields uniform spherical particles with a characteristic size of 20±5 nm.² The synthesis process generates nanoparticles with a surface plasmon resonance peak at 420 nm and a zeta potential of −28 mV, both indicative of excellent colloidal stability and prevention of particle aggregation.² Functional groups present in the neem extract facilitate reduction of silver precursors and subsequent stabilization through phytochemical coating, eliminating the need for synthetic reducing agents or stabilizers. This biomimetic approach contrasts with conventional chemical reduction methods and produces materials with inherent biocompatibility.

Characterized by transmission electron microscopy and surface spectroscopy, neem-synthesized AgNPs demonstrate uniform morphology and minimal size distribution.² The reduction process involves the interplay of phenolic and polysaccharide constituents in the plant extract, generating particles that retain antioxidant properties from the leaf matrix. Such green-synthesized particles exhibit superior biocompatibility compared to chemically synthesized counterparts, with toxicity profiles establishing clear therapeutic windows in both tissue and aquatic models.

## Antimicrobial mechanisms

AgNPs exert broad-spectrum antibacterial activity through multiple mechanistic pathways. The primary antimicrobial action involves sustained release of silver ions, which disrupt bacterial cell wall integrity and denature intracellular proteins.¹ In wound microenvironments, silver ion release is potentiated by local pH gradients and oxidative stress, generating a concentration-dependent bactericidal effect that scales with nanoparticle loading.¹ In composite formulations incorporating [[polydopamine]], photothermal mechanisms further enhance antimicrobial activity—near-infrared radiation elevates local temperature, synergizing with chemical ion toxicity to establish a multi-modal killing system.¹

The efficacy of this dual-mechanism approach is exemplified in bacterial keratitis, where a [[polydopamine]]-silver-[[quercetin]] nanocomposite achieved 97% bacterial inhibition, surpassing conventional topical [[levofloxacin]] (77.4% efficacy).³ Beyond simple bacteriostasis, the polydopamine scaffold provides anti-angiogenic activity through [[quercetin]]-mediated suppression of endothelial cell tube formation, addressing the corneal neovascularization that complicates infectious keratitis.³

---

## Clinical applications in ophthalmology

Bacterial keratitis remains a serious threat to vision, characterized by corneal stromal opacity, edema, and neovascularization-driven scarring. The dual-functional nanocomposite comprising mesoporous [[polydopamine]], silver nanoparticles, and [[quercetin]] (Q/AMP) demonstrated superior clinical efficacy in both in vitro and in vivo models.³ In animal models of infected corneas, Q/AMP reduced corneal opacity scores from 2.5 (with [[levofloxacin]]) to 0.8, effectively alleviating ocular inflammation and pain while preventing pathological neovascularization.³ The broad-spectrum antibacterial activity, combined with endothelial cell growth inhibition, makes this composite a promising therapeutic strategy for infectious keratitis that resists conventional antibiotic treatment.

## Infected wound repair

Full-thickness infected wounds present a complex clinical challenge requiring sequential stage-adaptive responses across hemostasis, inflammatory, and proliferative healing phases. A functionally graded bilayer polyurethane sponge integrating silver nanoparticles with [[polydopamine]] addresses this complexity through spatial and temporal control of therapeutic factors.¹ The hydrophobic upper layer, fabricated by chemical foaming and dopamine-assisted reduction, carries a [[polydopamine]] coating embedded with AgNPs (PUF@P-Ag). This upper layer establishes a triple antibacterial system: the acidic microenvironment generated by lower-layer polyurethane degradation synergizes with sustained silver ion release from AgNPs and near-infrared photothermal effects to suppress bacterial proliferation across the inflammatory phase.¹ Concurrently, the [[polydopamine]] coating attenuates oxidative stress, accelerating transition to the proliferative phase where a sustained weakly acidic milieu supports endothelial proliferation and angiogenesis.¹ In rat models of full-thickness infected skin defects, this bilayer platform significantly accelerated wound closure relative to conventional dressings.

## Aquaculture antiparasitic therapy

Argulosis, a parasitic infection caused by *Argulus siamensis*, poses a major threat to freshwater aquaculture. Neem-synthesized silver nanoparticles demonstrated concentration-dependent antiparasitic efficacy against this ectoparasite infesting *Labeo rohita* (Indian major carp).² The 96-hour half-effective concentration (EC₅₀) was 9.1 mg/L, with 100% antiparasitic efficacy achieved at 21.19 mg/L.² Environmental scanning electron microscopy revealed dose-dependent nanoparticle deposition on the parasite surface, causing severe structural damage culminating in parasitic death.² Critically, the therapeutic index was 3.10, calculated from an acute toxicity LC₅₀ of 28.25 mg/L in fish, establishing a robust safety window that permits therapeutic dosing without host toxicity.² This concentration-dependent profile makes green-synthesized AgNPs a sustainable alternative to conventional antiparasitic chemotherapeutics, addressing environmental and regulatory constraints in aquaculture.

---

## Photoelectric sensing and wearables

Beyond antimicrobial applications, silver nanoparticles have been integrated into transparent ionogel matrices doped with silver nanowires for flexible, self-driven photosynaptic devices suitable for wearable UV monitoring.⁴ The particle-triggered photothermoelectric transduction, enhanced by the electric double-layer effect of transparent nanowire electrodes, converts absorbed UV photons into amplified, synaptic-like electrical responses.⁴ When embedded into eyeglass patches, this platform provides continuous, real-time environmental UV monitoring with visual feedback for early warning, all while maintaining optical transparency and mechanical flexibility without obstructing vision.⁴ This application leverages the photothermal properties of AgNPs distinct from their antimicrobial ion release, demonstrating the versatility of nanoparticle platforms across therapeutic and sensing domains.

---

## Biocompatibility and safety profile

Green-synthesized silver nanoparticles from neem extract exhibit favorable in vitro and in vivo biocompatibility across tissue and aquatic models, with clear concentration-dependent toxicity thresholds establishing therapeutic windows.² The 96-hour LC₅₀ in fish (28.25 mg/L) and therapeutic indices in both keratitis and aquaculture applications (3.10–7.0 depending on indication) permit safe, targeted dosing.²³ Polydopamine-based composites further attenuate oxidative stress through antioxidant properties inherited from the dopamine scaffold, reducing the risk of off-target inflammation.¹³ This multi-level biocompatibility—inherent to green synthesis, amplified by composite design, and validated across diverse tissue contexts—positions AgNPs as sustainable, tissue-compatible nanotherapeutics suitable for clinical translation.
