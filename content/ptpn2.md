**Protein Tyrosine Phosphatase Non-Receptor Type 2 (PTPN2)** is a negative regulator of immune signaling that suppresses [[ifng|interferon-gamma]] signaling and [[t-cell|T-cell]] activation, making it an important target for cancer immunotherapy.¹ Its inactivation permits [[stat1]] phosphorylation, restoring T-cell-mediated anti-tumor immunity. Two complementary approaches have emerged to modulate PTPN2: dual [[ptpn1]]/PTPN2 inhibitors based on benzothiophene [[dfmp|DFMP]] scaffolds and selective PTPN2 [[protac|PROTAC]]-mediated protein degraders, exemplified by compound 25 and [[pd-305|PD-305]], both demonstrating potent anti-tumor efficacy in preclinical models.¹²

---

## Mechanism

PTPN2 exerts immunosuppressive function by dephosphorylating [[stat1|STAT1]], thereby preventing the transcriptional activation of interferon-responsive genes required for T-cell differentiation and anti-tumor immunity. Pharmacological inhibition of PTPN2 catalytic activity permits [[stat1|STAT1]] phosphorylation (pSTAT1) to accumulate, restoring downstream signaling and enhancing T-cell-mediated anti-tumor responses. In this capacity, PTPN2 acts as a molecular brake on immune activation, making its inactivation a rational strategy for overcoming immune evasion in cancer.¹

## Dual PTPN1/PTPN2 Inhibitors

Benzothiophene [[dfmp|difluoromethyl phosphonate (DFMP)]] inhibitors represent a potent and structurally differentiated scaffold for dual inhibition of [[ptpn1]] and PTPN2. Structure-based design, enabled by late-stage iridium-catalyzed C-H borylation to access C5/C7-disubstituted architectures, produced marked improvements in potency and selectivity.¹ Lead compound 25 exhibits good potency, high selectivity for both [[ptpn1]] and PTPN2, and favorable pharmacokinetic properties. Systematic vector optimization further enhanced potency and selectivity while maintaining acceptable pharmacokinetic profiles. A key mechanistic discovery revealed that [[slc19a1]], a folate transporter family member, mediates cellular uptake of [[dfmp|DFMP]] inhibitors, establishing transporter activity as a determinant of bioavailability and cellular efficacy.¹ In MC38 mouse tumor models, compound 25 induced dose-dependent [[stat1|STAT1]] phosphorylation, demonstrating in vivo target engagement and immune pathway activation.

## PTPN2-Selective Degraders

Targeted protein degradation via [[protac|PROTAC]] technology enables complete elimination of PTPN2 rather than catalytic inhibition, potentially achieving more comprehensive immunological relief. [[pd-305|PD-305]] represents a major advance in PTPN2 degrader design, developed through ring-opening scaffold-hopping to identify alkyne-containing PTPN2-targeting ligands suitable for [[protac|PROTAC]] construction.² Optimization of linker architecture and [[cereblon]] ligand engineering yielded a compound featuring a rigid linker and naphtholactam-based [[cereblon]] recruitment group. [[pd-305|PD-305]] achieves exceptional subnanomolar potency with a degradation DC50 of 0.25 nanoMolar, representing an 868-fold improvement over the prior PTPN2 degrader [[pvd-06|PVD-06]].² Furthermore, [[pd-305|PD-305]] demonstrates greater than 20-fold selectivity for PTPN2 over [[ptpn1]], substantially reducing off-target effects.

Compared to earlier PTPN2 degraders, [[pd-305|PD-305]] exhibits markedly improved physicochemical properties: lower molecular weight, fewer hydrogen bond donors and acceptors, and reduced lipophilicity, all of which support enhanced drug-like behavior and bioavailability.² In interferon-γ stimulated HT-29 cells, [[pd-305|PD-305]] achieves nanomolar antiproliferation potency approximately 80-fold more potent than the advanced clinical candidate [[ac484|AC484]], demonstrating the superior therapeutic potential of complete protein degradation over catalytic inhibition.² In vivo, [[pd-305|PD-305]] achieves nearly complete PTPN2 degradation in tumors and potently suppresses tumor growth in mouse models, establishing [[protac|PROTAC]]-based PTPN2 degradation as a validated cancer immunotherapy strategy.
