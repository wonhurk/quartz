**NOX4** is a membrane-bound [[nadph-oxidase]] that catalyzes NADPH-dependent superoxide production and stands as a critical mediator of oxidative stress-driven ferroptosis across multiple disease contexts. Abundantly expressed in [[astrocytes]], NOX4 drives reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation that directly triggers lipid peroxidation and regulated cell death.¹ In [[alzheimer-disease|Alzheimer's disease]], NOX4 overactivation is controlled by the microRNA [[mir-10b-3p]], a regulatory axis suppressible by the [[diminazene|Ang-(1-7) agonist diminazene]].¹ In [[pancreatic-cancer|pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)]], NOX4 emerges as a core ferroptosis-related gene co-expressed with [[gpx4|GPX4]], [[ctsb|cathepsin B]], [[tfrc|TFRC]], and inflammatory mediators, establishing it as a multifunctional oxidative stress and cell-death regulator.²

---

## Mechanism

NOX4 catalyzes the reduction of oxygen to superoxide radical (O₂⁻•) through one-electron transfer from NADPH. Unlike other NADPH oxidases, NOX4 is constitutively active and directly couples to hydrogen peroxide production rather than requiring assembly of a cytosolic activation complex.¹ The generated ROS then drives the Fenton reaction, converting ferrous iron (Fe²⁺) into ferric iron (Fe³⁺) while simultaneously generating hydroxyl radicals that attack polyunsaturated fatty acids embedded in cell membranes. This ROS-driven lipid peroxidation serves as the molecular signature of ferroptosis, a form of regulated cell death mechanistically distinct from [[apoptosis]] and [[necrosis]].

---

## Alzheimer's disease and neuronal ferroptosis

In Alzheimer's disease models, NOX4 overexpression in astrocytes drives aberrant oxidative stress that precipitates ferroptosis in neighboring neurons.¹ The astrocytic-neuronal axis is controlled by the miR-10b-3p/NOX4 regulatory network, wherein dysregulation of miR-10b-3p—driven by amyloid-beta and tau pathology—releases repression of NOX4, leading to unchecked ROS generation and neuronal loss. This ferroptotic pathway contributes substantially to cognitive decline in both early and late-stage disease models. Diminazene, an agonist of the Ang-(1-7) system, suppresses this cascade by restoring miR-10b-3p-mediated NOX4 repression, effectively attenuating astrocytic neuroinflammation and preventing ferroptotic neuronal death.¹ These findings position NOX4 as a therapeutic target in neurodegenerative disease where oxidative damage outpaces cellular antioxidant defenses.

---

## Pancreatic cancer ferroptosis signature

In pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, integrative multi-omics analysis identified NOX4 as one of six core ferroptosis-regulatory genes alongside [[gpx4|GPX4]], [[ctsb|cathepsin B (CTSB)]], [[tfrc|transferrin receptor 1 (TFRC)]], [[hif1a|hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha (HIF1A)]], and [[tgfb1|transforming growth factor-beta 1 (TGFB1)]].² This gene signature is significantly enriched in ferroptosis, HIF-1 signaling, and TGF-β inflammatory pathways, all hallmarks of the immunosuppressive, fibrotic PDAC microenvironment. Expression of NOX4 and its co-expressed partners correlates with infiltration of both pro-inflammatory M1 and immunosuppressive M2 macrophages, suggesting NOX4 participates in orchestrating immune evasion alongside metabolic adaptation in pancreatic cancer.² Targeting NOX4 within this multigenic ferroptosis-regulatory network offers a strategy to overcome treatment resistance and sensitize tumors to cell-death induction.

---

## Therapeutic targeting and regulation

Diminazene suppresses NOX4-mediated oxidative stress through activation of the Ang-(1-7) receptor axis, effectively decoupling ROS production from ferroptosis induction in Alzheimer's disease contexts.¹ Beyond diminazene, the natural polyphenol [[resveratrol]]—identified through multi-omics screening as a ferroptosis inducer in pancreatic cancer—likely exerts synergistic effects by downregulating NOX4 alongside other core ferroptosis effectors, enhancing glutathione depletion and sensitizing tumor cells to death.² The dual capacity of NOX4 to propagate both pathological (neurodegeneration) and potentially exploitable (cancer therapy) ferroptosis highlights the disease-specific contexts in which NOX4 modulation becomes therapeutic.
