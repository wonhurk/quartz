**Actin cytoskeleton** is a dynamic protein network composed of actin filaments and associated regulatory complexes, essential for cell migration, immune function, and the maintenance of cellular mechanical homeostasis. The organization and dynamics of cortical actin are tightly controlled by signaling complexes including the [[wave2|WAVE2 complex]] and the [[arp2-3-complex|ARP2/3 complex]], whose coordinated activity generates branched-filament architectures at the cell periphery that drive lamellipodial protrusion. Disruption of actin cytoskeleton dynamics—whether through direct viral attack by [[hiv-nef|HIV Nef]] or through stress-induced cellular adaptation in radiation-resistant [[pgcc|polyploid giant cancer cells]]—fundamentally alters immune competence and therapeutic susceptibility, making actin remodeling a critical nodal point linking pathogenic perturbation to disease progression and treatment failure.

---

## Mechanism and organization

The actin cytoskeleton organizes into functionally distinct pools that respond dynamically to cellular signals. Cortical actin at the cell periphery drives lamellipodial protrusions essential for cell migration and immune cell trafficking, while the actomyosin network maintains cellular tension and mechanical homeostasis throughout the cytoplasm.¹ The [[arp2-3-complex|ARP2/3 complex]] nucleates branched actin filaments at the leading edge, creating the characteristic architecture of lamellipodia. Upstream of ARP2/3, the [[wave2|WAVE2 complex]] integrates signals from Rho family GTPases and receptor tyrosine kinases to coordinate actin polymerization with directional migration and immune responses, making it a central hub for translating extracellular cues into cytoskeletal remodeling.

## HIV-mediated disruption and immune dysfunction

[[hiv-nef|HIV Nef]] and [[hiv-gag|Gag proteins]] weaken CD4+ T cells by directly disrupting the actin cytoskeleton, resulting in defective cell migration and impaired immune synapse formation—hallmark immunological deficits of HIV-induced immunodeficiency.¹ Using multi-modal analysis combining ultrastructural microscopy, time-lapse imaging, and chemical inhibition, researchers identified HIV Nef as a major disruptor of the [[wave2|WAVE2]]/[[arp2-3-complex|ARP2/3]] signaling axis at the lamellipodia of primary CD4+ T cells. Nef induces inhibitory phosphorylation of WASF2, a core WAVE2 complex component, thereby suppressing ARP2/3 activation and preventing branched actin nucleation at the cell front. The resulting lamellipodial abnormalities—two distinct morphologies indicative of ARP2/3 inhibition—are strictly Nef-dependent both in vitro and in primary infected cells, and are directly recapitulated by small-molecule ARP2/3 inhibition alone. By crippling lamellipodial protrusion and actin dynamics, Nef-mediated disruption of the actin cytoskeleton undermines T-cell motility and immune synapse assembly, contributing to the progressive CD4+ depletion and cellular dysfunction characteristic of AIDS pathogenesis.

## Actin remodeling and mechanical dormancy in radiation-resistant cancer

Beyond infectious disease, actin cytoskeleton remodeling serves as a biomechanical hallmark of [[mechanical-dormancy|mechanical dormancy]] in radiation-resistant cancer cells. [[pgcc|Polyploid giant cancer cells (PGCCs)]] arising from radiation-induced DNA damage and clonal selection undergo extensive cortical actin reorganization coupled with nuclear enlargement and pronounced biomechanical stiffening.² This mechanical dormancy phenotype is paradoxically characterized by suppressed proliferation yet sustained expression of invasion-associated markers, representing a latent therapeutic threat. Using integrated nanomechanical single-cell profiling that combines cytoskeletal imaging with nanoscale stiffness mapping, researchers identified cortical actin remodeling as a central mechanobiological signature of radiation resistance in breast cancer PGCCs.² The dormant cells maintain invasive capability despite growth suppression, indicating that actin cytoskeleton perturbation enables cells to adopt quiescent yet invasively competent states that escape conventional proliferation-targeting therapies. This mechanotype-guided framework positions actin remodeling as a predictive biomarker for radiation-refractory populations and a target for precision intervention strategies to prevent disease recurrence from dormant cellular reservoirs.
