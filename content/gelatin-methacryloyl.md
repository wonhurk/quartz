**Gelatin methacryloyl (GelMA)** is a photo-crosslinkable gelatin derivative that enables rapid synthesis of functional hydrogels for diverse biomedical applications ranging from molecular biosensing to temporally coordinated drug delivery. The material leverages photo-initiated covalent chemistry at 405 nm wavelength to achieve crosslinking in under one minute, combining rapid synthesis with excellent biocompatibility and tunable physicochemical properties.¹ GelMA's versatility stems from its dual capacity: it serves as a scaffold for programmable [[dna|DNA-polymer]] conjugation and as a platform for modular, multi-drug [[hydrogel|hydrogel]] assembly, where distinct microsphere formulations can independently control the release of drugs with disparate physicochemical properties.

---

## Synthesis and DNA Integration

GelMA enables rapid and environmentally benign synthesis of DNA-polymer conjugates through photo-initiated covalent grafting of [[dna|DNA strands]] directly onto the gelatin backbone.¹ This photografting approach, conducted at visible wavelengths (405 nm), eliminates the need for hazardous reagents and time-consuming multi-step protocols traditionally required for DNA-polymer preparation. The resulting DNA-functionalized GelMA preserves the native programmability of DNA while inheriting the biocompatibility and hydrogel-forming capacity of gelatin, creating a bifunctional biopolymer suitable for integrated biosensing and therapeutic applications.

---

## Biosensing Applications

GelMA-based hydrogels have been engineered as molecular valve biosensors for sensitive and label-free detection of small-molecule contaminants in complex matrices.¹ The sensing architecture integrates DNA [[aptamer|aptamers]] into the GelMA network such that target binding induces conformational changes in the aptamer, triggering controlled cleavage of the hydrogel cross-linked structure and altering fluid flow dynamics through an integrated paper substrate. For [[aflatoxin-b1|aflatoxin B1]] detection, this GelMA valve sensor achieved a limit of detection of 34.22 pM—well below European regulatory limits—over a dynamic range of 50–750 pM, with naked-eye readout achievable within 607–1052 seconds depending on analyte concentration.¹ The platform demonstrated spike-recovery rates of 81–98% in real-world samples, highlighting its robustness in complex biological and environmental matrices. This naked-eye detection capability, combined with minimal hydrogel consumption per sensor (0.2 µL), positions GelMA hydrogels as a promising diagnostic tool for point-of-care and field-based contamination screening.

## Modular Multi-Drug Delivery

GelMA microspheres function as specialized delivery modules within a composite [[injectable-modular-microgel|injectable modular microgel]] platform, enabling programmable and temporally coordinated release of multiple therapeutics from a single formulation.² The modular strategy pairs GelMA microspheres—optimized for hydrophilic small molecules and proteins—with [[alginate|alginate]] microspheres that selectively encapsulate hydrophobic drugs, such that each microspherical component maintains independent control over its cargo release kinetics. By tuning matrix concentrations and gel crosslinking density, release profiles can be precisely adjusted across diverse drug classes, from small-molecule chemotherapy agents to large proteins. The composite formulation exhibits outstanding injectability through standard clinical needles, rapid self-healing to restore structural integrity after injection, and sustained in vivo retention that extends the therapeutic window far beyond conventional monolithic hydrogels.

---

## Overcoming Chemotherapy Resistance

The modular microgel platform has demonstrated clinical potential in reversing [[chemotherapy-resistance|chemotherapy resistance]] through temporally sequenced polypharmacology. Sequential delivery of [[carboplatin|carboplatin]] followed by [[rsl3|RSL3]]—an ferroptosis inducer—overcame intrinsic resistance in tumor models, extending mouse survival by 10 days compared to conventional single-drug or non-coordinated dual-drug hydrogels.² In a complementary application, coordinated release of doxorubicin and paclitaxel, which exploit distinct cancer cell death pathways, achieved a 3.53-fold reduction in tumor mass relative to traditional formulations. These results underscore how GelMA-based modular assembly converts the challenge of multi-drug coordination into a tunable advantage: by segregating drugs into distinct microspheres, their relative timing and stoichiometry can be optimized independently, unlocking synergistic efficacy unattainable by passive mixing or fixed-ratio delivery.

---

## Material Properties and Design Rationale

GelMA's utility across diverse applications rests on its unique material properties. Native gelatin imparts inherent biocompatibility and mammalian cell-adhesive capacity, which the methacryloyl modification preserves while enabling rapid photochemical crosslinking. The resulting hydrogels exhibit rapid self-healing—crucial for maintaining mechanical integrity and sustained drug release following injection through narrow-gauge needles—and superior in vivo retention compared to unmodified gelatin or purely synthetic alternatives. The material's transparency to visible light (405 nm) allows localized curing within deep tissue without requiring ultraviolet exposure, reducing phototoxicity and broadening clinical applicability. These combined attributes—biocompatibility, photocurability, injectability, self-healing, and prolonged tissue residence—position GelMA as a platform technology bridging the gap between rapid prototyping of functional hydrogels and translational clinical deployment.
