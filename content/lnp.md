**Lipid nanoparticles (LNPs)** are clinically established carriers for nucleic acid therapeutics, enabling systemic and local delivery of [[sirna]], [[mrna-vaccine|mRNA]], and gene-editing machinery to treat cancer, genetic disorders, and inflammatory diseases. LNPs encapsulate charged oligonucleotides within ionizable lipid shells that facilitate cellular uptake and endosomal escape, protecting their payloads from degradation while mediating tissue-specific biodistribution. Contemporary applications span [[sirna]]-mediated oncogene silencing, mRNA-encoded [[pe7|prime editor]] delivery for precision gene correction, and immune-remodeling mRNA delivery that reprograms the tumor microenvironment. Key formulation challenges — storage stability, tissue tropism, and anti-PEG immunogenicity — are being addressed through functional lyoprotectant systems, [[heparosan]] polysaccharide coatings, and structure-guided lipid design.¹⁻⁷

---

## Technology and delivery routes

LNPs are assembled from ionizable cationic lipids, phospholipids, cholesterol, and PEGylated lipids, with the ionizable component protonating in acidic endosomes to trigger membrane fusion and cytosolic payload release. Multi-route administration enables versatile biodistribution: intravenous delivery achieves systemic targeting of liver and spleen, intrathoracic administration transfects lung tissue, intracoronary infusion reaches cardiac endothelium, intramyocardial injection enables cardiomyocyte editing, and intraperitoneal dosing delivers mRNA to peritoneal and intestinal epithelial populations.⁶⁷ Cellular tropism varies by route and lipid composition, with non-cardiomyocyte populations (endothelial cells, fibroblasts) readily transfected across routes, while cardiomyocyte editing requires direct intramyocardial administration and optimized lipid formulations such as 18:1 TAP10.⁶

## Cancer applications

In multiple myeloma, LNPs delivering [[sirna]] targeting PSMD1 — a regulatory subunit of the 19S proteasome — reduced tumor growth in xenograft models, overcame proteasome inhibitor resistance, and prolonged survival while sparing normal cells.¹ PSMD1 depletion induced polyubiquitinated protein accumulation and apoptosis, with proteomic signatures indicating activation of immune-related pathways. Beyond cytotoxic payloads, LNPs can reprogram the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment by delivering immune-remodeling mRNAs encoding transcription factors IRF8 or NF-κB-inducing kinase (NIK). These payloads activate antigen-presenting cells, increase type 1 conventional dendritic cell populations, and prime antitumor CD8+ T cells, generating durable responses in multiple syngeneic mouse models.⁵ Coadministration of immune-remodeling mRNA with [[ovalbumin]] mRNA elicited approximately 10-fold enhancement in antigen-specific CD8+ T cell responses and established long-term immunological memory, positioning these formulations as adjuvants for cancer vaccines.⁵

## Genetic disease applications

LNP delivery of mRNA-encoded [[pe7|prime editors]] has demonstrated therapeutic correction of monogenic disorders. In a murine model of citrullinemia type I (CTLN1) caused by pathogenic ASS1 mutations, systemic LNP delivery of PE7 mRNA and synthetic [[pegrna|pegRNA]] achieved 24% hepatocyte correction in neonates and 13% in juveniles after three doses, fully normalizing blood citrulline and ammonia concentrations, restoring urea cycle function, and correcting behavioral defects.² Editing was liver-confined with minimal indel formation and off-target activity. For Duchenne muscular dystrophy, the cardiotropic 18:1 TAP10 LNP delivered adenine base editor components to patient-derived hiPSC-cardiomyocytes, achieving 80% on-target correction and restoring dystrophin expression; intramyocardial injection in humanized DMD mice yielded dystrophin-positive cardiomyocytes in the left ventricle.⁶ These findings establish LNPs as transient, redosable alternatives to viral vectors for precision genome editing.

## Inflammatory disease applications

In inflammatory bowel disease, intraperitoneal LNP delivery of mRNA encoding efferocytosis-enhancing factors enabled functional engineering of colonic epithelial cells in vivo. Engineered epithelial cells with elevated phagocytic capacity cleared apoptotic debris, attenuated intestinal inflammation, and halted disease progression in murine colitis models, demonstrating that in situ cellular reprogramming can restore tissue homeostasis without ex vivo manipulation.⁷ This approach leverages the ability of LNPs to transfect epithelial barriers following regional administration, expanding therapeutic applications beyond the liver and immune compartments traditionally targeted by systemic LNP delivery.

## Formulation optimization

Storage instability of liquid LNP formulations necessitates cold-chain logistics that limit global distribution. A functional lyoprotectant strategy incorporating N-acetylcysteine (NAC) — a mucolytic agent — into sucrose-based matrices enables [[lyophilization]] while conferring mucus-penetrating capability.³ Lyophilized LNPs preserved physicochemical integrity, maintained [[sirna]] encapsulation, and achieved efficient gene silencing in mucus-hypersecretory lung disease models through sequential NAC-mediated mucolysis and intracellular RNAi.³ To address anti-PEG immune responses that compromise repeat dosing and cause hypersensitivity reactions, [[heparosan]] polysaccharide — a naturally occurring, biodegradable glycan — has been explored as a PEG-free coating.⁴ Heparosan-engineered mRNA-LNPs demonstrated efficacy comparable to PEGylated counterparts with minimal tissue damage, negligible immune activation, and elimination of anti-PEG antibody responses in vivo.⁴ Tissue tropism is further refined through lipid tail engineering: high-throughput screening in human induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes identified 18:1 TAP10 as a lead cardiotropic LNP, enabling 36% cardiomyocyte transfection at intramyocardial injection sites.⁶
