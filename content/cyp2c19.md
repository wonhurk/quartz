**CYP2C19** is a cytochrome P450 monooxygenase enzyme expressed predominantly in hepatocytes that catalyzes the oxidative metabolism and clearance of numerous pharmaceuticals. The enzyme mediates the primary elimination pathway for [[ainuovirine]], a non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor for [[HIV-1]] therapy, and substantially influences the bioactivation of [[clopidogrel]], a critical [[antiplatelet-agents|antiplatelet agent]].¹ CYP2C19 activity is frequently modulated by co-administered medications, particularly [[proton-pump-inhibitors|proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)]], which exhibit variable inhibitory potency and thereby modulate the clinical efficacy of CYP2C19-dependent substrates.²

---

## Mechanism

CYP2C19 catalyzes monooxygenation of substrate drugs, introducing or unmasking functional groups that facilitate subsequent conjugation and elimination. The enzyme's oxidative metabolism is followed by phase II enzymatic processing—predominantly glucuronidation—resulting in water-soluble conjugates suitable for excretion into urine and bile. CYP2C19-mediated metabolism is subject to pharmacokinetic inhibition by competing substrates and inhibitor drugs; this competitive inhibition is clinically significant when two or more CYP2C19-dependent drugs are co-administered, leading to elevated plasma concentrations of one or both agents.

---

## Ainuovirine metabolism

[[Ainuovirine]] is a novel non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor whose clearance is primarily mediated by CYP2C19 through monooxygenation to the major circulating metabolite M341, followed by glucuronidation and fecal excretion.¹ Notably, the majority (>60%) of an administered ainuovirine dose is excreted unchanged in feces within 240 hours, indicating that while CYP2C19 catalyzes the primary oxidative elimination pathway, hepatic metabolism accounts for a minority of total drug clearance.¹ The relatively modest role of CYP2C19 in ainuovirine disposition, combined with its favorable pharmacokinetic profile (half-life 43.5 hours, target maximum concentration 327 ng·Eq./mL at 3.42 hours), supports ainuovirine's suitability as an anchor antiretroviral agent for HIV-1 treatment, with reduced vulnerability to CYP2C19-mediated drug interactions.¹

## Clopidogrel efficacy and PPI interactions

[[Clopidogrel]] is a thienopyridine antiplatelet agent that requires CYP2C19-catalyzed bioactivation to its active metabolite, which irreversibly inhibits platelet aggregation and prevents thrombotic events in patients with acute coronary syndrome or receiving coronary stents.² [[Proton-pump-inhibitors|Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)]] are widely co-prescribed with antiplatelet therapy to reduce gastrointestinal bleeding risk; however, certain PPIs function as potent CYP2C19 inhibitors and thereby reduce clopidogrel bioactivation, impairing its antiplatelet efficacy.² Strong CYP2C19 inhibition by specific PPIs significantly elevates the incidence of cardiovascular events—including stent thrombosis, myocardial infarction, and recurrent ischemic episodes—in patients receiving concomitant clopidogrel therapy.²

The degree of CYP2C19 inhibition varies considerably among different PPI classes and individual agents, necessitating clinical stratification based on CYP2C19 inhibitory potency.² Weak or non-inhibitory PPIs pose substantially lower risk of clopidogrel efficacy reduction compared to strong CYP2C19 inhibitors, making PPI selection an important pharmacological consideration in patients requiring dual antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy. This pharmacokinetic interaction exemplifies the broader clinical principle that CYP2C19 activity can be modulated by comedications, directly affecting therapeutic outcomes for metabolism-dependent substrates.
