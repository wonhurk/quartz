**Quercetin** is a bioactive flavonoid polyphenol endowed with potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and antimicrobial properties that has become a cornerstone molecule in nanomedicine-based therapeutic strategies. Its diverse pharmacological mechanisms—including free radical scavenging, normalization of [[cancer-associated-fibroblasts]], promotion of [[mitophagy]], and inhibition of pathological angiogenesis—enable efficacy across multiple disease contexts when integrated into nanocarrier systems. When coordinated within engineered nanoplatforms alongside [[manganese]] ions, [[silver-nanoparticles]], and immunomodulatory compounds, quercetin achieves synergistic therapeutic amplification through rational multi-component design, exemplifying the promise of nanomedicine in treating complex pathologies driven by oxidative stress and stromal remodeling.¹

---

## Mechanism

Quercetin exerts its therapeutic effects as a potent free radical scavenger, mitigating reactive oxygen species-induced cellular damage and apoptosis when incorporated into nanocarrier systems.¹ In renal tissue, quercetin activates the [[nrf2|Nrf2/HO-1]]/[[gpx4|GPX4]]/SOD1 signaling axis, which simultaneously alleviates oxidative stress overload and promotes mitophagy—the selective autophagy of damaged mitochondria—in tubular epithelial cells, thereby restoring mitochondrial homeostasis and cellular bioenergetics.² Beyond its antioxidant role, quercetin normalizes the fibroblastic phenotype, suppressing [[cancer-associated-fibroblasts]] and their pathological production of extracellular matrix, thereby relieving the physical and immunological barriers that prevent immune cell infiltration into tumors.¹ Quercetin's anti-angiogenic activity arises from its capacity to inhibit endothelial cell proliferation, migration, and tube formation, preventing pathological neovascularization that amplifies inflammation and tissue damage.³

---

## Cancer immunotherapy

Quercetin has emerged as a pivotal component in next-generation carrier-free nanoreshaping strategies that simultaneously disrupt stromal fibrosis and relieve tumor immunosuppression. When coordinated with [[manganese]] ions and the [[indoleamine-2-3-dioxygenase-1|IDO1]] inhibitor NLG919 in a one-pot nanoassembly approach, quercetin-manganese [[qn-np|nanoreshapers]] achieve efficient tumor accumulation and microenvironment-responsive drug release.¹ Within the tumor, quercetin normalizes [[cancer-associated-fibroblasts]] and suppresses extracellular matrix deposition, physically opening access corridors for immune cell infiltration. The coordinated manganese component simultaneously generates hydroxyl radicals that trigger immunogenic cell death and activates the [[sting|STING]] pathway to amplify type I interferon production, while NLG919 disrupts tryptophan/kynurenine catabolism to eliminate a major axis of tumor-intrinsic immunosuppression.¹ This multi-mechanistic coordinated action produces robust synergy with anti-[[pd-l1|PD-L1]] immunotherapy, generating cooperative suppression of primary tumor growth and pulmonary metastases.¹

## Acute kidney injury

Quercetin plays a critical restorative role in targeted nanointerventions for ischemia-reperfusion injury. When loaded into mesoporous [[cerium-dioxide]] nanospheres bearing RGD targeting ligands that bind integrin receptors overexpressed under oxidative stress, quercetin promotes mitophagy in renal tubular epithelial cells while simultaneously activating the Nrf2/HO-1/GPX4/SOD1 axis.² This integrated mechanism both restores mitochondrial function and scavenges excess ROS, reducing oxidative stress-induced apoptosis while normalizing aberrant macrophage polarization toward pro-reparative phenotypes.² In mouse models of acute kidney injury, the RGD-decorated quercetin-loaded nanotherapeutic improved renal function, ameliorated pathological damage, and suppressed inflammatory infiltration, positioning quercetin as a cornerstone molecule in organ-protective nanotherapies.²

## Bacterial keratitis and corneal disease

Quercetin exhibits exceptional broad-spectrum antibacterial and anti-angiogenic efficacy when formulated in mesoporous polydopamine carriers laden with [[silver-nanoparticles]], producing synergistic activity that exceeds either agent alone.³ In bacterial keratitis models, the [[q-amp-nanocomposite|Q/AMP nanocomposite]] achieved a 97.0% bacterial inhibition rate—substantially exceeding the 77.4% efficacy of conventional antibiotic eye drops (levofloxacin)—while simultaneously suppressing corneal neovascularization through quercetin's inhibition of endothelial proliferation and tube formation.³ The dual antimicrobial and anti-angiogenic mechanisms work in concert: quercetin suppresses the pathological blood vessel formation that amplifies inflammation and facilitates pathogenic dissemination, while [[silver-nanoparticles]] provide direct microbicidal activity, together reducing corneal opacity and protecting against permanent vision loss.³

## Wound healing

Quercetin serves as a critical anti-inflammatory and antioxidant component in multilayered electrospun delivery architectures designed to coordinate stage-matched drug release across the complex healing trajectory of wounds with excessive exudate.⁴ In a "nano-in-nano" microsphere-embedded fiber design, quercetin is housed within an intermediate layer of drug-loaded microspheres (2.5–3.5 μm diameter) embedded in nanofibers, positioned to release over an extended timeline matching the anti-inflammatory phase of healing while co-loaded amikacin in the outer layer (150 nm diameter) addresses acute bacterial infection.⁴ The hydrophobic innermost ethyl cellulose layer leverages interfacial capillary forces to transport wound exudate away from the tissue interface, while the hydrophilic outer layer rapidly absorbs excess fluid through capillary-mediated transport, creating a multi-functional microenvironment that simultaneously controls infection, suppresses damaging inflammation, and supports tissue regeneration.⁴

## Nanoplatform integration and delivery enhancement

The therapeutic potential of quercetin has been substantially amplified through rational integration into diverse nanotechnology platforms, each optimized for specific anatomical targets and disease contexts. Mesoporous [[cerium-dioxide]] nanospheres provide both remarkable ROS-scavenging intrinsic capacity and tunable drug-loading properties while bearing targeting ligands such as RGD peptides that bind integrin receptors overexpressed in injured tissues, enabling active targeting and reduced systemic toxicity.² Polydopamine-based mesoporous nanocomposites offer high surface area for coordinated loading of quercetin alongside [[silver-nanoparticles]], enabling dual functionality through rational synergistic pairing.³ Electrospun fiber networks represent an alternative platform architecture for wound healing, where quercetin-loaded microspheres can be embedded within polymeric nanofiber matrices to achieve precise, sustained-release kinetics synchronized with distinct phases of tissue repair.⁴ Across all platforms, nanocarrier integration consistently enhances quercetin's bioavailability, reduces off-target distribution, enables rational synergy with complementary therapeutic agents, and addresses the inherent solubility and absorption limitations of the free polyphenol.

## Synergistic multi-agent formulations

Quercetin achieves substantially amplified therapeutic efficacy when co-formulated with select molecular partners within engineered nanoplatforms. Coordination with [[manganese]] ions creates oxidative burst capacity within the tumor microenvironment, generating hydroxyl radicals and STING-mediated interferon production that synergize with quercetin's CAF-normalizing and immune-infiltration-promoting actions.¹ Co-loading with [[silver-nanoparticles]] produces dual antimicrobial and anti-angiogenic activity superior to either agent in isolation, with the resulting Q/AMP nanocomposite exceeding conventional antibacterial eye drops by 20 percentage points in bacterial inhibition while simultaneously suppressing corneal neovascularization.³ Pairing quercetin with the IDO1 inhibitor NLG919 provides a two-pronged attack on immune evasion: quercetin relieves physical stromal barriers while NLG919 eliminates metabolic immunosuppression through tryptophan/kynurenine blockade.¹ These coordinated formulations exemplify rational nanomedicine design, in which quercetin's inherent multi-functional nature is leveraged through simultaneous delivery of mechanistically complementary agents.
