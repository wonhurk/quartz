**Lateral flow immunoassay** is a rapid point-of-care diagnostic technology that enables visual detection of biomarkers and pathogens through antibody-antigen interactions on porous substrates, without requiring specialized instrumentation or trained personnel. The platform operates through a sandwich immunoassay format in which a capture antibody immobilized on the test zone binds an antigen, which is subsequently recognized by a detection antibody conjugated to a signal-generating agent. Recent innovations have substantially enhanced assay sensitivity and functionality through the integration of [[au-pt-nanozymes|nanozyme-based signal amplification]], oriented antibody immobilization techniques that preserve both antibody accessibility and catalytic activity, and abiotic antibody mimics that replace conventional biological proteins with engineered polymer nanoparticles. These advances position lateral flow immunoassays as practical diagnostic tools aligned with WHO REASSURED criteria for decentralized testing in resource-limited settings.¹

---

## Mechanism

Lateral flow immunoassays operate through the sequential binding of sample biomarkers to capture and detection antibodies, generating signal at the test zone through enzymatic color development. The fundamental principle relies on conjugation of signal-generating molecules to detection antibodies; conventional approaches use gold nanoparticles, which generate weak colorimetric signals limiting sensitivity in point-of-care formats. [[Au@Pt nanozymes]]—core-shell nanoparticles combining gold cores with platinum shells—possess intrinsic peroxidase-mimicking activity that catalyzes substrate transformation, producing signal brightness substantially exceeding passive gold nanoparticles.² However, traditional antibody immobilization methods—physical adsorption and covalent conjugation—often diminish both antibody antigen-binding reactivity and nanozyme catalytic efficiency through steric hindrance or surface deactivation.

Oriented antibody immobilization addresses this challenge through aptamer-mediated capture of the antibody fragment crystallizable region, positioning antigen-binding domains optimally for recognition while preserving nanozyme peroxidase activity.² This approach enables simultaneous optimization of molecular recognition and catalytic signal generation, critical for achieving ultrasensitive lateral flow formats. Advanced nanozyme architectures further amplify sensitivity; hierarchical mesochannel-confined nanozyme reactors such as [[fe-mpp-t|FeMPPt]] achieve 125-fold signal enhancement compared to conventional gold nanoparticles and 5-fold improvement over non-porous nanozymes of equivalent size through spatial confinement catalysis and high-surface-area interception mechanisms.³ The mesoporous architecture concentrates reactants around platinum nanozyme sites, dramatically amplifying colorimetric signal and enabling detection of biomarkers at concentrations previously inaccessible to visual lateral flow assays.

An emerging alternative to immunological detection employs abiotic antibody mimics—engineered hydrogel polymer nanoparticles composed of aromatic amino acid monomers—that functionally replace biological antibodies in sandwich immunoassay formats. These biomolecule-free probes achieve antigen recognition efficacy comparable to native antibodies through optimized spatial arrangement of anionic and aromatic groups, enabling competitive inhibition of viral spike-receptor interactions while eliminating the complexity and cost of antibody production.⁴

## Infectious disease diagnostics

Lateral flow immunoassays have emerged as critical diagnostic tools for high-consequence viral pathogens where rapid point-of-care confirmation directly impacts clinical outcome and outbreak control. The development of broad-lineage [[lassa-virus-nucleoprotein|Lassa virus]] lateral flow rapid diagnostic tests (RDTs) targeting the viral nucleoprotein demonstrates the platform's capability for sensitive detection of genetically diverse pathogen strains.¹ Using optimized monoclonal antibody pairs, these assays achieve approximately 64-fold greater sensitivity than commercially available antigen tests while maintaining 100% specificity across clinical specimens.¹ Critically, these RDTs demonstrate lineage-dependent sensitivity differences across genetically diverse [[lassa-fever|Lassa virus]] strains, reflecting the evolutionary complexity of endemic viral populations; this heterogeneous analytical performance improves broad diagnostic applicability across geographic regions.¹ The lateral flow format provides rapid visual readout and maintains stability at ambient temperatures without requiring cold-chain infrastructure, features directly aligned with WHO REASSURED framework criteria for decentralized diagnostics in resource-limited African settings where [[lassa-fever|Lassa fever]] is endemic.¹

[[SARS-CoV-2|Coronavirus]] diagnostics similarly benefit from lateral flow immunoassay advances, with abiotic antibody mimics enabling entirely biomolecule-free detection platforms for [[sars-cov-2|SARS-CoV-2]] spike antigens.⁴ The polymer nanoparticle-based detection probes recognize epitopes overlapping acetohydrolase-binding sites on the spike receptor-binding domain, enabling competitive inhibition of spike-[[ace2|ACE2]] interactions with efficacy matching conventional antibodies while eliminating biological constraints.⁴

## Biomarker detection in point-of-care settings

Lateral flow immunoassays have expanded beyond infectious disease into detection of disease biomarkers requiring both rapid turnaround and quantitative accuracy for clinical decision-making. [[Anti-mullerian-hormone|Anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH)]] serves as a key reproductive biomarker for ovarian reserve assessment; oriented Au@Pt nanozyme-based lateral flow immunoassays achieve sensitive AMH detection through sandwich immunoassay using monoclonal antibody pairs targeting distinct epitopes, with signal generation amplified by the peroxidase-mimicking activity of the nanozyme conjugate.² The dual-mode detection capability of hierarchical mesochannel-confined nanozyme reactors enables simultaneous qualitative and quantitative multiplex biomarker detection, as demonstrated through clinical validation for [[cardiac-troponin-i|cardiac troponin I]] and [[alpha-fetoprotein|alpha-fetoprotein (AFP)]].³ Troponin I serves as the gold-standard cardiac injury biomarker for myocardial infarction diagnosis, and the FeMPPt-enhanced lateral flow format achieved 125- to 230-fold lower detection limits compared to conventional gold nanoparticle assays.³ Alpha-fetoprotein detection, essential for hepatocellular carcinoma surveillance, similarly benefited from the 1300-fold sensitivity improvement provided by hierarchical nanozyme reactors.³ Clinical validation across 234 serum samples from clinical laboratories and 34 samples from emergency departments demonstrated quantitative performance comparable to or exceeding reference laboratory standards, validating practical applicability in high-volume clinical workflows.³

## Novel amplification and recognition approaches

Innovation in signal amplification continues to drive lateral flow immunoassay sensitivity gains beyond traditional gold nanoparticle architectures. Hierarchical mesoporous channel-confined nanozyme reactors represent a paradigm shift in colorimetric amplification, combining three complementary enhancement mechanisms—electrostatic adsorption, high-specific-surface-area interception, and mesoporous confinement catalysis—to concentrate catalytic activity and amplify signal output.³ This architecture enables ultrasensitive dual-mode detection supporting both qualitative visual readout and quantitative measurement in point-of-care settings without requiring instrumental analysis.³

Abiotic antibody mimics constructed from rationally designed polymer nanoparticle libraries offer an orthogonal approach to improving assay robustness and manufacturability.⁴ These entirely synthetic probes, selected from libraries varying aromatic amino acid composition (N-acryloyl-L-tryptophan, N-acryloyl-L-tyrosine, N-acryloyl-L-phenylalanine), achieve spike antigen targeting through optimized three-dimensional presentation of chemical functional groups rather than biological sequence recognition.⁴ Integration of abiotic mimics into multiplex lateral flow formats for simultaneous detection of multiple viral or bacterial antigens eliminates cold-chain storage requirements, enhances thermal stability, and reduces manufacturing complexity inherent to biological antibody production, positioning these systems for deployment in austere point-of-care environments.⁴
