**cycA** is a bacterial gene encoding a cyclophilin-like protein with dual roles spanning ammonia metabolism and eukaryotic cancer signaling. The eukaryotic ortholog [[cyclin|cyclophilin A (CYPA)]] functions as a critical molecular glue protein recruited by tri-complex inhibitor drugs to form a synthetic inhibitory complex with [[ras|GTP-bound RAS proteins]], thereby blocking oncogenic RAS signaling in cancer therapy.¹ Resistance to these [[tri-complex-inhibitors|tri-complex inhibitors (TCIs)]] emerges through mutations that either disrupt drug-RAS-CYPA complex assembly or activate compensatory [[ras|RAS]]-[[braf|BRAF]] interactions, necessitating second-generation inhibitors and combination strategies to maintain therapeutic efficacy.¹

---

## Mechanism

The molecular glue function of CYPA is central to tri-complex inhibitor action. These drugs bind the active, [[ras|GTP-bound state of RAS]] and simultaneously recruit CYPA to form a ternary synthetic complex that is mechanistically distinct from the native [[ras|RAS-BRAF]] interaction. This drug-induced assembly blocks the canonical oncogenic signaling cascade by preventing [[ras|RAS]] from engaging its downstream effectors.¹ The stability and function of this complex depend critically on the integrity of key [[ras|RAS]] residues and appropriate drug-binding geometry, making it exquisitely sensitive to mutations that disrupt any component of the three-body interaction.

---

## RAS inhibitor resistance

Resistance to [[tri-complex-inhibitors|tri-complex inhibitors]] in [[ras|RAS]]-mutant cancers emerges through convergent molecular mechanisms that undermine the synthetic complex. [[ras|RAS]] Y64 mutations directly disrupt the binding pocket required for [[daraxonrasib|daraxonrasib]] and CYPA engagement, preventing ternary complex formation and allowing uninhibited signaling.¹ Conversely, [[ras|RAS]] Y71 mutations and kinase-dead [[braf|BRAF]] variants enhance the competing [[ras|RAS-BRAF]] interaction, enabling cells to bypass the inhibitor-imposed signaling blockade by preferentially adopting native, rather than drug-stabilized, protein configurations.¹

Novel [[tri-complex-inhibitors|tri-complex inhibitors]] have been developed to overcome Y64-driven resistance, while combination therapies targeting kinase-dead [[braf|BRAF]] mutations simultaneously address the pathway's adaptive flexibility. These approaches restore synthetic complex stability and prevent bypass signaling, improving durable response rates in treatment-resistant populations.¹

## Bacterial ammonia oxidation

In acidophilic ammonia-oxidizing bacteria including "Candidatus Nitrosacidococcus tergens" sp. RJ19, cycA is present but non-essential for ammonia oxidation across a broad pH range (2.5–7.0).² The gene exhibits pH-dependent expression, increasing above pH 6.0 concurrent with enhanced ammonia oxidation activity and stoichiometric ammonium-to-nitrite conversion.² Despite the apparent absence or downregulation of cycA and other metabolic genes under acidic stress, these organisms maintain robust nitrogen transformation, suggesting that alternative metabolic pathways and regulatory mechanisms compensate for cycA loss.² Below pH 6.0, the organism shifts toward nitrifier-denitrification, producing nitrous oxide through increased norB expression rather than relying on cycA-mediated pathways.²
