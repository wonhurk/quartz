**SIRT1** is a [[nad|NAD+]]-dependent deacetylase that functions as a critical cytoprotective enzyme in ischemia-reperfusion injury across multiple organ systems, particularly the brain and liver. Its activity is mechanistically tied to cellular [[nad|NAD+]] bioavailability, and its deacetylase function is essential for mitigating inflammatory injury and promoting tissue recovery following both cerebral and hepatic ischemia-reperfusion insults.¹² The enzyme exerts organ-specific protective effects through the inhibition of the [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] inflammatory pathway in the brain and deacetylation of [[eif2a|eIF2α]] to suppress endoplasmic reticulum stress in hepatic macrophages.

---

## Mechanism

SIRT1 catalyzes the NAD+-dependent removal of acetyl groups from protein substrates, using NAD+ consumption as a mechanism to sense cellular energy status and metabolic demand. The deacetylase activity is directly proportional to intracellular [[nad|NAD+]] availability, a constraint that becomes particularly severe in aged organisms and in tissues undergoing ischemic stress. SIRT1 exerts its cytoprotective effects through substrate-specific deacetylation: in the context of neuroinflammation, it suppresses acetylation of the [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] pathway, thereby reducing transcription of pro-inflammatory genes; in hepatic macrophages, it deacetylates [[eif2a|eIF2α]] to prevent its phosphorylation-driven activation of the integrated stress response.

---

## Cerebral ischemia-reperfusion and neuroprotection

Following cerebral ischemia-reperfusion injury (CIRI), SIRT1 activation by [[electroacupuncture]] upregulates both protein expression and enzymatic deacetylase activity of the enzyme.¹ Elevated SIRT1 activity concurrently suppresses phosphorylation of the [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] pathway, shifting the cellular signaling environment toward anti-inflammatory signaling. This molecular brake on NF-κB reduces transcription of pro-inflammatory mediators that exacerbate neuronal injury. Critically, SIRT1 activity also orchestrates a phenotypic transformation of [[astrocytes]], driving the transition from the neurotoxic A1 phenotype—characterized by elevated C3 expression—to the neuroprotective A2 phenotype. This astrocytic phenotypic switch is coupled to enhanced synaptic recovery and improved neurological function, including restoration of cerebral blood flow and behavioral performance.¹ The specificity of SIRT1 in this pathway is confirmed by the observation that inhibition of SIRT1 with [[selisistat]] reverses all neuroprotective benefits of [[electroacupuncture]], demonstrating that SIRT1 is the essential target for therapy.

---

## Hepatic ischemia-reperfusion and age-related vulnerability

In hepatic ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI), SIRT1 function is compromised by age-related decline in [[nad|NAD+]] bioavailability, particularly within hepatic macrophages. Reduced SIRT1 activity impairs its capacity to deacetylate [[eif2a|eIF2α]], leading to uncontrolled eIF2α acetylation and subsequent activation of the integrated stress response in the endoplasmic reticulum.² This [[eif2a|eIF2α]]-driven endoplasmic reticulum stress precipitates a cascade of pro-inflammatory polarization in hepatic macrophages, increasing their production of proinflammatory cytokines including [[tnf-alpha|TNF-α]], [[il-1-beta|IL-1β]], and [[inos|iNOS]].² These cytokines amplify hepatocellular injury during the late phase of IRI, making aged donor organs particularly vulnerable to transplantation-induced damage. The age-dependent loss of SIRT1 function thus represents a major barrier to effective utilization of aged donor livers in transplantation.

---

## Therapeutic restoration via NAD+ supplementation

[[nmn|Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN)]] supplementation restores [[nad|NAD+]] levels in aged hepatic macrophages, directly reviving SIRT1 deacetylase activity.² NMN-driven SIRT1 restoration reduces [[eif2a|eIF2α]] acetylation, alleviates endoplasmic reticulum stress, and potently suppresses the expression of proinflammatory cytokines TNF-α, iNOS, and IL-1β.² By restoring SIRT1 function, [[nmn|NMN]] supplementation reverses pro-inflammatory macrophage polarization and significantly mitigates hepatic ischemia-reperfusion injury in aged organisms. This therapeutic strategy holds particular promise for improving the viability and long-term function of marginal aged donor livers in transplantation settings, where it directly addresses the mechanistic root of age-related vulnerability.
