**BCMA** (B-cell maturation antigen) is a [[tumor-necrosis-factor-receptor-superfamily|TNF receptor superfamily]] protein expressed on the surface of B cells and bone marrow plasma cells that serves as a pivotal therapeutic target in relapsed/refractory [[multiple-myeloma|multiple myeloma (RRMM)]]. As a ligand for B-cell activating factor (BAFF) and proliferation-inducing ligand (APRIL), BCMA drives plasma cell survival and immunoglobulin production, making it essential for malignant plasma cell homeostasis. The selective expression of BCMA on B cells and plasma cells with minimal off-target tissue distribution renders it an ideal therapeutic antigen for both antibody-drug conjugate and [[car-t-cells|CAR-T cell]] approaches. Despite substantial clinical benefit from BCMA-directed therapies, immune escape mechanisms and antigen-independent resistance remain significant barriers to achieving durable remissions in all patients.¹

---

## Clinical efficacy of BCMA-targeted antibody-drug conjugates

[[Belantamab mafodotin]], an antibody-drug conjugate delivering the microtubule-disrupting agent monomethyl auristatin F to BCMA-expressing cells, has demonstrated unprecedented efficacy when combined with conventional salvage therapy. In a phase 1/2 trial, belantamab mafodotin at a recommended dose of 1.9 mg/kg every 8 weeks was combined with carfilzomib, lenalidomide, and dexamethasone (KRd-b) in patients with RRMM who had received at least one prior line of therapy. Among 19 heavily pretreated patients, the overall response rate reached 89.5%, with 78.9% achieving very good partial response or better.¹ Critically, all patients achieving complete response attained minimal residual disease negativity, a marker strongly predictive of prolonged survival. At a median follow-up of 19.3 months, the combination delivered exceptional durability: 24-month progression-free survival and overall survival rates were 74.3% and 85.1%, respectively, substantially exceeding historical controls for this refractory population.¹

---

## CAR-T cell therapy and immune resistance

[[car-t-cells|BCMA-directed CAR-T cell therapy]] has demonstrated substantial clinical benefit in relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma, achieving deep remissions in patients refractory to conventional chemotherapy and proteasome inhibitor–based regimens. Engineered T cells expressing chimeric antigen receptors specific for BCMA recognize and eliminate malignant plasma cells with remarkable specificity. However, immune escape represents a critical clinical challenge limiting long-term durability. Malignant cells develop resistance through antigen downregulation, alterations in costimulatory ligand expression, and remodeling of the bone marrow microenvironment to suppress T cell function and persistence.²

---

## Combination strategies and CD74 synergy

Preclinical evidence suggests that dual targeting of [[bcma|BCMA]] and [[cd74|CD74]], another B-cell surface antigen, may circumvent immune-escape features and enhance CAR-T cell activity. [[cd74|CD74]] is frequently co-expressed with BCMA on myeloma cells and participates in antigen presentation and immunomodulation. Targeting both antigens simultaneously may broaden the epitope landscape, reduce the selective pressure favoring antigen-loss variants, and potentially engage additional immunological pathways that amplify CAR-T cell function.² This dual-antigen strategy is being explored as a mechanism to achieve deeper remissions and delay or prevent therapeutic resistance.

---

## Ocular toxicity and safety profile

The primary adverse event limiting [[belantamab-mafodotin|belantamab mafodotin]] tolerability is keratopathy, an ocular toxicity characterized by corneal epithelial degeneration. In the KRd-b combination study, keratopathy occurred in 94.7% of patients, yet was predominantly grade 1 to 2 in severity and reversible upon treatment completion or dose modification.¹ Grade ≥3 keratopathy developed in 31.6% of patients and was comparable to historical single-agent rates despite the 8-week dosing interval, indicating that combination therapy does not substantially increase severe ocular toxicity incidence.¹ Importantly, keratopathy did not mandate permanent treatment discontinuation in any patient, and ophthalmologic monitoring enables early detection and management. Other adverse events, including hematologic and gastrointestinal toxicities, were consistent with the known profiles of the underlying KRd chemotherapy regimen and did not represent unexpected complications from BCMA targeting.
