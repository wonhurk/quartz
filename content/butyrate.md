**Butyrate** is a short-chain fatty acid with dual therapeutic roles as a ferroptosis-driving coordination modulator and a microbiota-derived metabolite essential for intestinal homeostasis. In cancer immunotherapy, butyrate serves as a precision regulator of copper metal-organic frameworks, enabling redox-responsive nanocrystal formation that unleashes ferroptotic cell death in colorectal cancer through glutathione depletion and amplified lipid peroxidation.¹ Simultaneously, butyrate is produced endogenously by commensal bacteria and functions as an anti-inflammatory metabolite that strengthens epithelial barriers, suppresses macrophage-driven inflammation via [[hif1|HIF-1]] signaling, and protects against both age-related and inflammation-associated gut dysfunction.² In the context of [[colitis|ulcerative colitis]], butyrate can be chemically conjugated to [[poly-vinyl-alcohol|poly(vinyl alcohol) (PVA)]] nanoparticles for colon-targeted delivery, enabling sustained release of both the butyrate moiety and co-formulated therapeutics.³

---

## Mechanism

Butyrate exerts its ferroptotic effects through coordination modulation of [[hmoi|copper metal-organic frameworks (Cu-MOF)]], a principle exemplified by the engineered nanoplatform HMOI. In this system, butyrate-mediated coordination chemistry yields uniform, redox-responsive nanocrystals that exploit the elevated glutathione (GSH) pool characteristic of the tumor microenvironment.¹ Within tumor cells, the Cu-MOF catalyzes Cu(II)/Cu(I) cycling driven by intracellular GSH, simultaneously depleting the antioxidant reserve and amplifying lipid peroxidation—a lethal dual blow that triggers ferroptotic cell death. This redox activation is coupled to in situ synthesis of a novel xCT inhibitor (SLZC96) via bioorthogonal copper-catalyzed azide-alkyne cycloaddition (CuAAC), further intensifying redox stress and blocking the cystine/glutamate antiporter essential for GSH synthesis.¹ The platform is functionalized with hyaluronic acid to enhance CD44-mediated tumor accumulation, ensuring intratumoral SLZC96 activation and minimizing systemic exposure.

In the intestinal context, butyrate operates through a distinct anti-inflammatory signaling axis mediated by [[hif1|HIF-1]] suppression.² Butyrate, produced by commensal bacteria including Butyricimonas synergistica and Parabacteroides goldsteinii, is selectively enriched following dietary interventions such as [[neu5ac|N-acetylneuraminic acid (Neu5Ac)]] supplementation.² At the epithelial level, butyrate enhances tight-junction protein expression and stabilizes mucosal architecture, directly strengthening barrier integrity against luminal pathogens and inflammatory insults.² In immune cells, butyrate suppresses HIF-1α expression predominantly in macrophages, dampening pro-inflammatory polarization and promoting a tissue-reparative phenotype—an effect that extends to both aging-associated and dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced colitis contexts.²

For colon-targeted delivery in [[colitis|ulcerative colitis]], butyrate is covalently conjugated to [[poly-vinyl-alcohol|PVA]] nanoparticles embedded within an inulin hydrogel (the [[ua-pb-gel|UA@PB/Gel]] system).³ The colonic esterase environment cleaves the butyrate-PVA linkage, triggering localized release of both free butyrate and any co-encapsulated therapeutic—for example, ursolic acid in the UA@PB/Gel formulation.³ This esterase-responsive mechanism coupled with inulin's colonic degradation ensures sustained and site-specific delivery, minimizing systemic exposure while amplifying anti-inflammatory effects in the diseased colon.

## Cancer immunotherapy applications

Butyrate-modulated Cu-MOF nanocrystals (HMOI) represent an emerging ferroptosis platform that simultaneously disrupts cancer cell redox defenses and strengthens antitumor immune responses in colorectal cancer.¹ The ferroptotic damage induced by [[hmoi|HMOI]] is accompanied by immunogenic stress signaling, triggering the release of damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) that activate dendritic cells and promote CD8+ T-cell infiltration into the tumor microenvironment. In vivo studies demonstrated that [[hmoi|HMOI]] treatment significantly increased CD8+ T-cell infiltration and interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) production, reflecting enhanced antitumor immune activity operating in concert with ferroptotic pressure.¹ The combination of redox disruption and immune activation resulted in significant tumor regression while systemic toxicity remained minimal—a favorable safety profile attributable to the tumor microenvironment-selective activation of the butyrate-modulated platform.

## Intestinal homeostasis and colitis treatment

Beyond cancer, butyrate plays a central physiological role in maintaining intestinal barrier integrity and resolving inflammation in aging and inflammatory bowel disease.² Commensal bacteria enriched by dietary supplements produce elevated fecal butyrate concentrations, which locally suppress macrophage infiltration and polarization toward pro-inflammatory phenotypes while preserving tissue-reparative macrophages.² At the epithelial level, butyrate enhances the expression of tight-junction proteins, maintaining mucosal architecture and preventing bacterial translocation in both age-related and DSS-induced [[colitis]] models.² Mechanistically, butyrate-mediated suppression of [[hif1|HIF-1α]] in inflammatory cells is a key driver of these anti-inflammatory effects; colonic [[hif1|HIF-1α]] protein signals, predominantly localized to macrophages and immune infiltrates, are significantly reduced following butyrate enrichment.²

In [[colitis|ulcerative colitis]], the [[ua-pb-gel|butyrate-conjugated PVA nanoparticle-inulin hydrogel system (UA@PB/Gel)]] provides a clinically relevant delivery platform that amplifies butyrate's therapeutic benefit.³ In a dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced [[colitis|colitis]] model, [[ua-pb-gel|UA@PB/Gel]] effectively alleviated colonic inflammation, restored epithelial barrier integrity, reduced pro-inflammatory cytokine expression, and modulated gut short-chain fatty acid metabolism—all while demonstrating minimal systemic toxicity.³ This composite system, composed entirely of Generally Recognized As Safe (GRAS) materials, exemplifies how butyrate chemistry can be precisely engineered to maximize local anti-inflammatory efficacy in the diseased intestine while minimizing off-target effects.

## Microbiota-derived production and regulation

Butyrate production by commensal bacteria is a critical driver of intestinal health, and selective enrichment of butyrate-producing taxa is achievable through dietary interventions. Specific bacterial species including Butyricimonas synergistica and Parabacteroides goldsteinii are selectively enriched by dietary [[neu5ac|N-acetylneuraminic acid (Neu5Ac)]], a sialic acid abundant in bird's nest and human milk.² Enrichment of these taxa directly correlates with increased fecal butyrate concentrations without global disruption of microbial diversity, indicating a targeted mechanism of microbiota modulation.² This selectivity is clinically relevant, as butyrate-producing bacterial populations appear to be depleted in aging and inflammatory bowel disease; restoring them through dietary supplementation represents a rational strategy for re-establishing intestinal homeostasis in these conditions.
