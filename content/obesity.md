**Obesity** is a complex metabolic disease characterized by persistent adipose tissue expansion and systemic metabolic dysfunction, typically triggered or exacerbated by high-fat diet consumption. At the molecular level, obesity involves profound metabolic reprogramming from oxidative phosphorylation via the TCA cycle to glycolytic pathways, reduced circulating [[igf-1|IGF-1]] levels, enhanced adipose tissue hypoxia, and dysregulation of ferroptosis pathways that drive multi-organ injury.¹ Elevated plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 ([[pai-1|PAI-1]]) promotes thrombosis and age-related morbidity, while gut microbiota dysbiosis mediates systemic complications including insulin resistance, hepatic steatosis, and cancer progression.²,³

---

## Pathophysiology

Transcriptomic analyses of obese human and murine hearts reveal that obesity-associated metabolic reprogramming is marked by a shift from TCA cycle oxidative metabolism to glycolysis, alongside disrupted fatty acid homeostasis and identification of ferroptosis as a key regulatory node in myocardial injury.¹ High-fat diet consumption leads to hyperleptinemia, hypertriglyceridemia, reduced plasma [[igf-1|IGF-1]], and glucose intolerance across both type 1 and type 2 diabetes contexts.¹,⁴ The enhanced hypoxic microenvironment of expanding adipose tissue creates a pathological niche that sustains chronic inflammation and metabolic derangement.² [[pai-1|PAI-1]], an inhibitor of fibrinolysis, is exponentially correlated with body weight in diet-induced obesity; hepatic PAI-1 mRNA expression in obese mice exceeds healthy controls by more than tenfold, contributing to thrombotic risk in age-related diseases.⁵

Gut microbiota dysbiosis plays a central mechanistic role in obesity pathogenesis. Neomycin-sensitive bacterial strains mediate anti-obesity effects through production of metabolites such as [[brassicasterol]], which upregulates hepatic [[apoa4|APOA4]] to facilitate lipid transport and suppress inflammation.⁶ Conversely, pathogenic shifts in microbial composition drive systemic complications, with high-fat diet reshaping microbial interactions to promote disease progression in distant organ systems.³

---

## Cardiovascular complications

High-fat diet-induced obesity causes cardiac hypertrophy with increased left ventricular dimensions and wall thickness, interstitial fibrosis, and contractile dysfunction characterized by lower fractional shortening, reduced ejection fraction, and intracellular calcium derangement.¹ The underlying mechanisms involve [[oxidative-stress|oxidative stress]], apoptosis, ferroptosis, and [[mitochondrial-dysfunction|mitochondrial injury]] marked by declined PGC1α and UCP-2 expression.¹ [[arachidylcarnitine|Arachidylcarnitine]] emerges as a top discriminative metabolite in obese patients, linking lipid metabolism dysregulation to cardiac pathology.¹ Elevated [[pai-1|PAI-1]] further exacerbates cardiovascular risk by promoting thrombus formation; one dose of siPAI-1 nanoparticles decreases thrombus weight after complete ligation of the inferior vena cava in both young and aged mice, and increases survival in aged mice four days post-ligation.⁵

---

## Hepatic progression

Obesity-associated hepatic steatosis advances to metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), increasing risk for cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma.⁸ The shift from oxidative to glycolytic metabolism disrupts hepatic fatty acid homeostasis, creating a lipotoxic environment that drives inflammation and fibrosis.¹ Neomycin-sensitive gut bacteria modulate hepatic metabolism by elevating [[brassicasterol]] levels, which upregulate [[apoa4|APOA4]] to improve lipid transport and glucose metabolism while suppressing inflammatory pathways.⁶ Liver biopsy remains the reference method for detecting MASH and grading steatosis, though its invasive nature limits repeated monitoring; CT-based composite biomarkers offer a non-invasive alternative for accurately identifying MASH and steatosis grade in people with obesity.⁸

---

## Cancer associations

Obesity and high-fat diet promote breast cancer stemness and progression through gut microbiota-dependent mechanisms.³ Dietary-induced alterations in gut microbial composition reshape metabolic pathways, with *Streptococcus mutans* driving leucine accumulation that enhances cancer cell stemness and metastatic potential.³ The interplay between microbial dysbiosis, metabolite production, and cancer progression highlights the gut-tumor axis as a critical pathway linking obesity to oncogenesis, though the precise mechanisms by which dietary-induced gut microbiota alterations influence cancer progression warrant further exploration.³

---

## Psychiatric comorbidity

Individuals with [[schizophrenia]] or [[bipolar-disorder|bipolar disorder]] face a significantly elevated risk of obesity, primarily driven by weight gain from [[psychiatric-medications|antipsychotic and mood-stabilizing medications]] and lifestyle factors.⁷ The application of digital self-monitoring tools remains underexplored in psychiatric populations despite their potential as scalable solutions for weight management.⁷ [[mobile-health-assisted-weight-management-intervention|Mobile health-assisted weight management interventions]] offer preliminary effectiveness and implementation feasibility for patients with severe mental illness, addressing gaps in care for this high-risk population.⁷

---

## Therapeutic approaches

Emerging therapeutic strategies target multiple pathways implicated in obesity pathogenesis. Cardiac-specific [[igf-1|IGF-1]] overexpression mitigates high-fat diet-induced myocardial remodeling, contractile dysfunction, [[mitochondrial-dysfunction|mitochondrial injury]], and ferroptosis, restoring metabolic homeostasis without affecting systemic insulin sensitivity.¹ Hypoxia-activated phototheranostic probes ([[cbh-o|CBH-O]]) enable targeted photoacoustic imaging of visceral adipose tissue followed by photothermal modulation that induces white adipose tissue browning, enhances lipolysis, and alleviates inflammation, leading to 15.6% body weight reduction, 65% adipose tissue mass reduction, and significant decreases in serum cholesterol, triglyceride, and insulin levels.² 

[[pai-1|PAI-1]]-silencing RNA therapeutics delivered via siRNA-lipid nanoparticles achieve 90% knockdown of plasma PAI-1 lasting ten days after administration, with 70% knockdown in obese mice, normalizing supraphysiologic PAI-1 concentrations and prolonging life span.⁵ [[semaglutide|Semaglutide]] at 1 mg per week enables insulin dose reduction in adults with type 1 diabetes and obesity using automated insulin delivery systems, demonstrating a relationship between insulin dose reduction and weight loss.⁴ Microbiota-targeted interventions using [[cordyceps-militaris-polysaccharide|Cordyceps militaris polysaccharide (CMP)]] restore gut microbial homeostasis in a manner dependent on neomycin-sensitive bacteria, with [[brassicasterol]] treatment alone replicating CMP's anti-obesity effects through [[apoa4|APOA4]]-mediated lipid transport and inflammatory suppression.⁶
