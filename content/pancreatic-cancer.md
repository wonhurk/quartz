**Pancreatic cancer** is a highly aggressive solid tumor malignancy characterized by intrinsic therapeutic resistance, poor clinical outcomes, and a complex tumor microenvironment that shields malignant cells from conventional chemotherapy and immune surveillance.¹ With a five-year survival rate below 12%, pancreatic cancer remains the third leading cause of cancer-related death in the United States despite decades of therapeutic development.² Recent advances have illuminated multiple targetable molecular vulnerabilities, including cancer-specific genomic instability inducible by CRISPR/Cas9, metabolic dependencies on glutamine and oxidative phosphorylation, tumor-associated antigens amenable to radioimmunotherapy and CAR T cell targeting, and DNA repair deficiencies exploitable by [[parp-inhibitors|PARP inhibitors]] in genetically defined patient subsets.³⁴⁵

---

## Precision genome targeting

CRISPR/Cas9 technology has been adapted as a cancer-specific treatment modality by inducing [[dna-double-strand-breaks|DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs)]] at sites harboring somatic mutations unique to pancreatic cancer cells.⁶ When nine genomic sites are simultaneously targeted using cancer-specific single guide RNAs, tumor growth inhibition exceeds 90%, driven by ongoing chromosomal rearrangements and chromosomal instability that peaks immediately before cell death.⁶ Remarkably, CRISPR/Cas9-induced DSBs are approximately three-fold more cytotoxic than an equivalent number of radiation-induced DSBs, with 87% of structural variants arising not directly from the initial breaks but from subsequent chromosomal catastrophe.⁶⁷ Cells that survive initial CRISPR/Cas9 targeting retain susceptibility to subsequent DSB induction at different genomic sites, achieving more than 87% additional growth inhibition and offering a sequential targeting strategy to eliminate resistant clones.⁶

## Immunotherapy approaches

Two distinct immunotherapeutic modalities have demonstrated robust preclinical efficacy in pancreatic cancer models. The cell-surface oncofetal antigen [[plap|placental alkaline phosphatase (PLAP)]] is overexpressed in pancreatic cancer and serves as a theranostic target for both positron emission tomography imaging and radioimmunotherapy.⁸ The humanized antibody H12F3 conjugated to zirconium-89 ([[zr89-dfo-h12f3]]) enables precise tumor localization with sustained uptake peaking at 120 hours, while the lutetium-177 conjugate ([[lu177-dota-h12f3]]) achieves complete suppression of subcutaneous xenograft growth at a dose of 3.7 MBq.⁸

Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cell therapy targeting B7-H3, a cell surface protein overexpressed across multiple solid tumors, has advanced to phase 1/2 clinical trials using murine-derived constructs.⁹ Fully human B7-H3-specific single-chain variable fragments generated by phage display overcome the immunogenicity limitations of murine sequences, with the lead candidate [[y111|Y111]] CAR demonstrating superior antitumor activity compared to the clinically advanced 376.96 and MGA271 constructs.⁹ In pancreatic cancer, neuroblastoma, and glioblastoma xenograft models, Y111 CAR T cells induce complete tumor responses, tumor rejection, and significant survival benefits while maintaining favorable tolerability profiles.⁹ Lipid nanoparticle delivery systems enable in vivo CAR T cell engineering, with CD19-targeting mRNA formulations achieving 2.7-fold greater CAR T cell generation and 2.3-fold enhanced tumor growth inhibition in pancreatic cancer models.¹⁰

## Metabolic vulnerabilities

Pancreatic cancer exhibits marked metabolic plasticity that paradoxically creates therapeutic opportunities when exploited through dietary and pharmacological interventions. A [[ketogenic-diet|ketogenic diet]] increases tricarboxylic acid cycle activity and elevates cellular reliance on glutamine-related metabolites in response to reduced glucose availability.² Combining glutamine metabolism inhibitors—[[cb-839|CB-839]] or [[6-diazo-5-oxo-l-norleucine|6-diazo-5-oxo-L-norleucine (DON)]]—with ketogenic dietary intervention produces robust antitumor effects in preclinical models, validating this metabolic vulnerability as a rational therapeutic strategy.²

The antidiabetic drug metformin exerts antiproliferative effects in pancreatic cancer by targeting ATP synthase subunit e (ATP5I), a protein essential for maintaining F₁F₀-ATP synthase dimer stability and mitochondrial cristae morphology.¹¹ Metformin disrupts ATP synthase oligomerization and inhibits oxidative phosphorylation, triggering accumulation of vestigial assembly intermediates, reduction of the NAD⁺/NADH ratio, and compensatory increases in glycolysis.¹¹ CRISPR-Cas9 inactivation of ATP5I phenocopies metformin treatment and confers resistance to biguanide antiproliferative effects, confirming ATP5I as the relevant cellular target.¹¹

Carbonic anhydrase isoforms IX and XII, cancer-associated enzymes that regulate tumor pH and hypoxia-driven survival, represent validated chemosensitization targets.¹² Bis-triazole-linked benzenesulfonamides exhibit potent and selective nanomolar inhibition of hCA IX (K_I = 25.1 and 28.1 nM for lead compounds 2g and 4g) and hCA XII (K_I = 35.2 and 11.6 nM) while sparing off-target isoforms hCA I and II.¹² These inhibitors significantly enhance the antiproliferative effects of azacitidine and sorafenib in pancreatic cancer cells under both normoxic and hypoxic conditions, overcoming therapeutic resistance mediated by tumor microenvironment acidification.¹²

## DNA repair-targeted therapy

PARP inhibitor niraparib demonstrates clinical efficacy in patients with advanced pancreatic cancer harboring pathogenic variants in homologous recombination repair genes, including [[brca2|BRCA1/2]], ATM, PALB2, and CHEK2.⁵ This genomically-guided precision medicine approach exploits synthetic lethality in DNA repair-deficient tumors, where PARP inhibition triggers catastrophic genome instability and cell death. The clinical validation of PARP inhibitors in platinum-sensitive pancreatic cancer with germline BRCA1/2 variants has established a biomarker-driven therapeutic paradigm, with ongoing investigations evaluating efficacy in broader populations with somatic DNA repair deficiencies.⁵

## Non-pancreatic contexts

Phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase 1 ([[pck1|PCK1]]), an enzyme canonically associated with gluconeogenesis, exhibits context-dependent oncogenic or tumor suppressor functions across different cancer types.¹³ In pancreatic cancer, PCK1 promotes cell proliferation, enhances colony formation, and stimulates DNA synthesis through interaction with Cyclin D3, establishing a mechanistic link to CDK4/6 inhibitor resistance similar to that observed in breast cancer but absent in liver cancer.¹³ This tissue-specific variability complicates therapeutic targeting but suggests that PCK1 inhibitors—including everolimus and auranofin—may overcome CDK4/6 inhibitor resistance when this molecular pathway is activated.¹³

---

## Novel agents and combination strategies

The collective evidence from precision genome editing, immunotherapy, metabolic inhibition, and DNA repair targeting underscores that overcoming pancreatic cancer's intrinsic therapeutic resistance requires multitarget combination approaches rather than single-agent strategies.⁴ CRISPR/Cas9-induced chromosomal instability achieves superior cytotoxicity compared to radiation while maintaining cancer specificity.⁶⁷ Fully human B7-H3 CAR constructs and [[plap|PLAP]]-targeted radioimmunotherapy expand the immunotherapeutic arsenal beyond checkpoint blockade.⁸⁹ Metabolic rewiring through [[ketogenic-diet|ketogenic diet]] combined with glutamine metabolism inhibitors ([[cb-839|CB-839]], [[6-diazo-5-oxo-l-norleucine|DON]]) exploits tumor metabolic dependencies, while carbonic anhydrase IX/XII inhibitors restore chemosensitivity in hostile tumor microenvironments.²¹² The clinical validation of [[parp-inhibitors|PARP inhibitors]] in genomically defined patient subsets with homologous recombination repair deficiencies establishes a precision medicine framework applicable to 5–10% of pancreatic cancer cases.⁵ These complementary mechanisms collectively address the multifaceted resistance biology of pancreatic cancer and provide a rational foundation for next-generation combination clinical trials.
