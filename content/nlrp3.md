**NLRP3** is a NOD-like receptor protein that assembles into a multiprotein inflammasome complex, orchestrating pro-inflammatory responses in acute tissue injuries.¹ The inflammasome recruits the adaptor [[asc|ASC]] and the protease [[caspase-1|Caspase-1]], enabling the proteolytic release of interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α).¹,³ NLRP3 inflammasome activation is amplified by reactive oxygen species accumulation and mitochondrial dysfunction, perpetuating tissue damage during ischemic and inflammatory stress.² Multiple pharmacological approaches targeting NLRP3 through distinct mechanisms—[[nadph|NADPH]] in ischemic stroke, [[phd1|PHD1]] degradation in acute liver injury, and [[sirt2|SIRT2]] agonism in myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury—have demonstrated convergent neuroprotective and cardioprotective effects, establishing NLRP3 as a high-priority therapeutic target.

---

## Mechanism

The NLRP3 inflammasome is a multiprotein signaling platform consisting of NLRP3, the adaptor protein [[asc|ASC]], and the cysteine protease [[caspase-1|Caspase-1]]. Upon sensing pathological danger signals—including reactive oxygen species, mitochondrial damage, and metabolic perturbations—NLRP3 undergoes conformational changes promoting its oligomerization and recruitment of ASC. ASC, in turn, recruits and activates Caspase-1, which catalyzes the proteolytic cleavage of pro-IL-1β and pro-TNF-α into their mature, bioactive forms. The resulting cytokine secretion drives the inflammatory cascade characteristic of acute tissue injury. NLRP3 inflammasome activation creates a positive feedback loop when coupled with mitochondrial dysfunction and ROS accumulation, perpetuating inflammation during ischemic insults and toxic challenges.

## Ischemic stroke

In acute ischemic stroke, [[nadph|NADPH]] (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate) has emerged as a potent neuroprotective agent targeting NLRP3 as one of five key molecular targets identified through network pharmacology analysis and molecular docking.¹ In a transient middle cerebral artery occlusion model, NADPH treatment at 7.5 mg/kg reduced infarct volume by approximately 50%, attenuated cerebral edema from 81% to 76%, and improved neurological function by 58%.¹ Mechanistically, NADPH suppressed NLRP3 inflammasome activation by reducing NLRP3 expression by roughly 14% and simultaneously decreasing [[asc|ASC]] and [[caspase-1|Caspase-1]] levels by 24% and 30%, respectively, thereby attenuating IL-1β release by 16%.¹ Beyond inflammasome inhibition, NADPH reinforced blood-brain barrier integrity by upregulating the tight junction protein zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1) by approximately 29% and reduced proteolytic mediators—matrix metalloproteinase-9 by 23% and caveolin-1 by 50%—thereby limiting vascular permeability and secondary edema formation.¹

## Acute liver injury

In acetaminophen-induced acute liver injury, the protein [[phd1|prolyl hydroxylase domain enzyme 1 (PHD1)]] has emerged as a critical metabolic regulator of NLRP3-driven hepatic inflammation. The compound [[sh-26|SH-26]], a proteolysis-targeting chimera, induces selective PHD1 degradation, which in turn suppresses the downstream inflammatory cascade.² SH-26-mediated PHD1 degradation attenuated NLRP3 inflammasome activation by suppressing reactive oxygen species accumulation and reversing mitochondrial dysfunction in hepatocytes.² This dual suppression of oxidative stress and inflammasome signaling provided robust in vivo protection against acetaminophen-triggered hepatic inflammation and necrosis.² The convergence of PHD1 inhibition with NLRP3 suppression suggests that metabolic regulation through hypoxia-inducible factor signaling downstream of PHD1 modulates inflammasome assembly, thereby linking cellular energy homeostasis to innate immune responses in liver injury.

## Myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury

In the heart, [[sirt2|SIRT2]] activation has proven effective at suppressing NLRP3-mediated inflammation during myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury. Salvianolic acid B (SaB), identified through proteomic analysis and molecular docking as a natural SIRT2 agonist, was encapsulated into nanoparticles to enhance cardiac accumulation and bioavailability.³ In myocardial ischemia-reperfusion models, the SaB nanoformulation reduced infarct area by 41%, outperforming conventional mitochondrial-targeted antioxidants.³ The cardioprotective mechanism operated through the SIRT2/NLRP3 pathway: SIRT2 activation suppressed NLRP3 inflammasome assembly and the downstream release of IL-1β and TNF-α, thereby attenuating the inflammatory cascade that drives reperfusion-mediated myocardial necrosis.³

---

## Convergent therapeutic principles

The success of NADPH in ischemic stroke, PHD1 degradation in acute liver injury, and SIRT2 agonism in myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury reveals a unifying principle: NLRP3 inhibition through biochemically distinct pathways—involving redox modulation, metabolic regulation via hypoxia signaling, and NAD+-dependent deacetylation—converges on suppression of inflammasome assembly and pro-inflammatory cytokine release. These three independent disease models, spanning the central nervous system, liver, and heart, demonstrate that NLRP3 functions as a universal molecular focal point for acute tissue injury. This convergent therapeutic efficacy positions NLRP3 as a high-priority target for developing broad-spectrum treatments across diverse acute inflammatory and ischemic conditions.
