**Sunitinib** is a multi-targeted tyrosine kinase inhibitor that represents the standard first-line therapy for advanced [[renal-cell-carcinoma|renal cell carcinoma (RCC)]] and [[lung-cancer|non-small cell lung cancer]], blocking the activities of [[pdgfr|PDGFR]], [[vegfr|VEGFR]], [[kit|KIT]], and other kinases critical to tumor angiogenesis and proliferation.¹ However, its clinical utility is severely limited by the inevitable emergence of acquired resistance, a process increasingly understood to center on metabolic rewiring that enables cancer cells to evade [[ferroptosis]]-induced cell death. Recent mechanistic studies reveal that TKI-resistant tumors upregulate the deubiquitinase [[usp20|USP20]], which stabilizes the ferroptosis suppressor [[gpx4|GPX4]], preventing its proteasomal degradation and maintaining cellular antioxidant capacity despite ongoing TKI exposure.¹ Disrupting the USP20–GPX4 axis through genetic deletion or pharmacological inhibition restores ferroptosis sensitivity, transforming TKI resistance from an insurmountable barrier into a druggable metabolic vulnerability.¹

---

## Mechanism and pharmacology

Sunitinib exerts its antitumor effects through competitive inhibition of the ATP-binding pocket in receptor tyrosine kinases, disrupting multiple signaling cascades simultaneously. Its broad selectivity profile encompasses [[pdgfr|PDGFR-α and PDGFR-β]], which drive tumor cell proliferation and survival, [[vegfr|VEGFR1–3]], the central regulators of tumor angiogenesis, and [[kit|KIT]], [[flt3|FLT3]], and [[ret|RET]], rendering sunitinib particularly effective in tumors dependent on these pathways. This multi-kinase approach establishes a dual mechanism: direct suppression of malignant cell proliferation combined with durable inhibition of neovascularization, explaining sunitinib's robust initial therapeutic responses across RCC, lung cancer, and gastrointestinal stromal tumors.

---

## Clinical indications

Sunitinib holds approval as first-line monotherapy for patients with [[renal-cell-carcinoma|advanced renal cell carcinoma]], where it significantly prolongs progression-free and overall survival compared to cytokine-based approaches.¹ Similarly, in [[lung-cancer|advanced non-small cell lung cancer]], particularly ALK-negative histologies, sunitinib demonstrated superior efficacy to conventional chemotherapy. The drug's early clinical adoption across these indications reflects its ability to overcome constitutive signaling that would otherwise drive relentless tumor growth; however, clinical durability remains constrained by the nearly universal emergence of resistance within 12–18 months of initiating therapy.

## Acquired resistance and ferroptosis evasion

The mechanism of TKI resistance has emerged as a paradigm of metabolic adaptation rather than simple genetic bypass. When TKI-resistant [[renal-cell-carcinoma|RCC]] and [[lung-cancer|lung cancer]] samples are interrogated, [[usp20|USP20]] expression is dramatically elevated compared to treatment-naive tumors.¹ USP20 acts as a critical post-translational regulator, binding directly to [[gpx4|GPX4]] and catalytically removing K48-linked polyubiquitin chains that normally target the ferroptosis suppressor for degradation by the proteasome. By sustaining GPX4 protein levels, USP20 preserves the tumor's antioxidant buffering capacity — specifically glutathione-dependent lipid peroxide detoxification — even as sunitinib attempts to trigger [[ferroptosis]] through nutrient starvation and oxidative stress. This axis is clinically relevant: co-overexpression of USP20 and GPX4 in RCC and lung cancer patient samples strongly correlates with poor prognosis and reduced time to progression.¹ Genetic ablation of USP20 in resistant cell lines completely reverses the ferroptosis-resistant phenotype, restoring sunitinib-induced cell death. Furthermore, pharmacological inhibition of USP20 resensitizes resistant tumors to sunitinib-induced ferroptosis in vivo, demonstrating proof-of-concept for this therapeutic axis.

## SBRT combination therapy in oligometastatic RCC

A phase 2 clinical trial evaluating sunitinib combined with stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) in treatment-naive [[oligometastatic-renal-cell-carcinoma|oligometastatic renal cell carcinoma]] reveals substantially improved outcomes compared to sunitinib monotherapy.² Patients receiving SBRT plus sunitinib achieved an objective response rate of 83.3%, compared to 29.2% in the monotherapy control arm — a clinically significant and statistically robust improvement (p < 0.001).² The median progression-free survival extended from 6.3 months with sunitinib alone to 17.3 months in the combination arm, with a hazard ratio of 0.42 favoring SBRT (95% confidence interval 0.21–0.84; p = 0.015).² At one year, the local control rate in the SBRT-treated lesions reached 91.5%, demonstrating durable ablation of oligometastatic burden. Safety remained manageable, with grade 3 toxicity rates nearly identical between groups (54.2% with SBRT combination versus 50.0% with monotherapy; p = 0.773), and patients in the SBRT arm reported trends toward improved quality of life, suggesting favorable tolerability despite multimodal treatment.² These findings establish SBRT as a powerful synergistic partner to sunitinib in the oligometastatic setting, possibly by triggering immunogenic cell death that primes tumors for TKI sensitivity or by eliminating angiogenesis-dependent sanctuary sites.

## Overcoming resistance through ferroptosis resensitization

The discovery that sunitinib resistance is fundamentally driven by ferroptosis evasion opens a novel therapeutic paradigm: instead of developing next-generation TKIs, the field can now target the USP20–GPX4 axis to restore drug sensitivity in resistant tumors. Pharmacological inhibitors of USP20, when combined with sunitinib in previously resistant xenografts, provoke marked tumor growth suppression and ferroptotic cell death.¹ This approach sidesteps the lengthy drug development cycle associated with creating new kinase inhibitors and leverages pre-existing TKI resistance as a biomarker for ferroptosis vulnerability. Ongoing clinical trials are evaluating whether USP20 inhibitors can restore sunitinib efficacy in advanced RCC and lung cancer patients who have previously progressed on TKI monotherapy, potentially transforming treatment-resistant disease into a manageable chronic condition.
