**Von Hippel-Lindau protein (VHL)** is an E3 ubiquitin ligase that serves as a critical molecular recruiter in proteolysis-targeting chimera (PROTAC) technology for selective and potent protein degradation. As a core component of the [[ubiquitin-proteasome-system|ubiquitin-proteasome system]], VHL catalyzes the polyubiquitination of target proteins brought into proximity by engineered PROTAC degraders, marking them for proteasomal destruction.¹ The protein has emerged as a validated and highly tunable E3 ligase platform for therapeutic development, with systematic optimization of linker chemistry enabling rational engineering of increasingly potent degraders with unprecedented sub-nanomolar activity.

---

## Mechanism

VHL functions as the key E3 ubiquitin ligase component within PROTAC-mediated protein degradation pathways. A PROTAC is a bifunctional small molecule containing a target-protein binding moiety on one end, connected through a chemical linker to a VHL-binding ligand on the other end. Upon PROTAC-mediated assembly of a ternary complex between the target protein and VHL, the E3 ligase catalyzes K48-linked polyubiquitin chain synthesis on the substrate, targeting it for 26S proteasome-mediated degradation.¹ The efficiency of this degradation process is critically dependent on the kinetic binding properties of the linker-ligand interaction with VHL, making linker architecture a primary design variable for rational PROTAC optimization. Importantly, VHL-mediated protein degradation requires both functional E3 ligase activity and an intact ubiquitin-proteasome system, establishing clear mechanistic constraints on degrader efficacy and selectivity.

## PROTAC design and linker engineering

Systematic optimization of PROTAC degraders recruiting VHL has been facilitated through high-throughput characterization studies using focal molography, a biophysical technique enabling simultaneous affinity measurements of hundreds of compounds in multiplexed format. Investigations into [[dna-protac|DNA-VHL ligand conjugates]]—synthetic precursors containing a DNA tag fused to VHL-binding ligands—revealed that amino acid linker hydrophobicity is the dominant structural parameter controlling VHL binding affinity.² Two libraries of 20 DNA-VHL compounds with diverse amino acid linkers were synthesized by solid-phase amide coupling and characterized in both singleplex and 20-plex multiplexed focal molography formats. The multiplexed approach achieved 20-fold higher experimental throughput while preserving strong structure-activity relationship correlations with lipophilicity, validating focal molography as a robust platform for PROTAC optimization studies.² These empirical findings establish design principles for rational engineering of linker-E3 ligase interactions and provide a roadmap for iterative improvement of VHL-recruiting degraders.

## Acute myeloid leukemia and ENL degradation

VHL-recruiting PROTAC degraders have achieved unprecedented potency in targeting the eleven-nineteen leukemia protein ([[enl|ENL]]), a critical chromatin-associated dependency in acute myeloid leukemia. The lead VHL-based ENL PROTAC degrader, [[ms108|MS108]], represents the culmination of comprehensive structure-activity relationship optimization and delivers transformative improvements over earlier-generation compounds. MS108 achieves 5.8-fold higher ENL degradation potency (DC₅₀ = 0.6 ± 0.05 nM) and 18-fold stronger antiproliferation activity (GI₅₀ = 1.19 ± 0.03 nM) compared to the first-generation MS41 degrader in MV4;11 AML cells.¹ Critically, MS108 induces robust and highly selective ENL degradation in a manner strictly dependent on VHL E3 ligase activity, functional [[ubiquitin-proteasome-system|ubiquitin-proteasome system]] machinery, dose, and time.¹ The compound also displays improved pharmacokinetic properties, positioning VHL-recruiting ENL degraders as compelling therapeutic leads for AML and validating the broader VHL-PROTAC platform for oncology.

## Modular platform for multiple protein substrates

Beyond ENL, VHL has been validated as an E3 ligase recruiter for degradation of other disease-relevant proteins, including [[hif-2-alpha|HIF-2α]], demonstrating its broad applicability as a modular platform for PROTAC development across multiple cancer types and indications. The mechanistic requirement for functional E3 ligase activity and proteasomal processing establishes clear constraints on degrader design, yet this specificity also enables highly selective degradation of intended targets with minimal off-target protein ubiquitination. Continued optimization of linker hydrophobicity, warhead binding affinity, and cellular permeability through iterative structure-activity relationship studies positions VHL as a cornerstone component of next-generation therapeutic PROTAC degraders, with potential applications extending across oncology and other protein-dependency-driven diseases.
