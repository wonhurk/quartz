**Acetylcholinesterase (AChE)** is a serine hydrolase that rapidly cleaves [[acetylcholine]] at cholinergic synapses and neuromuscular junctions, terminating acetylcholine-mediated signaling. Beyond its classical neurological role, AChE functions as a molecular hub integrating neuroimmune communication through the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway, which suppresses innate immune activation and chronic inflammatory cascades.¹ Inhibition of AChE by agents such as [[galantamine]] enhances acetylcholine accumulation and activates this anti-inflammatory axis, while reactivation of AChE poisoned by [[dichlorvos|organophosphate compounds]] through [[hi-6|HI-6]]-based interventions restores enzymatic function across the [[blood-brain-barrier|blood-brain barrier]] using brain-targeted nanoparticle systems.²

---

## Mechanism

AChE-mediated hydrolysis of [[acetylcholine]] is essential for precise temporal control of cholinergic neurotransmission. The enzyme's substrate specificity and rapid turnover rate (one AChE molecule hydrolyzes ~5000 acetylcholine molecules per second) allow fine regulation of synaptic signaling in both central and peripheral nervous systems. Beyond the synapse, AChE orchestrates neuroimmune communication by controlling the availability of [[acetylcholine]] for immune cell receptors, particularly the α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor on macrophages and dendritic cells. When acetylcholine engages these receptors, it triggers a cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway that suppresses the nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB) signaling cascade, dampening the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines including [[tumor-necrosis-factor-alpha|TNF-α]], [[interleukin-6|IL-6]], and [[interleukin-1-beta|IL-1β]]. Thus, AChE inhibition paradoxically reduces inflammation by elevating acetylcholine levels and activating immunosuppressive signaling.¹

## Autoimmune disease treatment

In [[systemic-lupus-erythematosus|systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)]], aberrant immune activation drives both systemic inflammation and secondary hypertension, with consequent renal injury and end-organ fibrosis. The cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway is often compromised in SLE, contributing to uncontrolled B cell and T cell activation. Long-term administration of the AChE inhibitor [[galantamine]] (3 mg/kg/day) in female NZBWF1 SLE mice markedly improved survival and reduced blood pressure through sustained elevation of acetylcholine.¹ This intervention also decreased multiple markers of renal injury, including urinary albumin excretion, kidney injury molecule-1 (KIM-1), and neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL), while simultaneously reducing renal fibrosis.¹ Circulating levels of soluble [[tumor-necrosis-factor-receptor-1|TNFR1]], a marker of systemic inflammation and a mediator in cardiovascular pathology, were substantially reduced in galantamine-treated SLE mice. The therapeutic effect extended to immune cell populations: galantamine suppressed splenic [[cd19-antigen|CD19+]] B cells and kidney-infiltrating [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cells]], supporting the hypothesis that AChE inhibition restores immune regulation through the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway.

## Organophosphate poisoning and reactivation

Organophosphate compounds such as [[dichlorvos|dichlorvos (DDVP)]] and pesticides irreversibly inhibit AChE by phosphorylating the enzyme's active-site serine residue, leading to catastrophic acetylcholine accumulation, cholinergic crisis, and neurological collapse. Reactivation of central nervous system AChE is the principal therapeutic goal in organophosphate poisoning but faces a critical barrier: the [[blood-brain-barrier|blood-brain barrier (BBB)]] severely restricts the penetration of hydrophilic AChE reactivators such as [[hi-6]].² The BBB challenge necessitates specialized delivery vehicles to achieve effective therapeutic concentrations of HI-6 in the brain. RVG-peptide-conjugated zeolitic imidazolate framework nanoparticles (RVG-PEG2000-HI-6@ZIF-8) were engineered to overcome this barrier by exploiting BBB-penetrating capacity through RVG peptide targeting.² These nanoparticles achieved an encapsulation efficiency of 80.74%, a particle size of 177.33 nm, and successful brain delivery, resulting in superior central AChE reactivation compared to conventional HI-6 administration.² Intranasal administration proved particularly effective, yielding an AChE reactivation rate of 48.35% in [[dichlorvos]]-poisoned mice—a substantial improvement over intravenous routes.² These advances underscore the importance of specialized brain-targeted delivery systems for restoring AChE function in the context of acute organophosphate toxicity.

## Neurodegenerative disease contexts

AChE's role in the cholinergic system extends to neurodegenerative disorders including [[alzheimers-disease|Alzheimer's disease]], where progressive cholinergic neuronal loss contributes to cognitive decline. While AChE inhibition is not itself sufficient to arrest neurodegeneration, restoration of central cholinergic neurotransmission through AChE inhibitors remains a symptomatic therapeutic strategy. The blood-brain barrier challenge encountered in organophosphate treatment similarly complicates the delivery of neuroprotective agents to the Alzheimer's brain, necessitating advanced formulation approaches such as chitosan-coated bilosomes and intranasal delivery systems to enhance brain bioavailability.

---

## Novel delivery systems

Advances in nanomedicine have enabled precise targeting of AChE-modulating drugs across the [[blood-brain-barrier|blood-brain barrier]]. RVG-peptide-conjugated zeolitic imidazolate frameworks successfully deliver the AChE reactivator [[hi-6]] to the central nervous system in organophosphate poisoning models, achieving superior reactivation kinetics compared to conventional antidotes.² Intranasal routes, which bypass extensive hepatic metabolism and afford direct olfactory-to-brain transport, demonstrate particular promise for both reactivation agents and neuroprotective molecules, establishing a paradigm for future CNS-targeted therapies leveraging AChE biology.
