**Caspase-1** is a pro-inflammatory cysteine protease that serves as the catalytic engine of the [[nlrp3|NLRP3 inflammasome]], a multi-protein signaling complex central to innate immune activation. Its primary function is to process and cleave pro-inflammatory cytokines [[il1b|interleukin-1β (IL-1β)]] and [[il18|interleukin-18 (IL-18)]] into their active, secreted forms, driving pyroptotic cell death and amplifying inflammatory cascades.¹ Beyond canonical inflammasome-mediated pyroptosis, Caspase-1 has emerged as a core orchestrator of [[panoptosis]] — a combinatorial form of programmed cell death integrating apoptotic, necroptotic, and pyroptotic pathways — making it a powerful target for both limiting pathological inflammation in stroke and overcoming chemotherapy resistance in cancer stem cells.² The pathway is negatively regulated by [[nadph]], whose elevation suppresses Caspase-1 expression and inflammasome-dependent cytokine production.¹

---

## NLRP3 inflammasome assembly and cytokine activation

The NLRP3 inflammasome is a multiprotein complex whose assembly culminates in Caspase-1 recruitment and activation. Upon sensing of damage-associated molecular patterns or pathogen-associated molecular patterns, [[nlrp3|NLRP3]] undergoes conformational change and recruits the adapter protein ASC and pro-Caspase-1, forming the canonical inflammasome structure. Once activated through oligomerization, Caspase-1 catalyzes the proteolytic cleavage of pro-IL-1β and pro-IL-18 into their mature, bioactive forms, enabling their secretion and paracrine signaling on tissue-resident and infiltrating immune cells.¹ This Caspase-1-driven activation of IL-1β is essential for initiating pyroptotic cell lysis, which further amplifies danger signaling and recruits innate lymphoid cells and monocytes to sites of injury or infection.

## Ischemic stroke and blood-brain barrier disruption

In the pathophysiology of ischemic stroke, NLRP3-Caspase-1 axis activation represents a critical mechanism linking initial cerebral ischemia to secondary neuroinflammatory damage. Following transient middle cerebral artery occlusion, NLRP3 inflammasome activity and Caspase-1-mediated IL-1β production drive blood-brain barrier (BBB) disruption through upregulation of matrix metalloproteinase-9 and caveolin-1, compromising tight junction integrity and permitting peripheral immune cell infiltration.¹ Conversely, suppression of Caspase-1 expression — achieved through elevation of [[nadph]] — preserves BBB integrity by reinforcing tight junction protein expression, including zonula occludens-1, while simultaneously reducing infarct volume, cerebral edema, and neurological deficit.¹ This dual protective mechanism — both preserving barrier function and limiting inflammation — positions Caspase-1 as a rational therapeutic target in acute ischemic stroke.

## PANoptosis and cancer stem cell vulnerability

Recent discovery that Caspase-1 functions as a master regulator of PANoptosis has revealed unprecedented therapeutic opportunities in chemotherapy-resistant malignancies. In renal cancer stem cells, activation of the Caspase-1-dependent PANoptosis pathway — driven by the microtubule-targeting nanocomposite ARDAP@SPION-PEI — triggers simultaneous engagement of apoptotic, necroptotic, and pyroptotic death mechanisms, overwhelming cellular survival programs.² This PANoptosis induction is directly coordinated by the transcription factor [[zinc-finger-protein-148|zinc finger protein 148 (ZFP148)]], which transactivates key Caspase-1-associated genes including [[gsdmd|gasdermin D (GSDMD)]], receptor-interacting serine/threonine kinase 3 (RIPK3), and the inflammasomal cytokines IL-1β and IL-18.² By engaging multiple, redundant death pathways simultaneously, Caspase-1-mediated PANoptosis bypasses the adaptive resistance mechanisms that permit renal cancer stem cells to evade conventional chemotherapy and radiotherapy, offering a pathway to overcome one of oncology's most intractable challenges.

---

## Therapeutic modulation and future directions

The dual role of Caspase-1 in both pathological inflammation and therapeutic cell death induction presents a therapeutic paradox amenable to context-dependent targeting. In neuroinflammatory disease, direct or indirect Caspase-1 inhibition via [[nadph]] elevation or inflammasome assembly blockade mitigates secondary ischemic damage while preserving neuroprotective innate immunity.¹ Conversely, in chemotherapy-resistant cancers, engineered nanoparticles and small molecules that activate Caspase-1-dependent cell death pathways — particularly the PANoptosis axis — show promise in renal, ovarian, and other cancer stem cell populations.² Strategic deployment of Caspase-1-targeted therapeutics thus depends upon disease context: suppression in acute ischemic stroke versus activation in malignancy. Future therapeutic development should focus on cell-type-selective and tissue-compartmentalized targeting strategies to maximize anti-inflammatory or anti-tumor efficacy while minimizing off-target effects.
