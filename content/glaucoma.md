**Glaucoma** is the leading cause of irreversible blindness worldwide, characterized by progressive degeneration of the optic nerve and loss of retinal ganglion cells. While current therapies predominantly target intraocular pressure (IOP) reduction, emerging evidence demonstrates that oxidative stress and inflammation within ocular tissues drive disease progression independently of IOP elevation.¹ The [[limk2|LIM kinase 2 (LIMK2)]] and its signaling partner [[cofilin]] represent key regulators of actin cytoskeleton dynamics, and their dysregulation is implicated in glaucomatous neurodegeneration.² Recent therapeutic innovations—including isoform-specific [[limk2|LIMK2]] protein degraders via PROTAC technology and hydrogen-releasing nanomaterials—address the mechanistic and biochemical underpinnings of glaucoma, offering a paradigm shift toward comprehensive disease management.

---

## Mechanism

Glaucoma pathogenesis involves a complex interplay of mechanical and biochemical mechanisms. Elevated intraocular pressure damages retinal ganglion cells through mechanical stress on the optic nerve head; however, accumulating evidence indicates that oxidative stress and inflammatory cascades in the retina and optic nerve drive progressive neuronal loss even in low-tension glaucoma, where IOP remains within normal ranges.¹ The [[limk2|LIM kinases (LIMK1 and LIMK2)]] phosphorylate and inactivate [[cofilin]], a regulator of actin filament depolymerization and cytoskeletal dynamics. Dysregulation of the LIMK/[[cofilin]] axis impairs synaptic plasticity and neuronal integrity, contributing to retinal ganglion cell death in glaucomatous tissues.² The cross-talk between pressure-induced mechanical signaling and oxidative radical accumulation amplifies neuronal vulnerability, suggesting that single-modality interventions targeting IOP alone are insufficient for comprehensive glaucoma management.

---

## LIMK2-Directed PROTAC Degraders

Conventional LIMK inhibitors target the kinase domain but do not address noncatalytic functions of these proteins, limiting therapeutic efficacy. Isoform-specific [[limk2|LIMK2]] degraders utilizing PROTAC (proteolysis-targeting chimera) technology represent a breakthrough approach, selectively targeting LIMK2 for ubiquitin-mediated proteasomal degradation while sparing [[limk1|LIMK1]].² These degraders achieve superior potency and selectivity compared to dual reversible LIMK1/2 inhibitors. The isoform specificity is driven by favorable orientation bias, lysine accessibility patterns, and enhanced ternary complex formation with ubiquitin ligase machinery.² By eliminating both catalytic and noncatalytic functions of LIMK2, these PROTACs comprehensively disrupt pathogenic signaling in glaucomatous neural tissues, offering a chemical probe framework for translating this mechanism into clinical therapeutics.

---

## Hydrogen-Based Antioxidant Therapy

Beyond kinase-directed approaches, hydrogen-releasing nanomaterials provide a complementary strategy addressing the oxidative and inflammatory pathology underlying glaucoma. Hydrogenated silicene nanosheets (HyNDs) are two-dimensional nanoagents composed of surface-modified hydrogenated silicene that self-decomposes under physiological conditions to release molecular hydrogen.¹ The sustained hydrogen release is prolonged by surface coating with d-α-tocopheryl polyethylene glycol succinate (TPGS), which acts as a shielding matrix.¹ HyNDs efficiently scavenge reactive oxygen species in ocular tissues and simultaneously suppress pro-inflammatory biomarkers, including downregulation of oxidative stress-associated gene expression in both cellular and animal glaucoma models.¹ Critically, HyNDs reduce intraocular pressure while demonstrating negligible cytotoxicity to retinal and optic nerve tissues, making them a biocompatible therapeutic option.¹ This hydrogen-mediated approach provides an additional layer of neuroprotection distinct from conventional IOP-lowering medications.

---

## Multi-Modal Glaucoma Management

The convergence of LIMK2 PROTAC degraders and hydrogen nanotherapy exemplifies an emerging paradigm in glaucoma treatment: simultaneous targeting of mechanical (IOP elevation) and biochemical (oxidative stress, inflammation, cytoskeletal dysregulation) pathogenic mechanisms. Rather than relying solely on IOP reduction, comprehensive glaucoma management increasingly incorporates antioxidant and anti-inflammatory interventions that address the intrinsic neurodegeneration of retinal ganglion cells and optic nerve axons.¹² This multimodal strategy holds promise for slowing or halting disease progression across the spectrum of glaucoma phenotypes, including high-tension and low-tension variants that show disparate IOP dynamics but share common oxidative and inflammatory pathology.
