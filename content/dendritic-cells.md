**Dendritic cells (DCs)** are professional antigen-presenting cells that orchestrate adaptive immunity through functionally distinct subtypes with divergent programs: type 1 conventional DCs (cDC1) specialize in cross-presentation of exogenous antigens to [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cells]] and constitute the primary cellular target for cancer immunotherapy, while type 2 conventional DCs (cDC2) execute homeostatic maturation programs that promote peripheral tolerance through TSLP receptor signaling and regulatory T cell (Treg) polarization.¹ ² DC maturation is potently activated through the cGAS-STING pathway, triggered by pattern recognition receptors including DNGR-1 (CLEC9A), and by damage-associated molecular patterns released during immunogenic cell death.³ ⁴ Recent therapeutic strategies harness DC biology through mRNA-based nanovaccines encoding immune-remodeling factors delivered via lipid nanoparticles, establishing DCs as central nodes for both initiating antitumor immunity and maintaining autoimmune tolerance.⁵

---

## Subtype specialization and function

The cDC1 subset mediates cross-presentation of exogenous antigens to CD8+ T cells through DNGR-1 (CLEC9A)-dependent recognition of immunogenic cargo.³ F-actin exposure on autophagosome surfaces following immunogenic cell death licenses DNGR-1 binding and promotes efficient uptake by cDC1, enabling robust priming of antigen-specific CD8+ T cell responses.³ In multiple cancer models, cDC1 activation correlates with tumor clearance rates approaching 90% when coupled with adjuvant signaling pathways.³ ⁵

In contrast, cDC2s execute a homeostatic maturation program characterized by coordinated upregulation of tolerance-associated transcripts including tissue-restricted self-antigens, Treg-polarization machinery, and the thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP) receptor complex comprising IL-7Rα and TSLPR.² Functionally, IL-7Rα⁺CCR7⁺ homeostatically matured cDC2s (DC2hm) exhibit potent capacity to convert naive CD4+ T cells into FOXP3+ regulatory T cells, establishing a critical mechanism of peripheral tolerance.² TSLP stimulation recapitulates the DC2hm phenotype in human ex vivo models and humanized mice, positioning this cytokine as a central regulator of DC-mediated tolerance and a potential therapeutic target for autoimmune disease.²

## cGAS-STING pathway activation

DC maturation is potently driven by cGAS-STING pathway stimulation, achieved through biomineralized nanoparticle delivery of double-stranded DNA and divalent cations including Mn²⁺.⁴ DNA-loaded manganese phosphate nanoadjuvants (DNA@MnP) exhibit pH-responsive release properties, achieving 72% Mn²⁺ and 78% dsDNA release at pH 5.5 within 12 hours.⁴ Following cytosolic delivery, released Mn²⁺ augments cGAS recognition of dsDNA, producing cascade amplification that significantly promotes DC maturation with 2.10-fold elevation in maturation rate compared to controls.⁴ This activation not only enhances antigen-specific humoral immunity — achieving 16-fold increases in IgG titers — but also elicits balanced Th1/Th2 responses and activates both innate and adaptive antitumor immunity.⁴

Ferroptosis-driven immunogenic cell death further amplifies cGAS-STING signaling by triggering release of damage-associated molecular patterns and generating type I interferon production, establishing durable immunological memory that effectively suppresses tumor metastasis.¹

## Immunogenic cell death and cargo recognition

Immunogenic cell death (ICD) releases tumor-associated antigens and damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) including [[hmgb1|HMGB1]] and calreticulin, driving DC maturation and subsequent CD8+ T cell immunity.³ ⁶ ⁷ However, ICD also generates suppressive by-products that limit therapeutic efficacy. By leveraging cellular autophagy, ICD-primed autophagosome engineering selectively enriches immunogenic cargo-containing autophagosomes (ICAPs) that co-package tumor-associated antigens and DAMPs while depleting inhibitory components.³ F-actin exposure on ICAP surfaces enables DNGR-1-dependent recognition by cDC1, promoting efficient uptake and cross-presentation.³ ICAP-mediated lysosomal escape potentiates CpG-TLR9 signaling, creating amplification of adaptive immunity and achieving approximately 90% tumor clearance in peritoneal metastasis mouse models.³

In triple-negative breast cancer, rosmarinic acid/selenium nanoparticles (RA-Se@M) trigger robust ICD evidenced by calreticulin exposure, HMGB1 release, and DC maturation, significantly suppressing tumor growth and promoting profound intratumoral infiltration of cytotoxic T cells when combined with anti-PD-1 therapy.⁷ Similarly, flexoelectricity-boosted piezoelectric materials induce severe ICD under ultrasonic stimulation, triggering DC maturation and T cell activation that achieves 83.7% tumor inhibition and prevents lung metastasis.⁶

## mRNA nanovaccine platforms

mRNA-based nanovaccines deliver immune-remodeling factors or tumor antigens to DCs via lipid nanoparticles, driving potent antitumor immunity.⁵ ⁸ Immune-remodeling mRNAs encoding NF-κB-inducing kinase (NIK) or interferon regulatory factor 8 (IRF8) activate antigen-presenting cells in tumors, significantly increasing activated cDC1 populations, immunostimulatory cytokines, and antitumor CD8+ T cell priming.⁵ IR-mRNA-loaded LNPs elicit durable antitumor responses in multiple syngeneic mouse tumor models through both intratumoral and intravenous delivery.⁵ Coadministration of IR-mRNA with ovalbumin mRNA produces approximately 10-fold increases in antigen-specific CD8+ T cell responses and sustained long-term memory, effectively preventing tumor growth in vaccinated mice.⁵

Antigen-angiotensin II fusion mRNA nanovaccines enhance tumor antigen immunogenicity by promoting cDC1 maturation and increasing the cDC1 subpopulation, together enhancing antigen presentation function and eliciting potent antigen-specific CD8+ T cell responses.⁸ Vaccination with antigen-ANG II mRNA nanovaccines expressing different tumor antigens effectively inhibits tumor growth across multiple mouse models, an effect greatly compromised in cDC1-deficient Batf3⁻/⁻ mice, confirming the cDC1-dependence of this therapeutic mechanism.⁸

## Lipid droplet-mediated regulation

Lipid droplet accumulation paradoxically inhibits DC maturation and CD8+ T cell expansion while preserving memory T cell responses, providing negative feedback on excessive inflammation.¹ This regulatory mechanism enables control of side effects from type I interferon-driven inflammation in the late stages of immunotherapy, as accumulated lipid droplets suppress DC maturation and downstream CD8+ T cell expression without affecting memory T cell populations.¹ Excessive type I interferon is ultimately degraded by acidic hydrolases in lysosomes, preventing local inflammation while maintaining durable immunological memory.¹

---

## Peripheral tolerance and autoimmune regulation

DC-mediated peripheral tolerance through TSLP-driven cDC2 homeostatic maturation represents a critical regulatory mechanism in autoimmune disease.² The DC2hm phenotype exhibits coordinated expression of tolerance-associated transcripts, regulatory T cell polarization machinery, and the TSLP receptor complex, converting naive CD4+ T cells into FOXP3+ Treg cells with high efficiency.² Multi-omics analysis of human splenic DC maturation trajectories reveals this homeostatic program as a dominant pathway in cDC2 biology, positioning DC2hm as potential mediators of human peripheral tolerance and providing mechanistic insights for tolerance-inducing therapeutic strategies in autoimmune disease.²
