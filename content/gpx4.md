**Glutathione Peroxidase 4 (GPX4)** is a selenoprotein-dependent antioxidant enzyme that catalyzes the reduction of lipid peroxides and hydrogen peroxide using glutathione (GSH) as the reducing substrate, serving as the critical molecular brake on [[ferroptosis]], an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death.¹ GPX4 downregulation is a hallmark event driving ferroptotic cell death across multiple disease contexts, including pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, hepatocellular carcinoma, and high-altitude pulmonary edema. Conversely, elevated GPX4 expression is a key mechanism by which tumors maintain intrinsic ferroptosis resistance, making GPX4 status a pivotal determinant of therapeutic efficacy in ferroptosis-inducing strategies.

---

## Mechanism in ferroptosis

GPX4 prevents ferroptosis by catalyzing the reduction of lipid peroxides (LPO) that accumulate in cell membranes when polyunsaturated fatty acids undergo iron-catalyzed oxidation.¹ The enzyme operates in concert with the cystine/glutamate antiporter [[slc7a11|SLC7A11]], which imports cystine for GSH synthesis; both factors must be functionally intact to maintain the cellular redox balance.² Downregulation or inactivation of GPX4 depletes the cell's capacity to detoxify lipid peroxides, causing their accumulation and driving ferroptotic cell death. Single-cell transcriptomic studies have revealed that GPX4 dysregulation occurs primarily in endothelial and epithelial cell populations under stress conditions, making these cell types particularly vulnerable to ferroptosis when GPX4 is suppressed.¹

## Cancer applications

Therapeutic exploitation of GPX4 inhibition has emerged as a powerful strategy across multiple malignancies where conventional chemotherapy fails. In [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma]], supramolecular nanocomposites that downregulate both GPX4 and [[slc7a11]] concurrently deplete intracellular GSH and trigger robust ferroptosis while simultaneously activating immunogenic cell death. These ferroptosis-inducing nanotherapies exhibit synergistic efficacy when combined with [[anti-pd-l1-antibody|anti-PD-L1 antibodies]], establishing durable immunological memory and substantially improving tumor control.² In [[pancreatic-cancer|pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)]], GPX4 is identified as one of six core ferroptosis-related genes (alongside [[ctsb]], [[nox4]], [[tfrc]], [[hif-1a]], and [[tgfb1]]) significantly enriched in ferroptosis and hypoxia-inducible factor signaling pathways, making it a high-priority therapeutic target.³ Small molecules including [[resveratrol]] effectively downregulate GPX4 protein expression in PDAC, suppress tumor cell viability, and enhance ferroptosis sensitivity in vitro.

---

## Ferroptosis resistance and therapeutic bypass

Tumors exhibit intrinsic ferroptosis resistance by maintaining elevated GPX4 expression as part of a broad antioxidant defense program. Metformin overcomes this resistance by simultaneously disabling both GPX4-mediated lipid peroxide detoxification and [[slc7a11]]-dependent GSH synthesis, thereby sensitizing tumor cells to ferroptosis even under hypoxic conditions where conventional ferroptosis induction fails.⁴ In this context, metformin works synergistically with lactate oxidase, which converts abundant intratumoral lactate (produced via the Warburg effect) into hydrogen peroxide, further driving iron-dependent lipid peroxidation. This dual mechanism—suppressing endogenous antioxidant defenses while exogenously generating ferroptotic triggers—represents a rational approach to circumvent GPX4-mediated tumor resistance.

## Pulmonary hypoxia and immunological consequences

In the context of [[high-altitude-pulmonary-edema|high-altitude pulmonary edema (HAPE)]], GPX4 downregulation in pulmonary endothelial cells under hypoxic conditions initiates ferroptosis and promotes [[nets|neutrophil extracellular trap (NET)]] formation, exacerbating tissue damage and edema.¹ GPX4 modulates NET formation through multiple signaling axes, including [[nrf2|Nrf2]] nuclear translocation, ERK1/2 and NF-κB phosphorylation, and the [[hmgb1|HMGB1]]-TLR4/MyD88 signaling cascade. Therapeutic augmentation of GPX4 levels effectively attenuates pulmonary edema, restores lung function, and suppresses markers of both ferroptosis and NETosis, establishing GPX4 as a target for mitigating hypoxia-driven pathology.¹ This mechanism also implicates GPX4 in [[lung-cancer]] pathogenesis and suggests that ferroptosis-inducing therapies must account for the differential GPX4 status across diverse lung cell populations.

---

## Coordination with selenoprotein and antioxidant networks

GPX4 functions within a broader antioxidant ecosystem that includes [[thioredoxin-reductase]] and other selenoproteins. [[selenium-nanoparticles]] can potentiate ferroptosis by reducing overall selenoprotein bioavailability, thereby suppressing GPX4 activity. Similarly, natural compounds including [[quercetin]] and [[resveratrol]] synergize with ferroptosis induction by attenuating GPX4 expression alongside other antioxidant nodes.³ The transcriptional control of GPX4 intersects with [[hif-1a|HIF-1α]] signaling; under hypoxia, GPX4 dysregulation is partially mediated through HIF-dependent transcriptional repression, creating a vicious cycle where hypoxia both impairs GPX4 function and promotes ferroptosis.

---

## Tumor immunogenicity and checkpoint blockade

Ferroptosis triggered by GPX4 downregulation is immunogenic, releasing [[hmgb1|damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs)]] including HMGB1 and inducing dendritic cell activation that promotes [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cell]] infiltration.² This immunogenic dimension makes GPX4-targeting strategies uniquely synergistic with immune checkpoint inhibition. In hepatocellular carcinoma, the combination of ferroptosis-inducing nanocomposites (which suppress GPX4 and [[slc7a11]]) with [[anti-pd-l1-antibody|anti-PD-L1 antibodies]] substantially enhances tumor control and establishes immunological memory, outperforming either modality alone.

---

## Clinical and therapeutic implications

GPX4 status has emerged as a critical biomarker predicting both ferroptosis sensitivity and immunotherapy responsiveness. Tumors with elevated baseline GPX4 expression exhibit intrinsic resistance to ferroptosis-inducing agents unless GPX4 suppression is achieved through combination strategies such as metformin co-administration. Conversely, GPX4 downregulation in response to hypoxia or therapeutic stress can paradoxically drive pathological ferroptosis in non-malignant tissues (notably pulmonary endothelium), necessitating tissue-selective drug delivery or GPX4-sparing therapeutic approaches for conditions such as HAPE. Future therapeutic development should focus on identifying pharmacological GPX4 inhibitors that achieve tumor-selective suppression while sparing protective GPX4 function in vital organs.
