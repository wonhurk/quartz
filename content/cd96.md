**CD96** is an immune checkpoint protein expressed on tumor-infiltrating [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cells]] that drives exhaustion and dysfunction when engaged by its ligand [[cd155|CD155]], which is enriched on quiescent [[persister-tumor-cells|persister tumor cells (PTCs)]].¹ The CD155–CD96 axis represents a critical vulnerability in chemotherapy-resistant colorectal cancer: [[cd155]] expression on PTCs that survive [[neoadjuvant-therapy|neoadjuvant chemotherapy]] engages CD96 on infiltrating CD8+ T cells, promoting a dysfunctional phenotype characterized by mitochondrial dysfunction and suppressed anti-tumor activity.¹ CD96 blockade or genetic depletion diverts CD8+ T cells away from exhaustion and restores memory-like phenotypes with enhanced metabolic and effector capacity, establishing CD96 as a target for overcoming post-treatment immunosuppression.

---

## Mechanism

The CD155–CD96 interaction represents a ligand–receptor checkpoint axis within the tumor microenvironment of chemotherapy-treated colorectal cancer. Quiescent PTCs that persist after [[neoadjuvant-therapy|neoadjuvant chemotherapy]] express elevated levels of [[cd155]], which engages CD96 on infiltrating CD8+ T cells.¹ This engagement initiates a signaling cascade that enforces an exhaustion program, characterized by downregulation of mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, reduced glycolytic capacity, and diminished production of cytotoxic effector molecules. The metabolic constraint imposed by CD96 engagement parallels the dysfunction observed in chronic viral infections and other chronic antigen exposure contexts, wherein checkpoint proteins restrict T cell function to prevent autoinflammation. In the PTCs microenvironment, however, this mechanism allows immunologically silent tumor cells to evade immune surveillance despite the presence of tumor-reactive CD8+ T cells.

---

## CD96 blockade reverses T cell exhaustion

Anti-CD96 monoclonal antibody therapy effectively reverses CD8+ T cell exhaustion in the context of chemotherapy-resistant colorectal cancer.¹ Upon CD96 blockade, tumor-infiltrating CD8+ T cells undergo a phenotypic reorientation characterized by enhanced mitochondrial function, increased glucose metabolism, and restoration of cytotoxic granule expression. CD96 depletion simultaneously promotes differentiation toward memory-like CD8+ T cell phenotypes with superior proliferative potential and durable anti-tumor capacity.¹ The memory-like state induced by CD96 blockade is mechanistically distinct from terminal effector differentiation: these cells retain central memory markers while acquiring enhanced mitochondrial function and reduced metabolic constraints, positioning them for sustained tumor surveillance and long-term disease control.

---

## CD96-deficient CAR-T cells against persister tumors

The therapeutic power of CD96 targeting extends to engineered T cell therapies. Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cells engineered to lack CD96 expression and targeting [[epcam|epithelial cell adhesion molecule (EpCAM)]] — a surface marker enriched on [[persister-tumor-cells|PTCs]] — demonstrate remarkable therapeutic efficacy against chemotherapy-resistant colorectal cancer.¹ EpCAM-targeted CD96-deficient [[car-t]] cells simultaneously recognize PTCs through engineered receptor specificity and evade the immunosuppressive CD155–CD96 checkpoint, thereby combining antigen specificity with checkpoint resistance in a single cellular product. This approach overcomes two major barriers to CAR-T efficacy in solid tumors: the specificity gap in CAR-T target selection and the microenvironmental exhaustion imposed by endogenous checkpoint engagement. The combination of EpCAM targeting with CD96 deficiency enables robust PTC eradication and durable tumor control in preclinical models.

---

## Persister tumor cells and therapy resistance

The CD96–CD155 axis is particularly relevant in the context of [[neoadjuvant-therapy|neoadjuvant chemotherapy]]-induced tumor heterogeneity. Although [[neoadjuvant-therapy|neoadjuvant chemotherapy]] achieves significant tumor cytoreduction in colorectal cancer, a subset of [[persister-tumor-cells|PTCs]] escape direct cytotoxic effects through quiescence and slow cycling. These PTCs exhibit aggressive stem-like traits and establish an immunosuppressive microenvironment through elevated [[cd155]] expression, thereby engaging CD96 on tumor-infiltrating T cells and creating a dual barrier to immune-mediated elimination: both intrinsic (quiescence-driven) and extrinsic (checkpoint-mediated) resistance mechanisms. The identification of CD96 as a checkpoint enforced specifically in the PTC microenvironment reveals a previously unrecognized therapeutic vulnerability, exploitable by either checkpoint blockade or engineered cellular therapies deficient in CD96 function.
