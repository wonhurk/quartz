**Lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC)** is a distinct subtype of [[non-small-cell-lung-cancer|non-small cell lung cancer]] characterized by aberrant activation of multiple signaling pathways, particularly [[wnt3|Wnt3]]-driven [[wnt-signaling|Wnt/β-catenin signaling]] and [[pi3k|PI3K]] pathway dysregulation. In the context of [[idiopathic-pulmonary-fibrosis|idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF)]], LUSC arises from [[at2-cells|alveolar type 2 (AT2) cell]]-derived metaplastic basal cells and exhibits heightened susceptibility to metastasis through aberrant PI3K activation and [[cancer-associated-fibroblasts|cancer-associated fibroblast (CAF)]]-mediated paracrine signaling.¹ The disease is further characterized by enriched [[nrf2|NRF2]]-linked antioxidant responses that promote stress adaptation and enable escape from oxidative damage, a feature directly linked to the fibrotic microenvironment of IPF.

---

## Mechanism and pathogenesis

LUSC within IPF originates from a distinctive cellular lineage. Multi-omics analysis, including single-cell RNA sequencing and digital spatial profiling, revealed that AT2 cells undergo transdifferentiation into metaplastic basal cells within usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) tissue. These basal cells, in turn, give rise to LUSC tumors, establishing a direct molecular link between pulmonary fibrosis and carcinogenesis.¹ 

Both fibrotic lung tissue and associated LUSC tumors display robust activation of [[wnt-signaling|Wnt signaling pathways]], with [[wnt3|WNT3]] as the dominant transcriptional driver.¹ This pathway hyperactivation confers a stress-adapted phenotype that enables cancer cells to tolerate the oxidative milieu of the fibrotic lung. Paralleling this pathway activation, LUSC within IPF shows marked enrichment of [[nrf2|nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (NRF2)]]-dependent antioxidant response genes, reflecting an adaptive reprogramming that dampens oxidative sensitivity and promotes survival under oxidative stress.¹ This dual pathway engagement — Wnt3-driven proliferation combined with NRF2-mediated antioxidant buffering — creates a permissive microenvironment for malignant transformation in the fibrotic lung.

[[pi3k|PI3K signaling]] aberrations are independently frequent in LUSC and constitute a major driver of metastatic progression. In advanced-stage patients, elevated PI3K pathway activation is strongly correlated with increased metastatic burden and poor clinical outcomes. The pathway is activated both intrinsically within cancer cells and extrinsically through paracrine signaling from [[cancer-associated-fibroblasts|CAFs]].² CAFs infiltrating LUSC tumors secrete pro-migratory factors including [[hepatocyte-growth-factor|hepatocyte growth factor (HGF)]] and activate PI3K signaling in adjacent tumor cells, promoting migration and invasion. This crosstalk also supports CAF trans-differentiation and proliferation, establishing a self-reinforcing tumor-promoting feedback loop.²

## Therapeutic strategies

[[pi3k|PI3K α inhibition]] has emerged as a rational therapeutic strategy in LUSC. [[cyh33|CYH33]], a highly selective PI3K α inhibitor in clinical trials, dose-dependently suppresses LUSC cell motility by blocking PI3K signaling and disrupting cytoskeletal integrity.² In vivo studies demonstrated that oral CYH33 significantly attenuates metastasis in orthotopic xenograft models, with mechanistic analyses revealing decreased CAF infiltration into primary tumors. Notably, CYH33 concurrently suppresses CAF trans-differentiation and proliferation, reducing HGF secretion and thereby interrupting the CAF-mediated paracrine loop that sustains tumor dissemination.²

Disruption of the [[wnt-beta-catenin-pathway|Wnt/β-catenin pathway]] offers complementary therapeutic potential. Targeting this pathway in stress-adapted LUSC cell lines restores sensitivity to oxidative damage, effectively reversing the NRF2-driven antioxidant adaptation that normally protects against oxidative stress.¹ This approach is particularly relevant in the IPF-associated LUSC context, where Wnt3 hyperactivation is a defining feature of both the fibrotic and malignant compartments.

For resectable stage II–IIIA squamous NSCLC, perioperative immunotherapy combining [[serplulimab|serplulimab]] with taxane-based chemotherapy ([[nab-paclitaxel]] or paclitaxel) and carboplatin has been explored in phase II trials.³ This simplified four-cycle perioperative regimen seeks to leverage checkpoint blockade to enhance immune control of residual disease while maintaining feasibility and tolerability around surgery.
