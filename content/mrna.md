**mRNA therapeutics** are nucleotide-based drugs delivered via [[lipid-nanoparticles|lipid nanoparticles (LNPs)]] to enable in vivo cell engineering and functional cell reprogramming across tissues. Unlike traditional nucleotide therapies, mRNA-LNP platforms permit organ-specific targeting through rational design of ionizable lipid scaffolds, achieving both high delivery efficiency and sustained therapeutic efficacy across repeated administrations.¹²³⁴ The modular nature of mRNA payloads — encoding antitumor receptors, immunomodulatory cytokines, or cell-survival proteins — positions these therapeutics as versatile tools for precision medicine applications spanning immunotherapy, inflammatory disease, and oncology.

---

## Mechanism and delivery optimization

mRNA-LNP therapeutics operate through a two-stage process: ionizable lipids at physiological pH neutralize mRNA and form nanoparticle cores, which subsequently traverse cellular membranes via receptor-mediated or non-specific endocytosis. Once internalized, mRNA is released into the cytoplasm for translation by host ribosomes. The critical determinant of therapeutic efficacy is organ-specific targeting — achieved by systematically optimizing ionizable lipid chemistry, [[lipid-nanoparticles|LNP]] composition ratios, and formulation buffers.¹ Spleen-tropic delivery has been accomplished through β-hydroxy thioether ionizable lipid scaffolds, exemplified by 113-AA-C8C14, which achieved a 57-fold enhancement in mRNA expression in the spleen compared to benchmark selective organ-targeting (SORT) LNP formulations.¹ Lung-targeted delivery represents an orthogonal strategy: the ionizable lipid Lipid-392 achieved over a 100-fold improvement in lung delivery efficiency with 99% targeting specificity, enabling functional gene editing in both endothelial and epithelial cells at approximately 70% efficiency.² Notably, repeated administrations of mRNA-LNPs did not diminish delivery efficiency, supporting their use in multidose therapeutic regimens and demonstrating tolerability even at high doses of 12 mg/kg.

## CAR-T cell engineering

In vivo CAR-T cell engineering via spleen-tropic mRNA-LNPs represents a paradigm shift in cell therapy manufacturing, circumventing the need for ex vivo cell expansion and systemic reinfusion. When loaded with mRNA encoding a CD19-specific chimeric antigen receptor, 113-AA-C8C14 LNPs generated 2.7 times more [[car-t-cells|CAR T cells]] in vivo compared to control formulations.¹ These engineered CAR-T cells exhibited substantially enhanced effector function, with 2.9-fold and 3.7-fold increases in interferon-gamma and tumor necrosis factor-alpha secretion, respectively.¹ This augmented T cell response translated into a 2.3-fold greater suppression of tumor growth in a mouse model of pancreatic cancer, demonstrating the potential of in vivo CAR-T engineering for overcoming the manufacturing bottlenecks inherent to traditional cell therapy approaches.

## Inflammatory bowel disease

Beyond immunotherapy, mRNA-LNP platforms enable functional cell engineering for inflammatory disease. In inflammatory bowel disease, LNP-mediated mRNA delivery to colonic epithelial cells enhanced [[efferocytosis|efferocytic]] capacity — the ability of epithelial cells to clear apoptotic debris — and in turn suppressed pathological inflammation.³ Intraperitoneal administration of efferocytosis-enhancing mRNA-loaded nanoparticles markedly attenuated intestinal inflammation in murine colitis models and halted disease progression.³ This approach represents a proof-of-concept for in situ epithelial cell engineering as a therapeutic modality for mitigating tissue damage in inflammatory conditions, where restoration of epithelial barrier function and clearance capacity addresses the underlying pathophysiology rather than suppressing immune responses systemically.

---

## Cancer applications and tumor microenvironment targeting

Biomimetic mRNA-LNPs coated with cancer cell membrane-derived components represent an emerging strategy for enhanced tumor targeting. LNPs were functionalized with nanoghosts — membrane vesicles obtained from [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)]] cells — leveraging the natural composition of tumor membranes to promote both homotypic and heterotypic adhesion in the tumor microenvironment.⁴ The membrane-associated proteins on the nanoghost shell mediated direct binding with TNBC cells and exhibited significantly higher affinity for cancer-associated fibroblasts, conferring tissue-specific recognition that uncoated LNPs lack.⁴ This biomimetic engineering approach enables mRNA delivery to stromal and malignant compartments of tumors, expanding the therapeutic payload opportunities beyond conventional cytotoxic mRNA to include immunomodulatory and cell-reprogramming sequences.

---

## Safety and tolerability

mRNA-LNP formulations have demonstrated robust safety profiles across preclinical and emerging clinical contexts. High-dose tolerance has been established at 12 mg/kg without adverse immunogenicity or organ toxicity.² The sustained mRNA delivery efficiency across repeated administrations — without diminishment of efficacy — supports the feasibility of multidose therapeutic regimens essential for chronic inflammatory diseases and recurrent malignancies.² Furthermore, mRNA-LNPs enable efficient gene editing through co-delivery of mRNA and single-guide RNA scaffolds or antisense oligonucleotides, with median effective doses (ED₅₀) as low as approximately 0.05 mg/kg, reducing systemic exposure compared to free nucleic acids.²
