**Auranofin** is a multi-target small-molecule kinase inhibitor that overcomes distinct mechanisms of cancer therapy resistance across malignancies. Through context-dependent inhibition of [[pkc-iota|protein kinase C-ι (PKC-ι)]] in non-small cell lung cancer and [[pck1|phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase 1 (PCK1)]] in breast cancer, auranofin disrupts both immunosuppressive and metabolic pathways that confer resistance to checkpoint inhibitors and CDK4/6-directed therapies.¹²

---

## Mechanism

Auranofin functions as a dual-pathway inhibitor, targeting mechanistically distinct resistance mediators depending on cancer context. In the immune microenvironment, auranofin pharmacologically inhibits [[pkc-iota|PKC-ι]], an atypical protein kinase that regulates [[yap1]]-dependent transcription of the chemokine [[ccl7]].¹ In metabolic resistance pathways, auranofin inhibits [[pck1|PCK1]], a gluconeogenic enzyme that acquires non-canonical oncogenic function by stabilizing [[cyclin-d3|Cyclin D3]] and promoting [[cdk4|CDK4]]/[[cdk6|6]]-dependent cell cycle progression.² These mechanistically distinct targets demonstrate the versatility of auranofin as a multi-indication therapeutic.

---

## Non-small cell lung cancer and immunotherapy resistance

In [[nsclc|non-small cell lung cancer]], auranofin reverses resistance to anti-PD-1 antibody therapy by targeting a macrophage-mediated immunosuppressive axis. High [[pkc-iota|PKC-ι]] expression correlates with poor responsiveness to anti-PD-1 therapy and establishes an immunosuppressive microenvironment characterized by elevated [[tam|tumor-associated macrophage (TAM)]] infiltration and reduced [[cd8|CD8+ T cell]] numbers.¹ PKC-ι controls this phenotype by regulating Yap1-dependent transcription of [[ccl7]], which recruits immunosuppressive macrophages. Pharmacological inhibition of PKC-ι by auranofin reduces TAM accumulation and simultaneously promotes CD8+ T cell infiltration, effectively remodeling the immune microenvironment toward anti-tumor immunity.¹ In combination with anti-PD-1 antibodies, auranofin-mediated PKC-ι blockade triggers CD8+ T cell-dependent antitumor responses that control tumor growth in both situ and subcutaneous models.¹

---

## Breast cancer and CDK4/6 inhibitor resistance

In [[breast-cancer|breast cancer]], auranofin targets a metabolic-cell cycle interface that mediates resistance to CDK4/6 inhibitors. PCK1, classically characterized for its role in gluconeogenesis, functions as an oncogenic driver in breast cancer where it directly interacts with [[cyclin-d3|Cyclin D3]].² This PCK1-Cyclin D3 interaction stabilizes the complex with [[cdk4|CDK4]]/[[cdk6|6]], enabling continued cell cycle progression despite CDK4/6 inhibitor treatment.² PCK1 upregulation thus represents a key mechanism of acquired resistance, and its inhibition by auranofin restores CDK4/6 inhibitor sensitivity. Auranofin demonstrates synergistic tumor suppression when combined with CDK4/6 inhibitors in breast cancer models, offering a therapeutic strategy for patients with acquired resistance.²
