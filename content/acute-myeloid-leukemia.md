**Acute myeloid leukemia (AML)** is a molecularly heterogeneous hematologic malignancy characterized by clonal expansion of immature myeloid cells and defined by recurrent genetic alterations that dictate prognosis and therapeutic sensitivity. The disease exhibits multiple molecular dependencies, including the YEATS domain-containing acyl-lysine reader [[enl|ENL]], the histone demethylase [[lsd1|lysine-specific demethylase 1 (LSD1)]], and the receptor tyrosine kinase [[kit|KIT]], each exploitable through emerging therapeutic modalities.¹⁻³ Approximately 30% of AML cases harbor [[flt3-itd|FLT3 internal tandem duplication (FLT3-ITD)]] mutations associated with poor prognosis and high relapse risk, while [[tp53|TP53]]-mutant AML represents a particularly aggressive subtype frequently co-occurring with chromosomal deletions affecting ribosomal protein gene networks.²⁴ Treatment strategies increasingly reflect molecular stratification, with agents such as [[cpx-351|CPX-351]] demonstrating selective benefit in myelodysplasia-related disease and engineered [[car-t-cells|CAR-T cells]] designed to penetrate the protective bone marrow microenvironment.⁵⁶

## Molecular landscape

AML is defined by recurrent genetic alterations that create discrete molecular subgroups with differing prognoses and therapeutic vulnerabilities. [[flt3-itd|FLT3-ITD]] mutations occur in approximately 30% of cases, driving constitutive activation of downstream STAT5, ERK, and AKT signaling pathways that promote proliferation and survival.² While FLT3-directed tyrosine kinase inhibitors have improved clinical outcomes, acquired resistance mediated by secondary kinase domain mutations—particularly the gatekeeper F691L substitution—remains a major challenge. The multi-kinase inhibitor [[nintedanib]], originally approved for fibrotic lung diseases, retains activity against FLT3-ITD harboring the F691L resistance mutation, suppressing autophosphorylation and downstream signaling in cell lines, primary AML blasts, and xenograft models.²

[[tp53|TP53]]-mutated AML represents the most refractory molecular subset, with two-year overall survival of only 7% in the CPX-351 pivotal trial.⁵ A distinct subset exceeding 20% of TP53-altered cases exhibits recurrent deletions on chromosome 3p that encompass multiple ribosomal protein genes. When combined with co-occurring chromosome 5q deletions, these converging genomic events create a ribosomopathy-like phenotype characterized by global down-regulation of the ribosomal network and impaired protein synthesis, rendering cells selectively vulnerable to [[hsp90|HSP90]] inhibition.⁴ [[ddx41|DDX41]]-mutant AML, in contrast, demonstrates markedly superior survival with two-year overall survival reaching 70%, highlighting the prognostic importance of molecular classification.⁵

## Epigenetic dependencies

[[lsd1|LSD1]] is a FAD-dependent histone demethylase overexpressed across AML subtypes and represents a validated therapeutic target under clinical investigation. LSD1 binds in proximity to the [[kit|KIT]] transcriptional start site and regulates KIT expression through modulation of repressive H3K9me2 histone marks, with KIT overexpression preferentially marking [[lsd1-inhibitors|LSD1 inhibitor]]-sensitive AML cells.³ Pharmacological or genetic LSD1 interference represses KIT transcription and inhibits downstream ERK/mTOR signaling, with LSD1 inhibitor sensitivity correlating with KIT dependency in both cell line models and primary human AML specimens ex vivo.³ Co-targeting LSD1 and c-Kit augments anti-leukemic activity in KIT-dependent disease, suggesting rational combination strategies for this molecular subset.

The [[enl|eleven-nineteen leukemia protein (ENL)]], a YEATS domain-containing acyl-lysine reader, represents a critical dependency in AML amenable to targeted degradation through [[protac|proteolysis-targeting chimeras (PROTACs)]]. The second-generation ENL degrader [[ms108|MS108]] recruits the von Hippel-Lindau E3 ligase to achieve concentration-, time-, and ubiquitin-proteasome system-dependent ENL degradation with 5.8-fold higher potency (DC₅₀ = 0.6 nM) and 18-fold stronger antiproliferation activity (GI₅₀ = 1.19 nM) compared to the first-generation compound MS41 in MV4;11 cells.¹ MS108 displays robust selectivity for ENL degradation and improved pharmacokinetic properties, establishing it as a compelling therapeutic lead for ENL-dependent AML.

## Molecularly guided therapy

[[cpx-351|CPX-351]], a liposomal formulation of [[cytarabine]] and daunorubicin in a synergistic 5:1 molar ratio, demonstrates selective benefit in molecularly defined patient subgroups. In the pivotal phase 3 randomized trial, CPX-351 improved overall survival specifically in myelodysplasia-related AML (AML-MR) defined by World Health Organization fifth edition criteria, with median survival of 9.7 versus 6.8 months for standard 7+3 chemotherapy.⁵ The survival benefit was particularly pronounced among patients proceeding to hematopoietic cell transplantation, where two-year survival reached 76% with CPX-351 versus 27% with standard induction.⁵ Critically, no survival benefit was observed in TP53-mutated AML or other molecular subgroups, underscoring the importance of molecular stratification in treatment selection. TP53 allelic state further refines prognosis, with multihit TP53 alterations (multiple alleles altered via mutation, deletion, or copy-neutral loss of heterozygosity) conferring significantly worse survival than single-hit TP53 mutations.⁵

## Overcoming microenvironment resistance

The bone marrow microenvironment serves as a critical protective niche for leukemia cells, promoting survival of chemotherapy-resistant clones and actively inhibiting T cell infiltration—a major barrier to [[car-t-cells|CAR-T cell]] efficacy in myeloid malignancies.⁶ Engineering strategies targeting this resistance mechanism focus on enhancing CAR-T cell migration and bone marrow retention. CD33-directed CAR-T cells expressing constitutively active Rac1 (Rac1V12), which regulates cytoskeletal dynamics controlling membrane protrusion and migration, demonstrate enhanced migration capacity and preferential residence in the bone marrow in vivo.⁶ Rac1V12-expressing CD33 CAR-T cells display superior cytotoxicity against leukemia cells in transwell migration-dependent killing assays and achieve robust leukemia suppression with significantly prolonged survival in xenograft models.⁶ Mechanistically, Rac1V12 CAR-T cells residing in the bone marrow exhibit enhanced immunological memory phenotype and lower tonic signaling, a combination that promotes persistence and anti-tumor efficacy in vivo.⁶

## Novel therapeutic agents

[[ms108|MS108]] (compound 124) represents the most potent ENL degrader discovered to date, emerging from comprehensive structure-activity relationship optimization of the first-generation PROTAC MS41.¹ Beyond achieving nanomolar degradation potency, MS108 displays highly selective ENL degradation in a VHL- and ubiquitin-proteasome system-dependent manner with improved pharmacokinetic properties, providing both a chemical tool for the research community and a therapeutic lead for ENL-dependent AML.¹

[[nintedanib|Nintedanib]] was identified as a FLT3 inhibitor through analysis of drug-sensitivity data from the BeatAML database, with direct target engagement validated by computational docking, cellular thermal shift assay, and in vitro kinase inhibition.² In FLT3-ITD-mutant cell lines, primary AML blasts, and engineered Ba/F3 cells, nintedanib suppresses FLT3 autophosphorylation and downstream signaling, leading to cell cycle arrest and apoptosis.² Critically, nintedanib overcomes the gatekeeper F691L resistance mutation that confers resistance to first- and second-generation FLT3 inhibitors, demonstrating superior anti-leukemic efficacy compared to gilteritinib in F691L-expressing xenograft models.² This activity against resistance mutations positions nintedanib as a potential salvage therapy for FLT3-ITD AML following progression on approved FLT3 inhibitors.
