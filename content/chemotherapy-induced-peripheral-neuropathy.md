**Chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN)** is a major dose-limiting side effect of cancer chemotherapy characterized by progressive degeneration of sensory neuron axons that often persists for years following treatment cessation.¹ The condition significantly compromises quality of life in cancer survivors, creating urgent clinical need for neuroprotective therapeutics. Two complementary therapeutic strategies have recently emerged: [[memantine]], an [[nmda|N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA) antagonist]], being evaluated in randomized clinical trials; and selective inhibitors of [[map4k4|MAP4K4]], [[mink1|MINK1]], and [[tnik|TNIK]] kinases, identified through high-throughput screening in human induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived sensory neuron models.

---

## Mechanism

CIPN is initiated by chemotherapeutic agents, most commonly [[paclitaxel]] and other taxanes, which trigger axonal degeneration in peripheral sensory neurons through complex neurotoxic mechanisms.² The disease manifests as progressive loss of intraepidermal nerve fiber density and impaired sensory function, with symptoms often appearing or worsening months after chemotherapy completion. The persistent nature of CIPN suggests that initial chemotherapeutic injury initiates a cascade of self-perpetuating neurotoxic events that continue independently of circulating drug, necessitating direct neuroprotective intervention rather than simple dose avoidance.

---

## Memantine and NMDA-receptor antagonism

[[memantine|Memantine]], a non-competitive [[nmda|NMDA receptor antagonist]], represents the first pharmacological approach with clinical validation against CIPN.¹ A double-blind placebo-controlled randomized clinical trial demonstrated efficacy of memantine in managing established chemotherapy-induced neuropathy, providing proof-of-concept that NMDA-mediated signaling substantially contributes to CIPN pathogenesis. The mechanism underlying memantine's neuroprotection likely involves blockade of excessive calcium influx through NMDA receptors, which normally trigger excitotoxic cascades in sensory neurons. This trial positions memantine as an immediate clinical option for affected patients, particularly those developing CIPN during or shortly after active cancer treatment.

---

## STE20 kinase inhibition as a discovery-driven therapeutic

Translational progress in CIPN therapeutics was catalyzed by development of a human iPSC-derived sensory neuron platform capable of recapitulating [[paclitaxel]]-induced axonal degeneration in vitro.² Using this system, researchers screened a library of 192 kinase inhibitors and identified 19 hits that converged on three STE20 family kinases: [[map4k4|MAP4K4]], [[mink1|MINK1]], and [[tnik|TNIK]]. Critically, genetic knockdown studies revealed that multi-kinase inhibition—rather than single-kinase targeting—is required for robust neuroprotection, suggesting functional redundancy among these kinases in mediating paclitaxel neurotoxicity. Selective pharmacological inhibition of these STE20 kinases rescued paclitaxel-induced axon degeneration in both iPSC-derived sensory neurons and primary human dorsal root ganglia. In vivo validation in mouse CIPN models confirmed that STE20 kinase inhibitors preserve intraepidermal nerve fiber density, establishing a clear translational path from discovery screening to preclinical efficacy.

---

## Human iPSC platforms and therapeutic screening

The human iPSC-derived sensory neuron platform has become instrumental in understanding CIPN mechanisms and identifying actionable therapeutic targets.² Unlike traditional animal models or immortalized cell lines, iPSC-derived neurons retain human-specific biology and enable reproducible, scalable, high-throughput screening of thousands of compounds. This approach directly validates whether kinase inhibitors can block paclitaxel-induced degeneration in human sensory neurons, substantially improving clinical translation probability. The convergence of multi-kinase hits from unbiased screening not only identified the STE20 kinase family as a critical mediator of paclitaxel neurotoxicity, but also revealed that functional redundancy within this pathway necessitates broader pharmacological targeting rather than single-gene approaches.

---

## Clinical impact and future directions

CIPN remains a major barrier to optimal cancer treatment, forcing oncologists to reduce chemotherapy doses or discontinue effective agents to preserve patients' peripheral nerve function and quality of life.¹ The emergence of memantine as an immediate clinical option, combined with STE20 kinase inhibitors poised for clinical translation, offers the prospect of enabling full-dose chemotherapy delivery while maintaining sensory nerve integrity. Future strategies may involve combination approaches—such as prophylactic co-administration of STE20 kinase inhibitors during chemotherapy, or sequential memantine therapy for established neuropathy—to fully restore the therapeutic window of neurotoxic but effective cancer drugs.
