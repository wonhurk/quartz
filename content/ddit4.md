**DDIT4** (DNA damage-inducible transcript 4) is an mTOR inhibitor that acts as a cell-intrinsic suppressor of [[hiv|HIV]] expression in [[cd4-t-cells|CD4+ T cells]]. Identified through multiomic profiling of [[analytical-treatment-interruption|analytical treatment interruption (ATI)]] cohorts, DDIT4 expression is associated with delayed [[hiv|viral rebound]] following [[antiretroviral-therapy|antiretroviral therapy (ART)]] interruption independent of intact HIV reservoir status.¹ The gene was discovered alongside [[znf254|ZNF254]] as a key component of host-mediated HIV silencing mechanisms that may support ART-free remission strategies. Notably, DDIT4 expression is induced by [[metformin]], an inexpensive antidiabetic drug, creating a novel opportunity for therapeutic repurposing to achieve durable HIV control.

---

## Mechanism

DDIT4 suppresses HIV expression through direct [[mtor|mTOR]] inhibition in [[cd4-t-cells|CD4+ T cells]], a cell-intrinsic mechanism demonstrated both in vitro and in vivo.¹ The suppression of viral replication occurs independently of the size or composition of the intact [[hiv-reservoir|HIV latent reservoir]], indicating that DDIT4-mediated control acts on actively infected or reactivating cells rather than on the persistence of integrated proviruses. DDIT4 was identified alongside [[znf254|ZNF254]] through RNA sequencing of [[cd4-t-cells|CD4+ T cells]] from the A5345 non-interventional cohort, both genes showing consistent association with delayed [[hiv|viral rebound]] across multiple [[analytical-treatment-interruption|ATI]] cohorts including CLEAR, TEACH, and REDUC.¹ This convergent identification across independent cohorts strengthens the evidence that DDIT4 represents a genuine host factor governing [[hiv|HIV]] silencing.

---

## HIV rebound and ART-free remission

DDIT4 expression in [[cd4-t-cells|CD4+ T cells]] prior to [[antiretroviral-therapy|ART]] interruption predicts delayed [[hiv|viral rebound]], a clinically significant association observed across both interventional and observational cohorts.¹ Crucially, this association is not confounded by intact [[hiv-reservoir|HIV reservoir]] status, meaning that DDIT4-mediated control represents a distinct host defense mechanism separable from traditional measures of reservoir burden. The potential of DDIT4 and [[znf254|ZNF254]] as twin pillars of HIV silencing suggests that strategies enhancing their expression or activity could support the pursuit of [[hiv-cure-research|ART-free HIV remission]].¹² The convergence of cell-intrinsic suppression mechanisms with immune-mediated control offers a dual pathway toward durable viral control without continuous antiretroviral therapy.

---

## Metformin-induced HIV silencing

[[metformin|Metformin]], an inexpensive and widely available antidiabetic drug, induces DDIT4 expression in primary [[cd4-t-cells|CD4+ T cells]] and in cells isolated from [[antiretroviral-therapy|ART]]-suppressed people with [[hiv|HIV]].¹ In these cellular models, metformin-induced DDIT4 suppresses [[hiv|HIV]] replication, demonstrating proof-of-concept for therapeutic repurposing. The combination of metformin's accessibility, safety profile established over decades of diabetes treatment, and its ability to activate an endogenous HIV-silencing mechanism positions it as a compelling candidate for clinical evaluation in [[hiv-cure-research|ART-free remission strategies]].¹ This finding exemplifies how existing pharmacological tools, when integrated with deeper understanding of host antiviral mechanisms, may unlock novel avenues for HIV control without the burden of lifelong antiretroviral therapy.
