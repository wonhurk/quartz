**ERK (extracellular signal-regulated kinase)** is a serine/threonine kinase that functions as a central signaling node in the RAS/MAPK cascade, phosphorylated and activated by the upstream kinase MEK in response to diverse extracellular stimuli including receptor tyrosine kinases ([[flt3|FLT3]], [[kit|c-KIT]]), integrins ([[vla-4|VLA-4]]), and G-protein coupled receptors (β3-adrenergic receptor).¹²³⁴⁵ ERK phosphorylation drives critical cellular processes including proliferation, survival, differentiation, migration, invasion, and epithelial-mesenchymal transition, frequently operating in interconnected networks with PI3K/AKT/mTOR and STAT5 signaling pathways.⁴⁵⁶⁷ Its phosphorylation status serves as both a readout for pathway activation and a predictor of therapeutic response across multiple malignancies and developmental disorders.

---

## Mechanism

ERK activation occurs through a canonical kinase cascade initiated when extracellular signals engage cell surface receptors, triggering RAS-mediated recruitment and activation of RAF kinases, which phosphorylate MEK, which in turn phosphorylates ERK1/2 at conserved threonine and tyrosine residues.¹³ Receptor tyrosine kinases such as [[flt3|FLT3]] and [[kit|c-KIT]] represent major upstream activators in hematopoietic malignancies, where their constitutive activation through internal tandem duplications or point mutations drives sustained ERK phosphorylation and downstream transcriptional output.⁴⁵ The integrin [[vla-4|VLA-4]] activates ERK1/2 phosphorylation through integrin-linked kinase signaling to regulate hematopoietic stem cell self-renewal and lymphoid progenitor differentiation.² β3-adrenergic receptor engagement triggers ERK activation via Gαi/o-coupled signaling, modulating redox homeostasis through the ERK-Nrf2-GCLc axis.⁶

ERK signaling exhibits extensive cross-talk with parallel survival pathways. In acute myeloid leukemia driven by [[flt3|FLT3-ITD]] mutations, [[nintedanib]] suppresses FLT3 autophosphorylation and coordinately inhibits downstream STAT5, ERK, and AKT signaling.⁴ In colorectal cancer, the calcium-activated chloride channel [[ano1|ANO1]] activates both PI3K/AKT/mTOR and ERK pathways in parallel to stabilize the partial epithelial-mesenchymal transition state.⁷ In [[kit|KIT]]-dependent acute myeloid leukemias, c-Kit functions directly upstream of ERK/mTOR signaling, with dual pathway activation required for leukemic cell survival.⁵

## Cancer applications

### Rhabdomyosarcoma and adaptive resistance

In HRAS-mutant fusion-negative rhabdomyosarcoma, hyperactive RAS signaling drives tumorigenesis yet proves refractory to indirect pathway inhibition. Farnesyltransferase inhibitors such as tipifarnib suppress HRAS membrane localization and transiently blunt RAS effector signaling, but therapeutic response is limited by adaptive feedback reactivation of ERK signaling and compensatory upregulation of wild-type RAS isoforms.¹ The combination of farnesyltransferase inhibition with MEK inhibition prevents ERK reactivation, reduces ERK transcriptional output, restrains tumor progression, and induces terminal myogenic differentiation in HRAS-mutant rhabdomyosarcoma models, highlighting ERK pathway reactivation as a central mechanism of adaptive resistance to targeted therapies.¹

### Acute myeloid leukemia

[[flt3|FLT3]] mutations, particularly internal tandem duplications, occur in approximately 30% of acute myeloid leukemia cases and drive constitutive ERK phosphorylation alongside STAT5 and AKT activation.⁴ [[nintedanib|Nintedanib]], an oral multi-kinase inhibitor, suppresses FLT3 autophosphorylation and downstream ERK signaling, inducing cell cycle arrest and apoptosis in FLT3-ITD-mutant cell lines, primary AML blasts, and engineered models harboring secondary resistance mutations including the gatekeeper F691L mutation.⁴ In [[kit|KIT]]-dependent acute myeloid leukemias, pharmacological or genetic interference with [[kit|c-KIT]] represses ERK/mTOR signaling, and inhibiting c-Kit augments the anti-leukemic activity of LSD1 inhibitors in KIT-overexpressing AML cells.⁵

### Colorectal cancer metastasis

The calcium-activated chloride channel [[ano1|ANO1]] is frequently overexpressed in colorectal cancer and functions as a stabilizer of the partial epithelial-mesenchymal transition state, a hybrid cellular phenotype that confers metastatic plasticity and treatment resistance.⁷ ANO1 activates both PI3K/AKT/mTOR and ERK signaling pathways to maintain expression of mesenchymal markers (N-cadherin, vimentin, α-SMA) and EMT transcription factors (Zeb1, Snail, Twist) while sustaining a spatially organized hybrid subcellular architecture characterized by co-expression of epithelial (E-cadherin) and mesenchymal (vimentin) markers.⁷ ANO1 depletion disrupts ERK signaling, dismantles the organized partial EMT architecture, and selectively depletes the E-cadherin⁺/vimentin⁺ subpopulation, potently suppressing proliferation, migration, invasion, and inducing early apoptosis.⁷

### Glioblastoma

In U251 glioblastoma cells, β3-adrenergic receptor signaling activates the Gαi/o-ERK-Nrf2-GCLc pathway to regulate redox homeostasis and oxidative stress adaptation.⁶ The natural compound baicalin functions as a potent β3-adrenergic receptor inhibitor that triggers glutathione depletion and induces oxidative stress-mediated apoptosis through suppression of ERK-dependent Nrf2 stabilization and glutamate-cysteine ligase catalytic subunit expression.⁶ Blood-brain barrier-permeable probes enable simultaneous visualization of β3-adrenergic receptor expression and surrounding redox status, facilitating therapeutic discovery and monitoring in orthotopic glioma models.⁶

## Developmental and genetic disorders

In Noonan syndrome, a common autosomal dominant disorder characterized by facial dysmorphism and short stature, gain-of-function mutations in PTPN11 encoding the SHP2 phosphatase disrupt critical regulatory hydrogen bonds and stabilize the protein in an activated "open" conformation.³ The p.Asn308Ser mutation strengthens interactions with hub proteins including GRB2 and SRC, resulting in sustained RAS/MAPK activation and aberrant ERK phosphorylation.³ Phosphoproteomic analysis reveals that the mutation induces extensive nuclear phosphorylation events enriched in pathways related to chromatin organization and ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling, with aberrantly activated ERK phosphorylating [[swi-snf|SWI/SNF complex]] components and other chromatin regulators to drive the developmental phenotype.³

## Hematopoietic transplantation

The integrin [[vla-4|VLA-4 (very late antigen-4)]] represents a pivotal target for hematopoietic stem cell engraftment following allogeneic transplantation. [[vla-4|VLA-4]] agonists significantly promote donor HSC engraftment and long-term hematopoietic reconstitution by activating ERK1/2 phosphorylation, which enhances HSC self-renewal capacity in allogeneic transplantation and serial xenotransplantation models.² The [[vla-4|VLA-4]] agonist facilitates early immune reconstitution by augmenting T-cell differentiation from HSCs through ERK-dependent signaling, with reconstituted immune cells exhibiting potent antiviral effects without exacerbating acute [[graft-versus-host-disease|graft-versus-host disease]].² Importantly, [[vla-4|VLA-4]]-ERK signaling regulates lymphoid progenitor differentiation without inducing leukemogenic gene expression, underscoring its translational potential for promoting HSC engraftment and cellular immune reconstitution.²

## Visceral pain and affective disorders

In irritable bowel syndrome, a functional gastrointestinal disorder characterized by visceral hypersensitivity and prominent affective symptoms, ERK1/2 phosphorylation in the central amygdala contributes to the coordination of sensory and emotional dimensions.⁸ Optogenetic activation of pro-nociceptive parabrachial nucleus terminals projecting to the central amygdala exacerbates visceral hypersensitivity, anxiety-like behaviors, and ERK1/2 phosphorylation, whereas activation of anti-nociceptive basolateral amygdala terminals alleviates these phenotypes and reduces ERK phosphorylation.⁸ Intra-amygdalar infusion of the highly selective HDAC6 inhibitor [[acy-738|ACY-738]] alleviates visceral pain and negative affect-like behaviors in IBS-like rats, accompanied by reduced ERK1/2 phosphorylation alongside decreased p38 MAPK activation and attenuation of synaptic and neuroimmune alterations.⁸

## Therapeutic approaches

Combination strategies targeting ERK reactivation have emerged as a central theme in overcoming adaptive resistance to targeted therapies. In HRAS-mutant rhabdomyosarcoma, cotargeting farnesyltransferase and MEK prevents feedback ERK reactivation and restores terminal differentiation.¹ [[nintedanib|Nintedanib]] demonstrates multi-kinase inhibitory activity against [[flt3|FLT3]], suppressing ERK alongside STAT5 and AKT signaling and retaining efficacy against gatekeeper resistance mutations.⁴ In glioblastoma, the natural inhibitor baicalin targets upstream β3-adrenergic receptor signaling to suppress the ERK-Nrf2-GCLc redox adaptation axis.⁶ In irritable bowel syndrome models, the HDAC6 inhibitor [[acy-738|ACY-738]] reduces ERK1/2 phosphorylation alongside p38 MAPK to alleviate visceral hypersensitivity and affective symptoms.⁸ Across multiple contexts, ERK phosphorylation status serves as a pharmacodynamic biomarker for pathway engagement and therapeutic response, enabling rational combination strategies and real-time therapeutic monitoring.
