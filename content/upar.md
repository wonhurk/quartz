**uPAR** (urokinase plasminogen activator receptor) is a cell surface protein serving as a protumorigenic marker of brain tumor-initiating cells in [[glioblastoma]], with broad expression across solid tumors harboring [[tp53]] and [[ras]] pathway mutations.¹ Functionally, uPAR is upregulated in senescent, pro-fibrotic stromal cells within the tumor microenvironment, including [[glioblastoma]]-associated macrophages, where it sustains both malignant cell growth and the immunosuppressive tumor niche.² Genetic disruption of uPAR impairs protumorigenic characteristics and tumor growth in vitro and in vivo, establishing its biological necessity for tumorigenesis. uPAR-specific [[car-t|CAR-T cells]] represent a therapeutic breakthrough, simultaneously targeting both tumor cells and stromal support while safely reversing fibrosis and achieving durable tumor regressions without sustained myelosuppression.²

---

## Expression and tumor heterogeneity

Multiomic analysis of patient-derived [[glioblastoma]] specimens identified uPAR as a consistent marker of putative brain tumor-initiating cells within the considerable tumoral heterogeneity characteristic of this deadly CNS malignancy.¹ Beyond the brain, uPAR expression is broadly distributed across solid tumors enriched for [[tp53]] and [[ras]] pathway mutations, suggesting a convergent biological program linking genomic instability to protumorigenic cell surface phenotypes.² In the tumor microenvironment, uPAR is prominently expressed on senescent, pro-fibrotic fibroblasts and tumor-associated [[macrophages]], where it sustains the immunosuppressive niche that shields tumors from conventional therapies and limits T cell infiltration.²

---

## CAR-T cell targeting

The discovery of uPAR's broad expression across malignant and stromal compartments prompted development of uPAR-specific [[car-t|CAR-T cells]], which demonstrated potent antitumor activity in patient-derived [[glioblastoma]] xenograft models.¹ Unlike conventional CAR-T targets restricted to cancer cells, uPAR CAR-T cells achieve dual targeting—simultaneously eliminating GBM cells and the uPAR-positive stromal support cells within the tumor microenvironment.¹² This convergence proves therapeutically decisive: across diverse solid tumor models, uPAR CAR-T cells induce durable tumor regressions, eradicate systemic metastases, and reverse the fibrotic stroma that normally suppresses immune infiltration and T cell activation.²

---

## Microenvironment remodeling and fibrosis reversal

A critical advantage of uPAR CAR-T cells is their capacity to safely reverse tumor-associated fibrosis—a hallmark feature of solid tumor immunosuppression—without incurring sustained myelosuppression or systemic toxicity.² By targeting the senescent, pro-fibrotic stromal compartment directly, these engineered T cells reshape the immunological microenvironment from permissive to hostile for tumor growth. The fibrosis-reversing capability is further potentiated when uPAR CAR-T therapy is combined with senescence-inducing agents, which expand the uPAR-positive stromal pool and enhance CAR-T cell efficacy.² This synergy indicates that the senescent stromal niche represents an exploitable vulnerability, capable of transforming an immunosuppressive ecosystem into one permissive for durable antitumor immunity.
