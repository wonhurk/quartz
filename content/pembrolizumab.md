**Pembrolizumab** is a humanized monoclonal antibody checkpoint inhibitor that blocks the programmed cell death protein 1 ([[pd-l1|PD-1]]) receptor, reactivating anti-tumor T cell immunity across multiple solid malignancies. By disrupting the [[pd-l1|PD-1/PD-L1]] axis, pembrolizumab restores the ability of cytotoxic lymphocytes to recognize and eliminate cancer cells that exploit this pathway for immune evasion.¹ The agent has demonstrated durable antitumor activity in [[melanoma]], [[nsclc|non-small cell lung cancer]], hepatocellular carcinoma, gastric cancer, and [[clear-cell-renal-cell-carcinoma|renal cell carcinoma]], with efficacy increasingly understood through novel biomarkers beyond traditional [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] expression.²⁻⁶

---

## Mechanism of action

Pembrolizumab functions by binding to the PD-1 receptor on T cells, preventing its engagement with the ligands [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] and PD-L2, which are commonly upregulated on tumor cells and within the tumor microenvironment as adaptive resistance mechanisms. This blockade relieves T cell exhaustion and enables sustained effector function, proliferation, and cytokine production by tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes. The restoration of immune surveillance permits the recognition of neoantigens — both mutation-derived and those arising from dysregulated RNA processing — thereby expanding the repertoire of targetable tumor antigens.¹ Recent evidence indicates that the efficacy of pembrolizumab can be enhanced by modulating neoantigen abundance through inhibition of the nonsense-mediated mRNA decay pathway, particularly through reduced activity of the kinase [[smg1|SMG1]], which serves as a predictive biomarker for checkpoint inhibitor response.¹

## Cancer applications

### Hepatocellular carcinoma

In the phase II KEYNOTE-224 study, pembrolizumab monotherapy demonstrated durable antitumor activity and manageable safety in patients with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma, enrolling both sorafenib-treated and treatment-naïve cohorts. Long-term follow-up extending approximately 7 years for the sorafenib-treated cohort and 5 years for the treatment-naïve cohort confirmed sustained clinical benefit in a subset of patients, establishing pembrolizumab as a viable therapeutic option in this difficult-to-treat malignancy.²

### Gastric and gastroesophageal junction carcinoma

Pembrolizumab received FDA accelerated approval in May 2021 and regular approval in March 2025 for the treatment of unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive gastric or gastroesophageal junction carcinoma in combination with trastuzumab and platinum-based chemotherapy. The approvals were based on the randomized KEYNOTE-811 trial, in which the pembrolizumab combination arm achieved an objective response rate of 74.4% versus 51.9% in the placebo arm (P = 0.00006), with a median overall survival of 20.0 months compared to 16.8 months (hazard ratio 0.80, P = 0.004).³ Exploratory analyses revealed that treatment benefit was driven by patients with [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] combined positive score (CPS) ≥1, who comprised 85% of the study population, whereas those with CPS <1 showed no improvement, highlighting the importance of biomarker-driven patient selection.³

### Non-small cell lung cancer

In immunotherapy-naïve patients with [[nsclc|NSCLC]] who had received prior treatment, pembrolizumab was evaluated in combination with gemcitabine in a phase I/II study. While the regimen was safely administered, it did not demonstrate synergism compared with historical controls.⁵ However, the trial yielded critical insights into predictive biomarkers, as described below.

### Small cell neuroendocrine prostate cancer

Pembrolizumab has been investigated in combination with talabostat, an oral small molecule inhibitor of dipeptidyl peptidases (DPP4 and DPP8/9), in patients with [[scnc|small cell neuroendocrine prostate cancer]] — a lethal prostate cancer subset with limited treatment options. Talabostat triggers the inflammasome to prime immune cells, inducing IL-18 and IL-1β secretion and thereby bridging innate and adaptive immunity in support of pembrolizumab-mediated T cell activation.⁴

### Renal cell carcinoma

In a phase 1/2 trial evaluating the HPK1 inhibitor NDI-101150 as monotherapy or in combination with pembrolizumab, patients with [[clear-cell-renal-cell-carcinoma|clear cell renal cell carcinoma]] receiving monotherapy achieved an investigator-assessed overall response rate of 13.6%, including one complete response and two partial responses, with a clinical benefit rate of 27.3%. Pharmacodynamic analyses demonstrated inhibition of the biomarker phospho-SLP76 and increased activated CD8+ T cells and dendritic cells in the tumor microenvironment, supporting the mechanistic rationale for HPK1 inhibition in enhancing anti-tumor immunity.⁶

## Combination strategies

Beyond conventional chemotherapy backbones, pembrolizumab has been paired with immunomodulatory small molecules targeting distinct nodes of immune regulation. Talabostat, a [[dipeptidyl-peptidase-4|dipeptidyl peptidase]] inhibitor, activates the inflammasome pathway, generating pro-inflammatory cytokines that recruit and activate both innate and adaptive immune effectors.⁴ The HPK1 inhibitor NDI-101150 promotes T cell, B cell, and dendritic cell activation and recruitment into the tumor microenvironment, with early-phase data supporting safe combination with pembrolizumab at doses up to 100 mg once daily without reaching a maximum tolerated dose.⁶ These approaches exemplify the strategic layering of complementary immune-activating mechanisms to overcome checkpoint inhibitor resistance.

## Biomarkers and patient selection

Traditional biomarkers such as [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] expression by immunohistochemistry and tumor mutational burden have shown limited predictive power, prompting the development of more sophisticated metrics. In the KEYNOTE-811 gastric cancer trial, [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] CPS status stratified benefit, with no survival advantage observed in the CPS <1 subgroup.³ More recently, [[multiplex-immunofluorescence|multiplex immunofluorescence]]-based spatial analysis has enabled characterization of cell-cell relationships in the tumor microenvironment. In the phase I/II [[nsclc|NSCLC]] study combining pembrolizumab with gemcitabine, elevated frequencies of regulatory T cells in proximity to CD3+ T cells at baseline were associated with improved outcome (P < 0.05).⁵ Integrating this Treg/T cell proximity metric with overall T cell density yielded a tumor inflammation score that correlated significantly with disease response (P < 0.002), outperforming [[pd-l1|PD-L1]], tumor mutational burden, and gene expression profiles.⁵ This metric is postulated to capture an ongoing anticancer immune response, offering a more dynamic view of immunological competence than static single-marker approaches.

At the genomic level, reduced activity of the nonsense-mediated mRNA decay pathway kinase [[smg1|SMG1]] has emerged as a predictor of improved checkpoint inhibitor response across more than 1,000 patients. [[smg1|SMG1]] inhibition stabilizes transcripts containing premature termination codons — most of non-mutational origin — thereby reshaping the MHC class I-bound immunopeptidome and increasing neoantigen abundance to levels comparable to high mutation burden tumors.¹ This expands the neoantigen landscape accessible to pembrolizumab-activated T cells, offering a strategy to increase tumor immunogenicity independent of mutation load.¹
