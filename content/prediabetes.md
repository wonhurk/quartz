**Prediabetes** is a metabolic condition characterized by elevated blood glucose levels that fall below the diagnostic threshold for [[type-2-diabetes|type 2 diabetes mellitus]], representing a critical intervention window for disease prevention.¹ At its core, prediabetes involves dysregulation of multiple pathways: dysbiosis of the gut microbiota impairs metabolic homeostasis and glucose handling, while in the retinal vasculature, high-glucose conditions trigger [[ve-cadherin]] phosphorylation and endothelial barrier breakdown, initiating the cascade toward [[diabetic-retinopathy|diabetic retinopathy]].² The convergence of microbial dysbiosis and vascular endothelial dysfunction underscores why early intervention during the prediabetic stage is essential to prevent irreversible tissue complications.

---

## Mechanism

Prediabetes arises from multiple dysregulated mechanisms operating in parallel. At the systemic level, progressive insulin resistance and impaired beta-cell compensation lead to sustained hyperglycemia. Critically, dysregulation of the gut microbiota plays a pivotal role in the pathogenesis, as dysbiotic communities fail to maintain glucose homeostasis and promote metabolic inflammation.¹ In the retinal microvasculature, the earliest pathological event is high-glucose-induced phosphorylation of [[ve-cadherin]] at tyrosine 685, a key junctional protein anchoring endothelial cells and maintaining the blood–retina barrier.² This phosphorylation event promotes internalization of [[ve-cadherin]] from cell junctions, rapidly compromising endothelial barrier integrity and enabling leukocyte infiltration. High glucose simultaneously activates the hexosamine biosynthesis pathway, leading to increased O-linked β-N-acetyl glucosamine (O-GlcNAc) modification of intracellular proteins. This O-GlcNAcylation is coupled to upregulation of [[angiopoietin-2|angiopoietin 2 (Ang2)]], which disrupts pericyte-endothelial interactions and accelerates pericyte dropout from the retinal capillaries.² The cascade of VE-cadherin dysfunction → O-GlcNAc elevation → Ang2 upregulation → pericyte loss constitutes the initiating phase of vascular complications in prediabetes.

## Dietary modulation and metabolic restoration

Acetylated Konjac glucomannan (AcKGM) has emerged as a powerful dietary intervention capable of reversing prediabetic dysbiosis and metabolic dysfunction in a structure-dependent manner.¹ In vitro fermentation studies using fecal samples from prediabetic individuals revealed that AcKGM, particularly variants with higher degrees of acetylation (DS), selectively enriches beneficial Bacteroides species including *Bacteroides uniformis* and *Bacteroides coprocola*, both of which preferentially utilize the acetylated polymer.¹ In prediabetic animal models, AcKGM supplementation improved glucose tolerance and lipid metabolism, suppressed systemic inflammation, and ameliorated hepatic steatosis — effects that scaled with the degree of acetylation and the resulting enrichment of *B. uniformis*.¹ The mechanism involves acetylation-dependent metabolite production; highly acetylated AcKGM stimulates production of beneficial short-chain fatty acids and D-pantothenic acid, which promote metabolic health and insulin sensitivity.¹ This structure–function relationship establishes that modulating the chemical composition of dietary polysaccharides can be tuned to maximize beneficial microbiota remodeling and metabolic restoration before the transition to frank [[type-2-diabetes|type 2 diabetes mellitus]].

## Retinal vascular complications and therapeutic targeting

The retinal vasculature is uniquely vulnerable to early prediabetic damage, as retinal capillary endothelial cells exhibit heightened sensitivity to glucose-induced [[ve-cadherin]] dysfunction. Mechanistically, high-glucose and NDPKB-deficient conditions converge on Y685 phosphorylation of [[ve-cadherin]], disrupting the junctional complex and enabling vascular hyperpermeability.² Prevention of this phosphorylation event — achieved through a Y685F mutation that blocks phosphorylation — completely protected the retinal microvasculature against prediabetes-induced barrier breakdown, pericyte loss, and functional decline.² Importantly, Y685F-mutant mice maintained normal neuronal function and exhibited restored balance in neurovascular and mitochondrial pathways at the proteomic level, indicating that a single precise intervention can preserve retinal health across multiple biological systems.² Pharmacological approaches targeting O-GlcNAc modification show promise as alternative strategies to block the Y685-dependent Ang2 upregulation, offering pharmacological routes to prevent the early vascular complications of prediabetes without requiring genetic intervention.

---

## Clinical significance and prevention window

Prediabetes represents a metabolic state of critical reversibility — a window during which aggressive dietary, lifestyle, and pharmacological interventions can arrest or reverse progression to [[type-2-diabetes|type 2 diabetes mellitus]] and its major complications.¹ The recognition that prediabetic retinal vascular dysfunction emerges through discrete, targetable molecular events — principally [[ve-cadherin]] Y685 phosphorylation and O-GlcNAcylation-driven Ang2 upregulation — transforms the prediabetic state from a passive precursor into an actionable therapeutic opportunity.² Early diagnosis and intervention during prediabetes are now supported by evidence that microbiota-modulating dietary therapies can simultaneously improve glucose metabolism and suppress inflammatory pathways, while emerging anti-vascular-permeability strategies can prevent the initiation of retinopathy before it becomes clinically apparent.
