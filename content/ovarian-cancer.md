**Ovarian cancer** is a major gynecological malignancy characterized by high mortality and pervasive resistance to platinum-based chemotherapy. At the molecular level, disease progression is driven by suppression of [[ferroptosis]] through [[runx2|RUNX2]]-mediated signaling, which transcriptionally activates the [[yap1|YAP1]]/[[gls1|GLS1]] axis to inhibit iron-dependent cell death and confer cisplatin resistance.¹ This master regulatory program is further controlled by [[zdhhc14|ZDHHC14]], a palmitoyltransferase that S-palmitoylates [[runx2|RUNX2]] at specific cysteine residues, stabilizing its anti-ferroptotic function and enhancing chemoresistance both in vitro and in xenograft models.¹

---

## Chemoresistance mechanism

The [[runx2|RUNX2]] transcription factor occupies a central position in ovarian cancer chemoresistance by coordinately suppressing [[ferroptosis]] and enhancing survival under cisplatin exposure. [[zdhhc14|ZDHHC14]]-mediated S-palmitoylation of [[runx2|RUNX2]] drives its localization and transcriptional activity, enabling upregulation of [[yap1|YAP1]] and downstream activation of [[gls1|glutaminase 1 (GLS1)]].¹ This axis attenuates key ferroptosis hallmarks including depletion of [[gpx4|GPX4]], accumulation of reactive oxygen species, elevation of Fe²⁺ and malondialdehyde, lipid peroxidation, and GSH/GSSG imbalance.¹ When [[runx2|RUNX2]] palmitoylation is abolished through site-directed mutagenesis, both ferroptosis sensitivity and cisplatin responsiveness are restored in resistant cell lines and tumor xenografts, establishing the [[zdhhc14|ZDHHC14]]-[[runx2|RUNX2]] palmitoylation circuit as a critical determinant of therapeutic resistance.¹

## Detection and diagnosis

Early detection of ovarian cancer remains a major clinical challenge given its asymptomatic early-stage presentation. The [[wid-qec|WID-qEC]] DNA methylation test, applied to cervico-vaginal samples, has emerged as an epigenetic biomarker with significant association with ovarian cancer presence.² Originally validated for detection of [[endometrial-cancer|endometrial]] and cervical cancers, [[wid-qec|WID-qEC]] positivity in the absence of these malignancies correlates strongly with ovarian cancer: 54% of test-positive cases without endometrial or cervical cancer harbored ovarian cancer, yielding an adjusted odds ratio of 2.93 (95% CI 1.75–4.95).² This association is independent of age, menopausal status, hormone replacement therapy use, and family history of breast or ovarian cancer.² [[wid-qec|WID-qEC]] positivity also correlates with a higher number of lifetime ovulatory cycles (adjusted OR 2.67; 95% CI 1.06–7.50), a known ovarian cancer risk factor.² A two-step triage strategy combining plasma-based [[cell-free-dna-methylation-test|cell-free DNA methylation testing]] with [[wid-qec|WID-qEC]] has been proposed to improve detection sensitivity while maintaining clinical specificity.²

## Novel therapeutics

The failure of platinum-based regimens in advanced and recurrent ovarian cancer has driven development of non-platinum metal-based therapeutics. Heterobimetallic [[palladium-tin-allyl-complexes|Pd(II)/Sn(II) η³-allyl complexes]] bearing N-heterocyclic carbene (NHC) ligands represent a mechanistically distinct class synthesized via SnCl₂ insertion into the Pd–Cl bond.³ These compounds exhibit pronounced cytotoxicity across cisplatin-sensitive, cisplatin-resistant, and high-grade serous ovarian cancer (HGSOC) cell lines, with lead [[compound-2i|compound 2i]] and [[compound-2k|2k]] demonstrating remarkable tumor selectivity.³ [[compound-2i|Compound 2i]] retains potent activity in platinum-resistant patient-derived tumor organoids (PDTOs) while remaining inactive against non-malignant fallopian tube epithelial organoids, establishing a favorable therapeutic window.³ Mechanistically, [[compound-2i|2i]] primarily targets [[thioredoxin-reductase|thioredoxin reductase (TrxR)]] and mitochondria, triggering early cytochrome c release, loss of mitochondrial membrane potential, and downstream DNA damage consistent with a mitochondria-driven apoptotic pathway distinct from cisplatin's nuclear DNA crosslinking mechanism.³

A second orthogonal approach employs [[magnesium-oxide-nanoparticles|MgO nanoparticles]] formulated from konjac glucomannan (KGM), a natural polysaccharide serving as capping and stabilizing agent.⁴ These KGM-MgO nanoparticles, averaging 13.34 nm in diameter, induce concentration-dependent cytotoxicity in A2780 ovarian cancer cells while remaining non-hemolytic up to 100 μg/mL, demonstrating a biocompatible safety profile.⁴ Beyond oncology, KGM-MgO nanoparticles accelerate wound healing in vivo, achieving 82% wound contraction by day 14 with enhanced collagen deposition and re-epithelialization, and exhibit no vascular toxicity in chick chorioallantoic membrane models or phytotoxicity in Vigna radiata.⁴

## Prognostic markers

[[dhx37|DEAH-box RNA helicase 37 (DHX37)]], a conserved RNA helicase involved in ribosome biogenesis and RNA metabolism, exhibits context-dependent prognostic associations in ovarian versus breast cancer. In ovarian cancer, low [[dhx37|DHX37]] expression correlates with lower FIGO stage, absence of residual disease, and improved overall survival (P = 0.013), suggesting that reduced [[dhx37|DHX37]] marks a more favorable disease trajectory.⁵ This contrasts sharply with breast cancer, where low cytoplasmic [[dhx37|DHX37]] expression associates with adverse clinicopathological parameters and poor breast cancer-specific survival, particularly in estrogen receptor-positive patients.⁵ These opposing prognostic patterns underscore tissue-specific biological functions of RNA helicases and position [[dhx37|DHX37]] as a candidate biomarker for risk stratification in ovarian cancer, though functional validation remains necessary.⁵
