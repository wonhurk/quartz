**Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)**, encompassing ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease, is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the gastrointestinal tract characterized by dysregulated immune responses, compromised epithelial barrier function, and altered microbiota composition. The disease manifests through persistent intestinal inflammation driven by aberrant cytokine signaling—including IL-6, IFN-γ, and IL-2—and loss of tissue homeostasis mechanisms such as efferocytosis, the clearance of apoptotic cells that prevents secondary necrosis and inflammatory amplification. Recent therapeutic advances target multiple nodes of IBD pathophysiology: [[ptpro|PTPRO]] phosphatase inhibition to suppress pro-inflammatory cytokines via NF-κB pathway modulation, TL1A-neutralizing monoclonal antibodies to block DR3 signaling on T helper cells and innate lymphoid cells, and bioengineered platforms for spatiotemporally controlled drug delivery within colonic tissue.¹⁻³

---

## Pathophysiology

IBD arises from the convergence of immune dysregulation, epithelial barrier compromise, and microbial dysbiosis. The epithelial barrier normally prevents luminal antigens from triggering inappropriate immune responses, but in IBD this barrier is disrupted, allowing bacterial translocation and sustained activation of mucosal immune cells. [[ptpro|PTPRO]], a receptor-type protein tyrosine phosphatase, emerges as a pivotal regulator: its overactivity sustains inflammatory signaling through NF-κB, driving macrophage production of IL-6 and perpetuating the inflammatory cascade.² TL1A, a member of the tumor necrosis factor superfamily, signals through DR3 on T helper cells, innate lymphoid cells, and fibroblasts, amplifying type 2 and type 17 immune responses that characterize IBD.³ Defective efferocytosis further exacerbates inflammation by allowing apoptotic cells to undergo secondary necrosis, releasing damage-associated molecular patterns that recruit additional immune cells and perpetuate tissue injury.⁴

## Precision pharmacotherapy

### PTPRO inhibition

[[benzofuran-2-carboxylic-acid|Benzofuran-2-carboxylic acid]] derivatives represent a structure-guided approach to PTPRO inhibition. Lead compound 10j demonstrates potent and selective PTPRO inhibition (IC₅₀ = 0.54 μM) and suppresses IL-6 production in macrophages via NF-κB pathway modulation. In the DSS-induced murine colitis model, oral administration of 10j significantly attenuates disease severity, restores colon length, and suppresses IFN-γ and IL-2 levels. Further optimization yielded compound 30k with enhanced potency (IC₅₀ = 0.16 μM), superior in vivo efficacy, and an improved safety profile, establishing this scaffold as a promising pharmacological tool for phosphatase-targeted immunomodulation in IBD.²

### TL1A neutralization

The extended half-life monoclonal antibodies [[spy002|SPY002]] and [[spy072|SPY072]] selectively bind human TL1A with high affinity (K_D ≈ 31–35 pM) and potently inhibit DR3 signaling. Fc modifications attenuate effector function while increasing FcRn binding at acidic pH, resulting in enhanced pharmacokinetic profiles in nonhuman primates that support quarterly or biannual human dosing. Clinical proof of concept has been demonstrated in both ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease. In preclinical models including rat collagen-induced arthritis, humanized mouse psoriasis, and TNBS colitis, anti-TL1A treatment achieves efficacy comparable to TNF antagonists and anti-IL-23 therapies, with no drug-related adverse effects observed at exposures exceeding ten times anticipated clinical doses.³

## Bioengineered delivery platforms

### Light-activated bacterial biohybrids

A living biohybrid platform integrates optogenetically engineered [[shewanella-oneidensis|Shewanella oneidensis]] MR-1 with azo-bond covalent organic frameworks (TA-COFs) loaded with anti-inflammatory drugs [[magnolol|magnolol]] or [[4-iodobenzoic-acid|4-iodobenzoic acid]]. Under non-invasive red-light irradiation (660 nm), light-induced restoration of the metal-reducing pathway promotes extracellular electron transfer, cleaving azo bonds in the COF framework and triggering a 2.8-fold increase in drug release. Genetic modifications—expression of heat-shock genes (groES/thiF) and an acetate-to-TCA pathway (ato1/ato2/gltA)—confer 37°C tolerance and robust metabolism in the gut, overcoming the thermal inactivation and acetate utilization deficits of wild-type Shewanella. In DSS-induced colitis mice, oral administration of the biohybrid significantly alleviates inflammation, restores epithelial barrier integrity, and rebalances gut microbiota with enrichment of beneficial Akkermansia, Muribaculaceae, and Lachnospiraceae species. This electroactive living composite offers a generalizable strategy for light-programmed smart therapeutics.¹

### mRNA-mediated epithelial cell engineering

Lipid nanoparticle (LNP)-encapsulated [[mrna|mRNA]] enables functional editing of colonic epithelial cells in situ to enhance efferocytosis, the clearance of apoptotic cells critical for inflammation resolution and restoration of tissue homeostasis. Intraperitoneal administration of mRNA-loaded LNPs designed to boost efferocytic capacity markedly attenuates intestinal inflammation and halts disease progression in murine colitis models. This approach provides proof of concept that epithelial cells can be therapeutically reprogrammed in vivo, representing a promising avenue for mitigating inflammatory tissue damage without systemic immunosuppression.⁴

## Spatial transcriptomics and disease stratification

A spatially resolved atlas constructed from over three million cells across more than 100 intestinal tissue sections from non-diseased controls and patients with ulcerative colitis or Crohn's disease establishes a technical and biological benchmark for single-cell-resolved spatial transcriptomics in gastrointestinal tissue. Systematic evaluation of two imaging-based platforms reveals that CosMx achieves higher detection efficiency than Xenium across commercially available panels in both disease subtypes, with Xenium showing reduced performance associated with tissue type, block quality, and panel size. CosMx identifies regulatory T cell-associated biology in both ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease, findings validated through independent laboratory experiments and multiplex spatial multi-omics. Stability of CosMx data quality across variation in fixation time and sectioning procedures supports its operational feasibility for multi-center translational IBD research, enabling molecular-level disease stratification and personalized therapeutic selection.⁵
