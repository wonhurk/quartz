**Hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD)** is a common childhood infection caused by [[enterovirus|enteroviruses]], with four major pathogenic subtypes—[[coxsackievirus-a6|coxsackievirus A6 (CVA6)]], [[coxsackievirus-a16|coxsackievirus A16 (CVA16)]], [[enterovirus-a71|enterovirus A71 (EV-A71)]], and [[coxsackievirus-a10|coxsackievirus A10 (CVA10)]]—that circulate with distinct regional and seasonal epidemiological patterns.¹ The pathogenicity of CVA6 is governed by a single critical amino acid residue, [[vp3|VP3]]-E238 (glutamic acid), located at the viral capsid receptor-binding canyon, which is both necessary and sufficient for lethal disease in neonatal models.² This residue mediates direct interaction with [[kremen1|KREMEN1]], the essential viral entry receptor, and is maintained at high frequency (>93%) in circulating strains, reflecting a substantial fitness advantage in natural populations.

---

## Mechanism of CVA6 pathogenesis

The virulence of CVA6 is fundamentally determined by a single position within the [[vp3|VP3]] capsid protein. The naturally occurring glutamic acid at position 238 (E238) is situated at the edge of the receptor-binding canyon, a key structural domain responsible for viral cell entry. When this residue is altered to alanine (E238A), the mutation does not impair virion assembly or replication in cultured cells, yet it catastrophically reduces binding affinity to the essential murine entry receptor [[kremen1|KREMEN1]] and attenuates virulence by over 10,000-fold in neonatal mice models.² The E238A substitution correlates with dramatically lower tissue viral loads and reduced pathological injury, demonstrating that VP3-E238 is both necessary and sufficient for lethal disease manifestation.

The fitness advantage of E238 is evident in epidemiological surveys of naturally circulating CVA6 strains, where the glutamic acid residue dominates at frequencies exceeding 93%, suggesting strong positive selection despite its pathogenic consequences.² This dominance implies that the virulence determinant confers significant advantages for viral transmission or replication under natural conditions, potentially through enhanced receptor recognition or cellular tropism.

## Epidemiology and geographic variation

HFMD exhibits marked regional and seasonal variation driven by differential circulation of the four major enterovirus subtypes.¹ [[coxsackievirus-a6|CVA6]] has emerged as a particularly significant pathogen, capable of causing severe disease in infants and young children. The distinct epidemiological footprints of EV-A71, CVA16, CVA6, and CVA10 reflect differences in viral properties, host immunity, and population-level transmission dynamics. Understanding these patterns is critical for public health preparedness and resource allocation during outbreak seasons.

---

## Surveillance and rapid detection

Modern surveillance of HFMD has been transformed by wastewater-based epidemiology, which enables population-level monitoring of infection dynamics and viral subtype distribution without requiring individual clinical diagnoses.¹ This approach provides early warning of viral circulation and outbreak risk before peak clinical incidence.

Rapid point-of-care diagnosis has been accelerated by the development of integrated multiplex detection assays. A novel [[crispr-cas12a|CRISPR-Cas12a]]-based platform, combined with one-pot [[rt-rpa|reverse transcription recombinase polymerase amplification (RT-RPA)]], detects all four major HFMD subtypes with attomolar sensitivity within one hour.¹ This assay was integrated into a [[microfluidic-chip|microfluidic chip]] system—termed the Heparin-Inhibited CRISPR-Associated System Chip ([[hicas-chip|HICAS-Chip]])—that performs automated sample enrichment, purification, elution, and multiplexed detection in a single device.¹ During year-long wastewater surveillance in Guiyang City, China, the HICAS-Chip identified EV-A71 and CVA10 as the predominant circulating subtypes, with marked incidence peaks in June, November, and December.

---

## Vaccine development

The identification of VP3-E238 as the primary virulence determinant has enabled rational design of live-attenuated vaccine candidates. By introducing the attenuating E238A mutation into naturally circulating CVA6 strains, vaccine developers have created candidate formulations that eliminate pathogenicity while preserving viral replication capacity and immunogenicity.² Immunization with these E238A-engineered variants provides complete protection against lethal [[coxsackievirus-a6|CVA6]] challenge in animal models, establishing proof-of-concept for this virulence-targeted approach.² This strategy represents a paradigm for structure-guided rational vaccine design, wherein the molecular basis of pathogenesis directly informs the engineering of safe, effective immunogens.
