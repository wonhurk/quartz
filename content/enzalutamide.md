**Enzalutamide** is a potent [[androgen-receptor|androgen receptor (AR)]] inhibitor widely deployed as a standard-of-care therapeutic for [[castration-resistant-prostate-cancer|castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC)]]. Its mechanism involves direct blockade of AR-mediated transcription and nuclear translocation, yet development of drug resistance remains a critical clinical limitation. Recent mechanistic studies have revealed that resistant tumors suppress [[decorin]]-driven [[ferroptosis]], linking lipid remodeling failure to therapeutic escape.¹

---

## Mechanism

Enzalutamide binds the [[androgen-receptor|AR]] ligand-binding domain with high affinity, preventing the conformational changes necessary for AR nuclear translocation and transcriptional activation of androgen-responsive genes central to prostate cancer cell survival. The drug exhibits superior pharmacokinetic properties and blood-brain barrier penetration compared to earlier [[androgen-receptor|AR]] antagonists, enabling activity in castration-resistant disease where upregulation of intracellular androgens and alternative AR signaling pathways confer resistance to traditional chemical castration.

---

## CRPC therapy

Enzalutamide is a cornerstone therapeutic for metastatic and non-metastatic [[castration-resistant-prostate-cancer|CRPC]] across multiple lines of therapy. Its efficacy derives from dual mechanisms: AR-target occupancy and interference with AR nuclear trafficking. However, therapeutic resistance emerges through multiple pathways, including continued AR signaling via ligand-independent mechanisms, AR point mutations that alter drug binding, and metabolic reprogramming that limits drug bioavailability.

---

## Ferroptosis-dependent sensitivity and resistance

The mechanistic basis of enzalutamide efficacy has recently been reframed through the lens of lipid-dependent cell death. [[Decorin]], a matricellular proteoglycan suppressing prostate cancer progression, regulates [[ferroptosis]] sensitivity by phosphorylating [[acsl4|ACSL4]] through a calcium-dependent PKCβII mechanism, thereby promoting lipid remodeling and ferroptosis-mediated cytotoxicity.¹ In enzalutamide-resistant [[castration-resistant-prostate-cancer|CRPC]] cells, [[decorin]] expression is significantly downregulated, and this loss directly suppresses [[acsl4|ACSL4]]-dependent lipid remodeling, rendering resistant cells refractory to ferroptosis and thus insensitive to drug-induced cell death.¹ Importantly, [[androgen-receptor|AR]] nuclear translocation itself negatively regulates [[decorin]] transcription, establishing a feed-forward mechanism in which AR persistence — either through acquired mutations or ligand-independent activation — drives ferroptosis evasion.

---

## Overcoming resistance

[[Decorin]] restoration re-sensitizes enzalutamide-resistant cells to drug-induced [[ferroptosis]], demonstrating that ferroptosis reactivation is sufficient to restore therapeutic efficacy. [[Decorin]] overexpression not only re-sensitizes resistant cells in vitro but also significantly inhibits tumor growth and metastasis in xenograft models of drug-resistant disease under enzalutamide treatment, confirming that [[ferroptosis]]-dependent mechanisms are essential to antitumor activity.¹ This finding suggests that strategies targeting [[acsl4|ACSL4]] phosphorylation, calcium signaling, or [[decorin]] restoration may serve as rational approaches to overcome acquired enzalutamide resistance.

---

## Combination with radioligand therapy

The Phase II ARROW study evaluates combination therapy with the [[psma|prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)]]-targeting radioligand 131I-LNTH-1095 in patients with metastatic [[castration-resistant-prostate-cancer|CRPC]] who have progressed on prior [[abiraterone]] therapy.² This combination approach leverages both [[androgen-receptor|AR]] inhibition and radiation-mediated DNA damage to target [[psma|PSMA]]-avid tumor cells, with the potential to overcome cross-resistance mechanisms and delay the emergence of subsequent-line resistant clones.
