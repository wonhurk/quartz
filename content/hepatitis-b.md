**Hepatitis B** is a chronic viral infection caused by the hepatitis B virus (HBV) characterized by highly variable clinical progression and immune dysregulation. The disease pathogenesis is fundamentally shaped by overproduction of [[hbsag|hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg)]], a soluble form of the viral envelope protein that drives immune tolerance and permits persistent viral replication.¹ Chronic infection shows marked phenotypic heterogeneity linked to specific HBV genetic variants, particularly the A1762T/G1764A mutations, which dysregulate cholesterol metabolism and correlate with increased hepatic inflammation severity.² Progression to [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]] is driven by distinct cancer stem cell populations arising from endothelial compartments, with stemness markers [[ssr2|SSR2]] and [[ube2d3|UBE2D3]] serving as predictive biomarkers of tumor evolution and therapeutic resistance.³

---

## Mechanism of chronic infection

Hepatitis B establishes persistence through a sophisticated immune evasion strategy centered on massive [[hbsag|HBsAg]] overproduction. Unlike authentic virions, which contain the full envelope protein in a tightly regulated stoichiometry, HBsAg is released in vast molar excess as non-infectious subviral particles and soluble monomers. This antigenic excess exhausts and tolerizes the host's HBV-specific B and T cell responses, preventing viral clearance and allowing indefinite viral replication in hepatocytes.¹ The infected liver progressively accumulates hepatic inflammation and fibrosis as immune tolerance persists.

Genetic variation within the HBV genome significantly modulates disease severity and host metabolic response. The [[alt|alanine aminotransferase (ALT)]] and [[ast|aspartate aminotransferase (AST)]] levels — cardinal markers of hepatocellular injury — are substantially elevated in patients harboring the A1762T/G1764A variants compared to wild-type infection.² Comparative multi-omics profiling across variant-stratified patient cohorts revealed that these mutations profoundly dysregulate cholesterol metabolism, the most significantly perturbed pathway identified across integrated proteomic and metabolomic datasets. Among 201–226 altered metabolites detected in mutant-infected patients, six key proteins and metabolites emerged as robust biomarkers for distinguishing HBV variant groups and predicting clinical outcomes.²

## HBV-associated hepatocellular carcinoma

Chronic HBV infection is a major etiology of [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma]], accounting for a substantial global burden of HCC morbidity and mortality. The transition from chronic hepatitis to malignancy is orchestrated by cancer stem cells (CSCs) that acquire stemness features enabling tumor initiation, self-renewal, and resistance to conventional therapies.³ Integrated single-cell transcriptomic analysis identified the endothelial cell compartment as a critical reservoir of stemness-associated cells in HBV-HCC, with a putative stemness-enriched endothelial subpopulation (Cluster 4) harboring 21 differentially HBV-expressed genes.³

Cross-referencing single-cell and bulk transcriptomic datasets through weighted gene co-expression network analysis and random forest feature selection identified two high-confidence stemness biomarkers: [[ssr2|SSR2]] and [[ube2d3|UBE2D3]].³ Notably, high SSR2 expression coupled with low UBE2D3 expression was associated with poor clinical outcomes and shortened overall survival across independent HCC cohorts, suggesting that this stemness signature may predict tumor aggressiveness and therapeutic resistance. The mechanistic insights linking these stemness biomarkers to HBV-driven tumor progression and their potential as therapeutic targets merit further investigation.

## Novel therapeutic approaches

[[hbsag|HBsAg]] overproduction represents a rational and validated therapeutic target for achieving functional cure in chronic HBV infection. RG7834, the first-in-class small-molecule [[hbsag|HBsAg]] production inhibitor, demonstrated potent antiviral activity and advanced to Phase I clinical trials; however, development was halted due to unanticipated neurotoxicity.¹ Systematic structure optimization yielded (6S,10S)-57, a tetracyclic dihydroquinolizinone derivative achieving potent [[hbsag|HBsAg]] inhibition (EC50 = 0.63 nM) with outstanding hepatic selectivity (liver-to-blood tissue distribution ratio = 13.4) and crucially, no neurite outgrowth inhibition at concentrations up to 90 μM.¹ In an AAV-HBV transgenic mouse model, oral administration of (6S,10S)-57 reduced serum [[hbsag|HBsAg]] by 0.78–0.97 log₁₀ at physiologically achievable doses, demonstrating efficacy comparable to RG7834 while decoupling antiviral potency from off-target neurotoxicity.¹ These pharmacological advances position (6S,10S)-57 as a safer, orally bioavailable candidate for clinical development in chronic hepatitis B.
