**Prostate-Specific Membrane Antigen (PSMA)** is a cell-surface glycoprotein highly overexpressed in [[prostate-cancer|prostate cancer]] that functions as both a diagnostic and therapeutic biomarker with independent prognostic significance in advanced disease.¹ Unlike the current standard blood test [[psa|prostate-specific antigen (PSA)]], which lacks diagnostic specificity and generates frequent false positives, PSMA offers superior diagnostic accuracy through non-invasive detection in urine, plasma, and circulating tumor cells without requiring tissue biopsy.¹ PSMA-positive circulating tumor cells and extracellular vesicles are strong independent predictors of poor progression-free and overall survival in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer.³ The protein serves as the molecular target for transformative radioligand therapies—including [[lnth-1095|131I-LNTH-1095]] and [[177lu-psma-617|177Lu-PSMA-617]]—which are increasingly combined with androgen receptor inhibitors [[enzalutamide]] and [[abiraterone]] to achieve durable survival benefits in advanced disease.

---

## Mechanism and Expression

PSMA is a type II transmembrane glycoprotein with exquisite selectivity for prostate tissue and substantial upregulation in malignant cells compared to benign prostatic hyperplasia. The cell-surface accessibility of PSMA makes it inherently suited for both imaging agents and therapeutic ligands, enabling targeted molecular imaging and therapeutic delivery without invasive biopsy. This favorable therapeutic window—high expression in cancer relative to surrounding normal tissue—underpins the entire PSMA-directed diagnostic and therapeutic platform in advanced prostate cancer.

---

## Diagnostic Applications and Imaging

PSMA has emerged as a superior diagnostic biomarker to conventional PSA testing, offering greater diagnostic specificity and enabling accurate disease monitoring through non-invasive liquid biopsy. Urinary PSMA detection using advanced electrochemical aptasensors constructed with three-dimensional reduced graphene oxide and gold nanoparticles achieves ultrasensitive detection limits of 5 pg/mL, enabling precise quantification from minimally invasive samples.¹ The aptasensor platform utilizes PSMA-specific DNA aptamers immobilized on a three-working-electrode screen-printed platform, permitting simultaneous triplicate measurements from single samples and substantially improving analytical precision and reproducibility compared to conventional single-electrode biosensors.¹

PSMA-PET imaging has become the standard imaging modality for disease staging and molecular tumor volume assessment in men with suspected or confirmed advanced prostate cancer.² PSMA-PET provides superior visualization of disease burden and enables precise quantification of tumor extent, supporting treatment planning and real-time monitoring of therapeutic response in metastatic castration-resistant disease.

---

## Therapeutic Targeting and Combination Therapy

PSMA-targeted radioligand therapy represents a transformative treatment paradigm for men with metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer who have progressed on prior androgen-deprivation therapy. The radionuclide [[lnth-1095|131I-LNTH-1095]], an iodine-131-labeled PSMA-targeting small molecule, has been clinically evaluated in combination with the androgen receptor inhibitor [[enzalutamide]] in the ARROW phase II trial.² This dual-mechanism approach leverages direct radiotherapeutic cytotoxicity from 131I-LNTH-1095 alongside continued hormonal suppression, addressing both androgen signaling and PSMA-dependent disease burden simultaneously. Similarly, [[177lu-psma-617|177Lu-PSMA-617]], labeled with beta-emitting lutetium-177, is increasingly combined with androgen receptor pathway inhibitors [[enzalutamide]] and [[abiraterone]], particularly in patients with chemotherapy-resistant disease.³ These combination regimens reflect evolving clinical practice, wherein radioligand therapy is sequentially integrated into comprehensive treatment algorithms to maximize progression-free and overall survival in advanced disease.

## Prognostic Significance and Liquid Biopsy

PSMA expression on circulating tumor cells and extracellular vesicles serves as a powerful independent prognostic marker in men receiving PSMA-targeted therapy. In patients treated with [[177lu-psma-617|177Lu-PSMA-617]], elevated levels of PSMA-positive circulating tumor cells and PSMA-enriched extracellular vesicle proteins are strongly associated with worse progression-free and overall survival outcomes.³ Plasma extracellular vesicle proteomics identifies coordinated expression of multiple therapeutic targets including [[b7-h3|B7-H3]], [[trop-2|Trop-2]], and [[steap1|STEAP1]] on the same cancer cells, correlating with poor survival and PSMA-PET molecular tumor burden.³ Circulating PSMA-positive/EpCAM-positive and PSMA-negative/EpCAM-positive cell populations independently predict worse survival, supporting the integration of liquid biopsy-based circulating tumor cell enumeration and proteomic profiling into biomarker-driven clinical trial designs for improved patient stratification.

---

## Novel Detection Technologies

Advanced electrochemical aptasensors represent an emerging class of highly sensitive PSMA detection platforms that overcome limitations of conventional immunoassays for point-of-care diagnostics. Three-dimensional reduced graphene oxide nanostructures with embedded gold nanoparticles provide exceptional surface area and enhanced electron transfer kinetics, enabling efficient immobilization of PSMA-specific DNA aptamers with minimal variability.¹ This nanostructured biosensing interface amplifies electrochemical signals and permits ultrasensitive quantification from minimal sample volumes obtained non-invasively through urine collection.¹ The multiplexed three-electrode configuration enables simultaneous triplicate measurements from single samples, substantially improving analytical precision and reducing variability compared to conventional single-electrode biosensor platforms, paving the way for rapid clinical implementation.
