**Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS)** is a common adverse event triggered by activation and proliferation of immune effector cells during treatment with [[car-t-cell-therapy|T-cell-engaging immunotherapies]], characterized by excessive production of pro-inflammatory cytokines that flood the systemic circulation. The syndrome emerges as a consequence of dual-signaling engagement in [[car-t-cell-therapy|CAR-T cell therapies]] and bispecific antibodies targeting [[cd20]] or [[cd3]], and manifests predominantly early in treatment during the first cycle of therapy.¹² In older patients aged ≥70 years with [[mantle-cell-lymphoma|relapsed/refractory mantle cell lymphoma]] treated with [[brexu-cel|brexucabtagene autoleucel (brexu-cel)]], the cumulative incidence of any-grade CRS reaches 80% by day 30 post-infusion, though grade ≥3 events occur in only 9% of patients.¹ Similarly, the bispecific T-cell-engaging antibody [[mosunetuzumab]] induces CRS in approximately 65% of patients with [[richter-transformation|Richter transformation]], predominantly as low-grade toxicity.² Despite its frequency, CRS is generally manageable with supportive care and does not routinely necessitate discontinuation of therapy, allowing patients to achieve durable clinical responses and sustained remissions.

---

## Mechanism and onset

CRS emerges when T-cell-engaging immunotherapies engage their dual-signaling architecture to activate T cells in the presence of antigen-expressing malignant cells. This triggering mechanism is central to the therapeutic potency of [[car-t-cell-therapy|CAR-T cells]] and [[bispecific-antibodies]], which directly engage both T cells and tumor-associated antigens to drive rapid immune activation. The resulting T-cell proliferation and differentiation unleash a cascade of pro-inflammatory cytokines including interleukin-6, tumor necrosis factor-alpha, and interferon-gamma, which increase vascular permeability and trigger systemic inflammatory responses. The clinical manifestations — fever, hypoxia, and capillary leak syndrome — develop within hours to days of immune cell engagement and characteristically emerge during the first treatment cycle as the initial wave of T-cell activation peaks.

---

## CAR-T cell therapy–associated CRS

In the context of [[car-t-cell-therapy]] with [[brexu-cel]], CRS is highly prevalent and constitutes a defining feature of the immune response to treatment. A real-world analysis of 233 patients aged ≥70 years with [[mantle-cell-lymphoma|relapsed/refractory mantle cell lymphoma]] reported that by day 30 post-infusion, any-grade CRS occurred in 80% of patients, with only 9% experiencing grade ≥3 severity.¹ The predominance of low-to-moderate grade events reflects the tolerable nature of CRS in this population; patients were managed with standard supportive measures including antipyretics, intravenous hydration, and hemodynamic support without requiring therapy discontinuation. Despite the high incidence of CRS, [[brexu-cel]] achieved substantial clinical benefit, with 78% of patients attaining complete remission by day 100.¹ Critically, age alone did not predict inferior outcomes; rather, baseline performance status emerged as the strongest predictor of both progression-free and overall survival, with one-year overall survival of 74%.¹

---

## Bispecific antibody–associated CRS

[[Mosunetuzumab]], a bispecific CD20/CD3 antibody, demonstrates a similarly high incidence of CRS but with a predominantly low-grade safety profile.² In a cohort of 20 patients with [[richter-transformation|relapsed/refractory Richter transformation]], CRS developed in 65% of patients; nearly all cases were either grade 1 (20%) or grade 2 (40%), and no adverse events necessitated treatment discontinuation.² The clinical importance of this low-grade predominance lies in its compatibility with sustained therapy and durable disease control: patients achieved an overall response rate of 40% and a complete response rate of 20%, with complete responses durable beyond 20 months without further therapy.² Furthermore, two patients successfully transitioned to allogeneic stem cell transplantation while in complete remission following [[mosunetuzumab]] therapy, with no subsequent relapse.² The favorable toxicity profile of [[mosunetuzumab]], despite a 65% incidence of CRS, underscores that the frequency of an adverse event and its clinical tolerability are distinct properties; high incidence does not preclude therapeutic benefit when grades are predominantly low.

---

## Co-occurring immune toxicity and systemic effects

CRS frequently develops in tandem with [[immune-effector-cell-associated-neurotoxicity-syndrome|immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS)]], reflecting the systemic nature of immune activation in [[car-t-cell-therapy]] recipients. In the [[brexu-cel]] cohort of older patients, [[immune-effector-cell-associated-neurotoxicity-syndrome|ICANS]] occurred in 57% of patients at day 30 post-infusion, with 22% experiencing grade ≥3 severity.¹ The concurrence of CRS and [[immune-effector-cell-associated-neurotoxicity-syndrome|ICANS]] demonstrates that T-cell activation triggers multi-organ inflammatory effects; pro-inflammatory cytokines released systemically penetrate the blood-brain barrier and activate endothelial and resident immune cells in the central nervous system. Despite this dual toxicity burden, patients retained substantial clinical benefit and tolerability, confirming that early recognition and supportive management effectively mitigate clinical harm while preserving therapeutic efficacy.

---

## Clinical management and therapeutic compatibility

The high incidence of CRS has not proven prohibitive to the clinical use of [[car-t-cell-therapy|CAR-T cell therapies]] or bispecific T-cell-engaging antibodies, as the majority of events are low-grade and managed with supportive measures. The cornerstone of CRS management remains vigorous supportive care, including fever management, volume resuscitation, and hemodynamic monitoring.¹² Treatment discontinuation is rarely necessary; instead, temporary dose modification or brief treatment delay, combined with anti-inflammatory interventions and careful clinical monitoring, permits disease control while minimizing cumulative toxicity burden. The observation that durable clinical responses occur in patients who experience CRS—including sustained complete remissions, progression-free survival of years, and successful bridge to stem cell transplantation—confirms that CRS is compatible with meaningful therapeutic benefit and does not represent a barrier to the use of these potentially curative immunotherapies in [[mantle-cell-lymphoma|mantle cell lymphoma]] and [[richter-transformation|Richter transformation]].
