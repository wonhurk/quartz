**Ferrocene** is a versatile iron-containing organometallic compound that bridges cancer therapeutics and precision diagnostics through two distinct biomedical mechanisms. In oncology, ferrocene functions as a [[ferroptosis]] booster, efficiently catalyzing hydroxyl radical (•OH) generation via Fenton chemistry to sensitize cancer cells to iron-dependent cell death.¹ In diagnostic contexts, ferrocene serves as an electroactive material on electrochemical biosensor interfaces, catalytically amplifying detection signals for sensitive and long-term stable monitoring of circulating tumor markers.² The compound's redox-active properties and capacity for both destructive radical generation and constructive signal transduction exemplify its dual utility in modern cancer care.

---

## Mechanism

Ferrocene exerts its ferroptotic activity through iron-catalyzed redox cycling. When activated in the presence of cellular hydrogen peroxide or other reactive substrates, ferrocene efficiently facilitates the Fenton reaction, producing hydroxyl radicals that overwhelm the antioxidant defenses of cancer cells.¹ This radical generation is particularly potent when coupled with [[ferroptosis]] inducers that simultaneously deplete intracellular glutathione (GSH), the primary cellular defense against lipid peroxidation. In drug delivery contexts, ferrocene is incorporated into GSH-responsive disulfide-linked nanostructures, allowing its intracellular release only when exposed to the reducing environment of the tumor cell cytoplasm.¹ This compartmentalization ensures that ferroptosis is triggered specifically within target malignant cells while sparing healthy tissues.

In diagnostic applications, ferrocene's electrochemical properties enable signal amplification through catalytic decomposition of hydrogen peroxide. When immobilized on electrode surfaces alongside enzymatic components such as glucose oxidase, ferrocene catalyzes the reduction of H₂O₂ in a chronocoulometric assay, producing a secondary amplification layer that dramatically increases detection sensitivity while maintaining a near-zero background signal.²

## TNBC therapy

[[Triple-negative breast cancer|Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)]] lacks the hormone receptors and HER2 overexpression that characterize luminal and HER2-enriched subtypes, rendering conventional endocrine and HER2-targeted therapies ineffective. To address this therapeutic void, ferrocene has been integrated into the [[sor-rssf-nanoparticles|SOR@RSSF nanoparticle system]], a GSH-responsive nanoprodrug that synchronously releases ferrocene alongside the chemotherapeutic agent rhein and the ferroptosis inducer sorafenib.¹ This three-drug combination addresses multiple resistance mechanisms: sorafenib impairs the cellular ferroptosis defense machinery, ferrocene generates oxidative stress through Fenton-catalyzed hydroxyl radical production, and rhein delivers chemotherapeutic cytotoxicity. The resulting synergy triggers both ferroptosis and apoptosis within tumor cells, while the GSH-responsive disulfide linker ensures drug release occurs preferentially in the reducing microenvironment of cancer cells rather than in circulating blood or healthy tissues.¹ The nanoparticle platform exhibits markedly enhanced cellular uptake and specific release kinetics, positioning it as a promising approach for TNBC patients with limited therapeutic options.

---

## Tumor biomarker sensing

Beyond oncotherapy, ferrocene has become integral to next-generation electrochemical biosensing platforms for real-time monitoring of circulating tumor markers. In a representative immunosandwich architecture, ferrocene is immobilized on gold nanoparticles deposited onto carbon nanotube-coated electrodes, forming the first catalytic amplification layer.² Antibody-conjugated glucose oxidase-loaded covalent organic frameworks constitute the immunoprobe, and upon sandwich assembly, glucose oxidation produces hydrogen peroxide, which ferrocene subsequently catalyzes into electrochemical current. This dual amplification strategy—enzyme-generated H₂O₂ coupled with ferrocene-catalyzed electrochemistry—yields exceptional sensitivity for prostate-specific antigen and other disease biomarkers while maintaining long-term electrochemical stability.² The near-zero background signal achieved through this architecture addresses a critical limitation of conventional electrochemical sensors: the accumulation of interfering signals during extended monitoring in complex biological matrices.
