**PD-1 (programmed cell death protein 1)** is an immune checkpoint receptor expressed on activated T cells and [[foxp3|regulatory T cells]] that mediates tumor immune evasion through interaction with its ligand [[pd-l1|PD-L1]]. The PD-1/PD-L1 axis represents a critical negative regulatory pathway that suppresses CD8+ T cell effector functions and promotes immunosuppression within the tumor microenvironment.¹ Anti-PD-1 antibody therapy has emerged as a standard immune checkpoint inhibitor treatment across multiple malignancies including non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), colorectal cancer (CRC), cervical cancer, pleural mesothelioma, [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)]], and [[oral-squamous-cell-carcinoma|oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC)]], yet clinical response remains heterogeneous with 50-85% of patients exhibiting primary or secondary resistance depending on cancer type.³⁶⁷

---

## Mechanism

PD-1 functions as a co-inhibitory receptor that delivers negative signals to T cells upon engagement with [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] expressed on tumor cells or antigen-presenting cells. This interaction attenuates T cell receptor signaling, diminishes effector cytokine production including [[il-8|IL-8]], and impairs [[granzyme-b|granzyme B]]-mediated cytotoxicity.³ Within the tumor microenvironment, PD-1 expression marks exhausted CD8+ T cell populations that have lost proliferative capacity and effector function following chronic antigen exposure.³ Concurrently, PD-1 expression on [[foxp3|FOXP3+]] regulatory T cells amplifies their suppressive capacity, creating a multilayered immunosuppressive network that shields tumors from immune attack.⁴⁵

---

## Cancer applications

Anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibodies have demonstrated clinical efficacy across diverse tumor types, yet response rates vary substantially. In NSCLC, anti-PD-1 therapy induces durable tumor regression in approximately 20-30% of patients, with responsiveness inversely correlated with protein kinase C-ι (PKC-ι) expression.⁶ In dMMR/MSI-H metastatic colorectal cancer, 15-50% of patients experience primary or secondary resistance despite high tumor mutational burden, prompting development of transcriptomic clustering systems and eight-gene signatures to stratify patients who benefit from dual [[ctla-4|CTLA-4]] and PD-1 blockade.⁷ In pleural mesothelioma, DNA methylation profiling identifies four subsets with divergent immunotherapy outcomes: the LOW methylation subtype exhibits a T cell- and B cell-rich immune microenvironment and demonstrates the longest overall survival following PD-1/CTLA-4 combination therapy, whereas the CIMP (CpG island methylator phenotype) subtype harbors a depleted immune landscape and remains largely refractory.⁸

In cervical cancer, the immunosuppressive axis mediated by IGSF3-TNFR2 interaction drives regulatory T cell infiltration and activation, limiting anti-PD-1 efficacy. Disruption of this pathway with purpurogallin, a structure-based IGSF3 inhibitor, reduces [[foxp3|Treg]] abundance and synergizes with anti-PD-1 and anti-[[ctla-4|CTLA-4]] antibodies, with pretreatment followed by PD-1 blockade yielding prominent therapeutic efficacy.⁵ In [[oral-squamous-cell-carcinoma|OSCC]], elevated expression of both [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] and Cip2a (cancerous inhibitor of protein phosphatase 2A) correlates with poor prognosis, motivating combination strategies that unite tumor-intrinsic apoptosis induction with immune checkpoint modulation.⁹

## Resistance mechanisms

Multiple tumor-intrinsic and microenvironmental factors converge to mediate resistance to anti-PD-1 therapy. In NSCLC, high PKC-ι expression drives resistance through Yap1-dependent transcription of CCL7, which recruits tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) and suppresses CD8+ T cell infiltration. Pharmacological inhibition of PKC-ι with auranofin disrupts the PKC-ι-Yap1-CCL7 axis, reduces TAM accumulation, and restores CD8+ T cell-dependent antitumor immunity when combined with anti-PD-1 antibody.⁶ In [[oral-squamous-cell-carcinoma|OSCC]], Cip2a overexpression sustains tumor survival signaling and establishes an immunosuppressive microenvironment that blunts PD-1 blockade efficacy.⁹ Regulatory T cell-mediated immunosuppression represents another critical resistance mechanism: [[foxp3|FOXP3+]] PD-1+ [[tigit|TIGIT+]] Tregs accumulate in the tumor microenvironment and express immune checkpoints that reinforce suppression of effector T cells.⁴⁵

---

## Combination strategies

Dual immune checkpoint blockade targeting PD-1 and [[ctla-4|CTLA-4]] has become a standard combination strategy, particularly in melanoma and colorectal cancer, by simultaneously relieving distinct negative regulatory circuits governing T cell activation and effector function.⁷ Galectin inhibition represents an emerging complementary approach: nanobody-derived minibodies G1M1 and G7M8 selectively block galectin-1 and galectin-7, two glyco-immune checkpoints overexpressed in [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|TNBC]]. G1M1 alone achieves results comparable to anti-PD-1 therapy in limiting lung metastases and increasing CD4+ T cell infiltration while decreasing PD-1+ Tregs, and both minibodies enhance efficacy when combined with PD-1 blockade.¹

In [[t-cell-acute-lymphoblastic-leukemia|T-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (T-ALL)]], a subpopulation of bone marrow-enriched CCR4+ [[foxp3|FOXP3+]] regulatory T cells expresses PD-1 and [[tigit|TIGIT]], presenting an opportunity for combined CCR4-directed therapy with immune checkpoint inhibition.⁴ Ferroptosis induction creates a distinct synergistic mechanism: ferroptotic tumor cells release all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA), which activates CD38 and transcription factor EB (TFEB) in tumor-associated macrophages, driving MHC-II-dependent antigen presentation. This ferroptosis-immune axis synergizes with anti-PD-1 therapy across preclinical models, establishing a positive feedback loop wherein activated macrophages further promote immune-driven tumor ferroptosis.¹⁰

## Novel therapeutic approaches

Biomimetic nanoplatforms offer innovative strategies to overcome limitations of antibody-based PD-1 blockade. The siCip2a@PML system encapsulates Cip2a siRNA within lipid nanoparticles cloaked with PD-1-overexpressing macrophage membrane, enabling competitive blockade of PD-1/PD-L1 signaling while simultaneously suppressing tumor-intrinsic survival pathways. This dual-mechanism platform enhances tumor accumulation, promotes dendritic cell maturation, increases intratumoral CD4+ and CD8+ T cell infiltration, and restores antitumor immunity in [[oral-squamous-cell-carcinoma|OSCC]] models.⁹

Activated T cell extracellular vesicles ([[atevs|ATEVs]]) represent a cell-free therapeutic modality that turns immunologically cold tumors hot. [[atevs|ATEVs]] transfer genomic DNA into dendritic cells and tumor cells via [[granzyme-b|granzyme B]]-mediated nuclear delivery, boosting antigen presentation and priming antitumor immunity. When combined with PD-1 blockade, [[atevs|ATEVs]] synergistically overcome immune evasion, offering a scalable alternative to adoptive cell therapy.² Drug-free nano-redox levers that selectively disrupt glutathione metabolism in hypoxic tumor regions potentiate ferroptosis-mediated immune stimulation, creating a positive feedback loop that amplifies anti-PD-1 efficacy.¹⁰
