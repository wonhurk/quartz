**Gastro-oesophageal junction cancer** is a gastric malignancy arising at the interface between the stomach and esophagus, characterized by distinct molecular subtypes with emerging immunotherapeutic vulnerabilities. The disease has been molecularly stratified by two major therapeutic targets: [[cldn18.2|CLDN18.2]], a claudin protein with restricted expression in gastric and gastro-esophageal epithelium that serves as an immunogenic target for monoclonal antibodies and T cell-redirected therapies, and [[her2|HER2]], whose amplification identifies a molecularly-defined subset amenable to anti-HER2 plus immunotherapy combinations.¹ For patients with HER2-positive unresectable or metastatic disease, the combination of [[pembrolizumab|pembrolizumab]] (anti-[[pd-1|PD-1]] monoclonal antibody) plus [[trastuzumab|trastuzumab]] (anti-HER2 antibody) and platinum-based chemotherapy represents the current standard of care, approved by the FDA following the KEYNOTE-811 trial.¹ Treatment efficacy is significantly enriched in patients with elevated [[pd-l1|PD-L1 combined positive score (CPS)]], identifying a potential biomarker for patient selection and prognostic stratification.¹

---

## Molecular targets and resistance mechanisms

[[cldn18.2|CLDN18.2]] has emerged as a validated therapeutic target in gastric and gastro-oesophageal junction cancers, reflecting its restricted tissue expression pattern and essential role in tight junction architecture.² The protein serves as an immunogenic target for monoclonal antibody and T cell-redirected immunotherapies, with clinical development ongoing in this disease context. However, regulatory mechanisms can significantly impair therapeutic efficacy. [[kras|KRAS]] mutations drive O-GlcNAcylation of CLDN18.2, a post-translational modification that reduces its surface stability and accessibility to CLDN18.2-directed immunotherapy.² This mechanism represents a form of intrinsic and acquired resistance particularly relevant in gastrointestinal malignancies where KRAS alterations are common, suggesting that CLDN18.2-targeted approaches may require combination with RAS pathway inhibition or alternative resistance-modulating strategies in KRAS-mutant tumors.

[[her2|HER2]] amplification defines a molecularly-distinct subtype of gastro-oesophageal junction cancer with established responsiveness to anti-HER2-targeted therapy. The synergy between HER2 blockade via trastuzumab and immune checkpoint inhibition via [[pd-1|PD-1]] antagonism reflects both direct tumor cell death from growth factor pathway inhibition and immunological priming from HER2-antigen presentation to T cells, creating a complementary therapeutic effect.

## HER2-positive disease and KEYNOTE-811 outcomes

The KEYNOTE-811 trial established pembrolizumab plus trastuzumab and platinum-based chemotherapy as the standard therapeutic regimen for HER2-positive unresectable or metastatic gastro-oesophageal junction carcinoma. The randomized, multiregional trial enrolled 695 patients assigned to receive pembrolizumab or placebo, combined with trastuzumab and chemotherapy (5-fluorouracil or capecitabine plus cisplatin).¹ The trial demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in overall response rate (ORR), the primary efficacy endpoint: 74.4% in the pembrolizumab arm versus 51.9% with placebo (P = 0.00006).¹ Final overall survival (OS) analysis confirmed clinically meaningful benefit, with median OS of 20.0 months (95% CI, 17.8–22.1) in the pembrolizumab arm compared to 16.8 months (95% CI, 14.9–18.7) with placebo, corresponding to a hazard ratio of 0.80 (95% CI, 0.67–0.94; P = 0.004).¹ This approximately 3-month improvement in median OS represents a substantial clinical benefit in an advanced malignancy with historically poor outcomes.

Regulatory approval was expedited, with the FDA granting accelerated approval on May 5, 2021, based on the primary ORR analysis from the first 264 enrolled patients, followed by regular approval on March 19, 2025, upon completion of final OS analysis.¹ This dual-approval pathway reflects both the unmet medical need in HER2-positive gastric cancer and the consistent efficacy demonstrated throughout trial maturation.

## PD-L1 expression and biomarker-enriched response

While KEYNOTE-811 enrolled all HER2-positive patients regardless of [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] status, exploratory subgroup analysis revealed that pembrolizumab treatment benefit appeared concentrated in patients with elevated PD-L1 combined positive score (CPS) ≥1, comprising approximately 85% of the trial population.¹ In this PD-L1 CPS ≥1 subgroup, the hazard ratio for OS was 0.79 (95% CI, 0.66–0.95), maintaining statistical significance.¹ Conversely, in the PD-L1 CPS <1 population (15% of enrolled patients), pembrolizumab did not demonstrate OS improvement, with a hazard ratio of 1.10 (95% CI, 0.72–1.68).¹ These exploratory findings suggest that PD-L1 expression may enrich for patients most likely to derive survival benefit from pembrolizumab-based combination therapy, potentially guiding future treatment selection strategies, although prospective biomarker-stratified trial design would be required to formally validate PD-L1 CPS as a predictive marker in this population.
