**CD74** is a membrane protein that serves as a critical component of the [[app|APP]]-CD74 signaling axis, a intercellular communication pathway that mediates immune crosstalk between epithelial or tubular cells and macrophages. The axis orchestrates distinct inflammatory outcomes across cancer and kidney disease contexts through sustained [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] pathway activation and downstream immune effector responses.¹² As a receptor in the [[mif|MIF]]-CD74-CXCR4 signaling network, CD74 integrates stress signals from damaged or malignant epithelial compartments and translates them into macrophage-autonomous inflammatory programs that range from classical proinflammatory activation to pyroptotic cell death.

---

## Mechanism

The APP-CD74 axis operates as a damage surveillance pathway in which epithelial cells experiencing cellular stress activate amyloid precursor protein ([[app|APP]]), leading to generation of APP-derived fragments that signal to CD74-expressing myeloid cells. This ligand-receptor engagement triggers robust and sustained NF-κB pathway activation within macrophages, amplifying inflammatory transcriptional programs and, in specific pathophysiological contexts, initiating macrophage pyroptosis—an inflammatory form of programmed cell death characterized by inflammasome assembly and release of mature pro-inflammatory cytokines including IL-1β and IL-18.¹² The spatial organization of this axis within tissue microenvironments is functionally significant; epithelial cells expressing activation signals localize adjacent to CD74-expressing macrophages within specific tissue niches, concentrating receptor-ligand engagement at sites of cellular interaction.

---

## Lung adenocarcinoma and immune modulation

In lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), the APP-CD74 axis functions as a critical determinant of immunological responsiveness to checkpoint inhibitor therapy. Tumors with high immunotherapy-response meta-program signatures display selective enrichment of epithelial subpopulations characterized by high expression of interferon-responsive and [[mhc|antigen presentation genes]], including the E3-IFI6 epithelial compartment. This immunologically active epithelial subpopulation localizes spatially adjacent to the Ma-MHC myeloid subpopulation, and both cell types co-enrich in specific tissue niches where they interact predominantly through the APP-CD74 axis.¹ Engagement of CD74 by epithelial APP signaling promotes robust proinflammatory macrophage activation, characterized by upregulation of pro-inflammatory mediators that create an immunologically permissive tumor microenvironment. Functional studies demonstrate that overexpression of IFI6, the defining marker of the epithelial E3-IFI6 subpopulation, is sufficient to promote macrophage activation through CD74-dependent mechanisms in LUAD cell lines,¹ establishing the axis as a mechanistic link between epithelial immunogenic programming and myeloid-driven immune activation.

## Autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease

CD74 mediates pathological inflammation in autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease (ADTKD-UMOD), where mutations in [[umod|uromodulin (UMOD)]] aberrantly activate the APP-CD74 axis to drive macrophage pyroptosis and progressive renal injury. In individuals harboring the UMOD p.His36Tyr variant, mutant uromodulin protein triggers [[app|APP]] activation in renal tubular epithelial cells, which then engages CD74 on infiltrating macrophages.² This engagement sustains NF-κB pathway activation in macrophages, triggering pyroptosis and massive release of pro-inflammatory cytokines that orchestrate interstitial inflammation, fibrosis, and progressive renal dysfunction. The pathological mechanism is conserved across multiple UMOD variants, including the p.Trp31Cys substitution,² indicating that APP-CD74 activation represents a convergent pathway in uromodulin-associated kidney disease. Mechanistically, the pyroptotic response amplifies kidney inflammation through cell lysis and cytokine release, creating a feed-forward cycle of macrophage recruitment and activation that drives chronic interstitial remodeling.

---

## Therapeutic targeting

The APP-CD74 axis represents a pharmacologically actionable pathway for interrupting pathological inflammation in kidney disease. Small-molecule inhibitors targeting [[app|APP]] processing, such as ARN2966, effectively block CD74 engagement and suppress downstream NF-κB signaling.² In preclinical models of ADTKD-UMOD, ARN2966 administration attenuated macrophage pyroptosis, reduced interstitial inflammation, and prevented progressive renal injury,² establishing proof-of-concept for therapeutic intervention targeting this axis. The efficacy of APP inhibitors in blocking CD74-driven kidney pathology raises the potential for similar therapeutic strategies in other CD74-dependent inflammatory diseases, though clinical translation in malignancy remains to be explored.
