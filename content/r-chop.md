**R-CHOP** is a standard immunochemotherapy regimen combining [[rituximab]], [[cyclophosphamide]], [[doxorubicin]], [[vincristine]], and [[prednisone]] that serves as first-line treatment for diffuse large B-cell lymphoma ([[dlbcl]]).¹ The regimen integrates an anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody with conventional chemotherapy to achieve durable remissions in the majority of DLBCL patients. However, a critical limitation emerges in [[myc-bcl2-double-expressor-lymphoma|MYC/BCL2 double-expressor lymphoma (DEL)]], a molecularly defined DLBCL subset characterized by concurrent overexpression of proto-oncogenes [[myc]] and [[bcl2]], where R-CHOP monotherapy demonstrates substantially inferior outcomes.¹ The underlying pathogenic mechanism involves epigenetic dysregulation, which constrains chemotherapy efficacy and opens therapeutic opportunities for histone deacetylase inhibition.¹

---

## Mechanism and composition

R-CHOP operates through a synergistic combination of mechanisms targeting malignant B-cell survival and proliferation. [[rituximab]], a chimeric anti-[[cd20]] monoclonal antibody, depletes [[cd20]]-positive B cells through antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity and direct apoptosis induction. [[cyclophosphamide]] acts as an alkylating agent disrupting DNA integrity, while [[doxorubicin]] intercalates DNA and generates reactive oxygen species. [[vincristine]], a vinca alkaloid, disrupts microtubule polymerization and arrests cells in mitosis. [[prednisone]], a corticosteroid, potentiates B-cell elimination and modulates the tumor microenvironment. Together, these agents target malignant B-cell proliferation through complementary pathways, establishing R-CHOP as the backbone of modern DLBCL therapy.

---

## Clinical applications in DLBCL

R-CHOP remains the gold-standard first-line regimen for newly diagnosed DLBCL across diverse patient populations. The regimen achieves complete remission in approximately 60–70% of unselected DLBCL patients, with durable remissions sustained in the majority. Its clinical efficacy rests on [[rituximab]]'s capacity to sensitize malignant B cells to chemotherapy-induced death while simultaneously engaging innate immune mechanisms. The pivotal addition of [[rituximab]] to conventional CHOP chemotherapy dramatically improved overall survival compared to CHOP alone, establishing immunochemotherapy as the modern therapeutic standard.

---

## Limitations in MYC/BCL2 double-expressor lymphoma

Despite its widespread use, R-CHOP demonstrates poor efficacy in [[myc-bcl2-double-expressor-lymphoma|MYC/BCL2 double-expressor lymphoma]], a molecularly defined DLBCL subset with concurrent [[myc]] and [[bcl2]] overexpression.¹ These tumors exhibit aggressive biology characterized by enhanced proliferation through MYC and profound apoptosis resistance via BCL2, resulting in markedly inferior progression-free and overall survival following standard R-CHOP treatment.¹ The mechanistic basis underlying this resistance involves epigenetic dysregulation, including aberrant histone acetylation and chromatin remodeling, which perpetuates the malignant phenotype and constrains chemotherapy sensitivity.¹

## Prognostic stratification by extranodal invasion origin

Clinical outcomes following R-CHOP are substantially influenced by the anatomical origin of extranodal invasion, reflecting developmental ontogeny through germ layer specification.² The prognosis of extranodal lymphoma differs markedly by tissue of origin: mesoderm-originating extranodal invasion (ENI-mesoderm) associates with significantly inferior progression-free survival and overall survival compared to ectoderm-originating invasion (ENI-ectoderm) or endoderm-originating invasion (ENI-endoderm).² Correspondingly, distinct oncogenic mutation signatures characterize each germ layer origin—[[myd88]], [[pim1]], and [[tbl1xr1]] predominate in ENI-ectoderm, [[tp53]] and [[tet2]] in ENI-endoderm, and [[myd88]], [[pim1]], [[tbl1xr1]], and [[cd79b]] in ENI-mesoderm.² This germ layer-dependent molecular stratification suggests that future R-CHOP-based therapeutic refinement may require subtype-specific optimization based on the developmental origin of extranodal disease.²

---

## Enhancement with histone deacetylase inhibition

[[tucidinostat]], an oral selective histone deacetylase inhibitor, has emerged as a promising strategy to overcome R-CHOP resistance in [[myc-bcl2-double-expressor-lymphoma|MYC/BCL2 double-expressor lymphoma]].¹ By inhibiting HDAC activity, tucidinostat increases histone acetylation, reversing epigenetic silencing of pro-apoptotic genes and reducing expression of survival pathways, thereby restoring chemotherapy sensitivity in otherwise refractory DEL.¹ The combination of tucidinostat with R-CHOP represents a mechanistically motivated approach to improve outcomes in this high-risk DLBCL subset, with randomized clinical trial evidence supporting its efficacy.¹
