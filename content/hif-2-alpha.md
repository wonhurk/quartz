**HIF-2α** (hypoxia-inducible factor 2-alpha, encoded by [[epas1|EPAS1]]) is a transcription factor and master regulator of tumor progression in [[clear-cell-renal-cell-carcinoma|clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC)]] and [[gastric-cancer|gastric cancer]]. Its transcriptional control operates through distinct mechanistic circuits in different tumor contexts: in [[vhl]]-deficient ccRCC, HIF-2α expression is paradoxically sustained through a cytokine-driven enhancer mechanism dependent on [[jak1]]/[[stat3]] signaling, wherein STAT3 is recruited to distal enhancers that physically loop to the EPAS1 promoter;¹ meanwhile, [[socs3|SOCS3]] functions as a critical negative regulator such that its loss unleashes sustained HIF-2α transcription despite VHL loss. In gastric cancer, HIF-2α acts as a classical hypoxia-inducible transcription factor, directly activating [[itgb5|integrin β5 (ITGβ5)]] to promote exosomal release and metastatic dissemination.² Resistance to the HIF-2α inhibitor [[belzutifan]] reflects alternative transcriptional circuits that maintain HIF-2α expression despite pharmacologic blockade, suggesting that targeting upstream regulators ([[socs3]], [[jak1]]/[[stat3]]) or downstream effectors ([[itgb5]]) offers improved therapeutic efficacy.

---

## Mechanism

HIF-2α transcriptional control in ccRCC operates through a trans-acting cytokine-dependent pathway mechanistically distinct from classical VHL/HIF occupancy models. Genome-wide CRISPR screening in VHL-deficient ccRCC cells identified [[socs3|SOCS3]] as a key repressor of HIF-2α.¹ Loss of SOCS3, a suppressor of cytokine signaling, permits sustained activation of the [[jak1]]/[[stat3]] axis. Once activated, STAT3 is recruited to distal enhancers upstream of EPAS1 that establish long-range chromatin contact with the EPAS1 promoter, driving robust HIF-2α transcription independent of hypoxia-driven protein stabilization.¹ This enhancer-promoter looping architecture creates a feed-forward circuit where cytokine signaling maintains HIF-2α expression in normoxic and hypoxic contexts alike. CRISPRi-mediated disruption of these enhancer-promoter interactions markedly reduces HIF-2α expression and suppresses tumor growth, demonstrating that the enhancer architecture is functionally essential for ccRCC progression.

In gastric cancer, HIF-2α operates as a classical hypoxia-responsive transcription factor, directly occupying the [[itgb5|ITGβ5]] promoter and transcriptionally activating its expression under hypoxic conditions.² The resulting ITGβ5 upregulation is selective to hypoxic tumor regions and correlates with metastatic disease burden. Enhanced ITGβ5 incorporation into exosomal membranes increases the invasive capacity of tumor-derived extracellular vesicles, facilitating metastatic dissemination.

## Clear cell renal cell carcinoma

In ccRCC with [[vhl|VHL]] loss, HIF-2α emerges as the dominant driver of oncogenic transcription owing to its sustained expression through the JAK1/STAT3-enhancer circuit.¹ This mechanism distinguishes HIF-2α from other hypoxia-regulated factors subject to VHL-mediated degradation. [[socs3|SOCS3]] overexpression, or pharmacologic inhibition of JAK1/STAT3 signaling, markedly suppresses HIF-2α expression and impairs tumor growth in both in vitro and in vivo models.¹ Genetic loss or epigenetic silencing of SOCS3 in ccRCC tumors may unlock constitutive HIF-2α expression, establishing a mechanism of tumor selection and progression. The identification of this enhancer-driven circuit reveals a targetable mechanism distinct from direct HIF-2α protein inhibition, offering an alternative approach to disrupt HIF-2α-dependent tumor growth.

---

## Gastric cancer and metastasis

In gastric cancer, the hypoxia-HIF-2α-ITGβ5 axis drives metastatic dissemination through exosomal remodeling. HIF-2α directly occupies the [[itgb5|ITGβ5]] promoter via ChIP-validated binding sites, driving transcriptional activation under hypoxia.² Integrins incorporated into exosomal membranes enhance their interaction with recipient cell integrins and extracellular matrix components, facilitating tumor cell adhesion, invasion, and extravasation at distant sites.² Clinical relevance is underscored by the positive correlation between HIF-2α levels, ITGβ5 expression, and metastatic disease burden in patient-derived samples, with elevated expression associated with markedly reduced overall survival. The hypoxic microenvironment of gastric tumors thus selects for HIF-2α-high populations that actively promote exosomal-mediated metastatic escape.

## Resistance to HIF-2α inhibition

Resistance to the HIF-2α inhibitor [[belzutifan]] and related small-molecule antagonists reveals the existence of alternative transcriptional circuits that compensate for direct HIF-2α blockade.¹ In ccRCC, treatment with belzutifan may select for tumor subclones in which JAK1/STAT3 signaling is enhanced or SOCS3 expression is suppressed, enabling HIF-2α re-expression despite HIF-2α protein inhibition. Additionally, cross-talk between HIF-2α and complementary hypoxia-driven transcriptional programs—involving other HIF family members or NOTCH signaling—may provide redundant survival signals. These resistance mechanisms indicate that single-agent HIF-2α inhibition is often insufficient and that concurrent targeting of upstream regulators or downstream effectors is warranted for durable disease control.

---

## Therapeutic synergy and combination strategies

Combined inhibition of [[jak1]]/[[stat3]] signaling together with HIF-2α antagonism offers improved anti-tumor efficacy and delays resistance emergence.¹ STAT3 inhibitors or JAK1-selective inhibitors, when combined with belzutifan, suppress HIF-2α expression more completely by blocking the enhancer-driven transcriptional circuit. In gastric cancer, combination strategies targeting both HIF-2α and [[itgb5|ITGβ5]] show promise for reducing exosomal-mediated metastatic spread.² Pharmacologic HIF-2α inhibition coupled with integrin antagonists or exosome-neutralizing antibodies may simultaneously block both the transcriptional driver and its effector pathway. These findings suggest that understanding the enhancer architecture controlling EPAS1 and the downstream transcriptional networks activated by HIF-2α will enable rational design of multi-target therapeutic approaches that overcome intrinsic and acquired resistance.
