**Photodynamic therapy (PDT)** is a light-activated anticancer modality that harnesses photosensitizers — predominantly [[chlorin-e6|chlorin derivatives]] such as chlorin e6 — to generate cytotoxic reactive oxygen species (ROS) upon illumination with near-infrared (NIR) light.¹ The resulting oxidative burst induces both direct tumor cell death and immunogenic cell death (ICD), wherein dying cancer cells release damage-associated molecular patterns that reprogram the tumor microenvironment toward immune activation.² PDT's clinical appeal lies in its spatial selectivity, minimal systemic toxicity, and emerging capacity to overcome immunological barriers when integrated into multimodal nanoplatform systems combining photothermal therapy ([[PTT]]), ferroptosis, cuproptosis, and chemotherapy.

---

## Mechanism

PDT operates through a well-defined photophysical cascade. Upon NIR irradiation, photosensitizers are excited to singlet or triplet states, subsequently transferring energy to ground-state molecular oxygen to generate highly reactive singlet oxygen and other ROS species. These ROS rapidly oxidize cellular lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids, destabilizing subcellular compartments and triggering both apoptotic and immunogenic pathways. [[chlorin-e6|Chlorin e6]] and related chlorin derivatives are particularly effective due to their extended conjugation and tunable absorption spectra in the therapeutically favorable NIR window (650–900 nm), where tissue penetration is optimized.¹ Critically, the photochemical process generates not only immediate cytotoxic effects but also activates damage-sensing pattern recognition receptors, setting in motion a cascade of dendritic cell maturation and [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cell]] priming that establishes durable anti-tumor immunity.

## Multimodal Integration

PDT has emerged as a cornerstone modality within next-generation combinatory cancer therapeutics, where integration with complementary cell-death pathways substantially amplifies efficacy. In heterostructured nanoplatforms such as [[bi2se3-bise|Bi₂Se₃@BiSe]], the interfacial contact facilitates efficient charge carrier separation, enabling simultaneous activation of PTT and PDT functionalities under single NIR irradiation.¹ Such dual-modal platforms achieve robust tumor suppression while avoiding the complexity and instability of cocktail-based formulations requiring cocktail-based nanoplatforms that load multiple agents.

PDT synergizes equally well with iron-catalyzed cell death pathways. In metal coordination nanocomposites derived from platycodin-d and iron coordination, PDT-induced ROS generation combines with ferroptosis — wherein iron catalyzes lipid peroxidation — to deplete cellular [[gpx4|glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4)]] and accumulate lipid peroxides, thereby amplifying the immunogenic signal beyond either modality alone.² Analogously, copper coordination frameworks conjugate PDT with cuproptosis, leveraging copper-dependent metabolic disruption to synergistically ablate cancer stemness while simultaneously reprogramming glutamine metabolism through glutaminase inhibition.³ These integrated approaches establish a unified immunological re-programming paradigm that converts immunologically cold, T-cell-excluded tumors into T-cell-inflamed hot phenotypes.

## Immunological Enhancement and Tumor Microenvironment Remodeling

PDT-induced cell death is fundamentally immunogenic, triggering rapid dendritic cell maturation and [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cell]]-mediated anti-tumor responses. Beyond direct cytotoxicity, PDT promotes macrophage polarization toward the tumor-suppressive M1 phenotype, a critical step in converting permissive microenvironments into restrictive ones.¹ The ROS-mediated damage released during photodynamic ablation activates pattern recognition receptor signaling, particularly the STING pathway, driving type I interferon production and establishing durable immunological memory.

When combined with immune checkpoint blockade — specifically anti-[[pd-l1|PD-L1]] antibodies — PDT-treated tumors exhibit substantially enhanced response rates. The mechanism involves PDT's capacity to overcome adaptive immune resistance by simultaneously eliminating immunosuppressive cell populations (particularly regulatory T cells and tumor-associated macrophages polarized toward the immunosuppressive M2 state) while flooding the tumor microenvironment with activated dendritic cells and [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cells]]. This immunological synergy has demonstrated particular promise in establishing durable recurrence-free survival, with preclinical models achieving 100% protection against tumor rechallenge at extended follow-up intervals.³

## Cancer Applications

PDT has demonstrated clinical efficacy across multiple solid malignancies, particularly when deployed within multimodal frameworks combining complementary cell-death modalities. In **hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)**, metal coordination nanocomposites combining PDT with ferroptosis induction and chemotherapy achieve robust anti-tumor activity, with combination treatment alongside anti-[[pd-l1|PD-L1]] blockade inducing prolonged immunological memory responses in syngeneic models.² The high metabolic activity and immunosuppressive microenvironment of HCC make it uniquely suited to PDT's dual capacity for direct cytotoxicity and immune activation.

In **colorectal cancer (CRC)**, PDT-based nanoplatforms engineered with surface modifiers that enhance macropinocytosis-mediated cellular uptake achieve exceptional intratumoral drug accumulation. When conjugated with copper coordination frameworks and metabolic reprogramming agents, PDT elicits cuproptosis alongside ROS-driven cancer stemness ablation, converting immunologically silent tumors into CD8+ T-cell-inflamed lesions with high translational potential.³ Critically, this triple-modal strategy (cuproptosis, PDT, and metabolic intervention) establishes robust protective immunity against tumor rechallenge.

In **breast cancer**, PDT efficacy is enhanced through chemotherapeutic conjugation. [[chlorin-e6|Chlorin e6]] conjugates bearing doxorubicin payloads via labile disulfide and thioketal linkers enable controlled, stimuli-responsive release of the cytostatic agent under the reductive and oxidative conditions prevalent within tumor tissue and intracellular compartments. This strategy synergizes light-activated ROS generation with chemotherapy, achieving cytotoxicity both under irradiation and via dark (non-photochemical) mechanisms, with preferential nuclear accumulation of released doxorubicin.⁴

## Nanoplatform Engineering and Delivery Optimization

Advanced nanoplatform architectures have become essential to translating PDT's therapeutic potential. Heterostructured nanomaterials such as [[bi2se3-bise|Bi₂Se₃@BiSe]] exemplify a paradigm shift toward structurally simple, easily synthesized systems with inherent multifunctionality, where interfacial band engineering facilitates efficient photophysical processes without requiring external photosensitizer loading.¹ Such heterostructures achieve superior photothermal conversion and photodynamic activation compared to homogeneous nanoparticles, while simultaneously triggering macrophage polarization toward the tumor-suppressive M1 phenotype.

Metal coordination nanocomposites represent a parallel advance, wherein natural chlorin derivatives and other photosensitive compounds are incorporated into coordination networks with transition metals (iron, copper). These systems enhance charge carrier separation, improve photochemical efficiency, and enable simultaneous activation of multiple cell-death pathways — PDT, ferroptosis, and cuproptosis — under unified NIR irradiation.² Surface engineering with biocompatible polymers such as d-α-tocopheryl polyethylene glycol succinate (TPGS) dramatically enhances intracellular accumulation through macropinocytosis activation while preserving biocompatibility and reducing off-target toxicity.³

**Controlled release mechanisms** further optimize therapeutic efficacy. Labile chemical linkers — particularly disulfide bonds (reductively cleavable) and thioketal linkages (oxidatively cleavable) — enable stimulus-responsive conjugation of chemotherapeutic agents to photosensitizers. Upon cellular internalization and exposure to the reductive intracellular environment or ROS-rich microdomains, these linkers cleave, releasing conjugated payloads with precise spatiotemporal control.⁴ This approach simultaneously maximizes photodynamic efficacy and chemotherapy potency while minimizing systemic exposure to cytotoxic drugs.
