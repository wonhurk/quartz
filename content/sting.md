**STING (stimulator of interferon genes)** is a critical intracellular pattern recognition receptor that senses cytosolic double-stranded DNA and cyclic dinucleotides, triggering robust [[type-i-interferon|type I interferon (IFN-I)]] production and pro-inflammatory responses central to innate immunity and antitumor defense.¹ The canonical pathway begins with [[cgas|cGAS]] recognition of aberrant cytosolic DNA, generating cyclic GMP-AMP that binds and activates STING, leading to TBK1 and IRF3 phosphorylation and subsequent IFN-I transcription.²³ Beyond nucleic acid sensing, STING is uniquely activated by [[manganese-ions|Mn²⁺]] ions, which directly engage the receptor and potentiate downstream signaling independent of cGAMP.²⁴ This dual-input architecture positions STING as a central integrator of danger signals linking innate immune sensing to adaptive antitumor immunity.

---

## Mechanism

STING resides in the endoplasmic reticulum membrane and, upon activation by cGAMP or Mn²⁺, traffics to perinuclear compartments where it recruits and activates TBK1, which in turn phosphorylates IRF3 to drive IFN-I gene expression.³ The pathway is also activated by retrotransposon derepression: [[m1c|M1C]] protein drives [[line-1|LINE-1]] and HERV-K transcription through [[atm|ATM]]-dependent KAP1 phosphorylation, generating endogenous nucleic acid ligands that engage the cGAS-STING axis in PARP inhibitor-treated castration-resistant prostate cancer.⁹ In cancer contexts, STING activation triggers immunogenic cell death (ICD) characterized by release of damage-associated molecular patterns, maturation of dendritic cells, and enhanced cross-presentation of tumor antigens to CD8⁺ T cells.³⁵ The resulting type I interferon response promotes T cell infiltration, effector function, and the establishment of antigen-specific immunological memory, creating durable antitumor immunity.¹

## Cancer immunotherapy applications

STING-based immunotherapy has demonstrated efficacy across diverse malignancy types by converting immunologically "cold" tumors into "hot" inflamed microenvironments. Nanoparticle delivery systems exploit the Mn²⁺-STING axis: [[mno2-nanoparticles|manganese dioxide nanoparticles]] release Mn²⁺ within tumors, simultaneously alleviating hypoxia, depleting glutathione to enhance oxidative stress, and directly activating STING to drive ICD and dendritic cell maturation.³ The [[lpim|LPiM platform]] targets osteosarcoma osteolytic lesions via phytic acid-mediated bone tropism, achieving 93.5% tumor inhibition through combined sonodynamic therapy and Mn²⁺-triggered STING activation with endogenous immune amplification.³ Carrier-free coordination nanomedicines exemplified by quercetin-Mn²⁺-NLG919 assemblies simultaneously normalize cancer-associated fibroblast barriers, induce STING-dependent immune responses via Mn²⁺-triggered hydroxyl radical generation, and reverse tryptophan metabolic immunosuppression, enabling deep CD8⁺ T cell infiltration.⁴

Engineered bacterial vectors provide spatiotemporally controlled STING agonist delivery: genetically programmed *Escherichia coli* expressing the [[cda|cyclic di-adenosine monophosphate (CDA)]] synthase DacA selectively colonize hypoxic tumor regions and release the endogenous STING ligand upon laser-triggered lysis, overcoming the biodistribution and toxicity limitations of passive nanocarrier systems.⁸ Personalized cancer vaccine platforms leverage STING activation by co-delivering tumor lysates with dsDNA-templated manganese phosphate nanoadjuvants, which drive potent Th1-polarized immunity and synergize with anti-TIGIT checkpoint blockade to achieve 16-fold IgG titer increases, 2.10-fold dendritic cell maturation enhancement, and cooperative suppression of primary and metastatic disease.⁶

## Synergistic combinations

STING activation profoundly synergizes with multiple cell death modalities and therapeutic strategies. The [[i-tempest|iTempest platform]] combining tacrine and SR717 within hollow [[mno2-nanoparticles|manganese dioxide nanoparticles]] triggers [[ferroptosis|ferroptosis]] alongside type I interferon amplification (termed "IFN-I tempest"), establishing immunological memory while leveraging lipid droplet accumulation to self-limit late-stage inflammatory toxicity through dendritic cell maturation inhibition.¹ [[mvps|Violet phosphorus-manganese nanocatalysts]] disrupt redox homeostasis through NIR-II photocatalytic reactive oxygen species generation and photothermal-enhanced STING activation, achieving 85% tumor inhibition via "catalysis-photothermal-immunity" synergy.⁷ In cuproptosis-based therapy, bacterial delivery of [[cda|CDA]] amplifies copper-overload-mediated cell death by disrupting glycolytic metabolism, with STING-driven dendritic cell maturation and T cell immunity further potentiating the therapeutic effect.⁸ Across platforms, combination with anti-PD-L1 antibodies generates robust synergistic activity by coupling STING-mediated immune activation with checkpoint blockade, reversing T cell exhaustion and establishing durable responses.⁴⁶

## Inflammatory disease contexts

While STING activation drives beneficial antitumor immunity, aberrant STING signaling underlies pathogenic inflammation in multiple disease states. In [[acute-kidney-injury|acute kidney injury (AKI)]], excessive STING-dependent type I interferon and pro-inflammatory cytokine production exacerbates renal and hepatic damage.⁵ The tetrahydroquinoline-based inhibitor [[ksi-028|KSI-028]] represents a novel STING antagonist chemotype that engages the receptor through a noncanonical, likely allosteric, binding mode distinct from canonical ligand-binding site inhibitors, exhibiting sustained target engagement.⁵ KSI-028 potently suppresses STING signaling in both murine and human cells and demonstrates *in vivo* efficacy in cisplatin-induced AKI models, attenuating tissue injury and down-regulating STING-associated inflammatory gene expression.⁵ This therapeutic duality—activation for cancer immunotherapy versus inhibition for inflammatory disease—highlights STING as a critical rheostat governing the balance between protective immunity and pathogenic inflammation.

## Novel delivery platforms and agonists

Emerging STING delivery technologies address the pharmacokinetic and toxicity challenges inherent to systemic administration of hydrophilic cyclic dinucleotide agonists. The [[sendful|SENDFUL platform]] employs pH-responsive fusogenic liposomes modified with the pyridinium betaine-capped lipid DSPCPA, which remains negatively charged at physiological pH but rapidly converts to a cationic, membrane-fusogenic state in acidic tumor microenvironments (pH 6.5), enabling spatially confined lysosome-bypassing cytosolic STING agonist delivery with negligible systemic toxicity.² Biomineralized adjuvants generated by dsDNA-templated manganese phosphate mineralization achieve pH-responsive co-release of Mn²⁺ and DNA in acidic endosomes (72% Mn²⁺ and 78% DNA release at pH 5.5 within 12 hours), producing cascade amplification of [[cgas|cGAS]]-STING signaling and driving balanced Th1/Th2 responses with 8.31-fold M1/M2 macrophage polarization enhancement.⁶ These platforms collectively expand the therapeutic window of STING agonism by confining activation to diseased tissues while minimizing off-target inflammatory toxicity.
