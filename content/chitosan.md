**Chitosan** is a positively-charged polysaccharide biopolymer derived from the deacetylation of chitin, widely employed in site-specific drug delivery systems due to its inherent mucoadhesive, antimicrobial, pH-responsive, and tissue-adhesive properties. As a biocompatible and biodegradable natural polymer, chitosan forms the structural backbone of diverse pharmaceutical architectures including hydrogels, microspheres, films, and nanoparticle coatings, enabling targeted delivery across anatomical barriers ranging from the gastrointestinal tract to the blood-brain barrier. Its cationic nature facilitates electrostatic interactions with negatively-charged biological surfaces such as [[mucins]], making it particularly valuable for prolonged residence at mucosal interfaces and controlled release of therapeutic agents over 10 to 20+ hours.¹²³

---

## Physicochemical properties and formulation strategies

The biopolymer's primary amine groups confer pH-dependent solubility and charge density, enabling chitosan-based systems to respond dynamically to environmental pH gradients throughout the body. In acidic gastric conditions, chitosan remains positively charged and forms protective barriers around encapsulated drugs, while in the neutral-to-basic colonic environment, enzymatic degradation by microbial polysaccharidases triggers targeted release.¹ These properties are frequently exploited through dual-crosslinking strategies that combine chitosan with complementary polymers such as [[sodium-alginate]], [[gelatin]], and poloxamer to create multifunctional delivery platforms with superior mechanical properties and controlled biodistribution.¹²³ The [[adcxy-cur-sa-cs|double-crosslinked microsphere system]] exemplifies this approach, wherein chitosan's colon-targeting capabilities are integrated with calcium-chelated alginate networks to achieve both gastric stability and site-specific intestinal release.¹

## Gastrointestinal and mucosal delivery

Chitosan's mucoadhesive character has been systematically exploited for organ-selective drug localization within the gastrointestinal tract. Small-molecule [[mucin-binding-ligands]] combined with chitosan nanoparticles have demonstrated up to 10-fold increases in particle binding to specific GI organs and 4-fold improvements in selectivity compared to unmodified chitosan, translating to 9-fold elevation of budesonide concentration in the small intestine and 25-fold increases in tetracycline accumulation in the stomach.⁵ In the oral cavity, [[maleimide-functionalized-chitosan]] and [[cysteine-functionalized-chitosan]] derivatives incorporated into buccal films for triamcinolone acetonide delivery exhibit significantly enhanced mucoadhesive force and drug deposition at the oral mucosa, providing localized anti-inflammatory therapy with improved tissue residence compared to commercial oral pastes.⁶ For nasal administration, chitosan-poloxamer [[cetrorelix-hydrogel|hydrogel formulations]] achieve sustained peptide release over 24 hours with adhesive properties that maintain drug absorption kinetics distinct from oleogel counterparts.⁷

## Wound healing and tissue regeneration

In diabetic wound management, quaternary ammonium-functionalized and lipoic acid-modified chitosan matrices provide dual functionality as both adhesive scaffolds and antimicrobial agents. A cascade nanozyme hydrogel integrating dopamine-modified gelatin with quaternary ammonium-lipoic acid-modified chitosan (LQCS) demonstrated tissue adhesion, inherent antibacterial capability, and near-infrared-triggered nitric oxide and oxygen release, achieving 94.3% wound closure by day 21 in infected diabetic wound models while simultaneously eradicating bacteria, modulating macrophage polarization toward the M2 phenotype, and promoting neurovascular coupling.² Deep eutectic solvent-constructed chitosan eutectogels incorporating [[glabridin]] achieved over 99% antibacterial efficacy against *[[staphylococcus-aureus|Staphylococcus aureus]]* and *Escherichia coli* through disruption of bacterial membrane integrity, with sustained [[glabridin]] release exceeding 10 days and photostability protection that addressed the natural product's poor solubility.³

## Infectious disease applications

PEGylated chitosan-functionalized bimetallic composite nanoparticles represent an advanced iteration of chitosan-based antimicrobial delivery, conferring mucus-penetrating capabilities essential for pulmonary applications. Copper-manganese nanoparticles surface-modified with PEGylated chitosan synergistically induce bacterial cuproptosis-like death, eliminate drug-resistant biofilms, and promote macrophage polarization toward the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype. In methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus* (MRSA)-infected pneumonia mouse models, these multifunctional nanoparticles significantly improved survival rates and reduced inflammatory injury without systemic toxicity by disrupting bacterial membrane integrity and interfering with the respiratory chain.⁴

---

## Neurological applications

Chitosan-coated bilosomes have emerged as a platform for bypassing the blood-brain barrier through intranasal delivery, particularly for drug repurposing in neurodegenerative disease. Olmesartan-loaded chitosan-coated bilosomes optimized for particle size (184.89 nm), positive surface charge (+34.07 mV), and high encapsulation efficiency (87.36%) exhibited sustained release over 12 hours and achieved a 1.4-fold increase in brain-to-plasma area-under-curve ratio compared to conventional administration routes. In lipopolysaccharide-induced Alzheimer's disease rat models, intranasal chitosan bilosome formulations demonstrated superior cognitive improvement in behavioral assessments and reduced oxidative stress markers, validating chitosan's role in facilitating brain-targeted neuroprotective drug delivery.⁸

---

## Chemical functionalization strategies

The polymer backbone's susceptibility to chemical modification has enabled development of chitosan derivatives with enhanced performance characteristics. PEGylation of chitosan imparts mucus-penetrating properties critical for respiratory tract delivery, while quaternary ammonium substitution amplifies antimicrobial activity and maintains positive charge across broader pH ranges.²⁴ Thiol-bearing [[cysteine-functionalized-chitosan]] enables disulfide crosslinking and mucin interaction through covalent bonding, whereas [[maleimide-functionalized-chitosan]] permits selective conjugation with thiol-containing ligands for targeted delivery applications.⁶ These chemical modifications routinely achieve 4- to 25-fold improvements in localized drug concentration and tissue targeting efficiency, expanding chitosan's applicability from simple mucoadhesive excipient to precision-engineered delivery platform.⁵⁶
