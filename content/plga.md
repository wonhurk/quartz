**Poly(Lactic-co-Glycolic Acid) (PLGA)** is a biodegradable and biocompatible synthetic copolymer composed of lactic acid and glycolic acid monomers in tunable ratios, widely employed as a carrier platform for controlled drug delivery, tissue engineering, and targeted therapeutic systems. The polymer's capacity to encapsulate diverse therapeutic agents—from small-molecule drugs to macromolecules—while maintaining their stability and enabling site-specific or cell-based delivery makes it an exceptionally versatile platform for treating complex inflammatory and infectious diseases.¹ Beyond traditional passive drug delivery, PLGA's integration with cellular therapeutics creates hybrid systems that combine pharmacological efficacy with immunomodulatory and regenerative properties, addressing pathologies where single-mechanism interventions prove insufficient.

---

## Mechanism and controlled release

PLGA undergoes time-dependent hydrolytic degradation in aqueous biological environments, progressively breaking down into lactic acid and glycolic acid monomers that are subsequently metabolized through endogenous pathways. The degradation rate is precisely tunable by adjusting the lactic-to-glycolic acid molar ratio: glycolic acid-enriched formulations degrade rapidly, while lactic acid-rich compositions persist longer in tissue. Encapsulated drugs are released through multiple mechanisms—passive diffusion as the polymer matrix erodes, combined with active osmotic and concentration-gradient-driven release patterns that depend on drug hydrophobicity and particle microarchitecture.¹ A critical distinguishing property of PLGA nanoparticles is their pronounced pH-responsive behavior, remaining stable and intact during systemic circulation in physiologically neutral conditions while rapidly releasing their cargo in the acidic microenvironments of infection sites, inflamed tissues, and areas of metabolic dysfunction.²

## Sepsis-induced liver injury

In severe sepsis complicated by hepatic dysfunction and multi-organ failure, [[metformin]]-loaded PLGA microspheres have been integrated with [[mesenchymal-stem-cells|mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs)]] to create bioengineered hybrid spheroids termed [[mscargo|MSCargo]], a cell-particle system that synergizes cellular regeneration with sustained pharmaceutical delivery.¹ The MSCs contribute inherent anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, and immunomodulatory capacity, while the encapsulated [[metformin]] provides sustained protection of hepatocyte mitochondrial function and reduction of intracellular reactive oxygen species. In combination, MSCargo spheroids significantly enhance hepatocyte viability and metabolic function, shielding cells from oxidative stress and inflammatory cytokine-mediated injury that characterizes sepsis pathology.¹ When transplanted via the hepatic portal vein, MSCargo systemically reduced sepsis-associated organ dysfunction across the liver, kidneys, and lungs by modulating [[tlr4|TLR4]]/[[nf-kb|NF-κB]] signaling pathways while simultaneously promoting hepatic regeneration.¹ This approach directly addresses a critical clinical gap: FDA-approved drugs and biologics for sepsis demonstrate limited mortality reduction in clinical trials, creating urgent demand for alternative therapeutic paradigms integrating multiple pathways of disease suppression.

## Carbapenem-resistant pneumonia

PLGA nanoparticles encapsulating [[tigecycline]], a broad-spectrum tetracycline antibiotic active against multidrug-resistant bacteria, achieve targeted pulmonary delivery by being loaded into [[m2-macrophages|M2-type macrophages]], forming a cell-based nano-bionic drug delivery system designated PT@M2.² The macrophage carrier provides a protected niche enabling stable systemic circulation while maintaining nanoparticle integrity, followed by rapid cargo release upon reaching acidic infection microenvironments within infected lung tissue. When encountering carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae (CRKP), PT@M2 deploys a dual mechanism of therapeutic action: first, locally released tigecycline directly reduces the bacterial minimum inhibitory concentration, disrupts bacterial cell membranes, and induces accumulation of bactericidal reactive oxygen species;² second, the integrated macrophage compartment simultaneously remodels the local immune microenvironment to suppress excessive inflammatory responses while maintaining antimicrobial immunity.² At the molecular level, PT@M2 inhibits calcium ion influx by downregulating [[cacng6]], thereby indirectly suppressing the [[mapk]] pathway and preventing the hyperinflammatory cascade responsible for collateral tissue damage.² In preclinical pneumonia models, PT@M2 substantially reduced bacterial colony-forming units, ameliorated inflammatory lung injury, and improved pulmonary histopathology compared to tigecycline monotherapy.

## Therapeutic integration with cellular systems

PLGA-based delivery systems directly address unmet clinical needs in sepsis and multidrug-resistant bacterial infections, where conventional antibiotic monotherapy increasingly fails due to resistance mechanisms and host immune dysregulation. The polymer's tunable biodegradation kinetics, inherent biocompatibility, and minimal immunogenicity enable prolonged half-lives in circulation, reduced off-target toxicity, and enhanced therapeutic agent accumulation at disease sites. Beyond serving as passive drug carriers, PLGA's capacity for integration with live cellular therapeutics—whether [[mesenchymal-stem-cells]] for regenerative applications or [[m2-macrophages]] for nano-bionic antimicrobial delivery—enables unprecedented synergistic combinations of pharmacological, cellular, and immunological interventions. These hybrid systems exemplify an emerging therapeutic paradigm in which biodegradable polymers transition from inert vehicles to dynamic functional components of living therapeutic platforms, achieving clinical efficacy through simultaneous modulation of infection control, immune homeostasis, and tissue regeneration.
