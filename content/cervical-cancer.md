**Cervical cancer** is a prevalent gynecological malignancy whose clinical management is increasingly defined by precision diagnostics, multifaceted treatment resistance, and emerging immunotherapeutic strategies. Diagnosis has been advanced by the [[wid-qec|WID-qEC]] DNA methylation test, which detects cervical cancer through cervico-vaginal samples and unexpectedly identifies occult ovarian malignancies in WID-qEC-positive cases.¹ Treatment resistance remains the central therapeutic challenge: cisplatin-resistant disease can be reversed through [[simvastatin]] intervention targeting the [[caveolin-1|Caveolin-1]]-mediated PI3K/AKT signaling cascade,² while radiotherapy resistance is conferred by cancer-associated fibroblast-derived exosomes delivering BMP4 to activate Nrf2-dependent antioxidant defenses and suppress cuproptosis.³ The tumor microenvironment orchestrates immune evasion through an [[igsf3|IGSF3]]-TNFR2 axis that amplifies regulatory T cell function, a mechanism targetable with [[purpurogallin]] to restore anti-tumor immunity and synergize with checkpoint blockade.⁴

## Diagnosis and detection

The [[wid-qec|WID-qEC]] test represents a methylation-based diagnostic platform validated for cervical and endometrial cancer detection using cervico-vaginal samples. In a cohort of 1,269 women without endometrial or cervical cancer, 80 tested WID-qEC-positive, and 54% of these cases harbored ovarian cancer.¹ WID-qEC positivity was independently associated with ovarian cancer presence (adjusted OR 2.93) and with a higher lifetime number of ovulatory cycles (adjusted OR 2.67), a recognized ovarian cancer risk factor. While standalone positive predictive value for ovarian cancer remains modest in general populations, WID-qEC enables two-step triage approaches wherein positive results in the absence of cervical or endometrial pathology prompt ovarian cancer screening.¹

## Cisplatin resistance

Cervical cancer treatment is frequently compromised by acquired cisplatin resistance. [[Simvastatin]] restores cisplatin sensitivity in resistant cell lines (SiHa-DDP, C33a-DDP) by suppressing [[caveolin-1|Caveolin-1]] (CAV1) expression and downstream PI3K/AKT signaling.² Among seven statins tested, all exhibited markedly elevated IC₅₀ values in cisplatin-resistant cells compared to parental lines, yet [[simvastatin]] demonstrated superior chemosensitization potential with a synergistic combination index (CI < 1). Combined simvastatin-cisplatin therapy inhibited cell viability and migration while promoting apoptosis. Mechanistically, CAV1 knockdown in resistant cells recapitulated cisplatin resensitization, confirming its central role in chemoresistance. Epidemiologic data further support this pathway: statin utilization correlates with decreased cervical cancer incidence and mortality, underscoring the translational relevance of lipophilic statin repurposing.²

## Radiotherapy resistance

Cancer-associated fibroblast-derived exosomes (CAF-Exo) mediate radiotherapy resistance through coordinated delivery of bone morphogenetic protein 4 (BMP4), which orchestrates a dual-pathway defense mechanism. CAF-Exo activate the Nrf2-HO-1 antioxidant signaling axis while simultaneously suppressing cuproptosis regulators, conferring radioresistant phenotypes to recipient tumor cells.³ BMP4 was identified as the essential exosomal cargo driving this protective response. Functional assays demonstrated that BMP4 knockdown restored radiosensitivity in vitro and significantly enhanced radiotherapy efficacy in vivo. This tumor-stroma crosstalk establishes a previously unrecognized biological axis wherein exosomal BMP4 coordinates redox homeostasis and copper-dependent cell death pathways to enable treatment resistance. The pathway highlights therapeutic opportunities in targeting either exosome biogenesis or BMP4 signaling to circumvent radiotherapy failure.³

## Immune evasion and immunotherapy

Cervical cancer exhibits limited responsiveness to immune checkpoint blockade due to intricate immune evasion mechanisms. Elevated [[igsf3|IGSF3]] expression on tumor cells drives regulatory T cell (Treg) infiltration and function through a ligand-receptor interaction with TNFR2 on Tregs, activating NF-κB signaling and amplifying immunosuppression.⁴ Structure-based virtual screening identified [[purpurogallin]]—a conventional antioxidant compound—as an IGSF3 inhibitor that disrupts the IGSF3-TNFR2 interaction, reduces Treg abundance, and suppresses tumor growth. [[Purpurogallin]] synergizes with anti-PD-1 and anti-[[ctla-4|CTLA-4]] antibodies, with sequential therapy (purpurogallin pretreatment followed by PD-1 blockade) yielding prominent therapeutic efficacy. This study establishes IGSF3 as a pivotal regulator of the immunosuppressive microenvironment and unveils a sequential immunotherapy strategy for checkpoint-refractory cervical cancer.⁴

## Surgical management

[[Robotic-assisted-surgery|Robotic-assisted]] hysterectomy has emerged as the leading surgical approach for cervical cancer, with utilization rates increasing from 12.5% in 2014 to 55.9% by 2024, establishing parity with open surgery.⁵ In radical hysterectomy for cervical cancer, the robotic approach reduced operative time by 30 minutes, decreased intraoperative transfusions (2.5% vs 14.3%), and lowered unplanned reoperations (0.7% vs 3.9%) and surgical complications compared to traditional approaches. For endometrial carcinoma with pelvic lymph node dissection, robotic surgery reduced operative time by 48.5 minutes. While robotic and laparoscopic approaches yield similar outcomes in benign hysterectomies, robotic surgery incurs an additional cost of $2,790 per case. The decade-long trend reflects robotic surgery's establishment as a standard-of-care modality balancing surgical precision with oncologic safety.⁵

## Natural compounds

[[Luteolin]], a bioactive flavonoid extracted from [[celastrus-orbiculatus|Celastrus orbiculatus]] stem, exhibits anti-cervical cancer activity through suppression of carbonic anhydrase 2 (CA2).⁶ Celastrus orbiculatus is a traditional Chinese herb historically employed for eliminating wind and dampness, activating blood circulation, and detoxifying, with documented use in malignant tumors and rheumatoid arthritis. While prior research confirmed its anti-tumor activity, the cervical cancer mechanism had not been previously elucidated. [[Luteolin]]'s CA2-targeting mechanism represents a bridge between traditional herbal medicine and mechanistic oncology, offering translational potential for natural compound-based therapeutics.⁶
