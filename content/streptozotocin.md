**Streptozotocin** is a diabetogenic alkylating agent that reliably induces [[type-1-diabetes]] in rodent models through a single intraperitoneal injection at 65 mg/kg, triggering rapid destruction of pancreatic β-cells and resulting in profound glucose dysregulation and persistent hyperglycemia.¹ The injection initiates a comprehensive pathological cascade encompassing lipotoxicity, oxidative stress, inflammation, fibrosis, and apoptosis that extends beyond the pancreas to damage multiple organ systems including the kidneys, where [[diabetic-nephropathy]] emerges as an early and severe complication.¹ Streptozotocin-induced diabetes has become a foundational preclinical platform for evaluating protective agents and therapeutic interventions targeting diabetes-induced complications.

---

## Mechanism of action

Streptozotocin functions as an alkylating agent with selective cytotoxic activity toward pancreatic β-cells. Following intraperitoneal administration, the compound accumulates within insulin-secreting cells of the pancreatic islets, where it alkylates DNA and disrupts mitochondrial function, triggering apoptotic cell death.¹ The result is rapid depletion of functional β-cell mass and elimination of endogenous insulin production, establishing a severe hyperglycemic state that recapitulates the metabolic derangement characteristic of human [[type-1-diabetes-mellitus]], though with accelerated disease kinetics suitable for time-limited experimental investigation.

---

## Induction of type 1 diabetes

A single dose of streptozotocin at 65 mg/kg administered intraperitoneally reliably produces [[type-1-diabetes]] in rodent models characterized by profound and persistent glucose dysregulation.¹ Animals develop marked hyperglycemia within days of injection and maintain complete loss of glucose homeostasis throughout the experimental period. The streptozotocin-induced diabetic phenotype reproduces many pathophysiological features of autoimmune diabetes observed in spontaneous models, rendering it invaluable for experimental studies requiring reproducible, rapid disease onset with minimal inter-animal variability. The model's technical simplicity, dose-response tractability, and consistent metabolic derangement have established it as a cornerstone of diabetes-related research.

---

## Diabetic nephropathy and renal complications

Streptozotocin-induced diabetes reliably triggers early-onset [[diabetic-nephropathy]], manifesting within weeks as progressive renal dysfunction characterized by marked increases in 24-hour urine volume and urinary albumin excretion.¹ Renal tissue exhibits elevated enzymatic and molecular markers of injury including KIM-1, NGAL (neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin), and nephrin, signaling both glomerular and tubular damage.¹ Pro-inflammatory mediators accumulate significantly, including interleukin-6 (IL-6), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), and intercellular adhesion molecule-1 (ICAM-1), alongside elevated nuclear NF-κB that sustains a pathological inflammatory microenvironment.¹ These renal pathological features closely mirror the early complications of human diabetic kidney disease, providing a practical and predictive experimental platform for identifying and validating renoprotective interventions.

---

## Oxidative stress and redox dysregulation

The streptozotocin-induced diabetic state is accompanied by marked dysregulation of cellular redox homeostasis. Oxidative stress accumulates substantially, reflected in elevated malondialdehyde (MDA) and other lipid peroxidation products in renal and systemic tissues, while key antioxidant defenses become severely depleted.¹ Heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1), superoxide dismutase (SOD), and glutathione (GSH) levels drop significantly, reducing the capacity to neutralize reactive oxygen species and thereby amplifying oxidative damage.¹ Advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) and soluble receptors for AGEs (sRAGEs) accumulate to pathological concentrations, driving protein cross-linking and activation of pro-inflammatory RAGE signaling cascades.¹ This combination of elevated oxidative burden and antioxidant insufficiency underpins the cascade of inflammation, fibrosis, and apoptosis characteristic of streptozotocin-induced tissue injury, and represents a primary therapeutic target for cytoprotective interventions.

---

## Preclinical applications

Streptozotocin-induced diabetes has become the standard preclinical model for evaluating putative therapeutic strategies aimed at preventing or reversing diabetes-related complications. The model's reliability, reproducibility, rapid disease kinetics, and measurable pathological endpoints enable rigorous testing of protective agents across multiple organ systems and disease mechanisms.¹ Numerous pharmacological interventions have been systematically evaluated in this system, including natural products, synthetic small molecules, and biologics designed to activate cytoprotective signaling pathways including the Keap1-Nrf2 antioxidant response element axis.¹ The widespread adoption of the streptozotocin-induced diabetes model reflects both its technical accessibility and demonstrated predictive validity for identifying candidate therapeutics with potential clinical utility in human diabetes management.
