**Methionine** is an essential amino acid whose dysregulated metabolism drives [[oral-squamous-cell-carcinoma|oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC)]] and [[glioblastoma]] tumorigenesis through a [[prmt5|PRMT5]]-dependent feedforward mechanism. Abnormal methionine metabolism in both malignancies is strongly associated with poor patient prognosis, and selective methionine deprivation potently suppresses cancer cell proliferation and migration while sparing normal tissues.¹ The central oncogenic driver is [[prmt5|PRMT5]]-mediated symmetrical dimethylation (sDMA) at arginine 124 on the transcriptional coactivator [[yap|YAP]], which protects YAP from [[lats|LATS]]-mediated inhibitory phosphorylation and unleashes a methionine-dependent feedforward loop wherein YAP upregulates both [[prmt5|PRMT5]] and the methionine transporters [[slc3a2|SLC3A2]]/[[slc7a5|SLC7A5]].² Although systemic dietary methionine restriction causes severe toxicity, the engineered bacterial vector [[sgn1|SGN1]]—an attenuated Salmonella typhimurium expressing [[l-methioninase|L-methioninase]]—selectively depletes tumor methionine while remaining confined to tumor tissue, achieving superior anti-tumor efficacy without systemic harm.¹

---

## Mechanism

Methionine acts as a metabolic cofactor in a sophisticated epigenetic regulatory circuit centered on [[prmt5|PRMT5]]-catalyzed YAP methylation. The [[prmt5|PRMT5]] enzyme catalyzes symmetrical dimethylation at arginine 124 (R124sDMA) within a conserved LATS-targeting motif (HXRXXS) on [[yap|YAP]], a key effector of the Hippo pathway and transcriptional coactivator.² This R124 sDMA modification serves as a physiological brake on Hippo pathway-mediated growth suppression by shielding YAP from [[lats|LATS]]-catalyzed inhibitory phosphorylation at serine 127, thereby promoting YAP nuclear accumulation and transcriptional activation. Critically, elevated methionine bioavailability sustains this sDMA modification and drives YAP-dependent tumorigenicity through a potent feedforward mechanism: methylated YAP directly upregulates transcription of both [[prmt5|PRMT5]] itself and the methionine transporters [[slc3a2|SLC3A2]] and [[slc7a5|SLC7A5]], establishing a self-reinforcing methionine addiction circuit that intensifies tumor metabolic dependency.² This coupling of YAP activation to methionine transporter expression forces cancer cells into obligate reliance on exogenous methionine.

## Oral squamous cell carcinoma

Bioinformatic analysis of clinical [[oral-squamous-cell-carcinoma|OSCC]] samples revealed pronounced methionine metabolic dysfunction strongly predictive of poor patient outcomes.¹ In cultured [[oral-squamous-cell-carcinoma|OSCC]] cells, methionine deprivation—achieved through culture medium depletion—produces dramatic suppression of both proliferation and cellular migration, two hallmarks of metastatic competence. The robust sensitivity extends to patient-derived organoid models, where methionine withdrawal similarly impairs growth.¹ However, systemic dietary methionine restriction, while effective in subcutaneous xenograft models, induces severe toxicity in host animals, rendering this approach clinically untenable. To circumvent this toxicity challenge, [[sgn1|SGN1]], a genetically engineered attenuated strain of Salmonella typhimurium expressing [[l-methioninase|L-methioninase]], was developed as a tumor-selective methionine depletion platform. Following intravenous delivery, [[sgn1|SGN1]] exhibits remarkable tumor tropism—heavily colonizing implanted [[oral-squamous-cell-carcinoma|OSCC]] tumors while being rapidly cleared from systemic circulation and non-tumor organs.¹ Within tumor tissue, however, [[sgn1|SGN1]] persists for at least seven days, providing a sustained window for enzymatic methionine depletion via [[l-methioninase|L-methioninase]]-catalyzed conversion.¹ This selective tumor colonization translates into superior anti-tumor efficacy in orthotopic [[oral-squamous-cell-carcinoma|OSCC]] models without evidence of systemic toxicity in hematological, biochemical, or histopathological assays.¹

## Glioblastoma and radiotherapy synergy

In [[glioblastoma]], methionine metabolism fuels not only tumorigenicity but also radioresistance. Radiation activates the [[prmt5|PRMT5]]-[[yap|YAP]]-[[slc3a2|SLC3A2]]/[[slc7a5|SLC7A5]] axis, promoting cancer cell survival and treatment evasion.² Blockade of this pathway—achieved through [[prmt5|PRMT5]] inhibition to suppress [[yap|YAP]] R124 sDMA—sensitizes [[glioblastoma|GBM]] cells to ionizing radiation, a combination that significantly impairs intracranial xenograft growth.² In vivo, animals receiving combined [[prmt5|PRMT5]] inhibition and radiotherapy experience substantial extensions of overall survival, indicating that methionine metabolism and YAP-dependent growth signaling are critical therapeutic vulnerabilities in radiation-resistant [[glioblastoma]].²
