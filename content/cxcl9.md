**CXCL9** is a C-X-C motif chemokine secreted by macrophages within the tumor microenvironment in response to [[interferon-gamma|interferon-gamma (IFN-γ)]] stimulation. The chemokine acts as a potent T cell chemoattractant via [[CXCR3|CXCR3]] receptor engagement, mediating [[cd4-t-cells|CD4+ T cell]] infiltration into tumors and modulating the immune landscape in endocrine and hepatic malignancies. Notably, glucocorticoid exposure—a hallmark feature of hormonally active adrenocortical carcinoma—programs macrophages toward a specialized C1Q+ CD163+ phenotype that exhibits heightened CXCL9 secretion upon IFN-γ stimulation, establishing a critical link between metabolic dysregulation and tumor immunity.¹

---

## Mechanism

CXCL9 secretion is governed by a two-signal axis: glucocorticoid-mediated macrophage polarization followed by IFN-γ stimulation. In adrenocortical carcinoma, where systemic cortisol excess characterizes approximately 60% of cases, elevated glucocorticoid levels reprogram peripheral blood monocytes toward a phagocytic phenotype marked by high expression of [[CD163|CD163]] and the complement component [[C1Q]]. These C1Q+ CD163+ macrophages constitute a distinct subset that is primed—yet not spontaneously activated—for CXCL9 production.¹ Upon subsequent exposure to [[interferon-gamma|IFN-γ]] in the tumor microenvironment, these glucocorticoid-polarized macrophages secrete substantially higher amounts of CXCL9 than classical pro-inflammatory [[M1-macrophages|M1]] macrophages, establishing a glucocorticoid-dependent amplification loop for chemokine production.¹ The resulting CXCL9 gradient then recruits [[cd4-t-cells|CD4+ T cells]] expressing [[CXCR3]], facilitating adaptive immune infiltration into tumor tissues.

---

## Adrenocortical carcinoma

In adrenocortical carcinoma, CXCL9 emerges as a key mediator linking endocrine dysregulation to immune infiltration and checkpoint inhibitor response. ACC tumors are heavily infiltrated by [[CD68|CD68+]] [[CD163|CD163+]] macrophages independent of cortisol levels, yet the functional orientation of this macrophage compartment is critically shaped by glucocorticoid exposure; glucocorticoid-polarized macrophages are selectively primed for robust CXCL9 secretion upon IFN-γ stimulation.¹ Elevated intratumoral CXCL9 expression correlates with enhanced CD4+ T cell recruitment into the tumor microenvironment, a phenomenon that influences responsiveness to immune checkpoint inhibitors. In preclinical models, combined treatment with immune checkpoint inhibitors and concurrent glucocorticoid receptor blockade via [[mifepristone]] substantially reduces tumoral CXCL9 expression and dampens CD4+ T cell infiltration, suggesting that CXCL9 manipulation may be therapeutically relevant.¹ These findings position CXCL9 as a potential biomarker for predicting immunotherapy outcomes in cortisol-producing endocrine malignancies.

---

## Hepatocellular carcinoma

CXCL9 plays an important role in shaping the tumor immune microenvironment in hepatocellular carcinoma, where it participates in the [[CXCL9|CXCL9]]-[[CXCR3|CXCR3]] signaling axis. Spatial transcriptomics and integrated multi-omics analyses reveal that regions of high expression of upstream effectors are associated with impaired [[CXCL9|CXCL9]]-[[CXCR3|CXCR3]] signaling, leading to exclusion of cytotoxic T cells and a suppressive tumor immune microenvironment.² Conversely, in immunotherapy responders, CXCL9-CXCR3 signaling is preserved or enhanced, correlating with cytotoxic T cell infiltration and improved therapeutic outcomes. These observations establish CXCL9 signaling as a functional determinant of immune exclusion versus immune activation in HCC, with implications for predicting immunotherapy response and identifying candidates for checkpoint inhibitor treatment.
