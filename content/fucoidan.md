**Fucoidan** is a bioactive polysaccharide derived from brown seaweed that functions both as a direct therapeutic agent and as a multifunctional surface modifier for nanoparticle-based delivery platforms.¹ Its intrinsic bioactivities encompass [[reactive-oxygen-species|ROS]] scavenging, promotion of [[m1-macrophage|M1 macrophage]] polarization, suppression of [[vegf-a|VEGF-A]]-mediated angiogenesis, and potent antimicrobial properties.¹ When incorporated as a surface coating on nanoparticles targeting [[p-selectin]], fucoidan enables simultaneous orchestration of cancer cells and [[tumor-associated-macrophages]] through both receptor-dependent and receptor-independent pathways, fundamentally reshaping the tumor microenvironment while driving coordinated immune activation.¹ Its dual capacity as both bioactive component and targeted delivery scaffold has established fucoidan as a versatile platform for multimodal therapeutic strategies addressing complex inflammatory and malignant pathologies.

---

## Mechanism

Fucoidan operates through multiple mechanistic pathways independent of nanoparticle targeting. The polysaccharide reduces cellular [[reactive-oxygen-species]] production in cancer cells, directly suppressing oxidative stress-driven tumor progression signatures.¹ Simultaneously, fucoidan promotes [[m1-macrophage|M1 macrophage]] polarization away from the immunosuppressive M2 phenotype, driving pro-inflammatory immune activation within the tumor microenvironment.¹ The polysaccharide also suppresses [[vegf-a|VEGF-A]]-mediated angiogenic signaling, limiting blood vessel formation critical to tumor growth and metastasis.¹

When conjugated to nanoparticles targeting [[p-selectin]], fucoidan achieves receptor-mediated specificity while maintaining its intrinsic bioactivities. [[p-selectin]] is abundantly expressed on both cancer cells and tumor-associated macrophages, enabling dual-cell targeting through a single molecular interaction.¹ This dual-targeting architecture disrupts tumor-[[tumor-associated-macrophages|TAM]] adhesion, substantially reducing the formation of circulating hybrid cells that mediate tumor-immune evasion, while simultaneously activating immune surveillance pathways through fucoidan's receptor-independent mechanisms.¹

## Cancer immunotherapy

Fucoidan's immunomodulatory properties have been integrated into the FINAL platform (Fucoidan-docetaxel Immunomodulatory Nanoparticles as an Antitumoral Lancer), designed to coordinate simultaneous killing and immune reactivation in [[triple-negative-breast-cancer|triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)]].¹ FINAL nanoparticles combine [[p-selectin]]-directed targeting with [[docetaxel]] chemotherapy and fucoidan's intrinsic bioactivities, achieving breakthrough therapeutic outcomes in preclinical TNBC models including doubled survival duration.¹

The synergistic mechanism operates at two levels. First, [[p-selectin]]-mediated targeting enhances cellular uptake into both cancer cells and tumor-associated macrophages while disrupting the protective cell-cell adhesion that enables immune evasion.¹ Second, independent of this targeting, fucoidan's bioactivities drive broad immune activation: ROS reduction in cancer cells limits antioxidant escape, [[m1-macrophage|M1 macrophage]] polarization reorients anti-tumor immunity, and [[vegf-a|VEGF-A]] suppression starves angiogenesis.¹ RNA-seq transcriptomic profiling demonstrated that this system-level coordination simultaneously drives immune activation signatures while repressing tumor progression transcriptomes, providing molecular evidence for the coordinated tumor-immune dynamics achieved by fucoidan-modified nanoparticles.¹

## Dermatological applications

Fucoidan has been successfully deployed in transdermal microneedle systems for the treatment of acne vulgaris, a chronic inflammatory skin condition driven by [[propionibacterium-acnes|*Propionibacterium acnes*]] colonization in hair follicles.² A multifunctional microneedle formulation based on hyaluronic acid and fucoidan was functionalized with [[curcumin]]-loaded mesoporous polydopamine nanoparticles, generating a system with sustained-release pharmacokinetics, photothermal responsiveness, [[reactive-oxygen-species|ROS]] scavenging capacity, and antimicrobial activity.²

The fucoidan-[[curcumin]] microneedle system demonstrated 70.9% antibacterial efficacy against [[propionibacterium-acnes|*P. acnes*]] with good biocompatibility and maintained ROS-scavenging properties.² Critically, when combined with near-infrared irradiation, the system achieved 99.4% antibacterial eradication and restored skin thickness to near-normal levels, demonstrating the synergistic potential of fucoidan-mediated photothermal enhancement.² The microneedles dissolved rapidly upon insertion (within two minutes) and maintained mechanical strength throughout application, establishing a patient-friendly delivery platform suitable for outpatient acne management.²
