**IgA Nephropathy** (IgAN) is the most common primary glomerulonephritis worldwide, characterized by pathological deposition of immunoglobulin A immune complexes in the glomerular mesangium coupled with dysregulation of the alternative complement cascade, driving progressive kidney injury through both innate and adaptive immune mechanisms.¹ The underlying pathogenesis is mediated by [[baff|B-cell-activating factor (BAFF)]] and [[april|proliferation-inducing ligand (APRIL)]], which drive aberrant B-cell responses whose therapeutic neutralization has recently emerged as a cornerstone treatment strategy.²

---

## Pathogenesis: B-cell Activation and Complement Dysregulation

IgA nephropathy is a B-cell-driven autoimmune disease wherein overexpression of [[baff|BAFF]] and [[april|APRIL]] in secondary lymphoid tissues and the inflamed kidney microenvironment drives aberrant differentiation and activation of IgA-secreting plasma cells.² These cells produce IgA1-predominant immune complexes that deposit preferentially in the glomerular mesangium, triggering uncontrolled complement activation through the alternative pathway.¹ This cascade generates C3-rich glomerular deposits and recruits infiltrating [[macrophage|macrophages]] that perpetuate tissue-damaging inflammation, progressive mesangial proliferation, and ultimately glomerular filtration impairment.¹

---

## Telitacicept: Suppressing the B-cell Axis

[[telitacicept|Telitacicept]] is a recombinant fusion protein engineered to simultaneously neutralize both [[baff|BAFF]] and [[april|APRIL]], effectively suppressing the B-cell-permissive cytokine milieu that perpetuates pathogenic IgA-secreting plasma cell differentiation.² In Phase 3 trials, telitacicept monotherapy reduced median proteinuria by −71.4% at 6 months; when combined with [[glucocorticoids]], the reduction deepened to −84.1%, reflecting synergistic benefits of targeting both B-cell-specific and broader anti-inflammatory pathways.³ Concomitantly, serum IgA and IgG levels declined, and urinary biomarkers of macrophage-driven disease activity, including soluble CD163, significantly decreased, demonstrating therapeutic efficacy across multiple pathogenic compartments.³

---

## The Complement Paradox: Selective Regulation Over Blanket Inhibition

A pivotal finding in IgA nephropathy pathophysiology has revealed that complete complement inhibition paradoxically worsens disease. Animals with genetic [[c3|C3]] deficiency showed prevention of complement activation but paradoxically accumulated greater glomerular IgA deposition and heightened macrophage infiltration, reflecting critically impaired clearance of circulating pathogenic immune complexes.¹ This counterintuitive result exposed a previously unappreciated dual role for complement in IgAN: while complement components drive glomerular inflammation and tissue damage, they simultaneously facilitate the removal of pathogenic IgA-containing circulating immune complexes through complement-dependent opsonization and [[macrophage|macrophage]]-mediated phagocytosis.

In contrast, selective supplementation of [[complement-factor-h|complement factor H (CFH)]] — the principal negative regulator of alternative pathway amplification — achieved superior therapeutic outcomes by preserving immune complex clearance mechanisms while restraining pathogenic complement activation.¹ Mice receiving CFH supplementation demonstrated substantially reduced glomerular IgA and C3 deposition, diminished inflammatory cell infiltration, attenuated cytokine expression, and ameliorated proteinuria. Notably, the plasma burden of pathogenic IgA immune complexes declined as circulating [[c3|C3]] levels rose, indicating enhanced systemic clearance of deposited antigen via preserved complement-dependent opsonization. In human IgA nephropathy patients, those with naturally higher plasma CFH levels exhibited the protective phenotype: lower mesangial IgA and C3 deposition but elevated circulating C3 — directly validating CFH supplementation as a mechanistically grounded therapeutic approach superior to nonselective complement inhibition.¹
