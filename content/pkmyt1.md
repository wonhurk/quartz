**PKMYT1** (protein kinase, mitotic tyrosine kinase 1) is a serine/threonine kinase that serves as a critical negative regulator of cell cycle progression at the G2/M checkpoint by phosphorylating [[cdk1|CDK1]] at inhibitory sites (threonine 14 and tyrosine 15).¹ PKMYT1 functions redundantly with its homolog [[wee1|WEE1]] to maintain checkpoint integrity; dual inhibition of both kinases has emerged as a compelling therapeutic strategy in checkpoint-deficient tumors with elevated replication stress.¹ The expression and activity of PKMYT1 are regulated at the post-transcriptional level by [[mir-4695-5p|microRNA-4695-5p]], whose upregulation suppresses PKMYT1 expression and promotes apoptosis, a mechanism exploitable by biological therapies including [[bevacizumab]].²

---

## Mechanism

PKMYT1 maintains G2/M checkpoint control by catalyzing inhibitory phosphorylation of [[cdk1|CDK1]] at tyrosine 15 and threonine 14, thereby preventing cyclin B–CDK1 complex activation and entry into mitosis.¹ This checkpoint function is evolutionarily conserved and operates as part of the DNA damage response network, allowing cells to complete DNA replication and repair before undergoing mitosis. However, PKMYT1 activity is redundant with [[wee1|WEE1]], which phosphorylates the same CDK1 residues. This checkpoint redundancy provides a survival advantage in tumors that have lost p53 function or exhibit abrogated G1/S checkpoint control — conditions that elevate replication stress and create dependency on the G2/M checkpoint for survival. Targeting both kinases simultaneously overcomes this compensatory mechanism, forcing checkpoint-deficient cells with accumulated DNA damage into mitosis and triggering catastrophic mitotic failure.

## Therapeutic targeting with dual inhibitors

The discovery of selective PKMYT1/[[wee1|WEE1]] dual inhibitors represents a major advance in checkpoint-targeting therapy. A series of 1,7-naphthyridinone derivatives was rationally designed to engage both kinases while maintaining favorable kinome selectivity. Structural optimization, guided by crystallographic analysis and displacement of a conserved water molecule at the PKMYT1 hinge region, yielded compound 24 — a lead inhibitor achieving single-digit nanomolar potency against PKMYT1 in biochemical assays and demonstrating superior efficacy in cellular models.¹ Compound 24 achieves a PKMYT1 IC₅₀ of 4.9 nM for pCDK1 T14 phosphorylation inhibition and a pCDK1 Y15 IC₅₀ of 0.186 μM in HCC1569 triple-negative breast cancer cells, translating to functionally relevant checkpoint abrogation.

In colorectal cancer organoid models, compound 24 outperformed both the prior PKMYT1 inhibitor RP-6306 and the established [[wee1|WEE1]]-selective inhibitor AZD1775, with its superior efficacy correlating to dual engagement of both checkpoint kinases.¹ The compound also exhibits favorable in vitro absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion (ADME) properties and early safety profiles, positioning dual PKMYT1/WEE1 inhibition as a viable checkpoint-abrogation strategy in the clinic. This represents a shift from single-target [[wee1|WEE1]] inhibition, which achieves only partial checkpoint relief in the presence of intact PKMYT1 activity.

## Regulation by microRNA in glioblastoma

Beyond direct kinase inhibition, PKMYT1 expression and function are regulated by [[mir-4695-5p|miR-4695-5p]], a microRNA that directly targets the PKMYT1 3' untranslated region to suppress translation and promote degradation.² In glioblastoma cells (U87MG and U251 lines), the anti-angiogenic monoclonal antibody [[bevacizumab]] induces a dose-dependent apoptotic response that is mechanistically driven by upregulation of [[mir-4695-5p|miR-4695-5p]].² This upregulation of the microRNA leads to PKMYT1 suppression, which in turn promotes apoptosis through multiple pathways: loss of PKMYT1 activity compromises checkpoint integrity, allowing cells to progress through mitosis with unrepaired DNA damage. Co-treatment experiments using [[mir-4695-5p|miR-4695-5p]] inhibitors or PKMYT1 overexpression rescue vectors demonstrated that bevacizumab-induced apoptosis is substantially attenuated when [[mir-4695-5p|miR-4695-5p]] is inhibited or PKMYT1 is overexpressed, confirming the critical role of this pathway.² This multi-modal approach—combining biological therapies that modulate PKMYT1 expression with small-molecule kinase inhibitors—highlights the multiplicity of strategies for targeting this critical checkpoint node in cancer cells.
