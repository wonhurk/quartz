**BRCA1** is a nuclear phosphoprotein and core component of the [[homologous-recombination]] pathway essential for repair of DNA double-strand breaks. Its molecular function centers on recruitment to sites of DNA damage — marked by [[histone-h2ax|γH2AX foci]] — in an acetylation-dependent manner that requires active histone modification machinery.¹ [[atm|ATM]] coordinates with BRCA1 at damage sites to activate the DNA damage response, ultimately enabling accurate homologous recombination repair and cell survival following genotoxic stress. Germline pathogenic variants in BRCA1 predispose to multiple malignancies and create a state of synthetic lethality with [[parp-inhibitor]]s, rendering carriers exquisitely sensitive to PARP inhibition and chemotherapy — a therapeutic principle now clinically exploited in both pancreatic and colorectal cancers.

---

## Mechanism

BRCA1 is dynamically recruited to DNA damage sites through an acetylation-dependent mechanism that depends on histone modification state.¹ Upon induction of DNA double-strand breaks, BRCA1 localizes to γH2AX foci in coordination with [[atm]], where it orchestrates the early stages of homologous recombination by nucleating repair factor assembly. The recruitment process is tightly coupled to histone acetylation; attenuation of acetylation through [[fasn|fatty acid synthase (FASN)]] inhibition impairs both BRCA1 and [[atm]] localization to damage sites, creating a cascade that compromises DNA damage checkpoint control. This disruption manifests as elevated levels of unrepaired double-strand breaks and elevated phosphorylation events mediated by [[chk2]], amplifying apoptotic cell death in the presence of genotoxic agents.

---

## PARP inhibitor sensitivity and cancer applications

Pancreatic cancer patients harboring germline pathogenic variants in BRCA1 exhibit platinum sensitivity and are candidates for [[parp-inhibitor]] therapy, as demonstrated in phase II trials of niraparib in patients with ATM, BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, and CHEK2 pathogenic variants.² The synthetic lethality principle underpinning this sensitivity reflects the tumor cell's dependence on PARP-mediated single-strand break repair; when BRCA1-mediated homologous recombination is functionally compromised or genetically absent, cells cannot tolerate additional PARP inhibitor-induced replication fork collapse. In colorectal cancer, BRCA1 dysfunction similarly sensitizes tumors to both PARP inhibition and chemotherapy.¹

---

## Therapeutic combinations and FASN synergy

Recent evidence demonstrates that BRCA1-mediated DNA repair can be exploited through rational drug combinations targeting lipid metabolism. [[fasn|FASN]] inhibition — exemplified by TVB-2640 — suppresses de novo lipogenesis while simultaneously impairing BRCA1 recruitment to DNA damage sites through acetylation-dependent mechanisms.¹ When combined with chemotherapy such as irinotecan, FASN inhibition synergistically potentiates cell death by trapping tumors in unresolved DNA damage; this synthetic lethality is further amplified by addition of the [[parp-inhibitor|PARP inhibitor]] olaparib as maintenance therapy. This triplet strategy — FASN inhibitor, chemotherapy, and PARP inhibitor — exemplifies how understanding BRCA1's acetylation-dependent recruitment can guide rational multimodal therapeutic design in both locally advanced and metastatic colorectal cancers, and potentially in other BRCA1-proficient tumors where FASN is elevated.
