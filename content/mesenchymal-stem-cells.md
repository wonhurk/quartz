**Mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs)** are multipotent stromal cells derived from [[bone-marrow|bone marrow]] with significant therapeutic potential in regenerative medicine and inflammatory disease treatment. These cells possess the capacity to differentiate into multiple lineages—including osteogenic and angiogenic phenotypes—in response to molecular signals and microenvironmental cues.¹ MSCs exert therapeutic effects through both cell-autonomous mechanisms, including direct cellular differentiation and tissue replacement, and paracrine mechanisms via the secretion of growth factors and anti-inflammatory cytokines that modulate the immune response and promote tissue repair.²

---

## Mechanism

MSCs exhibit multipotent differentiation capacity that is tightly regulated by signaling pathways including the [[hippo-pathway|Hippo pathway]]. During osteogenic differentiation, key transcriptional regulators such as [[runx2|Runx2]] are upregulated, driving expression of mineralization markers including [[osteocalcin|osteocalcin (OCN)]] and osteopontin (OPN), which are accompanied by increased alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity and mineralized nodule formation as assessed by Alizarin red staining.¹ Concurrently, osteogenic differentiation in bone-regenerative contexts is coupled with angiogenic differentiation, wherein MSCs support vascular formation through production of angiogenic factors including [[vegf|VEGF]], [[mmp2|MMP2]], and [[mmp9|MMP9]].¹ This coupled osteogenesis-angiogenesis is essential for effective bone remodeling and repair, particularly in disease contexts where both bone formation and vascular integration are compromised.

MSCs also exert immunomodulatory effects through secretion of anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive molecules. When engineered into bioengineered hybrid spheroids comprising MSCs integrated with drug-loaded microparticles, they exhibit synergistic antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and immunomodulatory properties that exceed those of MSCs alone.² These hybrid constructs can be further enhanced through incorporation of therapeutic agents such as metformin, which amplifies the protective effects of MSC-based interventions.

## Sepsis-induced liver injury

Sepsis represents a complex systemic inflammatory response associated with multi-organ dysfunction and substantial mortality, currently treated primarily with antibiotics and organ-supportive care that may prove insufficient due to the multiple pathways of disease progression. Bioengineered [[mscargo|MSC-metformin hybrid spheroids]] (MSCargo) demonstrate remarkable efficacy in protecting hepatic tissue from sepsis-induced injury.² These constructs significantly enhance hepatocyte viability and functionality while protecting them from oxidative stress and inflammatory cytokine-induced damage.² When transplanted via the hepatic portal vein, MSCargo remarkably reduced sepsis-associated organ dysfunction across multiple organs—including liver, kidney, and lungs—through modulation of the [[tlr4|TLR4]]/[[nf-kb|NF-κB]] signaling pathway as well as enhancement of hepatic regeneration.² The immunomodulatory activity of MSC-based constructs reduces the inflammatory responses characteristic of [[sepsis]] and associated multi-organ dysfunction, offering a promising therapeutic alternative where conventional therapeutics may be insufficient.

## Bone regeneration in osteoporosis

Postmenopausal osteoporosis is a skeletal disorder marked by progressive bone mineral density decline and heightened susceptibility to fractures, arising from the combined effects of estrogen deficiency and disrupted bone-vascular coupling. Bone marrow-derived MSCs (BMSCs) are central to bone remodeling and can be therapeutically directed toward enhanced osteogenic and angiogenic differentiation. Evidence demonstrates that interventions promoting BMSC viability and suppressing BMSC apoptosis in a dose-dependent manner can significantly augment both osteogenic differentiation and angiogenic capacity in the context of postmenopausal osteoporosis.¹ The coupled osteogenesis-angiogenesis mediated by MSCs is indispensable for effective bone repair and regeneration, as the concurrent formation of bone matrix and supportive vasculature enables sustainable bone remodeling and fracture healing.

---

## Angiogenic factor production

Beyond their osteogenic capacity, MSCs function as potent sources of angiogenic signaling molecules critical for tissue regeneration. The upregulation of [[vegf|VEGF]], [[mmp2|MMP2]], and [[mmp9|MMP9]] by differentiated MSCs or MSC-endothelial cell cocultures promotes angiogenic tube formation and vascular integration into regenerating tissues.¹ Matrix metalloproteinases, particularly MMP2 and MMP9, remodel the extracellular matrix to facilitate endothelial cell migration and new vessel formation, while VEGF directly stimulates endothelial cell proliferation and survival. This angiogenic activity is particularly important in contexts where bone remodeling must be coupled with vascular formation, such as in fracture healing and osteoporosis-associated bone regeneration.

## Bioengineered hybrid spheroids

Recent advances have established MSC-based hybrid spheroid constructs as enhanced therapeutic platforms. [[mscargo|MSCargo]]—composed of mesenchymal stem cells integrated with metformin-loaded poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA) microspheres—exemplifies this approach, combining the inherent regenerative and immunomodulatory properties of MSCs with the bioavailability and sustained-release characteristics of drug-loaded [[plga|PLGA]] microparticles.² The synergistic integration of cellular and pharmaceutical components within these hybrid spheroids produces enhanced antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and immunomodulatory activities that exceed those of either component alone, enabling more effective protection of target tissues and modulation of pathological inflammatory responses.
