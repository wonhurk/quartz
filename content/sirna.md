**Small interfering RNA (siRNA)** is a class of therapeutic RNA molecules that achieve targeted gene silencing through activation of the [[rnai|RNA interference]] pathway, delivering durable anti-inflammatory efficacy with favorable biocompatibility.¹ These short, double-stranded RNA sequences function by directing the cellular RISC complex to degrade messenger RNA transcripts of disease-associated genes, providing a precision therapeutic approach distinct from small-molecule drugs in their ability to target previously undruggable pathways. The clinical translation of siRNA therapeutics, however, remains constrained by multiple biological barriers: gastrointestinal degradation and harsh pH environments in the digestive tract, mucus hypersecretion in respiratory diseases, rapid cellular clearance, and the inherent instability of formulations during storage and transport. [[lnp|Lipid nanoparticles (LNPs)]] have emerged as the dominant enabling technology for siRNA delivery, while emerging strategies incorporating lyoprotectants, hydrogel matrices, and stimuli-responsive chemistry are progressively overcoming these translational obstacles.²

---

## Mechanism and Delivery Challenges

siRNA therapeutics function through a fundamentally distinct mechanism from traditional small-molecule drugs. Upon cellular uptake, siRNA is loaded into the RNA-induced silencing complex (RISC), which uses the guide strand to recognize and cleave complementary messenger RNA transcripts with exquisite specificity. This RNAi-mediated pathway results in sustained gene silencing that persists as long as siRNA remains intracellular, achieving durable therapeutic effects that can outlast drug exposure. However, naked siRNA is rapidly degraded by serum nucleases and cannot cross cell membranes efficiently, necessitating sophisticated delivery vehicles.

The barriers to siRNA bioavailability vary by route of administration. For respiratory delivery, pathological mucus hypersecretion characteristic of chronic airway diseases impedes nanoparticle penetration and reduces siRNA accessibility to target lung epithelial and endothelial cells. For oral siRNA therapeutics intended to treat gastrointestinal inflammation, the harsh stomach acid environment, pancreatic proteases, and intestinal nucleases present formidable degradation challenges. Additionally, most nanoparticle formulations suffer from storage instability in liquid form, requiring expensive cold-chain logistics that limit clinical implementation in resource-limited settings.

## Respiratory Disease: Lyoprotectant-Enabled Lung Delivery

[[lnp|Lipid nanoparticles]] encapsulating siRNA with integrated lyoprotectant platforms have emerged as a transformative approach for respiratory gene therapy. A novel strategy combines N-acetylcysteine (NAC), a clinically approved mucolytic agent, within a sucrose-based lyoprotectant matrix, enabling lyophilization of siRNA-containing LNPs while simultaneously addressing the mucus barrier.² This integrated approach preserves physicochemical integrity and siRNA encapsulation during lyophilization, while the released NAC exerts extracellular mucolytic activity that permits nanoparticle penetration through pathological mucus. In murine models of mucus-hypersecretory lung disease, single-dose administration of lyophilized siRNA-LNPs achieved enhanced therapeutic outcomes through a dual-action complementary mechanism: extracellular NAC-mediated mucolysis coupled with intracellular RNAi-mediated inflammation suppression.²

This lyoprotectant strategy transforms the practical feasibility of siRNA therapeutics by enabling room-temperature storage and eliminating cold-chain requirements, a critical consideration for clinical translation in diverse healthcare settings.

## Fibrosis and Reproductive Disorders: Hydrogel-Integrated SMAD3 Silencing

Endometrial fibrosis and the resulting intrauterine adhesions represent a major cause of female infertility, driven by abnormal activation of the TGF-β/[[smad3|SMAD3]] signaling pathway. Sodium hyaluronate-based multifunctional hydrogels delivering [[smad3|SMAD3]]-targeting siRNA provide sustained gene silencing with enhanced cellular uptake and protection from enzymatic degradation.³ The injectable hydrogel architecture addresses the critical shortcomings of naked siRNA in this context: poor tissue uptake, rapid systemic clearance, and rapid enzymatic degradation. By providing sustained intracellular siRNA availability, the hydrogel platform achieves durable SMAD3 silencing that suppresses fibrotic gene expression programs and restores endometrial regeneration.

Beyond its anti-fibrotic efficacy, the sodium hyaluronate hydrogel system exhibits strong antibacterial activity against both gram-negative and gram-positive pathogens, favorable hemostatic properties, and the ability to promote endometrial angiogenesis and cellular proliferation.³ Preclinical studies demonstrate restoration of fertility in intrauterine adhesion models, establishing hydrogel-integrated siRNA delivery as a novel paradigm for reproductive disorders driven by fibrotic pathology.³

## Gastrointestinal Inflammation: Spatiotemporally Targeted Oral Delivery

The oral route of siRNA administration offers the potential for patient convenience and systemic acceptability, yet the extreme hostility of the gastrointestinal tract—encompassing gastric acid, pancreatic proteases, intestinal nucleases, and enzymatic mucus degradation—has historically rendered effective oral siRNA delivery intractable. A transformative approach integrates microalgal biotechnology with nanomedicine through a modular, three-component hydrogel system designed for colon-specific, site-selective delivery.¹ The system comprises an outer inulin-derived hydrogel layer that provides robust protection against harsh gastric and small intestinal conditions while simultaneously regulating gut microbiota homeostasis. Upon reaching the colon, the inulin hydrogel is selectively degraded by colonic bacteria, triggering site-specific release of an intermediate layer composed of negatively charged *Spirulina* microalgae. The *Spirulina* intermediate enhances mucosal adhesion through its intrinsic helical architecture, facilitating intimate contact with inflamed colonic epithelium.

Within this hierarchical architecture resides a core of dual-responsive [[lnp|lipid nanoparticles]] encapsulating [[tnf-alpha|TNF-α]]-targeting siRNA. These lipid nanoparticles incorporate a gemini-like cationic lipid with a disulfide linker, conferring sensitivity to the reducing environment and cathepsin B protease activity characteristic of inflamed colonic mucosa. Upon cellular internalization, the dual-responsive design triggers controlled intracellular release of siRNA, enabling efficient RISC loading and [[tnf-alpha|TNF-α]] mRNA cleavage. Clinical and preclinical evidence demonstrates that this integrated nano-in-microalgae hydrogel system significantly attenuates the progression of ulcerative colitis by sustaining [[tnf-alpha|TNF-α]] suppression while providing superior oral bioavailability compared to conventional formulations.¹

## LNP Optimization: Ionizable Cationic Lipids for Lung Targeting

The composition and ionizability properties of [[lnp|lipid nanoparticles]] critically determine their efficacy and safety profile. Systematic optimization of LNP formulation—including ionizable cationic lipid selection, N/P molar ratios, and formulation buffers—has yielded simplified lung-targeted LNP formulations with exceptional performance characteristics.⁴ Specifically, the ionizable cationic lipid Lipid-392 achieves over a 100-fold improvement in lung delivery efficiency compared to earlier formulations, with targeting specificity reaching 99 percent. Crucially, this optimized formulation demonstrates excellent tolerability even at high doses of 12 mg/kg, indicating a favorable safety margin for therapeutic development.

In vivo validation in acute lung injury models demonstrates that Lipid-392-based LNPs efficiently deliver mRNA to both endothelial and epithelial cells with gene editing efficiencies approaching 70 percent. For siRNA payloads, the optimized LNP achieves median effective doses of approximately 0.05 mg/kg, indicating exceptionally potent gene silencing. Importantly, repeated administrations of Lipid-392 LNPs do not reduce delivery efficiency, supporting the feasibility of multidose therapeutic regimens and long-term gene silencing strategies for chronic diseases requiring sustained suppression of pathological gene expression.⁴

## Storage Stability and Formulation Advancement

A critical barrier to siRNA therapeutics commercialization and global deployment is the storage stability of nanoparticle formulations. Lyophilization—freeze-drying of aqueous formulations to solid powders—offers an elegant solution, provided that the process does not compromise nanoparticle integrity or cargo encapsulation. The integration of functional lyoprotectants—saccharides and protective excipients that stabilize nanoparticles during freezing—has enabled the development of storage-stable siRNA-LNP powders that preserve encapsulation, siRNA integrity, and biological activity.² This advancement eliminates the requirement for continuous cold-chain logistics and enables room-temperature storage, dramatically expanding the practical feasibility of siRNA therapeutics in diverse clinical and resource-limited settings.
