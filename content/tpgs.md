**TPGS** (d-α-tocopheryl polyethylene glycol succinate) is a biocompatible polymeric surfactant that serves as a multifunctional coating agent for therapeutic nanocarriers, dramatically enhancing both drug delivery efficacy and immunological potency. Its central mechanism involves potent activation of tumor cell [[macropinocytosis]], which dramatically increases intracellular nanocarrier accumulation and enables intracellular therapeutic payload delivery.¹ Beyond its delivery function, TPGS acts as a metabolic modulator and antioxidative agent, facilitating [[glutamine]] metabolism reprogramming and sustained release of bioactive compounds in disparate disease contexts.

---

## Mechanism

TPGS operates through dual modalities: surface-engineering properties that enhance nanoparticle dispersity and cellular uptake, and intrinsic biochemical activity that modulates cellular physiology. When incorporated into nanoparticle formulations, TPGS surface-coatings activate tumor cell [[macropinocytosis]] with remarkable potency, significantly amplifying intracellular nanocarrier concentration — a prerequisite for achieving therapeutic efficacy in drug-resistant tumors.¹ The polymer's vitamin E moiety confers biocompatibility and mild antioxidative character, while its polyethylene glycol chain provides water solubility and prolonged circulation kinetics.

Beyond delivery, TPGS-based nanoplatforms reprogram cellular metabolism by potentiating [[glutaminase]] inhibition through coordinated therapeutic payloads. This metabolic intervention simultaneously enhances [[dendritic-cell]] maturation and T-cell priming, thereby bridging innate and adaptive immunity.¹ In oxidative-stress disease contexts, TPGS coating functions as a shielding matrix, regulating sustained release of bioactive species and enabling prolonged scavenging of reactive oxygen species.²

---

## Cancer immunotherapy

In [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer (CRC)]], TPGS-engineered nanoparticles have emerged as a powerful platform integrating cuproptosis induction, photodynamic therapy, and metabolic reprogramming. A purpurin-copper coordinated nanoplatform coated with TPGS (TPGS/P-C@Ce6 nanoparticles) exemplifies this tripartite strategy.¹ The TPGS surface coating activates macropinocytosis to achieve high intracellular nanocarrier accumulation, while encapsulated purpurin reprograms [[glutamine]] metabolism via [[glutaminase]] inhibition, enhancing dendritic cell maturation and initiating robust T-cell priming. Concurrently, copper ion-driven [[cuproptosis]] synergizes with chlorin e6-generated reactive oxygen species to ablate cancer stemness, converting immunologically cold tumors to T-cell-inflamed hot phenotypes.¹ This integrated approach overcomes both limited intratumoral drug accumulation and stemness-mediated immune evasion, establishing durable immunological memory with complete survival in rechallenged mice at 90 days post-treatment.

## Ocular antioxidative therapy

TPGS has proven invaluable as a coating polymer for sustained-release ocular nanotherapeutics addressing glaucoma-induced oxidative stress and inflammation. Hydrogenated silicene nanosheets (HyNDs) surface-modified with TPGS polymer coating enable self-adaptive hydrogen release within the ocular microenvironment.² The TPGS coating provides a shielding effect that prolongs hydrogen generation from the underlying biocompatible nanosheet, which undergoes self-decomposition under physiological conditions. HyNDs efficiently scavenge oxidative radicals and downregulate expression of oxidative stress-associated biomarkers, thereby reducing intraocular pressure while simultaneously mitigating inflammation.² The nanoagent exhibits negligible cytotoxicity and adverse effects on ocular tissues, establishing an antioxidative-anti-inflammatory dual-mechanism approach that advances clinical management beyond pressure-reduction monotherapy. This hydrogen-based platform addresses the fundamental pathological driver of glaucoma — oxidative stress-mediated optic nerve damage — complementing conventional intraocular pressure-lowering strategies.
