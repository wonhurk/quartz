**M2 macrophages** are a functionally polarized subset of immune cells characterized by anti-inflammatory activity, immunomodulatory properties, and the promotion of tissue regeneration. In contrast to pro-inflammatory M1 macrophages that drive pathogen clearance and tumoricidal responses, M2 macrophages suppress excessive inflammation, facilitate angiogenesis, and coordinate wound healing through secretion of cytokines such as IL-10 and TGF-β. These cells regulate key signaling cascades including [[mapk|MAPK]], the TrxR1/ROS/β-catenin anti-oxidation axis, and [[cacng6|CACNG6]]-mediated calcium ion homeostasis.¹⁴ Their functional versatility has positioned M2 macrophages at the intersection of three major therapeutic domains: as cellular carriers for targeted drug delivery in bacterial infections, as immunosuppressive barriers in cancer requiring reprogramming, and as mediators of tissue repair in diabetic and inflammatory disorders.

---

## Phenotype and polarization

M2 macrophage polarization is dynamically regulated by the local microenvironment and can be induced through bioactive nanomaterials. Selenium-albumin corona nanoparticles ([[h-se-nps|H@Se NPs]]) and pH-responsive zinc sulfide nanoparticles ([[sf-zns-hydrogel|ZnS NPs]]) promote M1-to-M2 phenotype conversion, suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokine production and enhancing reparative functions.⁴⁶ This phenotypic switch involves reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavenging, activation of thioredoxin reductase 1 (TrxR1), and downstream stimulation of the β-catenin pathway, which collectively sustain osteogenic differentiation and tissue regeneration in oxidative stress environments.⁴ The polarization state also determines macrophage interactions with other immune populations: in cholangiocarcinoma, high [[s100a12|S100A12]] expression correlates with increased M2 macrophage infiltration and neutrophil recruitment, creating an immunosuppressive niche.²

## Drug delivery in bacterial infections

M2 macrophages have been engineered as bionic drug carriers to overcome the dual challenges of poor antibiotic accumulation at infection sites and excessive inflammation-induced tissue damage. The [[pt-at-m2|PT@M2]] delivery system encapsulates [[plga|poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) (PLGA)]] nanoparticles loaded with tigecycline inside M2-type macrophages, enabling stable circulation, targeted lung delivery, and pH-triggered drug release in acidic microenvironments.¹ In murine models of carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae (CRKP) pneumonia, PT@M2 reduced bacterial colonies, disrupted bacterial membranes through reactive oxygen species accumulation, and alleviated pulmonary inflammation by downregulating [[cacng6|CACNG6]], thereby indirectly inhibiting the MAPK pathway.¹ This dual mechanism — direct bactericidal action combined with immune microenvironment remodeling — demonstrates the therapeutic potential of macrophage-based delivery platforms in multidrug-resistant infections.

## Cancer applications

### Immunosuppressive limitations

In the tumor microenvironment, M2-like tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs) impede effective anti-tumor immunity, particularly in glioblastoma (GBM), where they constitute the most abundant immune population. Despite their phagocytic capacity, M2-like TAMs exhibit insufficient antigen cross-presentation capability, failing to activate [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cells]] and thereby promoting immunotherapy resistance.⁵ Similarly, in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), elevated [[spp1|SPP1]] signaling drives M2-like macrophage polarization and creates a "cold" tumor microenvironment characterized by reduced immune infiltration and diminished responsiveness to [[anti-pd-1-therapy|anti-PD-1 therapy]].³ Therapeutic blockade of SPP1 suppresses tumor growth, restores CD8+ T cell effector function, and inhibits M2-like polarization, significantly enhancing checkpoint inhibitor efficacy.³

### Functional reprogramming

To overcome the immunosuppressive role of M2-like TAMs, photo-controllable nanotherapeutic agents have been developed to enhance their antigen cross-presentation capacity. The [[nps-ruin-m|NPs-RuIn-M]] platform comprises lanthanide nanoparticles coupled with M2-targeting peptides and a ruthenium-caged cysteine protease inhibitor, enabling precise delivery to orthotopic GBM lesions after focused ultrasound-mediated blood-brain barrier opening.⁵ Under near-infrared-IIb imaging guidance, 808 nm laser irradiation triggers up-conversion emission, releasing the cysteine protease inhibitor to modulate TAM proteolytic activity and thereby enhance antigen cross-presentation.⁵ This strategy transforms abundant M2-like TAMs from immunosuppressive bystanders into functional antigen-presenting cells, boosting CD8+ T cell activation and anti-tumor immunity in GBM.

## Tissue regeneration and diabetic complications

M2 macrophage polarization plays a central role in tissue repair, particularly in diabetic contexts where chronic oxidative stress, persistent inflammation, and impaired cellular function delay wound closure and bone regeneration. In diabetic periodontitis, selenium-albumin corona rinse ([[h-se-nps|H@Se NPs]]) promotes M1-to-M2 macrophage polarization, suppresses local inflammation, exerts anti-bacterial effects against dental plaque, and scavenges ROS to activate the TrxR1/ROS/β-catenin cascade, enhancing osteogenic differentiation and periodontal bone regeneration.⁴ In diabetic wound healing, injectable silk fibroin hydrogels encapsulating pH-responsive [[sf-zns-hydrogel|ZnS nanoparticles]] sustain controlled release of hydrogen sulfide and zinc ions, which restore fibroblast and endothelial cell function under oxidative stress, promote M2 macrophage polarization, and accelerate wound closure, re-epithelialization, and angiogenesis.⁶ Collectively, these nanomaterial-mediated approaches leverage M2 macrophages' intrinsic reparative programming to overcome the inflammatory and metabolic barriers characteristic of diabetic tissue injury.

---

## Novel materials and targeting strategies

Bioactive nanomaterials designed to induce or exploit M2 macrophage polarization represent an expanding frontier in regenerative medicine and immunotherapy. Selenium-based nanoparticles ([[h-se-nps|H@Se NPs]]) assembled with albumin corona exhibit superior anti-inflammatory and anti-bacterial properties compared to commercial selenium formulations, achieving M1-to-M2 conversion through ROS scavenging and TrxR1 activation.⁴ pH-responsive zinc sulfide nanoparticles synthesized via green aqueous-phase hydrothermal methods enable adaptive release kinetics tailored to the acidic wound microenvironment, supporting macrophage phenotype reprogramming and redox balance restoration.⁶ In oncology, M2-targeting peptides conjugated to photo-controllable lanthanide nanoplatforms ([[nps-ruin-m|NPs-RuIn-M]]) achieve selective TAM engagement with spatiotemporal control over immune modulation, offering a precision immunotherapy strategy for tumors with abundant M2-like macrophage infiltration.⁵
