## Mechanisms of immunotherapy resistance

Resistance to ICI therapy in NSCLC is mediated by overlapping immunosuppressive pathways within the TME. [[protein-kinase-c-iota|Protein kinase Cι (PKCι)]] functions as both a biomarker and critical mediator of poor ICI responsiveness; high PKCι expression correlates with resistance to [[anti-pd-1-antibody|anti-PD-1 therapy]], whereas low PKCι expression confers sensitivity.¹⁰ Mechanistically, PKCι drives Yap1-dependent transcription of CCL7, which recruits immunosuppressive [[tumor-associated-macrophages|TAMs]] and reduces CD8+ T cell infiltration. Pharmacological inhibition of PKCι with [[auranofin]] reduces TAM accumulation, promotes CD8+ T cell infiltration, and when combined with anti-PD-1 antibody, triggers a CD8+ T cell-dependent antitumor immune response.¹⁰

Multi-omics profiling has revealed that elevated infiltration of [[znf683|ZNF683]]+CD8+ T cells distinguishes immunotherapy responders from non-responders, with ZNF683 expression serving as a robust indicator of responsiveness.¹¹ Conversely, high-risk tumors exhibit a "cold" TME characterized by reduced immune infiltration, immunotherapy resistance, and enhanced SPP1 signaling. In vivo experiments demonstrated that anti-SPP1 treatment suppressed tumor growth, restored CD8+ T cell effector function, inhibited M2-like macrophage polarization, and significantly enhanced anti-PD-1 efficacy.¹¹ Acquired resistance to EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors introduces an additional layer of immunosuppression: [[afatinib]]-resistant cells exhibit the most pronounced immunosuppressive transcriptional features, characterized by elevated PD-L1 expression, downregulation of shared immune-modulated gene sets, and a cytokine profile favoring immunosuppression. Inhibition of IRE1α signaling partially reversed this immunosuppressive transcriptional signature, indicating a potential strategy for reactivating antitumor immunity.¹²

## Emerging therapeutic strategies

Novel approaches aim to overcome immunotherapy resistance through multi-modal mechanisms. [[bscapm|BSCAPM]], a multifunctional nanoreactor integrating photothermal, photodynamic, chemotherapy, and SiO2-mediated biological effects, induces PANoptosis—a newly identified form of immunogenic cell death integrating pyroptosis, apoptosis, and necroptosis—via ZBP1 activation.¹³ The nanoreactor features a bismuth-based core for photothermal functionality and co-delivers [[paclitaxel]] and phenoxyanthraquinone, disrupting mitochondrial homeostasis and triggering PANoptosome assembly. In vivo studies demonstrated that BSCAPM effectively initiated ZBP1-dependent PANoptosis, elicited robust damage-associated molecular pattern-mediated immune activation, and enhanced [[tumor-associated-antigens|tumor-associated antigen (TAA)]]-directed immunity when combined with anti-PD-1 therapy.¹³

Natural product-inspired agents are also emerging as immunomodulatory leads. [[liriopein-c|Liriopein C]], a methylated homoisoflavonoid with sub-micromolar cytotoxicity against NSCLC cells, induces apoptosis as confirmed by Annexin V/7-AAD staining and [[PARP]] cleavage, while simultaneously enhancing macrophage-mediated tumor cell clearance in vivo by increasing F4/80+CD11b+ peritoneal macrophage uptake of cancer cells.¹⁴ Combination strategies targeting PKCι with [[auranofin]], SPP1 pathway blockade, and biotin supplementation to mitigate [[alectinib]]-induced toxicity represent rational approaches to enhance immunotherapy efficacy and expand the therapeutic window in heavily pretreated NSCLC.
