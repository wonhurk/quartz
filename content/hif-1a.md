**HIF-1α** (hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha) is a master transcription factor that integrates cellular stress responses across hypoxia, inflammation, and metabolic dysfunction, functioning as a critical hub linking angiogenesis, ferroptosis, and immune evasion. As a key molecular target of [[nadph]] in neuroprotection, HIF-1α mediates blood-brain barrier integrity and pro-angiogenic signaling during ischemic injury.¹ Conversely, in inflammatory contexts including aging-related colitis, HIF-1 signaling suppresses pro-inflammatory macrophage infiltration while preserving tissue-reparative immune phenotypes.² Within the tumor microenvironment, [[il-17a|IL-17]]-driven HIF-1α/[[vegf|VEGF-A]] signaling orchestrates metabolic reprogramming and immune evasion cascades in melanoma brain metastases, driving CD73/adenosine-mediated checkpoint escape.³ In pancreatic cancer, HIF-1α functions as a core ferroptosis-related gene positioned within interconnected HIF-1 and [[ferroptosis]] pathways, acting alongside [[gpx4|GPX4]] and [[tfrc|TFRC]].⁴

---

## Mechanism

HIF-1α operates as a context-dependent transactivator whose expression and activity are precisely regulated by both hypoxic and normoxic signals. Under hypoxia, HIF-1α protein stabilizes and translocates to the nucleus, where it heterodimerizes with HIF-1β to activate target genes bearing hypoxia-response elements in their promoters. [[nadph]] elevates HIF-1α protein expression by approximately 77.1% in post-ischemic neural tissue, promoting a pro-angiogenic transcriptional program that includes [[vegf|VEGF-A]], angiopoietin, and erythropoietin.¹ The pathway can be further amplified by [[il-17a|IL-17]] signaling in inflammatory settings, where HIF-1α upregulation drives metabolic rewiring and immune evasion through downstream CD73/adenosine axis activation.³

HIF-1α's regulatory role extends to immune cell phenotyping: the HIF-1 signaling pathway mechanistically suppresses pro-inflammatory macrophage polarization while maintaining alternatively activated, tissue-reparative macrophage populations during intestinal homeostasis and age-related colonic inflammation.² This immunomodulatory function positions HIF-1α as a pivotal rheostat between pathological inflammation and regenerative immune responses, with profound implications for both aging-related and acute inflammatory diseases.

## Neuroprotection in ischemic stroke

HIF-1α represents one of five key molecular targets through which [[nadph]] exerts neuroprotection in ischemic stroke, specifically by reinforcing blood-brain barrier (BBB) integrity and stimulating angiogenesis.¹ NADPH treatment activated HIF-1α signaling and upregulated critical tight junction proteins, including approximately 29% increase in ZO-1 expression, while simultaneously reducing matrix metalloproteinase-9 (MMP-9) and caveolin-1 levels by 23% and 50%, respectively. These coordinated changes strengthened BBB structure and reduced cerebral edema from 81% to 76%, culminating in approximately 50% reduction in infarct volume and significant functional recovery in the transient middle cerebral artery occlusion (tMCAO) model.

The pro-angiogenic HIF-1α/[[vegf|VEGF-A]] axis further supports neuroprotection by promoting endothelial cell proliferation and neovascularization, re-establishing nutrient and oxygen delivery to perilesional tissue. Together, BBB reinforcement and angiogenesis establish HIF-1α as a central therapeutic node for ischemic stroke intervention via NADPH elevation.

## Inflammatory disease and aging

In intestinal homeostasis and colitis, HIF-1α operates as a homeostatic rheostat rather than a purely pro-inflammatory actor. Mechanistic transcriptomic analysis revealed that the HIF-1 signaling pathway mediates the anti-inflammatory effects of [[neu5ac|N-acetylneuraminic acid (Neu5Ac)]] supplementation in both age-related colonic dysfunction and dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced colitis.² Neu5Ac reduced colonic HIF-1α protein signals, predominantly localized to inflammatory cells, and suppressed HIF-1α expression in LPS-stimulated macrophages, thereby attenuating pro-inflammatory macrophage infiltration and polarization. Simultaneously, Neu5Ac preserved tissue-reparative macrophage phenotypes, maintained epithelial barrier integrity, and promoted epithelial regeneration while mitigating senescence-associated p53 activation.

This context-dependent role of HIF-1α—suppressive of inflammatory macrophage function while permissive of regenerative immunity—highlights the pathway as a therapeutic target for age-related gut dysfunction and inflammatory bowel disease.

## Tumor microenvironment and immune evasion

Within melanoma brain metastases, [[il-17a|IL-17]]-driven HIF-1α/[[vegf|VEGF-A]] signaling serves as a central metabolic regulator and immune evasion driver.³ IL-17 stimulation upregulates HIF-1α, which in turn activates VEGF-A transcription and downstream CD73/adenosine axis signaling. This IL-17→HIF-1α→VEGF-A→CD73/adenosine cascade creates a multi-layered checkpoint evasion system, enabling tumor cells to suppress CD8⁺ T cell activation and promote regulatory T cell infiltration. The immunosuppressive adenosine microenvironment further dampens anti-tumor immunity, allowing melanoma metastases to resist immune checkpoint blockade.

Therapeutically, dual targeting of IL-17 and CD73 represents a strategy to disrupt this immune evasion axis. Anti-IL-17 antibodies directly attenuate HIF-1α upregulation and CD73/adenosine axis activation, thereby significantly boosting the efficacy of concurrent anti-CD73 targeting and enhancing CD8⁺ T cell activation and residency.

## Ferroptosis in pancreatic cancer

HIF-1α functions as a core ferroptosis-related gene in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC), positioned within interconnected HIF-1 and [[ferroptosis]] pathways alongside [[gpx4|GPX4]], [[tfrc|TFRC]], and other key ferroptosis executors.⁴ Machine learning analysis of integrated transcriptomic datasets identified HIF-1α among six key ferroptosis genes (GPX4, CTSB, NOX4, TFRC, HIF1A, and TGFB1), all significantly enriched in ferroptosis, HIF-1, and TGF-β pathway databases. Single-cell transcriptome profiling confirmed these genes as core regulators within tumor-associated macrophages and PDAC epithelial cells, with marked correlations between expression and M1/M2 macrophage infiltration patterns.

The involvement of HIF-1α in the ferroptosis signature of PDAC establishes it as a convergence point between metabolic reprogramming, cell death regulation, and fibrotic microenvironment remodeling—three hallmarks of pancreatic cancer pathophysiology. Ferroptosis-inducing agents that modulate HIF-1α activity represent a therapeutic avenue for overcoming PDAC's notorious treatment resistance.

---

## Novel agents targeting HIF-1α

[[resveratrol]] was identified through integrative multi-omics analysis as a ferroptosis inducer that downregulates multiple HIF-1-associated core ferroptosis genes in pancreatic cancer.⁴ Resveratrol suppressed tumor cell activity, enhanced ferroptosis sensitivity, and induced glutathione depletion in PDAC cell lines; ferroptosis inhibitor intervention (Fer-1) partially reversed resveratrol-induced phenotypes, confirming ferroptosis as the primary death mechanism. The compound's multi-target activity—simultaneously engaging HIF-1, ferroptosis, and TGF-β pathways—positions resveratrol as a candidate polypharmacology agent for PDAC and potentially other HIF-1α-driven malignancies.

Combinatorial approaches targeting HIF-1α upregulation in specific microenvironmental contexts (e.g., anti-IL-17 in melanoma, NADPH elevation in stroke, [[neu5ac|Neu5Ac]] supplementation in colitis) underscore the therapeutic versatility of HIF-1α modulation across neuroprotection, inflammation, and cancer.
