**VP1 capsid protein** is the major structural protein of [[poliovirus]] and [[feline-calicivirus]], serving dual roles as a primary genetic surveillance marker and a potent immunogenic antigen. The VP1-encoding region is the standard molecular target for Sanger sequencing and strain characterization in poliovirus epidemiology, enabling precise identification and tracking of vaccine-derived and wild-type isolates in global surveillance programs.¹ When delivered through replication-deficient viral vector platforms such as [[feline-herpesvirus-type-1]], VP1 demonstrates excellent immunogenicity and protective efficacy, particularly when integrated into multivalent vaccine candidates.

---

## Genetic characterization and surveillance

VP1 sequences form the foundation of [[poliovirus]] molecular surveillance networks, providing discriminatory resolution sufficient to differentiate emergent strains and trace transmission chains. The VP1-encoding region is routinely amplified and sequenced by Sanger technique from fecal and wastewater isolates in endemic and high-risk regions.¹ In the Central African Republic, VP1-based genotyping identified eight distinct vaccine-derived [[poliovirus]] type 2 emergences between November 2021 and October 2023, including four derived from the historical Sabin-2 strain and four from the novel oral polio vaccine (nOPV2).¹ This genomic resolution enables epidemiologists to distinguish and track reversion events, assess vaccine strain stability over transmission chains, and detect recombination with related enteroviruses. The genetic characterization of VP1 has become essential for monitoring [[oral-polio-vaccine]] reversion risk in immunoglobulin-deficient individuals and populations with suboptimal herd immunity.

---

## Vaccine delivery applications

[[Feline-calicivirus]] VP1 has been successfully incorporated into replication-deficient viral vector platforms to achieve protective immunization. When expressed through a [[feline-herpesvirus-type-1]] ΔgL-based vaccine candidate, [[feline-calicivirus]] VP1 induced high-titer neutralizing antibodies and protective immunity against challenge infection in vaccinated cats.² The VP1 expression cassette was inserted by genetic replacement of the viral glycoprotein genes (gI/gE), demonstrating that VP1 can be effectively substituted for native herpesviral antigens without compromising immunogenicity or safety.² This approach has been extended to multivalent vaccine platforms, where [[feline-calicivirus]] VP1 was combined with feline panleukopenia virus VP2 in a single construct (rQD1 ΔgL/gI/gE-VP2-VP1), conferring protective immunity against three distinct pathogens in a single vaccination.²
