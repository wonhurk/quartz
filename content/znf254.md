**ZNF254** is a [[zinc-finger-protein|zinc finger protein]] and a cell-intrinsic host factor that suppresses HIV replication and is associated with delayed viral rebound following [[antiretroviral-therapy|antiretroviral therapy (ART)]] interruption. Identified through multiomic analysis of [[cd4-t-cells|CD4+ T cells]] from individuals across four clinical trials investigating [[analytical-treatment-interruption|analytical treatment interruption]], ZNF254 expression is significantly correlated with prolonged [[hiv-1|HIV]] control after therapy cessation.¹ In vitro and in vivo studies confirm that ZNF254 directly suppresses HIV expression, establishing it as a bona fide HIV-silencing factor with potential utility as both a biomarker and a therapeutic target.¹ The clinical significance of ZNF254 is amplified by the discovery that [[metformin]]—an affordable, widely available antidiabetic medication—induces ZNF254-related pathways and suppresses HIV in cells from [[antiretroviral-therapy|ART]]-suppressed people with HIV, suggesting a pathway toward [[antiretroviral-therapy|ART]]-free remission.¹

---

## HIV rebound control after ART interruption

ZNF254 emerged from a comprehensive transcriptomic profiling of [[cd4-t-cells|CD4+ T cells]] obtained before [[analytical-treatment-interruption|analytical treatment interruption]] in the A5345 non-interventional cohort, part of a larger multi-trial program examining immunological determinants of HIV rebound.¹ Across four independent clinical cohorts (CLEAR, TEACH, REDUC, and A5345), delayed viral rebound following [[antiretroviral-therapy|ART]] cessation was not explained by lower [[hiv-reservoir|HIV reservoir]] burden, pointing instead to host cell-intrinsic mechanisms.¹ The expression level of ZNF254 in [[cd4-t-cells|CD4+ T cells]]—independent of other immune factors—was directly associated with prolonged control of HIV replication, suggesting that endogenous HIV silencing rather than passive reservoir suppression governs post-treatment virologic control in some individuals.

---

## Mechanism of HIV suppression

ZNF254 suppresses HIV replication through direct molecular mechanisms validated in both cell culture and in vivo models. In vitro experiments using primary cells and peripheral blood mononuclear cells from [[antiretroviral-therapy|ART]]-suppressed individuals demonstrated that ZNF254 directly inhibits HIV expression.¹ These findings were corroborated by in vivo studies, confirming that ZNF254-mediated HIV silencing operates in living systems.¹ ZNF254 was identified alongside [[ddit4|DDIT4]], an mTOR pathway inhibitor, as a dual HIV-silencing factor in the transcriptomic analysis, indicating that both transcriptional and metabolic regulatory pathways may act in concert to establish post-ART viral control.¹

---

## Metformin-mediated activation and therapeutic potential

A major therapeutic opportunity arose from the discovery that [[metformin]], a first-line antidiabetic agent in use for decades, induces ZNF254 expression and suppresses HIV replication in primary cells and cells derived from [[antiretroviral-therapy|ART]]-suppressed people with HIV.¹ This metformin-mediated HIV silencing effect operates through ZNF254-related pathways, suggesting that an inexpensive, well-tolerated, globally available medication can activate endogenous HIV control mechanisms.¹ The potential for drug repurposing—leveraging an approved medication with established safety and tolerability profiles—represents a pragmatic path toward achieving [[antiretroviral-therapy|ART]]-free [[hiv-1|HIV]] remission, particularly in individuals selected for elevated baseline ZNF254 expression.

---

## Biomarker and clinical trial significance

The identification of ZNF254 across four independent clinical trial cohorts (CLEAR, TEACH, REDUC, and A5345) underscores its translational relevance as both a predictive biomarker and a potential intervention target.¹ Elevated CD4+ T cell ZNF254 expression prior to [[antiretroviral-therapy|ART]] interruption correlated with delayed viral rebound, suggesting that ZNF254 status could stratify individuals most likely to achieve sustained HIV control after treatment cessation.¹ The intersection of ZNF254 identification as an [[hiv-1|HIV]]-silencing factor with the discovery of its activation by [[metformin]] opens a clinically actionable pathway for supporting [[antiretroviral-therapy|ART]]-free remission strategies while simultaneously addressing comorbid metabolic disease in people living with [[hiv-1|HIV]].¹
