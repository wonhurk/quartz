**Rectal cancer** is a gastrointestinal adenocarcinoma of the rectum managed with [[neoadjuvant-therapy|neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy]] to downstage tumors and improve surgical outcomes. Recent molecular and imaging advances have revolutionized patient stratification and prognostication. [[circulating-tumor-dna|Circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA)]] assays measuring [[bcat1|BCAT1]] and [[ikzf1|IKZF1]] DNA methylation enable sensitive detection and longitudinal monitoring of treatment response, while advanced MRI techniques employing amide proton transfer-weighted (APTw) and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) imaging now permit preoperative prediction of [[tumor-budding|tumor budding]] grade—a critical prognostic marker historically evaluable only on surgical specimens.¹²

---

## Treatment response assessment via molecular biomarkers

The [[bcat1|BCAT1]]/[[ikzf1|IKZF1]] ctDNA assay represents a powerful noninvasive tool for detecting rectal adenocarcinoma and monitoring response to neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy. In untreated patients, multiplex qPCR-based quantification of methylated BCAT1 and IKZF1 achieves 87% sensitivity for cancer detection.¹ Following neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy, BCAT1 and IKZF1 methylation levels in tumor tissue decrease substantially in good responders, becoming equivalent to adjacent normal tissue—a molecular signature predictive of favorable histological response.¹ Conversely, poor responders retain elevated methylation levels, providing quantitative discrimination of treatment efficacy. Mechanistically, in vitro experiments on colorectal cancer cell lines demonstrated that radiation exposure alone does not directly alter BCAT1/IKZF1 methylation status, indicating that tissue methylation reductions observed clinically reflect changes in tumor cell biology and differentiation rather than direct DNA damage effects.¹

## Preoperative prognostication through imaging-based tumor budding prediction

[[tumor-budding|Tumor budding]]—defined as isolated cancer cells or small cell clusters at the tumor invasion front—is an independent histopathological prognostic marker strongly associated with aggressive behavior, poor overall survival, and elevated metastatic risk in rectal cancer. Traditional assessment requires surgical specimens, precluding preoperative risk stratification. Multiparametric MRI combining amide proton transfer-weighted (APTw) imaging and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) mapping now enables noninvasive preoperative tumor budding grade prediction. APTw imaging captures tissue acidity and cellular density linked to invasive phenotypes, while ADC maps reflect tissue microstructure and water diffusion patterns. Histogram analysis integrating both modalities yields robust preoperative prediction of tumor budding grade, permitting identification of high-risk patients before surgery and facilitating individualized treatment planning.²
