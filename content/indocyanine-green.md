**Indocyanine Green (ICG)** is an FDA-approved near-infrared dye serving as the only clinically available photothermal agent for cancer treatment.¹ Upon absorption of near-infrared light, ICG undergoes photon-to-thermal energy conversion while simultaneously generating [[reactive-oxygen-species|reactive oxygen species (ROS)]], enabling dual cytotoxic mechanisms against malignant cells. Despite its unique therapeutic potential, clinical translation has been severely hampered by poor chemical stability, rapid blood clearance, and insufficient intrinsic selectivity for [[tumor-microenvironment|tumor microenvironments]], necessitating sophisticated nanoencapsulation strategies to unlock its full therapeutic capacity.

---

## Mechanism

Photothermal therapy with ICG operates through two complementary pathways. Upon illumination with near-infrared laser light (typically 780–820 nm), ICG absorbs photons and dissipates the absorbed energy predominantly as heat, raising localized intratumoral temperatures above 60°C and causing irreversible protein denaturation and tumor cell death.¹ Concurrently, ICG generates [[reactive-oxygen-species|ROS]] through photochemical reactions, inducing apoptosis and triggering [[immunogenic-cell-death|immunogenic cell death (ICD)]] that activates innate immune responses. The integration of ICG into nanoparticle systems further amplifies these effects; for instance, [[mno2|MnO₂ nanoparticles]] co-assembled with ICG within bone-targeting liposomes simultaneously relieve tumor hypoxia and suppress the antioxidant glutathione barrier, dramatically enhancing ROS generation efficiency and activating the [[cgas-sting|cGAS-STING signaling pathway]] for sustained immunoamplification.²

## Sonodynamic therapy in osteosarcoma

Sonodynamic therapy represents a non-invasive extension of ICG-based treatment, harnessing ultrasound activation rather than laser irradiation. ICG-grafted bone-targeting nanoliposomes (LPIM) co-assembled with [[mno2|MnO₂]] nanoparticles navigate to osteolytic regions of osteosarcoma via phosphate-targeting motifs, achieving organ-specific localization within 24 hours.² Upon focused ultrasound exposure, the liposomes undergo cavitation-driven disruption, synchronously releasing ICG and manganese ions. This dual-action mechanism alleviates hypoxic stress in the [[tumor-microenvironment|tumor microenvironment]] while suppressing the tumor's antioxidant defenses, resulting in a 93.5% tumor growth inhibition rate in xenograft models. The release of Mn²⁺ further triggers the [[cgas-sting|cGAS-STING]] axis, amplifying dendritic cell activation and CD8⁺ T cell infiltration. This approach demonstrates that engineered ICG nanoliposomes can achieve complete osteosarcoma suppression while avoiding systemic toxicity.

## Thermoresponsive hydrogel immunotherapy

A complementary strategy employs ICG-loaded [[gold-nanorods|gold nanorods]] embedded within thermoresponsive [[chitosan]]-based double-network hydrogels for sustained tumor immunotherapy.³ The system integrates ICG with boronic acid-modified mesoporous silica, creating nanocomposites that respond to the acidic, enzyme-rich [[tumor-microenvironment|tumor microenvironment]]. Upon in situ gelation at body temperature, the hydrogel forms a durable depot that undergoes sequential release: first, physical degradation via [[chitosan|chitosan]] enzymatic cleavage, followed by chemical cross-linking inversion. As the hydrogel erodes, ICG is progressively released alongside mesoporous silica scaffolds, which cleave boronate esters in acidic conditions to further amplify ROS generation. This sustained-release paradigm enables prolonged engagement with tumor-associated macrophages, promoting their repolarization toward anti-tumor M1 phenotypes while simultaneously activating systemic immune infiltration. The [[chitosan]]-mediated immune modulation synergizes with photothermal and ROS-induced apoptosis, yielding marked suppression of both primary tumors and metastatic dissemination.

## Acoustic delivery via biosynthetic gas vesicles

Recent advances in ICG formulation have overcome the agent's critical pharmacokinetic limitations through conjugation with biosynthetic [[gas-vesicles|gas vesicles (GVs)]].¹ These protein-based nanostructures enable covalent incorporation of ICG with a 58% loading efficiency, resulting in uniform morphology (~200 nm diameter) and exceptional colloidal stability. Critically, ICG-GVs dramatically extend the agent's blood circulation half-life and increase systemic exposure (AUC), directly addressing the poor pharmacokinetics that have limited ICG's clinical utility. Upon focused ultrasound exposure, gas vesicle cavitation triggers intratumoral release of ICG, achieving therapeutic concentrations selectively within tumors while minimizing systemic distribution. Following laser irradiation, delivered ICG generates intratumoral temperatures exceeding 60°C, achieving complete tumor regression in xenograft models with prolonged survival and no detectable systemic toxicity. This acoustic-triggered delivery platform represents a clinically translatable strategy, offering precise spatiotemporal control over ICG release.

## Multimodal imaging integration

ICG-based nanoformulations enable simultaneous multimodal imaging for real-time visualization of therapeutic delivery and efficacy monitoring. ICG-GVs and other engineered platforms provide concurrent ultrasound imaging (via gas vesicle contrast), near-infrared fluorescence imaging (via intrinsic ICG fluorescence), and photoacoustic imaging (detecting ICG light absorption).¹ This theranostic capability allows clinicians to confirm tumor targeting, assess intratumoral drug deposition, and correlate imaging biomarkers with treatment response in real time. The convergence of diagnostic and therapeutic functions within a single ICG-nanoformulation reduces the number of administered agents and simplifies clinical monitoring workflows.

---

## Clinical translation outlook

The three primary nanoformulation strategies—bone-targeting ultrasound-responsive liposomes, thermoresponsive chitosan hydrogels, and gas vesicle-conjugated systems—collectively address ICG's foundational clinical limitations and establish a clear translation pathway. Each platform achieves complete tumor regression in preclinical models while maintaining excellent safety profiles. Future clinical development will likely prioritize gas vesicle-ICG conjugates given their superior pharmacokinetics and versatile multimodal imaging capabilities, though osteosarcoma-specific applications may preferentially adopt bone-targeting sonodynamic platforms. The integration of ICG into compositionally distinct nanoparticle systems demonstrates that rational materials engineering can unlock the potential of FDA-approved agents that have otherwise remained underutilized in cancer therapy.
