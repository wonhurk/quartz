**Gastric adenocarcinoma** is the most common histological type of gastric cancer, characterized by profound molecular and microenvironmental heterogeneity that fundamentally impacts treatment outcomes and therapeutic response.¹ The disease is driven by complex interactions between malignant epithelial cells and the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, particularly through a [[serglycin]]-[[cd44]]-mediated regulatory T cell axis that enforces immune evasion.² Unlike traditionally defined genomic subtypes based on [[her2]] status and microsatellite instability, the heterogeneous tumor microenvironments within individual gastric adenocarcinomas exhibit molecularly distinct "ecotypes" with specific signaling programs, metabolic states, and microbiome compositions that directly correlate with clinical prognosis and immunotherapy responsiveness.¹

---

## Mechanism

Gastric adenocarcinoma arises from dysplastic transformation of gastric epithelial cells and comprises a spectrum of molecular subtypes with distinct pathobiological features. At the histopathological level, the disease ranges from well-differentiated to poorly differentiated adenocarcinomas, often accompanied by significant desmoplastic stromal reaction. The neoplastic cells interact extensively with the tumor microenvironment through both cell-autonomous and non-cell-autonomous mechanisms. Tumor cell-derived [[serglycin]], a secreted proteoglycan enriched in malignant epithelial cells, drives a profound immunosuppressive state through engagement of [[cd44]] on infiltrating T lymphocytes, particularly [[regulatory-t-cells|regulatory T cells (Tregs)]].² This interaction activates the [[cd44]]-[[tgf-beta|TGFβ]] receptor I-[[smad3|SMAD3]] signaling cascade, which simultaneously promotes Treg differentiation and maintains their suppressive phenotype. The mechanism demonstrates remarkable sophistication: [[serglycin]]-[[cd44]] signaling enhances glycolytic metabolism and reactive oxygen species scavenging in Tregs, which attenuates oxidative modification at [[lag3|LAG3]] methionine 171, enabling LAG3 homodimerization and protection from PELI1-mediated K48-linked polyubiquitination and degradation.² Consequently, LAG3 surface expression increases substantially, amplifying inhibitory signals to effector T cells and consolidating the immunosuppressive microenvironment.

## Neoadjuvant chemotherapy

For patients with locally advanced gastric adenocarcinoma (cT2–4, N+, M0), neoadjuvant chemotherapy remains a cornerstone of curative-intent multimodal therapy. The standard-of-care regimen has been the SOX doublet—combining [[s-1]] (tegafur-gimeracil-oteracil potassium) with [[oxaliplatin]]—which improves overall survival compared to surgery alone. Recent phase II trials suggested that adding [[docetaxel]] to create the DOS triplet (docetaxel, oxaliplatin, S-1) might enhance pathological response rates, particularly in HER2-negative disease. A large propensity-score-matched retrospective cohort of 1,283 consecutive HER2-negative patients treated across four high-volume Chinese centers (2010–2024) demonstrated that DOS-based neoadjuvant therapy achieved significantly higher major pathological response (MPR) of 25.7% compared to 17.8% with SOX (OR 1.59, 95% CI 1.14–2.23).³ Critically, this superior efficacy was not accompanied by increased perioperative morbidity, with severe postoperative complications occurring in 2.2% of both arms. In multivariable Cox regression analysis, DOS reduced the risk of disease progression (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.59–0.93) and thereby prolonged disease-free survival relative to SOX, although median overall survival at 34.8 months of follow-up remained comparable.³ The survival advantage was particularly pronounced in pre-specified subgroups including patients aged ≥65 years, those with cT2–3 disease or cN0–1 nodal involvement, and individuals with proximally located tumors, suggesting that DOS-based neoadjuvant therapy should be preferentially considered in these cohorts.

## Molecular ecotypes

The advent of comprehensive multi-omics profiling has fundamentally transformed understanding of gastric adenocarcinoma heterogeneity beyond traditional histopathological and single-platform genomic classifications. A 15-layer atlas integrating 159 primary gastric adenocarcinomas with matched adjacent normal tissues aggregated data across genomics, epigenomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, multiple post-translational modifications, protein-protein interaction networks, metabolomics, and longitudinally sampled microbiome profiles.¹ Using cell-state deconvolution algorithms, this integrated analysis identified molecularly and microenvironmentally distinct tumor ecotypes that refine both histological subtypes and genomic classifications by capturing the full cellular and acellular architecture of each tumor's immune and stromal microenvironment. These ecotypes exhibit remarkably distinct transcriptional programs, signaling pathway activation patterns, metabolic states, post-translational modification landscapes, protein interaction networks, and microbiota compositions, each correlating with specific clinical outcomes and differential responsiveness to immunotherapy.¹ The multi-omics integration prioritized specific genomic and epigenomic aberrations linked to each ecotype and identified their associated vulnerabilities—both cell-autonomous dependencies and microenvironment-mediated resistance mechanisms.¹ Ecotype-specific analysis further revealed distinct proteomic "druggable" landscapes and post-translational modification signatures that identify targetable proteins within their native tumor microenvironment context, providing a rational framework for precision selection of therapeutic agents and combinations tailored to individual tumor ecotypes.
