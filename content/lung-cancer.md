**Lung cancer** is the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide,¹ with acquired drug resistance representing a major clinical obstacle to sustained therapeutic efficacy. Modern treatment relies on [[egfr-tkis|third-generation EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs)]] such as [[osimertinib]], yet resistance mechanisms encompass extracellular matrix remodeling, cell cycle dysregulation, and angiogenesis activation.¹ A critical metabolic escape route involves the [[usp20|USP20]]-[[gpx4|GPX4]] deubiquitinase axis, wherein USP20-mediated stabilization of GPX4 prevents ferroptosis-dependent cell death; both proteins are co-overexpressed in poor-prognosis patients.² Patient-derived organoid (PDO) models constructed from malignant pleural effusions now enable precision prediction of treatment response with 87.1% overall accuracy, transforming the diagnostic and therapeutic landscape by identifying resistance pathways at the individual patient level.¹

---

## Disease burden and resistance heterogeneity

Lung cancer's dominance as the leading cause of cancer mortality reflects both its high incidence and the profound challenge of drug resistance. The evolutionary pressures imposed by targeted therapy select for tumor subpopulations capable of simultaneously rewiring extracellular matrix composition, circumventing cell cycle control mechanisms, and activating neovascularization pathways. These overlapping adaptations create therapeutic heterogeneity that standard cell line models fail to capture, necessitating patient-centric approaches that preserve tumor microenvironmental architecture and genetic context.

---

## EGFR-TKI resistance and patient-derived organoid prediction

The clinical introduction of [[egfr-tkis|third-generation EGFR-TKIs]] such as [[osimertinib]] marked a transformative advance for patients with EGFR-mutant lung cancer, yet acquired resistance emerges within months to years in the majority of cases. To overcome the prognostic limitations of cell line-based assays, patient-derived organoid models have been developed from malignant pleural effusions through optimized Ficoll density-gradient separation and low-adhesion culture. These PDOs achieve construction success rates of 82.8% from malignant effusion samples and accurately predict clinical treatment responses with 91.7% specificity, 71.4% sensitivity, and 87.1% overall accuracy.¹ Transcriptomic analysis of treatment-resistant PDOs identified three convergent resistance pathways: extracellular matrix remodeling, dysregulation of cell cycle checkpoints, and activation of angiogenic signaling. Computational screening via Connectivity Map revealed that the nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drug ibuprofen synergistically enhances [[osimertinib]] cytotoxicity, offering a rational evidence-based combination strategy validated in both organoid and cell line models.¹

## Ferroptosis evasion and the USP20-GPX4 metabolic axis

Beyond proliferative adaptations, TKI-resistant lung cancers engage ferroptosis suppression as a core metabolic vulnerability. [[usp20|USP20]], a ubiquitin-specific peptidase, prevents proteasomal degradation of the ferroptosis suppressor [[gpx4|GPX4]] by catalyzing removal of K48-linked polyubiquitin chains, thereby maintaining antioxidant capacity and shielding cells from ferroptosis-induced death.² In primary patient specimens, [[usp20|USP20]] and [[gpx4|GPX4]] are co-overexpressed in both renal cell carcinoma and lung cancer, with elevated co-expression independently predicting poor prognosis and resistance to [[egfr-tkis|EGFR-TKI]] therapy.² Genetic ablation of [[usp20|USP20]] re-sensitizes resistant tumors to TKI-induced ferroptosis, and pharmacological [[usp20|USP20]] inhibition potentiates [[sorafenib]]-mediated tumor growth suppression in vivo. This [[usp20|USP20]]-[[gpx4|GPX4]] axis thus emerges as a high-priority druggable target for overcoming metabolic resistance and resensitizing relapsed disease.²

## Inhalable microbe-oncolytic virus consortiums

Emerging immunotherapies circumvent anatomical and immunological barriers to oncolytic virus efficacy through biomimetic integration of motile microorganisms with oncolytic agents. Inhalable consortiums of tumor-responsive PEGylated adenoviruses conjugated to the motile cyanobacterium [[synechococcus-wh8102|*Synechococcus* WH8102]] penetrate respiratory mucus and epithelial barriers via algal motility, reaching otherwise inaccessible tumor sites within the lung parenchyma.³ The conjugated algae deprive tumoral microenvironments of calcium ions, disrupting epithelial [[tight-junctions|tight junctions]] and enabling deep [[pegylated-adenoviruses|adenoviral]] penetration while simultaneously promoting [[immunogenic-cell-death|immunogenic cell death (ICD)]]. This dual mechanism activates both [[pegylated-adenoviruses|adenovirus]]-mediated and microbial-triggered antitumor immunity, overcoming the immunosuppressive microenvironment typical of lung cancer.³ Intranasal and inhaled delivery of these microbe-oncolytic virus consortiums demonstrates robust therapeutic efficacy in preclinical lung tumor models, establishing a novel paradigm wherein pathogen-mimicry and viral oncolysis cooperatively restore immunogenicity and deep tumor penetration.³
