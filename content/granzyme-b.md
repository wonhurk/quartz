**Granzyme B** is a [[serine-protease|serine protease]] naturally packaged within [[atevs|activated T cell-derived extracellular vesicles (ATEVs)]] that orchestrates a novel non-viral gene delivery mechanism to enhance anti-tumor immunity. Uniquely, granzyme B disrupts the nuclear envelope of recipient cells, enabling intranuclear translocation of double-stranded genomic DNA predominantly encoding [[antigen-presentation|antigen processing and presentation (APP)]] machinery.¹ This mechanism establishes a positive feedback loop in which enhanced antigen presentation drives increased T cell activation and intratumoral effector cell recruitment, thereby converting immunologically "cold" tumors into immunotherapy-responsive "hot" tumors.² When combined with [[pd-1|PD-1]] or [[tigit|TIGIT]] blockade, granzyme B-mediated ATEV delivery synergistically overcomes immune evasion and restores anti-tumor immunity in previously refractory malignancies.

---

## Mechanism

Granzyme B operates through a mechanistically distinct pathway from its canonical role in cytotoxic granule-mediated cell death. Upon entry into dendritic cells and tumor cells via [[atevs|ATEV]] uptake, granzyme B concentrates at the nuclear envelope and catalyzes its disruption, creating transient membrane ruptures that permit the passage of intact double-stranded genomic DNA into the nucleus.¹ The [[atevs|ATEV]]-associated DNA payload is notably enriched in genes encoding components of the [[antigen-presentation|antigen processing and presentation machinery]], including proteasomal and immunoproteasomal subunits, transporter-associated proteins, and [[mhc|MHC]] molecules.² Once delivered intranuclearly, this genomic DNA undergoes transient expression within recipient cells, markedly upregulating antigen processing capacity without requiring permanent genetic integration or triggering irreversible cellular transformation.

The system exhibits intrinsic self-limiting properties: DNase treatment of purified [[atevs|ATEVs]] effectively removes the extracellular vesicular DNA (EVDNA), ablating both APP upregulation and subsequent T cell activation—indicating that granzyme B-mediated nuclear disruption is both necessary and sufficient for DNA delivery and that genomic DNA represents the primary bioactive component.¹ This transient expression profile minimizes autoimmunity risk, as non-immune-related genes are not preferentially delivered and intranuclear expression naturally decays as cells dilute the exogenous nucleic acid.

## Cold tumor immunotherapy

The granzyme B–[[atevs|ATEV]] system has emerged as a cell-free therapeutic strategy specifically designed to overcome immune evasion in cold tumors and immunotherapy-refractory malignancies. In preclinical models, [[atevs|ATEVs]] alone restored antigen presentation machinery in suppressed tumor microenvironments, but their therapeutic potential was dramatically amplified when combined with [[pd-1|PD-1]] blockade.² The combination leverages complementary mechanisms: granzyme B–mediated intranuclear APP gene delivery establishes a self-propagating feedback loop of enhanced antigen presentation and T cell recruitment, while [[pd-1|PD-1]] inhibition removes the brake on T cell activation, creating optimal conditions for durable anti-tumor immunity. This synergistic strategy extends to [[tigit|TIGIT]]-based checkpoint blockade and combination approaches that simultaneously normalize tumor vasculature—such as the [[ring1|RING1 supramolecular inhibitor]]—which further enhances effector T cell infiltration and granzyme B secretion.³

## Packaging within activated T cell extracellular vesicles

The therapeutic advantage of granzyme B resides fundamentally in its incorporation into [[atevs|activated T cell-derived extracellular vesicles]], which function as acellular delivery vehicles. [[atevs|ATEVs]] are naturally secreted by activated T cells and package genomic DNA as their primary bioactive cargo—a feature distinct from conventional [[exosomes|exosomal]] or [[microvesicle|microvesicular]] pathways.² The [[atevs|ATEV]]-enclosed genomic DNA is enriched for immune-related genes; this selective packaging may reflect both the transcriptional state of activated T cells and active curation of the EVDNA pool.¹ Granzyme B's residence within [[atevs|ATEVs]] protects it from extracellular inactivation and premature degradation, concentrating its activity at the point of cell entry and maximizing the efficiency of nuclear envelope disruption and intranuclear DNA delivery.

## Checkpoint blockade synergy

Granzyme B–[[atevs|ATEV]]-mediated enhancement of antigen presentation synergizes potently with [[pd-1|PD-1]] and [[tigit|TIGIT]] inhibitors through complementary immunological mechanisms. In immunotherapy-refractory tumors, upregulated APP induced by granzyme B–[[atevs|ATEV]] delivery expands the pool of tumor-associated antigen-derived peptides presented on [[mhc|MHC]] molecules to effector T cells.² Simultaneously, [[pd-1|PD-1]] blockade relieves T cell exhaustion, permitting robust TCR signaling in response to the amplified antigen-presenting landscape. [[tigit|TIGIT]] inhibition complements this axis by blocking an orthogonal inhibitory signal on T cells, further lowering the threshold for T cell activation and proliferation.³ In combination, these approaches overcome multiple layers of immune evasion and restore durable anti-tumor immunity, particularly in poorly immunogenic tumors where antigen presentation is a rate-limiting step.

---

## Acellular immunotherapy potential

A defining advantage of the granzyme B–[[atevs|ATEV]] system is its acellular nature, circumventing many challenges associated with cell-based therapies. [[atevs|ATEVs]] are cell-free bioparticles that can be manufactured, stored, and administered without the logistical constraints of living cell therapies; they do not require ex vivo expansion, genetic engineering, or cryopreservation protocols optimized for cell viability.² Critically, the transient expression profile of granzyme B–delivered genomic DNA inherently limits autoimmune complications: non-immune-related genes are not delivered, and the intranuclear DNA naturally degrades and is diluted through cell division, preventing sustained off-target immune activation against host tissues.¹ These properties position [[atevs|ATEVs]] containing granzyme B as a promising platform for treating immunotherapy-refractory and cold tumors while maintaining a favorable safety profile compared to adoptively transferred T cells or engineered cell therapies.
