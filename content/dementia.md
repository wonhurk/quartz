**Dementia** is a major neurocognitive disorder affecting over 50 million people worldwide, with prevalence projected to triple by 2050.¹ The disease is characterized by progressive cognitive decline, memory impairment, and loss of functional independence arising from multiple pathological mechanisms. [[cerebral-small-vessel-disease|Cerebral small vessel disease (CSVD)]] represents a significant pathological substrate, accounting for a substantial proportion of dementia cases and characterized by [[white-matter-hyperintensity|white matter hyperintensities]], [[lacune|lacunes]], [[cerebral-microbleed|cerebral microbleeds]], enlarged [[perivascular-space|perivascular spaces]], and [[brain-atrophy|brain atrophy]].² The disease pathophysiology is closely linked to accelerated biological aging—a process reflecting cumulative vascular and metabolic physiologic burden that predicts both disease presence and longitudinal progression independently of chronological age.

---

## Pathophysiology

[[cerebral-small-vessel-disease|Cerebral small vessel disease]] is a leading cause of dementia and stroke in aging populations, making it among the most clinically relevant pathologies in neurocognitive aging.² The disease encompasses multiple distinct neuroimaging manifestations, each reflecting specific microstructural changes within brain tissue. [[white-matter-hyperintensity|White matter hyperintensities]] represent zones of ischemic white matter damage resulting from chronic hypoperfusion. [[lacune|Lacunes]] are small cavitary lesions resulting from prior small vessel occlusion and infarction. [[cerebral-microbleed|Cerebral microbleeds]] indicate chronic erythrocyte extravasation from small vessels due to impaired blood-brain barrier integrity and collagen dysfunction. Enlarged [[perivascular-space|perivascular spaces]] reflect degradation of perivascular pathways critical for interstitial solute clearance and waste removal. [[brain-atrophy|Brain atrophy]] indicates progressive neuronal loss and gliotic replacement.

A critical determinant of both CSVD presence and progression is accelerated biological aging.² Biomarker-based biological age, derived from routinely measured clinical parameters such as serum lipids, inflammatory markers, and metabolic indicators, provides risk stratification that extends beyond chronological age alone. This biological aging acceleration captures the cumulative vascular and metabolic burden driving dementia progression and predicts which patients are at highest risk for longitudinal worsening of CSVD manifestations.

## Epilepsy comorbidity and medication management

[[epilepsy|Epilepsy]] is an increasingly recognized comorbidity in dementia, occurring in a clinically significant fraction of affected individuals and requiring careful therapeutic management.³ The development of seizures in dementia introduces substantial treatment complexity, as [[antiseizure-medication|antiseizure medication (ASM)]] selection is associated with variable survival outcomes.³ Although clinical guidelines recommend newer ASM agents over older first-generation compounds, real-world evidence on whether specific ASM choices confer survival advantages in persons with concurrent dementia and epilepsy remains limited. Clinicians must balance seizure control against medication-related cognitive and systemic side effects, as polypharmacy and sedating drugs risk accelerating cognitive decline in this vulnerable population. The choice of initial ASM thus represents a critical treatment decision with implications extending beyond seizure suppression to overall survival and functional prognosis.

## Caregiver burden and care delivery

Dementia imposes profound emotional and financial burdens on families and healthcare systems globally. Informal caregivers—predominantly family members—provide the majority of daily care and experience disproportionately high rates of [[psychological-distress|psychological distress]], depressive symptoms, and perceived burden despite limited formal support.¹ The current supply chain supporting dementia care remains fragmented, with inadequate integration between family caregivers and formal community healthcare workers, resulting in duplicated efforts, missed care opportunities, and suboptimal quality of life for both patients and caregivers.

Recent innovations have addressed this fragmentation through the development of [[community-home-based-dual-supplier-cooperation|community home-based dual-supplier cooperation (CHDSC)]] models.⁴ These integrated care models employ [[artificial-intelligence|artificial intelligence (AI)]] to drive decision-assistance systems that coordinate care between family members and professional healthcare workers, optimizing resource allocation, clinical decision-making, and care quality. Pilot implementations in China and other settings have demonstrated improvements in care efficiency, patient quality of life outcomes, and measurable reductions in caregiver strain and burden.

## Psychosocial interventions for caregivers

Evidence-based psychosocial interventions targeting caregiver mental health have demonstrated substantial efficacy. [[mindfulness-based-intervention|Mindfulness-based interventions]] consistently demonstrate moderate-to-large effect sizes in reducing caregiver depression and [[psychological-distress|psychological distress]] across multiple populations, yet traditional delivery modalities—face-to-face sessions or audio-guided home practice—suffer from low adherence rates and scheduling inflexibility that limits real-world impact. [[virtual-reality|Immersive virtual reality (VR)]] technology offers a novel delivery platform that simulates restorative natural environments, minimizes external distractions, and standardizes mindfulness instruction while preserving temporal flexibility for users.¹ Early pilot evidence suggests that VR-based mindfulness interventions enhance engagement, retention, and affective outcomes compared with audio-only equivalents. Feasibility and acceptability studies are underway to establish effectiveness in improving psychological status and caregiver-recipient relationships among dementia caregivers, with potential for scalable deployment in home environments.
