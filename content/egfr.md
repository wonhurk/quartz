**EGFR (epidermal growth factor receptor)** is a receptor tyrosine kinase and validated therapeutic target in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and other malignancies harboring activating EGFR mutations or overexpression. Multiple generations of EGFR-targeted therapies have been developed, including tyrosine kinase inhibitors ([[gefitinib]], [[afatinib]], osimertinib, and fourth-generation agents like [[jin-a02|JIN-A02]]), monoclonal antibodies ([[cetuximab]]), and emerging platforms such as targeted protein degradation and bispecific [[t-cell-engagers|T-cell engagers]].¹ However, acquired resistance driven by escape mutations within the extracellular domain (e.g., S492R) and kinase domain alterations (e.g., C797S and T790M) remains a major clinical challenge, necessitating successive lines of increasingly selective therapies.²

---

## TKI therapy evolution

First-generation EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as [[gefitinib]] established the paradigm of targeted therapy in EGFR-mutant NSCLC, followed by second-generation irreversible inhibitors including [[afatinib]] that expanded kinase coverage.³ Osimertinib, a third-generation EGFR-TKI, was designed to overcome T790M-mediated resistance while sparing wild-type EGFR, and has demonstrated statistically significant progression-free survival extension when administered after definitive chemoradiotherapy in patients with unresectable stage III EGFR-mutated NSCLC.⁴ When administered empirically as second-line treatment following first- and second-generation TKI progression — even in cases where T790M mutation status remains unknown due to inaccessible tumor locations or inadequate rebiopsy specimens — osimertinib has proven to be a viable therapeutic option.⁵

---

## Resistance mechanisms

Acquired resistance to EGFR-targeted therapies arises through multiple molecular escape routes. The C797S kinase domain mutation confers resistance to osimertinib and represents a major clinical challenge, as no approved targeted therapies were available following disease progression on third-generation EGFR-TKIs until the development of fourth-generation agents.² [[jin-a02|JIN-A02]], a novel mutant-selective fourth-generation EGFR inhibitor, overcomes C797S-mediated resistance and demonstrates intracranial activity in NSCLC.² Resistance to monoclonal antibody therapy with [[cetuximab]] frequently arises from missense mutations within the extracellular domain of EGFR, including the clinically relevant S492R and G465R escape variants.¹

Multi-omics profiling of EGFR-TKI-resistant cell lines reveals differential dependencies on EGFR downstream pathways and activation of the [[unfolded-protein-response|unfolded protein response]] through the IRE1α pathway.³ Afatinib-resistant cells exhibit the most pronounced immunosuppressive-like transcriptional features, characterized by elevated [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] expression, minimal changes in MHC class I levels, and a cytokine profile favoring immunosuppression, suggesting poor prognosis and limited efficacy of anti-PD-1/PD-L1 immunotherapy in NSCLC patients with similar resistance profiles.³ Inhibition of IRE1α signaling partially reverses this immunosuppressive transcriptional signature, indicating a potential strategy to restore immune recognition.³

## Alternative therapeutic modalities

Beyond small-molecule TKIs, targeted protein degradation and T-cell redirection platforms offer orthogonal mechanisms to overcome EGFR-driven oncogenesis. The modular [[uptab|UPTAB (Ultrahigh-affinity Protein pairs fused to Targeting Binders)]] platform leverages orthogonal ultrahigh-affinity protein pairs to assemble lysosomal trafficking receptor-binding modules with EGFR-binding modules, achieving near-complete EGFR degradation across multiple cancer cell lines.⁶ In a breast cancer xenograft model, Type-I UPTAB demonstrated approximately 80% tumor growth inhibition, reduced EGFR levels in tumors, and significantly extended survival.⁶ The platform has been further engineered into degrader-drug conjugates by site-specific conjugation of cytotoxic payloads, which retain degradation capacity while exhibiting substantially enhanced anti-proliferative activity.⁶

Bispecific EGFR×[[cd3|CD3]] [[t-cell-engagers|T-cell engagers]] represent an immunotherapeutic approach that redirects T cells against EGFR-expressing tumor cells. Novel antagonistic EGFR antibodies have been developed that bind epitopes overlapping but distinct from the [[cetuximab]]-binding site while retaining high affinity for all major EGFR extracellular domain escape variants, including S492R and G465R.¹ When engineered into bispecific T-cell engagers with 1+1 or 2+1 stoichiometries, these antibodies efficiently induce T cell-mediated cytotoxicity against tumor cells expressing wild-type EGFR as well as clinically relevant escape variants, and demonstrate anti-tumor activity in syngeneic tumor models in vivo.¹

## Immunotherapy interactions

EGFR-mutant tumors exhibit distinct immunosuppressive features that influence response to immune checkpoint inhibitors. [[spatial-proteomics|Spatial single-cell proteotyping]] of pretreatment tumor tissue from 103 patients with metastatic NSCLC revealed that high [[cd73|CD73]] expression on cancer cells confers tolerance to immune checkpoint inhibitors specifically in EGFR/ALK-oncogene-positive NSCLC, potentially through M2 tumor-associated macrophage accumulation and aberrant angiogenesis.⁷ A three-variable spatial composite including [[cd73|CD73]] upregulation in EGFR-mutant NSCLC substantially outperforms [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] expression alone in predicting immunotherapy efficacy.⁸ Among various intratumoral lymphocyte subsets, only CD8+ T cells in direct physical contact with cancer cells were associated with immune checkpoint inhibitor efficacy, with CD39/CD103/Ki-67 positivity marking exhausted yet functional tumor-reactive CD8+ T cells.⁷ Immunosuppressive tumor-associated macrophages and cancer-associated fibroblasts serve as independent unfavorable adversaries in the tumor microenvironment.⁷

## Non-oncology contexts

While EGFR is primarily studied as an oncology target, its broader biological roles extend to other disease contexts. In [[chronic-kidney-disease|contrast-induced acute kidney injury]], electronic hospital monitoring systems have enabled epidemiologic investigation revealing a CI-AKI incidence of 10.53% among patients undergoing elective angiography, with a striking under-diagnosis rate of 92.27% in discharge documentation.⁹ Machine learning models developed from this monitoring data significantly outperformed conventional risk scoring systems for early screening of high-risk patients.⁹

---

## Novel targeted agents

Fourth-generation EGFR-TKIs such as [[jin-a02|JIN-A02]] represent the latest evolution in mutant-selective kinase inhibition, specifically designed to overcome C797S-mediated osimertinib resistance while maintaining intracranial activity in NSCLC.² In HER2-mutant NSCLC, zongertinib — an oral irreversible tyrosine kinase inhibitor that selectively inhibits HER2 while sparing wild-type EGFR — provides a first-line targeted treatment option that minimizes EGFR wild-type-associated toxic effects.¹⁰ The [[uptab|UPTAB]] platform's modular design enables simultaneous degradation of EGFR alongside other targets including c-MET, [[pd-l1|PD-L1]], and HER2 through dual-targeted and tri-targeted configurations.⁶
