**Paclitaxel** is a taxane-based microtubule-stabilizing chemotherapy agent widely used in combination regimens for solid tumors including [[non-small-cell-lung-cancer|squamous non-small cell lung cancer (sq-NSCLC)]] and metastatic [[breast-cancer|breast cancer]].¹ Its clinical utility is constrained by dose-limiting [[chemotherapy-induced-peripheral-neuropathy|chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy (CIPN)]], a severe neurotoxic side effect resulting from axon degeneration in sensory neurons.⁶ Recent advances have expanded paclitaxel's therapeutic scope through perioperative immunotherapy combinations, multifunctional nanoreactor platforms, and bacterial bioengineering for targeted delivery, while parallel efforts have achieved sustainable biosynthetic production of its chemical precursor [[taxadiene]] via metabolic engineering in [[salvia-miltiorrhiza|Salvia miltiorrhiza]] hairy roots.²

---

## Mechanism and clinical use

Paclitaxel exerts its cytotoxic effect by binding to β-tubulin subunits and stabilizing microtubules, preventing their dynamic reorganization during mitosis and ultimately triggering apoptosis in rapidly dividing [[cancer]] cells.⁴ In clinical practice, paclitaxel is frequently combined with platinum-based agents such as [[carboplatin]] to treat advanced [[non-small-cell-lung-cancer|NSCLC]]. A phase II exploratory trial evaluated a simplified four-cycle perioperative regimen combining [[serplulimab]]—an anti-PD-1 antibody—with paclitaxel (or nab-paclitaxel) and [[carboplatin]] in patients with resectable stage II-IIIA [[squamous-nsclc|sq-NSCLC]], demonstrating feasibility and efficacy in the neoadjuvant-adjuvant setting.³

---

## Chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy

[[chemotherapy-induced-peripheral-neuropathy|CIPN]] remains the principal dose-limiting toxicity of paclitaxel therapy, characterized by painful distal sensory neuropathy and axon degeneration.⁶ A human iPSC-derived sensory neuron platform was developed to model paclitaxel-induced axon degeneration, enabling high-throughput screening of 192 kinase inhibitors for neuroprotective activity. This screen identified 19 hits that converge on three STE20 family kinases—[[map4k4|MAP4K4]], [[mink1|MINK1]], and [[tnik|TNIK]]. Genetic knockdown studies confirmed that multi-kinase inhibition of these STE20 kinases is necessary and sufficient for neuroprotection. Selective pharmacological inhibition of MAP4K4, MINK1, and TNIK rescued paclitaxel-induced axon degeneration in both iPSC-derived sensory neurons and primary human dorsal root ganglia, and preserved intraepidermal nerve fiber density in a murine CIPN model.⁶ This strategy represents a translational neuroprotective approach that may enable full-dose paclitaxel administration without neurotoxic sequelae.

## Novel delivery systems

Recent nanomedicine platforms have sought to enhance paclitaxel's therapeutic index through controlled release, combination delivery, and immune co-stimulation. An [[injectable-modular-microgel]] system combining gelatin methacrylate and alginate microspheres achieved independent programmable release of [[doxorubicin]] and paclitaxel, leveraging their distinct mechanisms to reduce tumor mass 3.53-fold compared to conventional hydrogels.¹ Sequential delivery of [[carboplatin]] followed by the ferroptosis inducer [[rsl3]] from the same platform overcame chemoresistance and extended survival by 10 days in a murine tumor model.¹

A biohybrid chemo-immunotherapy platform was engineered by conjugating paclitaxel-loaded [[plga-nanoparticles|poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) nanoparticles]] to [[lactobacillus-rhamnosus-gg|Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG (LGG)]], a probiotic bacterium with acid-environment tropism matching the tumor microenvironment.⁴ The resulting PTX-NPs-LGG system demonstrated active penetration into deep tumor tissue, cytotoxicity via acidification-induced apoptosis, competitive exclusion of tumor-promoting bacteria, and activation of M1-type macrophage polarization. Delivered via thermo-sensitive hydrogel for peritumoral sustained release, PTX-NPs-LGG@gel exhibited superior anti-tumor efficacy in both 4T1 breast tumor lung metastasis and B16F10 postoperative recurrence models.⁴

A multifunctional nanoreactor designated [[bscapm|BSCAPM]] (Bi@SiO₂@COP@AQ4N&PTX@hybrid-membrane) co-delivered paclitaxel with phenoxyanthraquinone to induce [[panoptosis|PANoptosis]]—a newly identified form of immunogenic cell death integrating pyroptosis, apoptosis, and necroptosis.⁵ The bismuth-based core provided photothermal functionality, while the SiO₂ layer and covalent organic polymer platform synergistically disrupted mitochondrial homeostasis. Paclitaxel and phenoxyanthraquinone concurrently compromised nuclear DNA integrity and triggered mitochondrial stress, culminating in [[zbp1|ZBP1]]-dependent PANoptosome assembly and robust immune activation in [[non-small-cell-lung-cancer|NSCLC]] models.⁵

## Biosynthetic production

The industrial supply of paclitaxel has historically relied on semi-synthesis from [[taxadiene]], a diterpenoid precursor derived from Taxus species. Sustainable high-yield production of taxadiene was achieved through metabolic engineering of [[salvia-miltiorrhiza|Salvia miltiorrhiza]] hairy roots via heterologous expression of taxadiene synthase from Taxus wallichiana.² This strategy combined CRISPR-mediated disruption of SmCPS1—a competing geranylgeranyl pyrophosphate (GGPP) sink—with transcriptional reprogramming via SmWRKY61 overexpression to enhance terpenoid precursor pathways (MVA/MEP). Optimization of cultivation conditions yielded 65.17 ± 5.25 mg/kg fresh weight [[taxadiene]] in batch cultures, highlighting the potential of this platform for industrial-scale, cost-effective production of paclitaxel precursors.²
