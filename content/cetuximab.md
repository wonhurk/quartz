**Cetuximab** is a chimeric monoclonal antibody targeting the extracellular domain of [[egfr|EGFR]], a validated therapeutic target in cancers harboring wild-type [[kras|KRAS]].¹ The drug is administered as induction therapy in combination with [[folfiri|FOLFIRI]] chemotherapy and as biweekly maintenance monotherapy in metastatic colorectal cancer, where it provides durable progression-free and overall survival benefits. However, intrinsic and acquired resistance to cetuximab-based therapy remains a critical clinical challenge, frequently emerging from missense mutations within EGFR's extracellular domain that prevent antibody binding and enable tumor escape. Recent advances have yielded novel antagonistic EGFR-directed antibodies and bispecific T-cell engagers that recognize alternative epitopes on EGFR, retaining high affinity for cetuximab-resistant escape variants while preserving full enzymatic inhibition.¹

---

## Mechanism of action

Cetuximab binds a specific epitope on the extracellular domain of [[egfr|EGFR]], physically blocking ligand engagement and preventing receptor autophosphorylation. This blockade suppresses downstream phosphorylation cascades through the [[mapk1|MAPK]] and [[akt1|AKT]] pathways, leading to cell cycle arrest and reduced proliferation in EGFR-dependent tumor cells.¹ The therapeutic efficacy of cetuximab is restricted to [[kras|wild-type KRAS]] malignancies; patients with [[kras|KRAS]] mutations show minimal or no benefit from EGFR-targeted antibodies due to constitutive activation of downstream RAS effectors that bypass EGFR-dependent signaling.

---

## Clinical application in metastatic colorectal cancer

Cetuximab has become standard of care for chemotherapy-naïve and previously treated patients with [[kras|wild-type KRAS]] metastatic colorectal cancer. The TIME-PRODIGE-28 trial randomized patients to receive biweekly maintenance cetuximab monotherapy or observation after four months of [[folfiri|FOLFIRI]] plus cetuximab induction chemotherapy.² The induction phase combines irinotecan, folinic acid, and fluorouracil with concurrent cetuximab administration, followed by maintenance dosing at biweekly intervals. Circulating tumor DNA ([[ctdna|ctDNA]]) dynamics are being prospectively studied as potential biomarkers during and after induction therapy to identify patients most likely to benefit from maintenance monotherapy versus early treatment discontinuation.²

---

## Mechanisms of resistance

Intrinsic and acquired resistance to cetuximab frequently results from missense mutations in EGFR's extracellular domain that directly disrupt antibody binding while preserving EGFR signaling function. The clinically prevalent escape variants [[egfr-s492r|S492R]] and [[egfr-g465r|G465R]] constitute approximately 20–30% of cetuximab-resistant tumors, conferring high-level resistance through alteration of critical contact residues in the cetuximab-binding epitope.¹ These mutations enable cells to evade antibody-mediated growth inhibition while maintaining EGFR-dependent proliferative signals. Additional mechanisms of secondary resistance include upregulation of alternative receptor tyrosine kinases and bypass pathway activation, which enable cells to survive in the absence of EGFR engagement.

---

## Novel EGFR-directed antibodies and bispecific T-cell engagers

To overcome cetuximab resistance, antagonistic EGFR-directed antibodies have been rationally designed to bind overlapping yet structurally distinct epitopes from the cetuximab-binding site.¹ These engineered antibodies retain high binding affinity for all major EGFR extracellular domain escape variants, including S492R and G465R, while maintaining full capacity to inhibit EGFR phosphorylation and downstream signaling. The lead antibodies have been further engineered into bispecific EGFR × [[cd3|CD3]] T-cell engagers with 1+1 or 2+1 stoichiometries, combining EGFR-specific targeting with direct T-cell receptor engagement for enhanced anti-tumor cytotoxicity.¹ In preclinical models, these bispecific T-cell engagers efficiently induced T-cell-mediated killing of tumor cells expressing wild-type EGFR as well as clinically relevant resistance variants S492R and G465R, and demonstrated significant anti-tumor activity in syngeneic in vivo tumor models.
