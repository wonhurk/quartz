**Dipeptidyl peptidase 4 (DPP4)** is a serine exopeptidase that catalyzes the removal of dipeptides from the N-terminus of protein and peptide substrates, thereby regulating the activity of circulating hormones and endogenous factors. Its most clinically significant role is the enzymatic degradation and inactivation of [[glp-1|glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1)]], a cardioprotective incretin hormone central to postprandial glucose homeostasis.¹ DPP4 activity originates from two distinct sources: endogenous host production in pancreatic endothelial cells and a previously unrecognized microbial source, wherein the commensal bacterium [[bacteroides-acidifaciens|Bacteroides acidifaciens]] expresses a functional DPP4 isozyme (BaDPP4) that modulates both systemic metabolism and cardiac injury responses.² The pathophysiology of DPP4-mediated disorders spans metabolic dysfunction, ischemia/reperfusion injury, tumor immunity suppression, and degenerative disease, making DPP4 inhibitors cornerstone therapies across multiple clinical domains.

---

## Mechanism

DPP4 cleaves X-Pro↓Y and X-Ala↓Y dipeptide motifs from the N-terminus of target peptides, rendering substrates enzymatically inactive through rapid N-terminal modification. The enzyme operates as a serine protease, utilizing a catalytic triad characteristic of the serine exopeptidase family. In glucose homeostasis, DPP4 constitutes the primary mechanism of GLP-1 inactivation: immediately upon secretion from intestinal L-cells in response to nutrient absorption, GLP-1 undergoes rapid dipeptidyl cleavage by circulating DPP4, with a plasma half-life of less than two minutes.¹ This short-lived activation window limits the efficacy of GLP-1 signaling in glucose-dependent insulin secretion. Beyond glucose regulation, DPP4 targets and inactivates other cardioprotective peptides including glucagon, peptide YY, and stromal cell-derived factor-1, establishing a broad substrate repertoire relevant to metabolic and cardiovascular pathophysiology.

## Incretin-based diabetes therapy

In [[type-2-diabetes|type 2 diabetes]], impaired postprandial GLP-1 responses and reduced insulin secretion characterize the pathogenic mechanisms of hyperglycemia and progressive β-cell dysfunction. DPP4 inhibitors—including [[sitagliptin]], [[vildagliptin]], and [[linagliptin]]—represent a major class of glucose-lowering agents that extend GLP-1 half-life and amplify its insulin secretory effects.¹ By inhibiting enzymatic degradation, these agents achieve glycemic control while imposing a low risk of hypoglycemia, as insulin secretion remains glucose-dependent and shuts off when plasma glucose normalizes.¹ [[Sitagliptin]] has become a cornerstone oral antidiabetic agent, with a favorable safety profile permitting use as monotherapy or in combination with [[metformin]] and thiazolidinediones. However, DPP4 inhibitor use requires clinical vigilance: in patients with [[type-2-diabetes|type 2 diabetes]] initiating [[glp-1-receptor-agonist|GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs)]]—which bypass DPP4 degradation—cardiovascular adverse events including [[nonarteritic-anterior-ischemic-optic-neuropathy|nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy (NAION)]] have emerged as unanticipated complications, warranting risk-benefit reassessment in high-risk populations.¹

## Cardiac ischemia/reperfusion injury and the gut-heart axis

A recently discovered pathogenic role for microbial DPP4 has emerged in [[ischemia-reperfusion-injury|cardiac ischemia/reperfusion (I/R) injury]], revealing a previously unappreciated gut-heart axis. Following myocardial ischemia and reperfusion, intestinal hypoxia and elevated luminal lactate selectively enrich the commensal bacterium [[bacteroides-acidifaciens|Bacteroides acidifaciens]], which produces a functional DPP4 isozyme (BaDPP4) independent of host enzyme activity.² BaDPP4 degrades circulating [[glp-1|GLP-1]] and other cardioprotective peptides in the plasma, amplifying myocardial injury and cardiomyocyte loss.² In acute myocardial infarction patients with I/R injury, [[bacteroides-acidifaciens|B. acidifaciens]] abundance and BaDPP4 levels correlate robustly with clinical markers of myocardial damage, including troponin elevation and reduced ejection fraction.² Pharmacological inhibition of BaDPP4 using [[daurisoline]], a microbial DPP4-specific inhibitor, mitigates cardiac dysfunction and restores cardiomyocyte viability in murine I/R models.² These findings establish the hypoxia-lactate-BaDPP4 axis as a druggable target for microbiota-based cardioprotection and suggest that dysbiosis-driven alterations in microbial DPP4 activity contribute to the pathogenesis of ischemic heart disease.

## Cancer immunotherapy

DPP4 inhibition has emerged as a novel immunomodulatory strategy in tumor immunity. The broad-spectrum DPP inhibitor [[talabostat]], which targets DPP4 alongside DPP8 and DPP9, triggers inflammasome activation in immune cells, inducing robust production of interleukin-18 (IL-18) and interleukin-1β (IL-1β).⁴ This inflammasome-mediated cytokine secretion bridges innate and adaptive immune responses, licensing dendritic cell maturation and enhancing CD8+ T cell priming against tumor-associated antigens. In a phase 2 trial, [[talabostat]] combined with the [[pembrolizumab|anti-PD-1 checkpoint inhibitor pembrolizumab]] demonstrated enhanced anti-tumor immunity in patients with [[small-cell-neuroendocrine-prostate-cancer|small cell neuroendocrine prostate cancer (SCNC)]], a lethal malignancy with historically limited therapeutic options.⁴ The synergy between DPP inhibitor-driven inflammasome priming and anti-PD-1 blockade suggests that DPP4 inhibition may overcome immune suppression and tumor-intrinsic checkpoint upregulation across broader malignancy classes, positioning DPP4 as a target for rational immunooncology combination strategies.

## Degenerative disease and intervertebral disc degeneration

Emerging evidence demonstrates that DPP4 inhibition attenuates intervertebral disc degeneration (IVDD), a leading cause of chronic low back pain and functional disability. [[Type-2-diabetes|Diabetes]] exacerbates IVDD risk through dysregulated glucose metabolism and chronic inflammation; however, the causal mechanisms linking glucose homeostasis to disc health remained underexplored. [[Sitagliptin]], the prototypic DPP4 inhibitor, prevents IVDD progression through macrophage-nucleus pulposus cell crosstalk, wherein DPP4 inhibition suppresses macrophage-derived pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β) and shifts the immunophenotype toward alternatively activated (M2) macrophages.³ This macrophage-mediated immunomodulation reduces extracellular matrix degradation, alleviates the exacerbated disc degeneration observed in diabetic animals, and preserves intervertebral disc height and function.³ These findings position DPP4 inhibitors as disease-modifying agents for IVDD, particularly in diabetes-associated disc degeneration, and suggest broader applications of DPP4 inhibition in mitigating inflammatory degenerative disorders.

---

## Novel DPP4 inhibitors and targeted approaches

Beyond first-in-class [[sitagliptin]], the landscape of DPP4-targeting agents has expanded to address specific pathogenic contexts. [[Daurisoline]], a natural alkaloid and microbial DPP4-specific inhibitor, selectively targets BaDPP4 over host DPP4, enabling cardioprotection without global immunomodulatory complications and emerging as a lead candidate for microbiota-based precision medicine in ischemic heart disease.² [[Talabostat]], originally developed as a broad DPP inhibitor, has been repurposed for cancer immunotherapy through its dual targeting of DPP4, DPP8, and DPP9, exploiting inflammasome-dependent immune licensing to enhance checkpoint blockade efficacy in DPP-inhibitor-resistant malignancies.⁴ Further development of substrate-selective DPP4 inhibitors and microbial DPP4-targeting compounds may enable precision approaches tailored to distinct pathogenic mechanisms—incretin-based diabetes therapy, cardioprotection via dysbiosis reversal, immunooncology synergy, or degenerative disease arrest—thereby maximizing therapeutic benefit while minimizing off-target effects.
