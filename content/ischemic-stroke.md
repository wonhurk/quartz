**Ischemic stroke** is a major global cerebrovascular disease resulting from arterial occlusion and subsequent interruption of cerebral blood flow, triggering interconnected pathophysiological cascades including blood-brain barrier disruption, oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, [[calcium-dysregulation|calcium dysregulation]], mitochondrial dysfunction, and neutrophil extracellular trap formation. Therapeutic management centers on rapid reperfusion through [[intravenous-thrombolysis|intravenous thrombolysis]] with [[tenecteplase|tenecteplase]] or alteplase and [[endovascular-treatment|endovascular therapy]], while emerging neuroprotective strategies target specific molecular pathways to preserve neuronal viability and restore neurovascular integrity.¹²

---

## Pathophysiology

Ischemic stroke pathology unfolds through multiple convergent injury mechanisms initiated by oxygen-glucose deprivation. Blood-brain barrier breakdown permits vasogenic edema and inflammatory cell infiltration, while intracellular [[calcium-dysregulation|calcium overload]] activates excitotoxic cascades and promotes mitochondrial fragmentation.³ The [[rcan1|RCAN1.1L]] isoform translocates to mitochondria within the ischemic penumbra, where it functions as an RNA-binding protein that stabilizes activating transcription factor 2 (ATF2) mRNA by binding its 3'-untranslated region, culminating in pathological FIS1-dependent mitochondrial fission and intrinsic apoptosis.³ Postsynaptic density protein 95 (PSD95) forms a PDZ-independent interaction with neuronal nitric oxide synthase through its guanylate kinase-like domain, driving excessive nitric oxide production and excitotoxic neuronal death.⁴ Acute hyperglycemia significantly exacerbates ischemia-reperfusion injury through receptor for advanced glycation end products (RAGE) activation of the DIAPH1 signaling axis, promoting neutrophil extracellular trap (NET) formation that persists even after spontaneous glucose normalization and contributes to microvascular obstruction.²

## Reperfusion strategies

[[Endovascular-treatment|Endovascular therapy]] combined with [[intravenous-thrombolysis|intravenous thrombolysis]] represents the cornerstone of acute ischemic stroke management for large vessel occlusions. [[Glenzocimab|Glenzocimab]], a humanized antibody fragment targeting platelet glycoprotein VI, shows promise when administered alongside [[endovascular-treatment|EVT]] by increasing reperfusion rates while decreasing symptomatic hemorrhagic transformation risk through selective thrombus growth inhibition without excessive [[bleeding|bleeding]] risk.⁷ In patients older than 80 years presenting with minor stroke and proven vessel occlusion, [[tenecteplase|tenecteplase]] demonstrates safety and efficacy comparable to younger cohorts.¹¹ However, clinical outcomes are substantially influenced by patient-specific factors including [[atrial-fibrillation|atrial fibrillation]] status, with breakthrough strokes occurring despite oral anticoagulation carrying high recurrence and [[bleeding|hemorrhagic]] risk particularly when competing etiologies coexist.⁹ [[Remote-ischemic-conditioning|Remote ischemic conditioning]] efficacy as a non-invasive neuroprotective adjunct appears modulated by smoking status, requiring further investigation to identify optimal patient populations.¹⁰

## Neuroprotective interventions

[[Nadph|Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate (NADPH)]] exerts robust neuroprotection through multiple convergent mechanisms, reducing infarct volume by approximately 50% and improving neurological function by 58% at an optimal dose of 7.5 mg/kg in transient middle cerebral artery occlusion models.¹ Mechanistically, [[nadph|NADPH]] reinforces blood-brain barrier integrity by upregulating tight junction protein ZO-1 expression by 29% with electron microscopy-confirmed structural preservation, while simultaneously reducing matrix metalloproteinase-9 and caveolin-1 levels by 23% and 50% respectively.¹ It additionally suppresses NLRP3 inflammasome activation, decreasing NLRP3, ASC, Caspase-1, and interleukin-1β expression by 14%, 24%, 30%, and 16% respectively, and stimulates angiogenesis through molecular targets including HIF-1α, SRC, NLRP3, CASP3, and AKT1.¹

[[4-dimethylaminophenol|4-Dimethylaminophenol]], an ultralow molecular weight compound of only 137 Da identified through fragment-based screening, represents a novel class of protein-protein interaction inhibitor targeting the PSD95 guanylate kinase domain.⁴ X-ray crystallography confirms direct binding, and the compound disrupts the newly characterized PDZ-independent PSD95/nNOS interaction, reducing nitric oxide overproduction and excitotoxicity with favorable blood-brain barrier permeability and an established safety profile from its clinical use as a cyanide antidote.⁴ The RNA aptamer [[r1sr13|R1SR13]] competitively binds [[rcan1|RCAN1.1L]] protein, disrupting the RCAN1.1L-mtATF2-FIS1 axis to preserve mitochondrial integrity and prevent neuronal apoptosis in the ischemic penumbra.³

## Nanomedicine approaches

Advanced nanomaterial platforms enable spatiotemporally controlled multi-pathway intervention. [[S-e-ldh|S-E@LDH]], a layered double hydroxide nanosheet incorporating magnesium-aluminum-ruthenium with broad-spectrum enzyme mimetic activity, co-delivers the calcium chelator EGTA and anti-inflammatory salvianic acid A to implement triple synergistic regulation of [[calcium-dysregulation|calcium dysregulation]], oxidative stress, and inflammation.⁶ The acidic post-ischemic microenvironment triggers pH-dependent salvianic acid A release, reprogramming microglia toward the M2 phenotype and restoring brain homeostasis.⁶ [[Cxev|Plant-derived extracellular vesicles]] from Ligusticum sinense chuanxiong comprise 167-nm nanoparticles naturally enriched in phthalides including ligustilide and butylphthalide derivatives at approximately 60% content.⁵ Following systemic administration, [[cxev|CXEVs]] efficiently cross the blood-brain barrier with peak accumulation at 12 hours, reprogramming 30 key metabolites across seven pathways including arginine-proline, methionine, purine, and tyrosine metabolism to mitigate ammonia toxicity, oxidative stress, and energy failure.⁵ Concurrently, they activate VEGF signaling by upregulating VEGFA and NOS3 while normalizing KDR and MAPK1 expression, driving endothelial migration, tube formation, and vascular regeneration.⁵ G3702-loaded [[cxev|CXEVs]] engineered at optimal 1:2 weight ratio demonstrate exceptional 30-day stability with sustained release kinetics, outperforming free drug or blank vesicles in therapeutic efficacy.⁵

## Clinical factors and outcomes

Patient demographics and comorbidities substantially influence stroke outcomes and treatment response. Antiplatelet therapy with clopidogrel demonstrates significantly reduced efficacy when coadministered with proton pump inhibitors or potassium-competitive acid blockers, increasing ischemic event risk in stroke patients through pharmacodynamic interactions.⁸ [[Complicated-carotid-artery-plaques|Complicated carotid artery plaques]] characterized by intraplaque hemorrhage, thin or ruptured fibrous caps, and superimposed thrombi correlate with individual carotid geometry and local hemodynamics including wall shear stress and oscillatory shear index, requiring refined imaging-based and blood-based biomarkers for improved individual risk stratification.¹² Acute hyperglycemia represents a particularly deleterious factor that exacerbates cerebral ischemia-reperfusion injury independent of perioperative insulin-based glucose normalization, promoting NET formation through the RAGE/DIAPH1 axis and contributing to futile recanalization — a phenomenon potentially addressable through FPS-ZM1 or deoxyribonuclease I intervention.²
