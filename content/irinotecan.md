**Irinotecan** is a semisynthetic topoisomerase I inhibitor chemotherapeutic agent widely used in the treatment of metastatic [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer (mCRC)]]. Its mechanism of action involves inhibition of topoisomerase I, leading to stabilization of DNA-topoisomerase I cleavage complexes and triggering replication fork collapse and apoptotic cell death in rapidly dividing tumor cells.¹ Irinotecan is most commonly administered as part of the [[folfiri|FOLFIRI]] regimen, combining irinotecan with 5-fluorouracil and folinic acid, which provides synergistic cytotoxicity through complementary mechanisms of DNA damage.¹ When combined with the EGFR-targeted monoclonal antibody [[cetuximab]], FOLFIRI achieves superior tumor response in patients with advanced colorectal cancer.¹

---

## Clinical applications in colorectal cancer

Irinotecan has established itself as a cornerstone agent in the treatment of advanced [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer]]. The FOLFIRI regimen, combining irinotecan with 5-fluorouracil and folinic acid, represents the standard induction chemotherapy approach for patients with metastatic disease. When [[cetuximab]] — an EGFR-targeting monoclonal antibody — is added to FOLFIRI-based induction chemotherapy, the combination yields superior responses in advanced colorectal cancer, as demonstrated in randomized trials such as TIME-PRODIGE-28.¹ Following initial induction therapy, circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) dynamics provide real-time assessment of therapeutic response and inform decisions regarding maintenance therapy strategies, allowing clinicians to distinguish between responding and non-responding patients.¹

---

## Combination strategies and DNA damage response synergy

The therapeutic efficacy of irinotecan-containing regimens can be substantially enhanced through rational combination with agents targeting complementary metabolic and DNA repair vulnerabilities. [[tvb-2640|FASN inhibition]] — targeting fatty acid synthase — impairs the DNA damage response machinery by decreasing ATM expression and CHK2 phosphorylation while reducing BRCA1 and ATM recruitment to DNA damage sites in an acetylation-dependent manner.² When FASN inhibitors are combined with irinotecan, the resulting synergy potentiates chemotherapy-induced double-strand DNA breaks, enhances apoptotic cell death, and significantly delays tumor relapse in xenograft models.² This synergistic interaction can be further amplified by PARP inhibitors such as olaparib used as maintenance therapy, suggesting that multi-targeted combination strategies addressing both lipid metabolism and DNA repair pathways may substantially improve clinical outcomes in colorectal cancer.²

---

## Novel delivery systems for enhanced efficacy

Beyond conventional intravenous administration, irinotecan has been incorporated into innovative drug delivery platforms designed to enhance therapeutic efficacy in transarterial chemoembolisation (TACE) approaches. Sodium alginate sulfate microspheres have been developed as carriers achieving exceptionally high drug loading capacity — 68.0 mg/g for irinotecan hydrochloride — while maintaining sustained drug release exceeding 30 days.³ The self-expanding behavior of these microspheres, conferred by a gradient cross-linked structure formed at the aqueous-oil phase interface, improves embolization stability and reduces the risk of ectopic embolization.³ In preclinical vascular models, sodium alginate sulfate microsphere-based irinotecan delivery produced effective tissue necrosis without vascular recanalization, demonstrating promising applications for TACE in solid tumors including hepatocellular carcinoma and other vascularized malignancies.³
