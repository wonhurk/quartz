**Traumatic brain injury (TBI)** is an acute neurological condition characterized by a self-perpetuating pathophysiological cycle in which neuroinflammation and oxidative stress amplify each other, creating a therapeutic bottleneck that limits functional recovery.¹ This vicious cycle — wherein inflammatory activation drives oxidative stress, which in turn amplifies neuroinflammatory signaling — explains the incomplete efficacy of single-pathway interventions. Recent therapeutic innovations have overcome this limitation through two complementary strategies: concurrent targeting of both neuroinflammatory and oxidative stress pathways via bifunctional nanoplatforms, and direct brain delivery through [[blood-brain-barrier|blood-brain-barrier]]-bypassing intranasal routes that leverage natural neuroanatomical pathways.

---

## Pathophysiology

The acute phase of TBI initiates mechanical tissue disruption, triggering resident [[microglia|microglial]] activation and recruitment of peripheral immune cells. Activation of the complement cascade via [[c1qa]] drives pro-inflammatory cytokine production, while simultaneous generation of reactive oxygen species accumulates within injured neural tissue. Critically, these two processes form a bidirectional feedback loop: neuroinflammatory signaling amplifies oxidative stress through recruitment of activated macrophages and neutrophils, while oxidative damage perpetuates inflammatory signaling through danger-associated molecular pattern release. This self-amplifying cycle extends the pathophysiological injury window far beyond the initial mechanical insult, explaining why monotherapeutic approaches targeting either inflammation or oxidative stress independently achieve suboptimal functional recovery.

---

## Bifunctional Nanoplatform Strategy

A programmed streptavidin-condensed bifunctional nucleic acid nanoplatform ([[dz-g4-h-nps]]) was engineered to break this vicious cycle by simultaneously suppressing both pathways.¹ Constructed via rolling circle amplification, the platform integrates numerous [[dnazyme|C1qa-cleaving DNAzymes]] with peroxidase-like [[g-quadruplex]]/hemin complexes, achieving dual function within a single delivery vehicle. The integrated DNAzymes directly cleave [[c1qa]], the key complement-mediated neuroinflammatory trigger, thereby suppressing the inflammatory cascade at its source. Simultaneously, the embedded G-quadruplex/hemin complex catalyzes decomposition of reactive oxygen species through biomimetic peroxidase activity, neutralizing the oxidative stress arm of the cycle.

In preclinical acute TBI models, this multimodal approach produced markedly superior outcomes compared to individual monotherapies. The bifunctional nanoplatform significantly alleviated cardinal pathophysiological hallmarks including brain edema and cerebral blood flow deficits, while promoting comprehensive motor and cognitive function recovery and improving overall survival.¹ The streptavidin-condensed architecture conferred enhanced stability of the nucleic acid structures and increased drug-loading capacity, enabling sustained therapeutic activity within injured brain tissue.

---

## Intranasal mRNA-LNP Brain Delivery

Complementary innovation has addressed the delivery challenge through intranasal administration of mRNA-loaded lipid nanoparticles ([[inbt-lnp|INBT LNPs]]), circumventing the [[blood-brain-barrier]] via natural neuroanatomical bypass routes.² The olfactory and trigeminal nerves, extending directly into the nasal epithelium, provide transneuronal and transmucosal pathways for direct brain access without systemic circulation. Systematic optimization of [[inbt-lnp|INBT LNP]] surface charge demonstrated that positively charged formulations achieved superior brain transfection efficiency and brain-specific mRNA expression while minimizing off-target delivery to peripheral organs.²

Co-delivery of mRNAs encoding [[bdnf|brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF)]] and [[il-10|interleukin-10 (IL-10)]] via [[inbt-lnp|INBT LNPs]] provided dual neuroprotection through orthogonal mechanisms. BDNF promoted neuronal survival and synaptic plasticity, while IL-10 suppressed the pro-inflammatory cytokine milieu and shifted microglial polarization from pro-inflammatory to neuroprotective phenotypes. In a repetitive mild traumatic brain injury model, this approach significantly reduced neuroinflammation, inhibited neuronal death, and improved cognitive function recovery.² The intranasal route afforded a noninvasive, patient-compliant administration method compatible with both acute emergency settings and chronic therapeutic management regimens.

---

## Functional Recovery and Outcomes

Both the bifunctional nanoplatform and intranasal mRNA-LNP strategies demonstrated comprehensive functional recovery across motor and cognitive domains in preclinical models. Motor function recovery, assessed through rotarod performance and open-field locomotion metrics, was significantly enhanced in both acute TBI and repetitive mild TBI cohorts treated with these approaches.¹² Cognitive recovery, evaluated through Morris water maze performance and novel object recognition tasks, similarly showed marked improvements compared to untreated and monotherapy-treated controls.¹² Beyond behavioral restoration, both strategies restored cerebral blood flow toward baseline levels, reduced brain edema measured by magnetic resonance imaging, and improved acute survival rates in severe TBI models. These comprehensive outcomes highlight the superiority of multimodal pathophysiological targeting over single-pathway interventions, positioning these nanoplatform and gene delivery approaches as paradigm-shifting strategies for acute and chronic TBI management.
