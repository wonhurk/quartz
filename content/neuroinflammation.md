**Neuroinflammation** is a dysregulated immune response in the nervous system characterized by persistent activation of resident innate immune cells—primarily microglia and infiltrating macrophages—and sustained production of pro-inflammatory cytokines that impairs neural regeneration and amplifies neuronal death. Unlike acute protective inflammation, pathological neuroinflammation perpetuates itself through vicious cycles in which inflammatory mediators and oxidative stress mutually reinforce one another, driving progressive cognitive decline, motor dysfunction, and tissue degeneration across diverse neurological disorders including [[peripheral-nerve-injury|peripheral nerve injuries]], Parkinson's disease, traumatic brain injury, [[radiation-induced-brain-injury|radiation-induced brain injury]], and spinal cord injury.¹²³⁴⁵ The two central therapeutic targets that have emerged are [[tlr4|Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)]] and [[csf-1r|colony-stimulating factor 1 receptor (CSF-1R)]], both of which govern the activation state and survival of inflammatory myeloid cells in injured neural tissue.

---

## Mechanism

Neuroinflammation is initiated when tissue damage or metabolic stress triggers pattern recognition receptors on microglia and infiltrating macrophages, leading to nuclear translocation of NF-κB and transcription of pro-inflammatory cytokines including TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-6. [[tlr4|TLR4]] serves as a master regulator of this cascade; its activation in the substantia nigra pars compacta, ventral tegmental area, and hippocampus sustains neuroinflammatory tone that outlasts the initial insult by weeks to months.² [[csf-1r|CSF-1R]] signaling drives macrophage proliferation, migration, and pro-inflammatory polarization in the injury microenvironment, with blockade of this receptor promoting macrophage apoptosis and reducing infiltration.¹ A defining feature of chronic neuroinflammation is the bidirectional amplification loop between oxidative stress and immune activation: reactive oxygen species trigger further microglial activation, while activated microglia generate additional oxidants through NADPH oxidase and other enzymatic pathways, creating a self-perpetuating cycle that resists single-pathway interventions.³

## Peripheral nerve injury

In [[peripheral-nerve-injury|peripheral nerve injury]], dysregulated neuroinflammation is a principal barrier to functional recovery. Microglial activation and macrophage infiltration persist for weeks after crush or transection injuries, with [[csf-1r|CSF-1R]] signaling identified as the dominant driver of macrophage accumulation at the injury site.¹ Temporal control of [[csf-1r|CSF-1R]] antagonism is critical: immediate post-surgical delivery of BLZ945—a [[csf-1r|CSF-1R]] inhibitor currently in clinical trials—suppressed early inflammation but paradoxically hindered regeneration, whereas delayed administration at seven days post-injury via ultrasound-guided injection of a nanocomposite hydrogel modulated ongoing inflammation and significantly enhanced nerve repair.¹ This temporal window reflects the biphasic role of macrophages, which transition from pro-inflammatory M1 to pro-regenerative M2 phenotypes, and premature suppression disrupts debris clearance necessary for axonal regrowth.

## Parkinson's disease

In Parkinson's disease, neuroinflammation driven by [[tlr4|TLR4]] activation contributes to both motor dysfunction and non-motor symptoms including anxiety, neither of which is adequately controlled by dopaminergic replacement therapies. Astragaloside IV, a natural triterpenoid saponin, directly binds [[tlr4|TLR4]] and inhibits downstream NF-κB signaling in microglia within the substantia nigra pars compacta, ventral tegmental area, and hippocampus.² In MPTP-induced parkinsonism, Astragaloside IV dose-dependently improved motor coordination and reduced anxiety-like behaviors, effects that were entirely abolished in [[tlr4|TLR4]]-deficient mice, establishing [[tlr4|TLR4]] as the obligate target.² By preserving dopaminergic and hippocampal neurons through unified anti-inflammatory signaling, the compound represents a multi-symptom therapeutic strategy addressing both motor and psychiatric dimensions of the disease.

## Traumatic brain injury

Traumatic brain injury, particularly repetitive mild TBI, establishes a self-perpetuating inflammatory-oxidative stress cycle that drives secondary brain injury long after the initial mechanical insult. A bifunctional nucleic acid nanoplatform integrating C1qa-cleaving DNAzymes with peroxidase-mimetic G-quadruplex/hemin complexes demonstrated that simultaneous disruption of complement-mediated inflammation and oxidative damage surpasses monotherapy approaches, alleviating brain edema, restoring cerebral blood flow, and improving motor, cognitive, and survival outcomes in mouse models.³ Intranasal delivery of mRNA-loaded lipid nanoparticles ([[inbt-lnp|INBT LNPs]]) bypasses the blood-brain barrier via olfactory and trigeminal pathways, enabling direct brain-specific transfection while minimizing peripheral off-target expression.⁶ Co-delivery of mRNAs encoding brain-derived neurotrophic factor and [[il-10|interleukin-10 (IL-10)]]—a potent anti-inflammatory cytokine—using [[inbt-lnp|INBT LNPs]] significantly reduced neuroinflammation, inhibited neuronal apoptosis, and restored cognition in repetitive mild TBI, establishing a noninvasive platform for mRNA therapeutics in CNS disorders.⁶

## Radiation-induced brain injury

Cranial irradiation for brain tumors induces progressive neuroinflammation and neurodegeneration that constrain therapeutic dose escalation and compromise long-term quality of life. Microglial activation persists for at least 84 days post-irradiation, sustaining a pro-inflammatory microenvironment that drives spatial working memory deficits.⁴ A water-soluble [60]fullerene derivative, [[c60-ser|C60-ser]], administered orally 24 hours after cranial irradiation, restored spatial memory performance to baseline levels and normalized microglial CD68 reactivity, indicating suppression of chronic neuroinflammatory tone.⁴ The neuroprotective effect of [[c60-ser|C60-ser]] reflects both its free-radical scavenging capacity and its modulation of inflammatory signaling, positioning carbon-based nanomaterials as radioprotectants for normal brain tissue during cancer therapy.

---

## Spinal cord injury

Acute neuroinflammation following spinal cord injury drives secondary degeneration through inflammatory myeloid infiltration and polarization. Multi-omics profiling of the early post-injury immune landscape identified a C/EBPβ-regulated S100a4⁺ macrophage population as a central effector of secondary injury.⁵ Targeting this macrophage axis represents a mechanistically grounded strategy to interrupt the inflammatory cascade at its upstream regulatory node, potentially preserving spinal cord tissue and function when delivered within the critical early therapeutic window.

---

## Therapeutic delivery strategies

Effective neuroinflammation management requires overcoming blood-brain barrier penetration and achieving spatial-temporal precision in drug release. Nanocomposite hydrogels combining decellularized nerve matrix with PLGA nanoparticles provide sustained, localized release of [[csf-1r|CSF-1R]] antagonists at injury sites when delivered via minimally invasive ultrasound-guided injection.¹ Rolling circle amplification platforms enable programmed assembly of bifunctional nucleic acid therapeutics that integrate anti-inflammatory DNAzymes with antioxidant catalytic modules into single nanostructures, consolidating multiple therapeutic mechanisms.³ Intranasal [[inbt-lnp|mRNA-LNP]] delivery exploits natural nose-to-brain pathways to achieve brain-specific transfection without systemic exposure, enabling patient-compliant protein replacement therapy for neurotrophic factors and anti-inflammatory cytokines.⁶ Across platforms, the convergent principle is that breaking the neuroinflammation-oxidative stress cycle through simultaneous pathway inhibition exceeds single-target efficacy.
