**Hyaluronic acid (HA)** is a negatively charged natural polysaccharide widely employed as a functional coating and targeting ligand in advanced nanoparticle-based therapeutic and diagnostic systems. Its primary mechanism of action exploits overexpression of the [[cd44|CD44 receptor]] on tumor cells and inflamed tissues, enabling receptor-mediated endocytosis and selective accumulation of HA-functionalized nanocarriers.¹ Beyond tumor targeting, HA's physicochemical properties—including negative charge density, mucus-penetrating capacity, and pH-responsive behavior—make it a cornerstone biomaterial across diverse precision medicine applications ranging from ferroptosis-based cancer therapy to oral peptide delivery and urine-based diagnostics.

---

## Mechanism of targeting and delivery

HA-mediated targeting relies on the specific interaction between the polysaccharide chain and [[cd44|CD44]], a transmembrane glycoprotein upregulated in multiple cancer types and sites of inflammation. Upon binding, HA-coated nanoparticles undergo clathrin- or caveolae-mediated endocytosis, facilitating intracellular cargo release.² The negatively charged HA backbone confers additional advantages in mucosal environments, where electrostatic repulsion with mucin fibers enhances penetration through intestinal and respiratory barriers, thereby increasing bioavailability of encapsulated therapeutics.² HA's pH-responsive degradation further supports site-specific drug release: at acidic gastric pH, HA remains largely intact, protecting cargo from enzymatic degradation, while near-neutral intestinal or tumor microenvironment pH triggers controlled release.²³

---

## Cancer therapy applications

HA functionalization has been integrated into multiple cancer nanotherapeutics, particularly those targeting redox-vulnerable and immunosuppressive tumor microenvironments. In colorectal cancer, a butyrate-modulated copper metal-organic framework coated with HA achieved CD44-mediated tumor accumulation and enabled glutathione-triggered intratumoral synthesis of an xCT inhibitor, inducing ferroptotic cell death and enhancing CD8+ T-cell infiltration.¹ Similarly, the [[dcth|DCTH]] nanoplatform—comprising a Cu₂O photothermal core, dendritic mesoporous organosilica shell, and outer HA layer—demonstrated synchronized release of hydrogen sulfide and Cu⁺ ions, triggering cuproptosis, mitochondrial dysfunction, and macrophage repolarization under near-infrared irradiation.³ The HA shell in this system not only directed tumor-specific delivery but also improved biocompatibility and vascular normalization within the tumor microenvironment.

In urinary cancer diagnostics, HA encapsulation of [[gold-nanoclusters|gold nanoclusters]] conjugated with dual-enzyme-responsive DNA probes enabled noninvasive, visual detection of tumor-associated enzymatic activity via lateral flow assay, providing a platform for real-time therapeutic monitoring without reliance on passive biomarker shedding.⁴

## Oral and transdermal drug delivery

HA-modified nanocarriers have overcome longstanding barriers to oral peptide delivery. A virus-inspired hollow mesoporous silica nanoparticle system co-functionalized with cell-penetrating peptides and HA achieved 97.6% ex vivo intestinal permeability for liraglutide, compared to 34.4% for unmodified silica.² The HA coating enhanced mucus penetration and caveolae-mediated transcellular transport, while preserving peptide stability under gastric conditions. This pH-responsive release profile—7% at acidic pH, 77% at intestinal pH—minimized premature degradation and maximized absorption efficiency.²

In dermatological applications, HA-based soluble microneedles provided transdermal delivery of curcumin-loaded mesoporous polydopamine nanoparticles for acne vulgaris treatment. The microneedle tips dissolved within 2 minutes of skin insertion, achieving sustained drug release and demonstrating 99.4% antibacterial efficacy against Propionibacterium acnes when combined with photothermal activation.⁵ The HA matrix conferred both mechanical strength and rapid dissolution kinetics, enabling painless, patient-friendly administration.

---

## Nanocarrier platforms and design principles

HA's structural versatility enables integration across diverse nanoarchitectures. In metal-organic frameworks, HA serves as both a stabilizing shell and targeting moiety, preventing premature leakage of redox-active metal ions while directing tumor accumulation.¹ In mesoporous silica systems, HA acts as a gatekeeper for pH-controlled cargo release and enhances mucosal penetration through charge-mediated interactions.² Microneedle formulations leverage HA's biocompatibility and dissolution properties to achieve rapid tip dissolution and sustained dermal drug deposition.⁵ In diagnostic nanosensors, HA encapsulation preserves the structural integrity of fluorescent [[gold-nanoclusters]] and DNA probes during systemic circulation and renal filtration, enabling stable urinary excretion and instrument-free detection.⁴
