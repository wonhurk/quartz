**Mucins** are glycoproteins selectively expressed in distinct regions of the gastrointestinal and respiratory tracts, serving as natural molecular anchors for regional drug delivery and mucosal retention.¹ Their organ-specific distribution patterns enable engineered pharmaceutical platforms to target active ingredients to precise anatomical sites—stomach, small intestine, or nasal cavity—through selective recognition by small molecule [[mucin-binding-ligands]] and polymer coatings.¹ This mucin-directed localization strategy dramatically amplifies local drug concentration while minimizing systemic exposure, with formulations achieving up to 25-fold elevation in target tissues and 4.7-fold greater mucosal retention compared to conventional delivery systems.²

---

## Structure and regional distribution

Mucins constitute a heterogeneous family of high molecular weight glycoproteins that form the structural and protective scaffold of mucosal barriers throughout the body. The gastrointestinal and nasal epithelia express distinct mucin repertoires, with tissue-specific patterns across the stomach, small intestine, and respiratory epithelium. This anatomical heterogeneity creates a unique opportunity for precision targeting: small molecule ligands engineered to recognize specific mucins in particular regions can direct nanoparticles and polymer-coated delivery vehicles preferentially toward those sites. Compared to conventional non-selective approaches such as unmodified [[chitosan]], mucin-selective binding ligands achieve up to a 10-fold increase in particle binding to target organs and up to a 4-fold increase in selectivity.¹

---

## Gastrointestinal drug localization

The gastrointestinal tract presents formidable barriers to oral drug delivery, including dramatic pH gradients, enzymatic degradation, variable transit kinetics, and regional differences in absorption capacity. Mucin-targeting platforms overcome these constraints by anchoring drugs to the mucosal layer at specific anatomical regions, concentrating the active ingredient precisely where it is needed. The corticosteroid [[budesonide]], used to treat inflammatory bowel disease, demonstrates a 9-fold increase in tissue concentration when localized to the small intestine through mucin recognition, compared to conventional formulations.¹ The broad-spectrum antibiotic [[tetracycline]] exhibits an even more dramatic 25-fold concentration elevation in the stomach when delivered via mucin-targeting platforms, enabling potent treatment of gastric infections while potentially reducing therapeutic doses.¹

## Nasal delivery and pH-independent mucoadhesion

Intranasal drug delivery offers direct access to nasal tissues and systemic circulation without gastrointestinal complications, but the therapeutic benefit is severely limited by rapid mucociliary clearance and brief mucosal residence time. Conventional mucoadhesives based on unmodified [[chitosan]] exploit electrostatic interactions between the positively charged polymer and negatively charged mucins, yet this mechanism fundamentally fails in the near-neutral pH environment of the nasal cavity, where chitosan loses its protonated charge and mucin-binding capacity. [[Quaternized chitosan]] (QCS) surmounts this limitation by carrying a permanent positive charge independent of pH, enabling robust and sustained electrostatic interactions with mucins under all physiological conditions.² [[Liposomes]] coated with QCS and encapsulating [[mometasone-furoate]], a potent anti-inflammatory corticosteroid for allergic rhinitis, demonstrate substantially superior performance. These [[qcs-mf-lip|QCS-MF-Lip formulations]] exhibit a 5.26-fold higher mucin-binding affinity compared to unmodified [[liposomes]] and achieve approximately 4.7-fold greater ex vivo mucosal retention compared to both conventional systems and the commercial nasal spray Nasonex®.² In a rat model of allergic rhinitis, QCS-MF-Lip treatment significantly reduced histamine levels, alleviated inflammatory symptoms, and demonstrated anti-inflammatory effects superior to Nasonex®, validating the clinical advantage of pH-independent mucoadhesion.²
