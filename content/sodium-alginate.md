**Sodium alginate** is a naturally-derived anionic polysaccharide that serves as a versatile crosslinkable matrix component in advanced multifunctional hydrogels for biomedical applications. Its primary mode of gelation is through Ca²⁺-mediated ionic crosslinking, which forms stable three-dimensional networks with tunable mechanical properties.¹ Sodium alginate functions most effectively when integrated into dual-network or composite architectures, where it is combined with complementary polymers such as [[chitosan]], [[collagen]], and biofunctionalized nanomaterials including reduced graphene oxide and Cu-TA nanosheets. This material platform has emerged as foundational to wound healing systems, intestine-targeted drug delivery platforms, and modular combination therapy formulations, offering superior biocompatibility alongside responsiveness to pH, enzymatic, and photothermal triggers.

---

## Mechanism of Network Formation

Sodium alginate forms hydrogel networks through rapid Ca²⁺-mediated ionic crosslinking via its guluronic acid blocks, creating a process independent of chemical reagents or harsh conditions.¹ The material exhibits dynamic reversibility when incorporated into dual-network systems with borate-crosslinked polymers, enabling reversible gel-sol transitions through dynamic borate ester bond formation and hydrogen bond reformation at fracture interfaces.² In composite architectures, sodium alginate serves dual structural and functional roles: it stabilizes the matrix architecture while permitting incorporation of functional adjuncts such as nanomaterials and bioactive small molecules. The polymer's inherent anionic character enables electrostatic interactions with cationic polymers like [[chitosan]], creating secondary crosslinking that enhances mechanical stability and confers mucosal adhesion properties critical for intestinal and wound-contact applications.

---

## Wound Healing Applications

Sodium alginate has demonstrated significant therapeutic potential in advanced wound dressing formulations, particularly when functionalized with electroactive and adhesive components. In dopamine-functionalized reduced graphene oxide/alginate systems loaded with [[thymol]]-chitosan nanoparticles, the hydrogel achieves exceptional biocompatibility with >90% cell viability and induces robust fibroblast migration at rates of approximately 71.41%.¹ Strong bioadhesion (approximately 8.26 kPa) enables sustained mucosal contact, essential for exudate absorption and microbial barrier formation. Mechanical reinforcement through reduced graphene oxide incorporation yields a compressive strength of 1.03 MPa and a 3.5-fold increase in storage modulus compared to non-reinforced matrices.¹ Antibacterial efficacy is substantial, with inhibition zones of approximately 20 mm against [[staphylococcus-aureus|*Staphylococcus aureus*]] and approximately 23 mm against [[escherichia-coli|*E. coli*]],¹ complemented by robust antioxidant activity exceeding 80%.¹

## Targeted Drug Delivery

Sodium alginate functions as the primary crosslinked matrix in dual-network microsphere systems designed for intestine-specific drug delivery. In dual-crosslinked polysaccharide microspheres combining glycated [[collagen]], sodium alginate, and [[chitosan]], the system achieves stable [[curcumin]] encapsulation while maintaining structural integrity in simulated gastric pH (≤2) and enabling controlled release under colonic pH conditions.² The glycated collagen-curcumin-sodium alginate-chitosan system (ADCXy-Cur-SA-CS) exhibits enhanced cellular antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity compared to non-glycated precursors, demonstrating the synergistic benefit of protein-polysaccharide composite architecture.² This formulation strategy directly addresses ulcerative colitis treatment by integrating antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, mucosal barrier repair, and gut microbiota modulation functions into a single therapeutic entity.

---

## Modular Combination Chemotherapy Platforms

Beyond single-agent systems, sodium alginate microspheres function as drug-specific modules within injectable hydrogel platforms enabling programmable temporal release coordination. In modular microgel architectures, alginate microspheres selectively encapsulate hydrophobic chemotherapeutics such as [[carboplatin]] and [[rsl3|RSL3]], while gelatin methacrylate microspheres carry hydrophilic agents.³ Sequential delivery of carboplatin followed by RSL3 from composite alginate-gelatin microgels overcame chemotherapy resistance and extended mouse survival by 10 days compared to conventional monolithic hydrogels.³ The modular platform's tunable release profiles extend to multi-drug systems combining [[doxorubicin]] and [[paclitaxel]], which achieved a 3.53-fold reduction in tumor mass versus traditional hydrogels,³ establishing sodium alginate as a critical enabler of coordinately-timed polypharmacology.

---

## Dual-Network Mechanical Enhancement

In dual-network systems combining covalent polyacrylamide networks with dynamic borate-crosslinked hydroxypropyl guar gum and sodium alginate, the polymer contributes to exceptional mechanical resilience. Incorporation of Cu-TA nanosheets as structural bridges yields hydrogels with strain capacity reaching 1997.7% and toughness of 1.53 MJ/m³, supporting applications in wearable devices and flexible antibacterial materials.² The dynamic reversibility of borate ester bonds and hydrogen bond reformation at fracture interfaces enables self-healing efficiency up to 91.0%,² permitting repeated loading cycles without permanent property loss. Under near-infrared irradiation, photothermal conversion through incorporated Cu-TA nanosheets coupled with the sodium alginate matrix achieves 99.0% bacterial killing against [[escherichia-coli|*E. coli*]],² combining mechanical robustness with antimicrobial efficacy critical for advanced wound contact applications.
