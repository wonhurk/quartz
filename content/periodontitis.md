**Periodontitis** is a chronic progressive inflammatory disease characterized by the destruction of periodontal tissues, alveolar bone, and dental attachment apparatus, ultimately leading to tooth mobility and tooth loss. The disease exhibits a high global burden and is strongly associated with systemic disorders including diabetes mellitus and cardiovascular disease.¹ Its pathogenesis is driven by dysregulated immune responses, particularly pro-inflammatory IL1B+ macrophage subsets that sustain tissue destruction and impair bone regeneration.² Mechanistically, periodontitis involves [[endoplasmic-reticulum-stress|endoplasmic reticulum stress]], [[mitochondrial-dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]], [[calcium-dysregulation|dysregulated intracellular calcium transport]], excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, and opportunistic bacterial colonization.¹ Contemporary therapeutic strategies have shifted from symptomatic management toward mechanism-driven interventions that simultaneously modulate inflammation, regenerate bone, and eradicate pathogens.

---

## Pathogenesis

The cellular foundation of periodontitis lies in the sustained activation of IL1B+ macrophages, a pro-inflammatory subset identified through single-cell transcriptomic profiling as the principal driver of tissue destruction.² These macrophages exhibit elevated expression of **SERPINB9** (termed PI9 in humans, Spi6 in mice), a serine protease inhibitor that promotes macrophage survival and perpetuates inflammatory responses by preventing apoptosis.² Machine learning integration of multiple single-cell RNA sequencing datasets confirmed SERPINB9 as a critical regulator governing the persistence of pathogenic macrophages in periodontal lesions.

At the subcellular level, [[endoplasmic-reticulum-stress|endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS)]] and [[mitochondrial-dysfunction|mitochondrial dysfunction]] have emerged as pivotal regulators driving pro-inflammatory macrophage activation.¹ Transcriptomic analyses revealed that these two pathological processes are mutually reinforced through dysregulated intracellular Ca²⁺ transport, establishing a feed-forward cycle that amplifies periodontal inflammation.¹ The disruption of ER-mitochondrial calcium crosstalk creates an osteoimmune microenvironment hostile to tissue repair and bone regeneration.

## Diabetes and systemic associations

Diabetes mellitus significantly aggravates periodontitis through multiple convergent mechanisms. The hyperglycemic state characteristic of diabetes drives disordered glucose metabolism, leading to ROS overproduction that inhibits osteogenic differentiation in periodontal tissues.⁵ Concurrently, elevated glucose levels accelerate alveolar bone destruction and increase dental plaque accumulation, which in turn induces localized inflammation.⁵ This bidirectional relationship creates a vicious cycle wherein diabetes exacerbates periodontal tissue destruction, while periodontal inflammation may worsen glycemic control. The disease is also epidemiologically linked to cardiovascular disease and other systemic disorders, establishing periodontitis as a condition with implications extending beyond the oral cavity.²

## Immunomodulation and macrophage repolarization

Therapeutic reprogramming of macrophage polarization from pro-inflammatory M1 to anti-inflammatory, tissue-protective M2 phenotypes represents a central strategy in contemporary periodontitis management. Drug repurposing screens based on FDA-approved compounds identified **[[azilsartan-medoxomil|Azilsartan medoxomil]]**, an angiotensin II receptor blocker, as a pharmacological inhibitor of SERPINB9.² By targeting SERPINB9, Azilsartan medoxomil promotes apoptosis of IL1B+ macrophages, attenuates inflammatory responses, and alleviates alveolar bone loss in experimental periodontitis models.²

An alternative immunomodulatory approach employs polydopamine nanoparticles (PDAB NPs) that leverage metal ion-chelating properties to scavenge excessive Ca²⁺, thereby disrupting the pathological ER-mitochondrial calcium crosstalk.¹ These dual-functional nanoparticles not only restore ER and mitochondrial homeostasis but also remodel macrophage polarization, mitigating periodontal inflammation while simultaneously promoting osteogenic differentiation of bone mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs).¹ Similarly, selenium-albumin corona nanoparticles ([[h-se-nps|H@Se NPs]]) promote M1-to-M2 macrophage polarization while providing additional anti-bacterial effects superior to commercially available selenium nanoparticles.⁵

## Regenerative therapies

Complete functional reconstruction of [[periodontal-bone-defects|periodontal bone defects]] remains clinically challenging due to the loss of periodontal ligament attachment and the pro-inflammatory osteoimmune microenvironment. Periodontal ligament mesenchymal stem cells (PDLSCs) serve as a crucial cellular resource for regenerating periodontal tissues, and their cell-free derivatives—exosomes (Exos)—represent a promising therapeutic strategy for inducing neo-bone growth.³ To overcome delivery challenges posed by the anatomical constraints of periodontal tissues, investigators have developed biomimetic carrier systems including piezoelectric fibrous membranes and [[osteoimmunomodulatory-microneedle|osteoimmunomodulatory microneedles (OIMN)]].

The piezoelectric membrane, composed of poly(L-lactide)/gelatin fabricated via electrospinning and coated with polydopamine, provides sustained release of PDLSC-derived exosomes while generating electrical cues that mimic the electrophysiological features of native bone.³ The membrane's electroactivity, combined with bioactive exosomes, creates a microenvironment conducive to cell recruitment, proliferation, angiogenic differentiation, and osteogenic differentiation.³ In rat periodontal bone defect models, piezoelectric membranes functionalized with PDLSC-Exos significantly enhanced vascularized bone regeneration compared to membranes lacking either exosomes or piezoelectric properties.³

The osteoimmunomodulatory microneedle platform integrates decellularized extracellular matrix (dECM) with PDAB nanoparticles loaded with bone morphogenetic protein 9 (BMP9) for localized delivery.¹ This system addresses the dual challenge of modulating the osteoimmune microenvironment while providing pro-osteogenic cues, thereby accelerating periodontal bone regeneration.¹

## Antimicrobial strategies

Given that dental caries, endodontic infections, and periodontitis are predominantly bacterial diseases capable of exacerbating systemic disorders, advanced antimicrobial strategies are essential. Traditional disinfectants exhibit limited efficacy, frequently necessitating repeated interventions and exposing patients to high recurrence risk.⁴ Multifunctional hybrid nanozymes offer a solution by integrating catalytic antibacterial activity with photothermal enhancement.

The [[pb-mos2-au|PB@MoS₂@Au nanozyme]]—consisting of a Prussian blue nanocube core decorated with a molybdenum disulfide shell and functionalized with gold nanoparticles—exhibits enhanced peroxidase-like activity and a high photothermal conversion efficiency of 37.4% within the mild-hyperthermia window (≈50°C).⁴ Combined treatment with PB@MoS₂@Au, low-concentration hydrogen peroxide (200 μM), and near-infrared irradiation achieved potent antibacterial efficacy against seven representative oral bacterial strains, with bactericidal rates as high as 95%.⁴ Critically, this combined treatment effectively eradicated bacteria within [[oral-biofilms|biofilms]], achieving killing rates exceeding 90%, and eliminated over 99.5% of *Enterococcus faecalis* biofilms from root canals in ex vivo tooth infection models.⁴

For diabetic periodontitis specifically, selenium-albumin corona nanoparticles provide a multifunctional approach combining anti-inflammation, antibacterial activity, and ROS scavenging.⁵ These nanoparticles suppress subgingival microbial colony formation while enhancing osteogenic differentiation in diabetic pathological environments by activating the thioredoxin reductase 1 (TrxR1)/ROS/β-catenin antioxidation cascade.⁵
