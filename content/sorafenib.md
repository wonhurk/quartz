**Sorafenib** is a multikinase tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) approved as first-line therapy for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma, renal cell carcinoma, and lung cancer, distinguished by its dual mechanism of receptor tyrosine kinase blockade and induction of [[ferroptosis|iron-dependent cell death]].¹ Its clinical efficacy is frequently constrained by adaptive resistance mechanisms that operate through distinct but therapeutically targetable pathways: in hepatocellular carcinoma, the endotrophin-[[cd44|CD44]]-STAT3 axis drives epithelial-mesenchymal transition and treatment failure,² while in renal and lung cancers, [[usp20|USP20]]-mediated stabilization of the antioxidant enzyme [[gpx4|GPX4]] enables ferroptosis evasion.⁵

---

## Mechanism

Sorafenib functions through coordinated inhibition of receptor tyrosine kinases—including [[flt1|FLT1/VEGFR1]]—and simultaneous impairment of the cellular ferroptosis defense machinery.¹ By targeting multiple kinase pathways, sorafenib disrupts angiogenesis and tumor proliferation signaling. Concurrently, it sensitizes cancer cells to [[ferroptosis]] by depleting glutathione reserves and compromising antioxidant capacity, rendering cells vulnerable to lethal lipid peroxidation when combined with iron-catalyzing agents such as [[ferrocene]].¹ This dual mechanism positions sorafenib as both a conventional targeted therapy and a ferroptosis-priming agent.

---

## Cancer applications

In hepatocellular carcinoma, pembrolizumab monotherapy has demonstrated durable antitumor activity in both sorafenib-treated and treatment-naïve patients, with median follow-up extending beyond seven years in sorafenib-exposed cohorts and five years in treatment-naïve populations.³ These findings establish the viability of immunotherapy rechallenge following sorafenib progression and highlight the potential for sequential treatment strategies in advanced HCC.

Sorafenib remains a cornerstone first-line agent for advanced renal cell carcinoma and lung cancer, where it exerts antiproliferative effects through inhibition of [[flt1|FLT1]] and downstream AKT1 signaling.⁶ In clear cell renal cell carcinoma, FLT1-centered epithelial-endothelial crosstalk mediates disease progression through VEGFA-AKT1 signaling networks, creating dependencies that sorafenib can exploit.⁶ However, acquired resistance to sorafenib—and related TKIs such as sunitinib—limits long-term disease control across these malignancies.

In triple-negative breast cancer, sorafenib has been incorporated into ferroptosis-sensitizing nanoprodrug platforms. The SOR@RSSF nanoparticle system co-delivers sorafenib with rhein (chemotherapeutic) and [[ferrocene]] (Fenton catalyst) in a glutathione-responsive formulation, achieving synchronous release and synergistic induction of both ferroptosis and apoptosis in TNBC cells.¹ This strategy addresses the lack of effective therapeutic targets in TNBC by leveraging metabolic vulnerabilities inherent to the tumor microenvironment.

## Resistance mechanisms

Sorafenib resistance in hepatocellular carcinoma is mechanistically driven by the endotrophin-[[cd44|CD44]]-STAT3 signaling axis. Endotrophin, a cleavage fragment of collagen VI α3, binds [[cd44|CD44]] on pericentral tumor cells and activates STAT3-dependent transcriptional programs that promote epithelial-mesenchymal transition, proliferation, and sorafenib refractoriness.² Hepatic stellate cell-derived endotrophin sustains this protumorigenic loop through a STAT3-mediated feedback circuit that continuously produces COL6A3. Genetic disruption of Col6a3 and Cd44 in obesity-associated HCC models markedly reduces tumor burden, restores sorafenib sensitivity, and suppresses fibroinflammation.²

In renal cell carcinoma and lung cancer, resistance to sorafenib and related TKIs is conferred by upregulation of [[usp20|ubiquitin-specific peptidase 20 (USP20)]], which binds and deubiquitinates [[gpx4|GPX4]], the master regulator of ferroptosis suppression.⁵ By removing K48-linked polyubiquitin chains from GPX4, USP20 prevents its proteasomal degradation and sustains cellular antioxidant capacity, enabling cancer cells to evade TKI-induced ferroptosis. Clinically, USP20 and GPX4 are co-overexpressed in RCC and lung cancer patients and correlate with poor prognosis.⁵

## Combination strategies

Carbonic anhydrase IX and XII inhibitors—particularly the bis-triazole-linked benzenesulfonamides [[compound-2g|2g]] and [[compound-4g|4g]]—significantly enhance sorafenib chemosensitivity in breast and pancreatic cancers under normoxic and hypoxic conditions.⁴ These [[carbonic-anhydrase-ix|CA IX/XII]]-selective inhibitors disrupt tumor pH regulation and hypoxia-driven survival pathways, thereby overcoming metabolic resistance to sorafenib and improving antiproliferative responses when combined with conventional chemotherapeutics.⁴

Pharmacological inhibition of [[usp20|USP20]] resensitizes TKI-resistant renal and lung tumors to sorafenib-induced ferroptosis in vivo, resulting in marked suppression of tumor growth.⁵ This strategy exploits ferroptosis evasion as a metabolic vulnerability and provides a rational therapeutic paradigm for overcoming acquired TKI tolerance. Similarly, dual knockout of Col6a3 and Cd44 in metabolic dysfunction-associated HCC restores sorafenib sensitivity and attenuates the fibrotic tumor microenvironment.²

Immunotherapy combinations leveraging pembrolizumab have shown sustained antitumor activity in sorafenib-experienced HCC patients, with seven-year follow-up data supporting the durability of checkpoint blockade following prior multikinase inhibitor therapy.³

---

## Novel formulations

The [[sor-rssf-nanoparticles|SOR@RSSF nanoparticle]] platform represents a glutathione-responsive nanoprodrug system engineered to co-deliver sorafenib with ferroptosis-boosting [[ferrocene]] and chemotherapeutic rhein.¹ Constructed using disulfide linkers (-SS-) that respond to intracellular GSH gradients, this system enables highly specific and synchronous drug release within the tumor microenvironment. Ferrocene generates hydroxyl radicals via the Fenton reaction, inducing oxidative stress, while sorafenib concurrently impairs ferroptosis defenses. The resulting lipid peroxide accumulation and GSH depletion synergistically trigger both ferroptosis and apoptosis selectively in TNBC cells, addressing the therapeutic challenge posed by tumors lacking conventional molecular targets.¹
