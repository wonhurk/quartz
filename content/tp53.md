**TP53** is a tumor suppressor gene encoding the p53 protein, a critical transcription factor that regulates cell-cycle arrest, apoptosis, and DNA repair in response to cellular stress. TP53 mutations define particularly aggressive disease subtypes across hematologic malignancies, characterized by markedly poor treatment outcomes.¹ TP53 alterations occur as single-hit or multi-hit events involving point mutations, chromosomal deletions, or copy-neutral loss of heterozygosity, each conferring distinct prognostic implications within and across disease contexts.

---

## Acute myeloid leukemia

[[acute-myeloid-leukemia|Acute myeloid leukemia (AML)]] bearing TP53 mutations represents one of the most aggressive molecular subgroups, with a 2-year overall survival of just 7%—dramatically lower than other AML subtypes including myelodysplasia-related AML (19%), DDX41-mutant AML (70%), and other-AML (37%).¹ This marked prognostic disparity prompted hierarchical molecular stratification in the pivotal CPX-351 trial, which evaluated whether the liposomal anthracycline-cytarabine combination offered survival benefit beyond standard 7+3 chemotherapy when stratified by TP53 mutational status.

TP53-mutant AML demonstrates primary resistance to [[cpx-351]] therapy. In the randomized trial, CPX-351 conferred no survival advantage in TP53-AML compared to standard cytarabine plus anthracycline, in stark contrast to the significant benefit observed in myelodysplasia-related AML.¹ The distinction between single-hit TP53 mutations (TP53single) and multi-hit alterations (TP53multi) further refines prognosis within the TP53-AML cohort, with TP53multi demonstrating median survival of 3.8 months versus 7 months for TP53single, suggesting a direct relationship between the degree of p53 functional loss and disease aggressiveness.¹ For TP53-AML patients undergoing hematopoietic cell transplantation, CPX-351 improved 2-year survival from 27% to 76%, implicating transplantation rather than induction therapy as the primary means of altering the otherwise dismal natural history.

## Chronic lymphocytic leukemia

In [[chronic-lymphocytic-leukemia|chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL)]], TP53 mutations identify a distinct patient subpopulation requiring tailored therapeutic strategies. The phase 3 SEQUOIA trial evaluated [[zanubrutinib]], a selective Bruton tyrosine kinase inhibitor, in treatment-naive CLL patients, including those with TP53 mutations previously considered high-risk.² This population was unsuitable for fludarabine-cyclophosphamide-rituximab (FCR) chemotherapy due to age or comorbidity, in contrast to the AMPLIFY trial design which explicitly excluded TP53-mutant patients.

Zanubrutinib achieved a 3-year progression-free survival of 84.3% in the overall SEQUOIA cohort, which included TP53-mutant CLL patients. When compared indirectly with acalabrutinib-venetoclax combination therapy evaluated in the similarly-structured AMPLIFY trial, zanubrutinib demonstrated durable disease control, with a 3-year PFS of 89.2% in the FCR-ineligible subset—which encompassed TP53-mutant patients—versus 78.9% for acalabrutinib-venetoclax.² This evidence positions zanubrutinib as a tailored therapeutic option for TP53-mutant CLL, providing meaningful benefit in patients requiring modified chemotherapy approaches due to age, fitness, or comorbidity.

## Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma

In [[diffuse-large-b-cell-lymphoma|diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL)]], TP53 mutations associate with a distinctive pattern of extranodal invasion determined by embryonic germ layer specification. Extranodal invasion originating from endoderm-derived tissues (ENI-endoderm) is characterized by mutations in TP53 and [[tet2]], in contrast to ectoderm-derived invasion enriched for MYD88, PIM1, and TBL1XR1 mutations, and mesoderm-derived invasion driven by MYD88, PIM1, TBL1XR1, and CD79B.³ The germ layer origin of extranodal disease carries significant prognostic implications independent of anatomic site: following [[r-chop|R-CHOP]] treatment, mesoderm-originating extranodal invasion associated with inferior progression-free and overall survival, whereas endoderm- and ectoderm-originating invasions demonstrated relatively superior outcomes.³ This framework suggests that TP53-mutant DLBCL originating from endodermal tissues may warrant intensified or alternative therapeutic approaches compared to anatomically similar tumors driven by distinct molecular mechanisms.
