**JAK1** (Janus Kinase 1) is a non-receptor tyrosine kinase that transduces signals from cytokines and growth factors through [[stat3|STAT3]]-dependent and [[stat1|STAT1]]-dependent pathways, playing essential roles in immune regulation, hematopoiesis, and cancer biology.¹ Upon ligand-induced activation of cytokine receptors, JAK1 undergoes trans-autophosphorylation and phosphorylates downstream effectors, initiating gene expression programs that control inflammation, interferon responses, and cell proliferation. Its activity is constrained by feedback inhibition through [[socs3|SOCS3]], a suppressor of cytokine signaling that ubiquitinates JAK1 for proteasomal degradation. In cancer and inflammatory disease, loss of SOCS3-mediated inhibition unleashes pathogenic JAK1 signaling, making selective JAK1 inhibition a major therapeutic strategy.

---

## Mechanism

JAK1 operates at the intersection of cytokine receptor signaling and transcriptional control. Upon cytokine binding, JAK1 associates with the intracellular domains of cytokine receptors and undergoes trans-autophosphorylation at conserved tyrosine residues. This activated kinase then phosphorylates [[stat3|STAT3]] and [[stat1|STAT1]] transcription factors on their C-terminal tyrosines, triggering their dimerization, nuclear translocation, and binding to target gene promoters and distal enhancers. In [[clear-cell-renal-cell-carcinoma|VHL-deficient clear cell renal cell carcinoma]], loss of the feedback inhibitor [[socs3|SOCS3]] hyperactivates JAK1, leading to uncontrolled [[stat3|STAT3]] recruitment to enhancers controlling [[hif-2-alpha|HIF-2α]] expression. The system is tightly integrated: while JAK1 activates transcription factors, SOCS3 normally restrains this activation through ubiquitin-mediated JAK1 degradation, and disruption of this axis is a hallmark of malignancy and chronic inflammation.

## Clear cell renal cell carcinoma and HIF-2α resistance

In [[clear-cell-renal-cell-carcinoma|ccRCC]], JAK1 sustains [[hif-2-alpha|HIF-2α]] transcription through a [[stat3|STAT3]]-dependent enhancer mechanism that drives resistance to [[hif-2-alpha|HIF-2α]] inhibitors such as belzutifan.¹ Using genome-wide CRISPR screening in VHL-deficient ccRCC cells, researchers identified [[socs3|SOCS3]] as a critical negative regulator of this circuit. Mechanistically, loss of SOCS3 activates the JAK1/[[stat3|STAT3]] signaling axis, causing [[stat3|STAT3]] to be recruited to distal enhancers located upstream of [[epas1|EPAS1]] (the HIF-2α gene). These enhancers form chromatin loops that physically contact the EPAS1 promoter, driving robust [[hif-2-alpha|HIF-2α]] transcription and tumor growth in a trans-acting, cytokine-dependent manner independent of classical hypoxia-driven regulation. This enhancer-mediated mechanism explains why direct [[hif-2-alpha|HIF-2α]] protein inhibitors fail to durable suppress tumor growth: the underlying JAK1/[[stat3|STAT3]]-driven transcriptional program persists and can reactivate [[hif-2-alpha|HIF-2α]] expression. Pharmacologic JAK1/[[stat3|STAT3]] inhibition or [[socs3|SOCS3]] overexpression markedly suppresses [[hif-2-alpha|HIF-2α]] expression and tumor progression in both cell culture and mouse xenograft models, suggesting that combined JAK1 and [[hif-2-alpha|HIF-2α]] inhibition is required to overcome therapeutic resistance in this disease.

## Melanoma and JAK1/STAT1-driven immunogenicity

In [[melanoma]], JAK1/[[stat1|STAT1]] signaling enhances interferon-driven tumor immunogenicity through coordinated upregulation of antigen presentation machinery.² The [[jq1|BET inhibitor JQ1]] augments this pathway through broad, proteome-wide effects on melanoma immunogenicity: it enhances JAK1/[[stat1|STAT1]] signaling and upregulates components of [[hla|HLA]] class I antigen processing and presentation machinery while simultaneously dampening tumoral expression of the immune checkpoint ligand [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] and the immunosuppressive enzyme [[ido1|IDO1]].² This multi-target rewiring improves recognition of melanoma cells by autologous tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) specific for the [[mart-1|MART-1]] epitope and tumor neoantigens, resulting in marked enhancement of CD8+ T cell proliferation and cytotoxic activity. The synergy between [[jq1|BET inhibition]] and JAK1/[[stat1|STAT1]]-amplified interferon signaling reveals that epigenetic manipulation can reshape tumor immunogenicity by broadly engaging interferon-responsive transcriptional networks, demonstrating the therapeutic potential of combining epigenetic and immune-checkpoint targeting.

## Therapeutic targeting across diseases

Selective JAK1 inhibitors have demonstrated clinical efficacy across multiple disease contexts by suppressing pathogenic cytokine-driven signaling. [[brepocitinib]], a first-in-class, oral, selective TYK2-JAK1 inhibitor, blocks cytokine signaling implicated in dermatomyositis pathogenesis, where type I interferon overproduction and IL-6-driven inflammation drive skin and muscle autoimmunity.³ [[ruxolitinib]], a JAK1/JAK2 inhibitor, effectively treats myeloproliferative neoplasms by suppressing the hyperactivated JAK-STAT axis driven by driver mutations in JAK2, CALR, or MPL. In a prospective, real-world study of polycythaemia vera patients treated with ruxolitinib, treatment-naive patients achieved mean hematocrit reductions of 6.2% compared to only 1.6% in previously JAK-inhibitor-treated patients, demonstrating that JAK1 inhibition is most efficacious before pathway rewiring and treatment resistance develop.⁴ Similarly, treatment-naive patients showed mean leukocyte reductions of 4.5 × 10³/µL compared to 1.4 × 10³/µL in pretreated patients, and thrombocyte reductions of 80.5 × 10³/µL versus 42.6 × 10³/µL, underscoring that early intervention with selective JAK1 inhibitors achieves superior disease control across multiple hematopoietic parameters. These clinical outcomes validate JAK1's universal role as a master regulator of pathogenic cytokine signaling across immune, hematopoietic, and inflammatory disease contexts.
