**Heat shock protein 90** (HSP90) is a ubiquitous molecular chaperone essential for protein homeostasis and tumor cell survival that has emerged as a major therapeutic target across multiple cancer types. HSP90 stabilizes numerous client proteins critical for malignant transformation, making its pharmacological inhibition a potent strategy for triggering oncogenic protein degradation. Clinical HSP90 inhibitors including [[pimitespib]], [[hypericin-sodium]], and [[wp-1303]] are approved or in late-stage development, with their efficacy enhanced through integration with tumor-selective [[protac]]-based protein degradation platforms and diagnostic imaging for patient stratification.¹ Recent discoveries have identified [[tp53]]-mutant [[aml]] with ribosomal protein gene loss as an exceptionally vulnerable subtype exhibiting synthetic lethal sensitivity to HSP90 inhibition, revealing new therapeutic opportunities for treatment-refractory hematologic malignancies.³

---

## Mechanism

HSP90 functions as a critical molecular chaperone regulating protein homeostasis by stabilizing and refolding client proteins essential for tumor cell survival and proliferation. Pharmacological inhibition of HSP90 destabilizes these client proteins, triggering their proteasomal degradation and subsequent apoptosis. The clinical utility of HSP90 inhibitors demonstrates direct correlation between tumor HSP90 expression levels and treatment efficacy, necessitating diagnostic tools for subtype-specific detection and patient stratification.

---

## Cancer applications

HSP90 inhibitors have achieved significant clinical progress across multiple solid and hematologic malignancies. [[Pimitespib]] has received regulatory approval in Japan for intestinal tumors, while [[hypericin-sodium]] is currently under US regulatory review and [[wp-1303]] is in Phase III development.¹ The broad therapeutic applicability of HSP90 inhibition extends across colorectal cancer, gastric cancer, and melanoma, with pharmacological optimization strategies enhancing tumor-selective uptake while reducing off-target organ accumulation.¹

---

## PROTAC synergy and tumor-selective degradation

A transformative advance in HSP90-directed therapy is the development of tumor-selective protein degradation platforms leveraging HSP90 as a bioorthogonal trigger. The HSP90-dependent bioorthogonal [[protac]] prodrug system (HBPROTAC) combines [[tz-pu]], a tetrazine-conjugated HSP90 inhibitor engineered for preferential tumor accumulation, with TCO-caged [[protac]] prodrugs that remain inert in circulation.² These caged prodrugs undergo inverse electron demand Diels-Alder cycloaddition reactions with Tz-PU within tumor tissue, releasing active PROTACs—including MZ1 and DT2216—that degrade oncogenic proteins such as [[brd4]] and [[bcl-xl]].² HSP90 inhibition by Tz-PU simultaneously enhances the degradation efficiency of target proteins through HSP90-mediated signaling pathways, achieving tumor-specific activation of protein degradation while minimizing off-target toxicity in normal tissues.² This platform has been validated in melanoma mouse models, establishing a broadly applicable strategy for spatiotemporal control of targeted protein degradation with substantially reduced systemic on-target toxicity.

## Diagnostic imaging and patient stratification

HSP90-targeted diagnostic imaging is critical for predicting treatment response and optimizing patient selection for HSP90 inhibitor therapy. Recent development of HSP90-targeted radiotracers, optimized through chemical modification, achieve high tumor uptake in colorectal and gastric cancer models—measuring 10.02 ± 2.05% and 5.02 ± 0.08% injected dose per gram, respectively—while minimizing accumulation in kidneys and liver (~2% and ~5% ID/g).¹ These tracers produce exceptional tumor-to-muscle contrast ratios exceeding 23:1, enabling high-resolution tumor delineation and comprehensive cancer staging and monitoring.¹ Clinical evaluation confirms robust translational potential, with standardized uptake values reaching approximately 5 at peak tumor concentration, thereby enabling accurate identification of HSP90-dependent tumors and rational selection of patients for HSP90 inhibitor therapy.

## TP53-mutant AML with ribosomal protein loss: a vulnerable subtype

Comprehensive genomic characterization of acute myeloid leukemia has uncovered a particularly vulnerable subtype: [[tp53]]-mutant [[aml]] bearing recurrent deletions encompassing ribosomal protein genes on chromosomes 3p and 5q. These deletions—notably del(3p) and del(5q)—occur in more than 20% of TP53-mutated AML cases and trigger global downregulation of the ribosomal network with markedly impaired protein synthesis.³ This ribosomopathy-like phenotype creates a synthetic lethal vulnerability to HSP90 inhibition, as the compromised translational capacity renders leukemic cells acutely dependent on HSP90-mediated protein quality control mechanisms.³ Chemical screening identified HSP90 inhibition as a selective therapeutic vulnerability in AML with low ribosomal protein gene expression, suggesting that translational stress may serve as a critical therapeutic entry point for this highly refractory patient population.³
