**Doxorubicin** is a first-line cytostatic chemotherapeutic agent widely employed in the treatment of solid tumors, including hepatocellular carcinoma and breast cancers. An anthracycline antibiotic, it exerts its cytotoxic effects through DNA intercalation and topoisomerase II inhibition, leading to double-strand breaks and apoptosis in rapidly dividing cells. Despite its clinical efficacy, doxorubicin's utility is constrained by dose-limiting cardiotoxicity, systemic side effects, inadequate intratumoral penetration, and burst release from conventional formulations.¹ Contemporary research has therefore focused on advanced nanotechnology-based delivery platforms—including [[ncd-hydrogel|thermosensitive acid-labile hydrogels]], [[liposomal-carriers|liposomal formulations]], [[sodium-alginate-sulfate-microspheres|alginate sulfate microspheres]], and stimuli-responsive nanoparticles—to achieve sustained, controlled release and improved drug distribution within the tumor microenvironment.

---

## Clinical challenges and delivery bottlenecks

The therapeutic window of doxorubicin is narrow, with systemic administration often resulting in significant off-target toxicity and subtherapeutic intratumoral concentrations. Conventional liposomal formulations achieve only modest tumor accumulation—approximately 4% injected dose per gram of tumor tissue after 24 hours—with limited extravascular penetration.² Although the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect has long been invoked to justify nanomedicine delivery, quantitative reassessment using second near-infrared aggregation-caused quenching probes reveals that microdistribution within the tumor interstitium is a more meaningful predictor of therapeutic efficacy than total macroscopic accumulation. Doxorubicin readily dissociates from liposomal carriers and remains sequestered in perivascular regions, failing to reach distant cancer cell populations.² Furthermore, in hepatocellular carcinoma—a disease characterized by abnormal tumor vasculature, elevated interstitial fluid pressure, and dense extracellular matrix—drug delivery is particularly compromised, necessitating alternative locoregional strategies.³

## Transarterial chemoembolization and sustained-release platforms

Transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) has emerged as a critical therapeutic modality for hepatocellular carcinoma, enabling precise locoregional drug delivery while minimizing systemic exposure. [[Sodium-alginate-sulfate-microspheres|Sodium alginate sulfate microspheres]] represent a major advance in TACE technology, offering superior drug-loading capacity (68.0 mg/g for irinotecan, 30-minute loading time for doxorubicin) and sustained release exceeding 30 days.⁴ These microspheres exploit multiple drug-loading groups within the alginate sulfate framework and exhibit self-expanding behavior due to a gradient cross-linked structure formed at the aqueous-oil interface. In porcine renal artery embolization models, the microspheres stabilize the embolized artery and induce tissue necrosis without vascular recanalization or ectopic embolization over 90 days.⁴

Percutaneous locoregional injection of thermosensitive hydrogels offers an alternative strategy for bridging liver transplantation in HCC patients. The [[ncd-hydrogel|NCD hydrogel]], incorporating iron oxide nanoparticles and doxorubicin into an ortho-ester-functionalized poly(N-isopropylacrylamide)-polyethylene glycol matrix, achieves sustained release of 77.4% over 72 hours at tumor pH 6.5, compared to only 26.8% from control formulations.¹ The acid-cleavable ortho-ester backbone enables pH-responsive drug liberation in the acidic tumor microenvironment, while the thermosensitive lower critical solution temperature of approximately 32°C allows sol-gel transition at body temperature, facilitating injection with minimal force (6.0 N maximum pressure) and forming a drug depot with prolonged retention.¹ Combined with magnetothermal therapy via embedded iron oxide nanoparticles, ultrasound-guided NCD delivery suppresses orthotopic liver tumor growth and extends the window for transplantation eligibility.

## Overcoming anti-PEG immunity in liposomal formulations

PEGylated liposomal doxorubicin (Lipodox/Doxil) has achieved widespread clinical adoption due to prolonged circulation and reduced cardiotoxicity. However, the emergence of anti-polyethylene glycol (PEG) antibodies—triggered by prior exposure to PEGylated products or environmental PEG—drives accelerated blood clearance and potentially fatal infusion reactions, severely limiting repeat dosing and combination regimens.⁵ Rational polymer architecture tuning offers a solution: short-chain, high-density PEG brushes (PEG500) grafted onto rigid mesoporous silica nanoparticles at 4.43 chains per nm² provide a steric barrier that minimizes epitope accessibility and prevents PEG backfolding.⁵ Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assays demonstrate near-background binding of anti-PEG IgG and IgM antibodies to these platforms, in stark contrast to the robust recognition of PEG2000-based Lipodox. In anti-PEG-immunized mice, doxorubicin-loaded mesoporous silica nanoparticles with dense PEG500 brushes preserve antitumor efficacy and achieve 100% survival, whereas conventional Lipodox induces fatal hypersensitivity mediated by complement activation.⁵

## Enhancing penetration via mechanical modulation and cavitation

The abnormal mechanical microenvironment of solid tumors—characterized by activated cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and dense extracellular matrix—forms an impenetrable barrier that excludes nanomedicines from reaching malignant cells. [[pfd-tk-cy|PFD-TK-Cy]] nanoparticles integrate biochemical CAF inhibition with physical ECM relaxation through a dual-action mechanism.⁶ Upon CAF-specific uptake, elevated intracellular reactive oxygen species trigger thioketal linker cleavage, releasing modified pirfenidone to suppress pro-fibrotic signaling and a cyanine-based photosensitizer to generate localized mild hyperthermia (approximately 43°C) for matrix softening. This spatiotemporally coordinated disruption of the CAF-ECM alliance reduces solid stress, improves blood perfusion, and enhances intratumoral delivery of Doxil in stroma-rich orthotopic breast cancers.⁶

[[Ultrasound-cavitation|Ultrasound cavitation therapy]] with FDA-approved [[microbubbles|microbubbles]] offers a complementary physical approach to enhance vascular permeability and drug delivery. In hepatocellular carcinoma models, treatment at moderate acoustic pressure (1.6 MPa) induces transient tumor perfusion loss, reduces interstitial fluid pressure, and significantly increases doxorubicin extravasation without causing tissue damage or hemorrhage.³ Excessive pressure (2.2 MPa) paradoxically impairs delivery by causing prolonged perfusion loss that prevents further drug transport, underscoring the importance of calibrated cavitation protocols.³

## Combination strategies and programmable multi-drug release

Resistance to doxorubicin monotherapy has motivated the development of combination regimens that exploit complementary mechanisms of action. [[Injectable-modular-microgel|Injectable modular microgels]] enable independent and programmable release of multiple drugs from a single formulation by combining distinct microsphere modules—gelatin methacrylate for hydrophilic agents and alginate for hydrophobic compounds.⁷ Sequential delivery of carboplatin followed by the ferroptosis inducer RSL3 overcomes chemotherapy resistance and extends survival by 10 days compared to conventional hydrogels, while controlled co-release of doxorubicin and paclitaxel reduces tumor mass 3.53-fold versus traditional monolithic systems.⁷ The platform's tunable release profiles, achieved by modulating matrix concentrations, accommodate agents ranging from small molecules to large proteins.

Conjugation of doxorubicin with natural [[chlorin-e6|chlorin e6]] photosensitizers creates dual-action agents combining photodynamic and chemotherapeutic modalities.⁸ These conjugates incorporate stimuli-responsive linkers—disulfide and thioketal fragments sensitive to reductive and oxidative conditions, respectively—to enable controlled intracellular drug release. In human breast adenocarcinoma MCF-7 cells, the conjugates internalize efficiently, exhibit both photo-induced and dark cytotoxicity, and liberate doxorubicin that accumulates in nuclei following linker cleavage.⁸

## Prodrug activation and organocatalytic nanozymes

[[Octa-amino-silsesquioxanes|Octa-amino silsesquioxanes]] function as metal-free nanozymes with aldolase-like organocatalytic activity, enabling intracellular prodrug activation via retro-aldol reactions.⁹ These ultrasmall, water-stable silsesquioxane frameworks exhibit stimuli-responsive catalysis modulated by reversible supramolecular aggregation and disaggregation. In human glioblastoma and metastatic melanoma cells, the nanozymes efficiently internalize and activate a doxorubicin prodrug, resulting in selective cytotoxicity without systemic activation.⁹ This cell-specific activation strategy minimizes off-target toxicity and represents a scalable, cost-effective platform bridging abiotic catalysis and biological function.
