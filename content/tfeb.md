**TFEB** (transcription factor EB) is a master transcriptional regulator controlling autophagy, lysosomal biogenesis, and innate immune function.¹ Its activity is tightly suppressed by [[mtorc1|MTORC1]]-mediated phosphorylation, which promotes cytoplasmic sequestration and blocks nuclear translocation, preventing transcriptional activity.¹ Nuclear entry of TFEB is additionally regulated by the [[pex5|peroxisomal import receptor PEX5]], which coordinates autophagy with metabolic homeostasis through taurine biosynthesis feedback loops.² TFEB activation by [[rar-alpha|retinoic acid signaling]] in tumor-associated macrophages drives [[mhc-class-ii|MHC-II]] expression and antigen presentation, creating a potent synergy with immune checkpoint blockade.³

---

## Mechanism

TFEB enters the nucleus only when dephosphorylated, allowing it to activate the transcription of autophagy and lysosomal genes. [[mtorc1|MTORC1]] suppresses this process by directly phosphorylating TFEB, a modification that promotes its binding to 14-3-3 proteins and cytoplasmic retention.¹ The [[flcn|FLCN]]-[[fnip1|FNIP1]]/FNIP2 complex acts as a negative regulator of MTORC1-mediated TFEB phosphorylation; inhibition of this complex paradoxically enhances TFEB nuclear translocation and activates its transcriptional programs.¹ Importantly, selective inhibition of MTORC1-mediated TFEB phosphorylation can be achieved without broadly suppressing MTORC1 canonical substrates, offering a therapeutic window in metabolic diseases.¹

Nuclear translocation of TFEB is also controlled through the [[pex5|PEX5]]-[[p38-mapk|p38 MAPK]] axis.² Under stress or senescence conditions, reduced PEX5 expression unleashes p38 MAPK signaling, which phosphorylates TFEB and impairs its nuclear accumulation, thereby suppressing autophagy.² Conversely, PEX5 overexpression restores TFEB nuclear translocation and enhances taurine biosynthesis through peroxisomal enzyme localization, establishing a positive feedback mechanism that sustains autophagy and resilience against senescent states.²

## MASH therapeutics

In metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), selective MTORC1-mediated TFEB inhibition emerges as a disease-modifying strategy. Byakangelicin, a natural compound, directly binds the [[flcn|FLCN]] protein at residues MET370 and PHE552, suppressing the FLCN-FNIP1/FNIP2 complex and thus preventing MTORC1-catalyzed TFEB phosphorylation.¹ In mouse models, this selective disinhibition of TFEB alleviates hepatic steatosis, reduces inflammatory markers, improves insulin sensitivity, and attenuates fibrotic remodeling.¹ Genetic deletion of hepatic Tfeb abolished byakangelicin's protective effects, while TFEB reintroduction restored disease resolution, establishing TFEB as the essential mediator of therapeutic benefit.¹ This selectivity—targeting TFEB phosphorylation without affecting other MTORC1 substrates—minimizes off-target side effects inherent to broad MTORC1 inhibitors.

## Senescence and peroxisomal regulation

During aging and replicative stress, [[pex5|PEX5]] expression declines in lung fibroblasts, triggering a cascade that impairs TFEB nuclear entry and autophagy.² Mechanistically, PEX5 loss activates [[p38-mapk|p38 MAPK]], which suppresses TFEB translocation to the nucleus and reduces autophagic flux.² In parallel, loss of PEX5 impairs peroxisomal taurine biosynthesis, depriving cells of this essential osmolyte and antioxidant.² Strikingly, exogenous taurine supplementation restores PEX5 levels in senescent cells and aged lungs through a positive feedback mechanism, simultaneously restoring autophagy and mitigating pro-senescent phenotypes.² This PEX5-taurine-TFEB axis represents a targetable pathway for age-related lung disease, where peroxisomal dysfunction underlies cellular senescence.

## Tumor immunity and ferroptosis

Ferroptotic tumor cells release all-trans retinoic acid (ATRA), which activates [[rar-alpha|retinoic acid receptor α]] (RAR-α) on tumor-associated macrophages, directly triggering TFEB nuclear translocation.³ Once activated, TFEB controls [[mhc-class-ii|MHC-II]] expression and drives autophagy-dependent antigen processing, enhancing the efficiency of antigen presentation to CD4+ T cells.³ Ferroptosis-mediated TFEB activation in macrophages is further amplified by activated immune cells, which promote additional ferroptotic tumor cell death—a positive feedback loop that potentiates anti-tumor immunity.³ Clinically, tumors exhibiting high ferroptosis gene signatures correlate with improved immunotherapy response, and combination of ferroptosis-inducing nanotherapies with [[anti-pd-1-antibody|anti-PD-1 antibodies]] synergizes to enhance durable tumor control.³
