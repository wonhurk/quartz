**Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs)** are a class of gastrointestinal protectants used to suppress gastric acid production and prevent ulceration in patients requiring antiplatelet therapy. Despite their therapeutic benefit in reducing peptic ulcer disease, PPIs present a significant pharmacodynamic liability when coadministered with [[clopidogrel]], an antiplatelet agent widely prescribed in patients following ischemic stroke and acute coronary syndrome. PPIs inhibit cytochrome P450 2C19 (CYP2C19), the hepatic enzyme responsible for converting [[clopidogrel]] to its pharmacologically active metabolite, thereby attenuating its antiplatelet efficacy and elevating the risk of thromboembolic events.¹ Critically, the degree of CYP2C19 inhibition varies substantially across individual PPI agents, creating a spectrum of drug interaction potencies that influences clinical outcomes.²

---

## Mechanism of CYP2C19 inhibition

[[clopidogrel]] is a prodrug requiring hepatic metabolism to exert its antiplatelet effect; its conversion to the active thiol metabolite depends on CYP2C19-catalyzed oxidation followed by esterase hydrolysis. PPIs are potent inhibitors of CYP2C19, and their presence in the systemic circulation competitively inhibits the metabolism of [[clopidogrel]], leading to dose-dependent reductions in the plasma concentration of the active metabolite. The consequence is diminished P2Y12 receptor antagonism on platelet surfaces, resulting in impaired platelet aggregation inhibition and restoration of thrombotic capacity.

---

## Clinical coprescription and cardiovascular risk

Despite well-established pharmacodynamic interactions, PPIs remain frequently coprescribed with [[clopidogrel]] in the acute management of ischemic stroke, driven by the clinical imperative to prevent stress-related gastroprotection and ulcer complications. However, this therapeutic convenience carries substantial cardiovascular risk. Studies examining patients receiving concomitant [[clopidogrel]] and PPI therapy have documented increased incidence of ischemic events and major adverse cardiovascular outcomes compared to patients receiving [[clopidogrel]] alone or with alternative gastrointestinal protective agents.¹ The clinical dilemma reflects a fundamental tension between short-term gastrointestinal safety and long-term cardiovascular protection, requiring individualized risk-benefit assessment and careful agent selection.

---

## Inter-agent variability in CYP2C19 potency

A central therapeutic principle emerging from pharmacoepidemiological studies is that not all PPIs are equivalent in their degree of CYP2C19 inhibition. Proton pump inhibitors can be stratified into two broad categories based on their potency as CYP2C19 inhibitors: those demonstrating strong inhibitory effects and those with weak or negligible enzyme inhibition.² The strong CYP2C19-inhibiting PPIs produce substantial reductions in [[clopidogrel]] bioconversion and consistently elevate cardiovascular event risk when coadministered, whereas weak or non-inhibiting agents preserve greater [[clopidogrel]] efficacy and may be safer alternatives in high-risk populations. Multinational comparative studies have highlighted significant geographic and prescribing variations in PPI selection, underscoring the need for precision-based gastric protection strategies that account for individual CYP2C19 inhibitory profiles.²

---

## Alternative agents: Potassium-competitive acid blockers

[[p-cab|Potassium-competitive acid blockers (P-CABs)]] represent an emerging class of gastrointestinal protectants with a distinct mechanism of action and potentially reduced pharmacodynamic interaction with [[clopidogrel]]. Unlike conventional PPIs, which irreversibly inhibit the gastric proton pump via covalent binding, P-CABs competitively antagonize the K+-dependent ATPase activity of the pump, offering comparable acid suppression with potentially less hepatic enzyme inhibition. Preliminary clinical investigations have compared the safety and efficacy of P-CABs versus traditional PPIs when coadministered with [[clopidogrel]] in ischemic stroke populations, with findings suggesting that P-CABs may preserve superior [[clopidogrel]] antiplatelet activity and reduce ischemic event risk.¹ As evidence accumulates, P-CABs are increasingly being evaluated as preferred alternatives for gastric protection in patients requiring continuous antiplatelet therapy.
