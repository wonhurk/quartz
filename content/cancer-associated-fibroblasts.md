**Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs)** are activated stromal fibroblasts resident within the tumor microenvironment that establish dual physical and immunological barriers restricting antitumor immunity. These cells fundamentally reshape the tumor milieu by depositing and maintaining dense extracellular matrix, secreting pro-migratory and pro-tumoral factors including [[hepatocyte-growth-factor|HGF]], and activating oncogenic signaling pathways in cancer cells—particularly the [[pi3k|PI3K]] pathway.¹ CAFs display marked functional heterogeneity, with specialized subpopulations including INHBA+ CAFs that drive epithelial-mesenchymal transition and immune evasion through coordinated multi-omics reprogramming.² Acting as independent unfavorable adversaries for [[immune-checkpoint-inhibitors|immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)]] efficacy, CAFs represent a major therapeutic bottleneck across solid tumors including non-small cell lung cancer, lung squamous cell carcinoma, breast cancer, and colorectal cancer.

---

## Mechanism

CAFs exert their tumor-promoting effects through complementary physical and molecular mechanisms. The dense extracellular matrix that CAFs establish creates a formidable physical barrier that impedes the infiltration and activation of antitumor immune cells. Simultaneously, CAFs secrete multiple immunosuppressive mediators and activate indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1 (IDO1)-dependent tryptophan catabolism, depleting local tryptophan pools and generating immunosuppressive kynurenine metabolites that silence effector T cells.² The [[pi3k|PI3K]] pathway serves as a critical signaling hub in CAF-mediated pro-tumoral activity. CAFs activate PI3K signaling in cancer cells through secretion of growth factors and soluble mediators, promoting tumor cell motility and metastatic capacity, while concurrent PI3K activation within CAFs themselves drives their proliferation and trans-differentiation into more activated phenotypes that secrete higher levels of pro-metastatic factors.¹

---

## Epithelial-Mesenchymal Transition and Malignancy

A specialized subset of CAFs designated INHBA+ CAFs has emerged as a principal driver of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and tumor aggressiveness in colorectal cancer and likely other solid malignancies. These cells execute a coordinated program of EMT induction and immunosuppression through multi-omics mechanisms that reprogram cancer cells toward invasive, migratory phenotypes while simultaneously fostering immune evasion.² The INHBA+ CAF-mediated axis thus couples physical malignant transformation with immune suppression, creating a synergistic mechanism whereby dissemination is coupled to resistance against antitumor immunity.

---

## Immunotherapy Resistance and ICI Barriers

Spatial single-cell proteotyping of metastatic non-small cell lung cancer reveals that CAFs function as independent unfavorable adversaries for [[immune-checkpoint-inhibitors|ICI]] efficacy, complementing the immunosuppressive effects of tumor-associated macrophages and the physical barriers imposed by cancer cells themselves.¹ The multi-layered CAF-mediated barrier operates through two synergistic mechanisms: the dense extracellular matrix physically excludes immune cells from the tumor core, preventing direct physical contact between CD8+ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes and cancer cells—a contact essential for ICI efficacy—while simultaneous CAF-mediated immunosuppression limits the amplitude and duration of anti-tumor T cell responses. This dual barrier explains why CAF abundance and activation status predict poor ICI response across multiple tumor types.

---

## Therapeutic Targeting via CAF Normalization

[[Quercetin]]-based approaches have emerged as a leading strategy for CAF normalization. Carrier-free nanoparticles coordinating manganese ions with quercetin and the IDO1 inhibitor NLG919 selectively normalize CAF phenotype and reduce extracellular matrix deposition, while simultaneously triggering potent STING pathway activation through manganese-catalyzed hydroxyl radical generation and disrupting the immunosuppressive tryptophan/kynurenine axis.² This coordinated tri-modal mechanism—CAF normalization, STING-mediated innate immunity activation, and tryptophan pathway blockade—synergistically overcomes both the physical and immunological CAF-mediated barriers. When combined with [[immune-checkpoint-inhibitors|anti-PD-L1 antibodies]], this nanoreshaping strategy achieves robust suppression of primary tumor growth and pulmonary metastases in preclinical models.

---

## PI3K-Targeted Approaches

The selective [[pi3k|PI3K]]α inhibitor CYH33 addresses CAF-mediated metastasis through dual targeting of intrinsic and CAF-mediated PI3K signaling in lung squamous cell carcinoma. CYH33 directly suppresses cancer cell motility by disrupting cytoskeletal dynamics, while concurrently decreasing CAF infiltration within primary tumors by blocking CAF proliferation and trans-differentiation. Critically, CYH33 suppresses CAF secretion of pro-migratory [[hepatocyte-growth-factor|HGF]] and other growth factors, thereby eliminating a key stromal mechanism of tumor cell migration.¹ The concurrent targeting of intrinsic cancer cell and CAF-mediated PI3K signaling appears essential for robust anti-metastatic efficacy, as CAF-derived paracrine activation of PI3K in cancer cells can compensate for direct PI3K inhibition within tumors cells alone.

---

## RNA Delivery to CAFs

Biomimetic lipid nanoparticles engineered with cancer cell membrane components achieve preferential targeting to cancer-associated fibroblasts by exploiting heterotypic cell-cell adhesion mechanisms inherent to the tumor microenvironment. These nanoparticles exhibit substantially higher affinity for CAFs than uncoated lipid nanoparticles, enabling efficient RNA delivery to a cell population previously challenging to target therapeutically.³ The heterotypic recognition between cancer cell membranes and CAF surface receptors provides a biologically grounded targeting strategy that complements small-molecule and antibody approaches, potentially enabling direct RNA interference or RNA-based immunotherapy targeting within the CAF compartment.
