**Janus Kinase (JAK)** — including [[jak1|JAK1]] and [[jak2|JAK2]] — is a non-receptor tyrosine kinase that serves as a critical molecular hub for signal transduction downstream of cytokine receptors, transducing inflammatory and hematopoietic signals through activation of [[stat|STAT]] and [[nfkb|NF-κB]] pathways. In rheumatoid arthritis, JAK hyperactivation by pro-inflammatory cytokines — including TNF-α, IL-1, and IL-6 — drives pathogenic synovial macrophage polarization and disrupts osteoblast-osteoclast balance, culminating in cartilage degradation and bone erosion.¹ Across pediatric minimally differentiated acute myeloid leukemia (AML-M0), activating JAK mutations occur in approximately 83% of cases, cooperating with [[runx1|RUNX1]] loss and [[ras|RAS]]/[[flt3|FLT3]] pathway alterations to establish stem cell-like gene expression programs and chemoresistance.²

---

## Mechanism

JAK proteins relay inflammatory signals from the cytoplasmic tails of cytokine receptors to intracellular substrates, most notably STAT transcription factors and NF-κB signaling complexes. Upon ligand engagement, JAK undergoes trans-autophosphorylation and phosphorylates tyrosine residues on both its receptor and downstream effectors, initiating a signaling cascade that promotes pro-inflammatory gene expression and immune cell differentiation. The pathway is activated acutely during innate immune responses to pathogens and chronic overactivation characterizes autoimmune diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis, where sustained JAK-STAT/NF-κB signaling perpetuates the inflammatory cycle. In the hematopoietic compartment, JAK mutations can activate receptor signaling independently of cytokine engagement, driving proliferative and survival signals in leukemic cells.

---

## Rheumatoid arthritis

In rheumatoid arthritis, synovial infiltration of pro-inflammatory M1-polarized macrophages drives JAK-STAT/NF-κB pathway hyperactivation in response to elevated TNF-α, IL-1, and IL-6.¹ This sustained signaling disrupts bone homeostasis by suppressing osteoblast differentiation and promoting osteoclast activation, resulting in progressive cartilage degradation and bone erosion characteristic of RA pathology. [[tofacitinib]], a selective JAK inhibitor, acts as a disease-modifying antirheumatic drug by suppressing the inflammatory cascade and promoting macrophage repolarization from the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype to the anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype.¹ This M1-to-M2 repolarization simultaneously resolves local inflammation and restores osteoblast function, creating a dual therapeutic effect. However, systemic JAK inhibition is associated with off-target toxicity and poor selective delivery to inflamed joints, limiting the long-term efficacy of free [[tofacitinib]].¹ Emerging nanotherapeutic approaches — such as macrophage-targeted delivery platforms coloaded with [[tofacitinib]] and osteoimmunomodulatory ions — aim to overcome these limitations by concentrating drug activity at sites of inflammation while minimizing systemic exposure.

## AML-M0 and chemoresistance

Pediatric minimally differentiated acute myeloid leukemia (AML-M0) is characterized by immature hematopoietic stem cell-like features and is molecularly defined by frequent loss-of-function alterations in [[runx1|RUNX1]] (26% of cases) coupled with activating mutations in signaling pathways, including JAK (83% of AML-M0 cases).² JAK mutations in AML-M0 cooperate with [[ras|RAS]] and [[flt3|FLT3]] pathway activation to establish an oncogenic signaling network that drives ligand-independent proliferation and suppresses differentiation. Notably, [[runx1|RUNX1]] disruption — which occurs in a significant fraction of JAK-mutant AML-M0 cases — enforces stem cell-like transcriptional programs characterized by global DNA hypermethylation and downregulation of genes encoding oxidative phosphorylation and ribosomal proteins.² This transcriptional configuration correlates with reduced in vitro sensitivity to multiple chemotherapy agents, including cytarabine and anthracyclines, establishing JAK-mediated chemoresistance as a major clinical obstacle.² The association between JAK mutations and [[runx1|RUNX1]] alterations predicts poor prognosis and highlights the need for precision therapeutic strategies that directly target JAK signaling or address the underlying stem cell-like phenotype through epigenetic modulation with hypomethylating agents.
