**Carboxymethyl chitosan (CMCS)** is a biocompatible polysaccharide derivative of [[chitosan]] that serves as an advanced matrix material for multi-modal therapeutic hydrogels combining oral and parenteral drug delivery with immunomodulation. Its chemical structure enables sequential responses to physiological cues—pH gradients in the gastrointestinal tract, temperature at the injection site, and enzymatic activity in the [[tumor-microenvironment]]—positioning CMCS as a versatile platform for precision medicine in cancer treatment, particularly [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer (CRC)]].¹

---

## Chemical composition and properties

CMCS is synthesized by chemical modification of chitosan, introducing carboxymethyl functional groups that enhance water solubility and biocompatibility while preserving the inherent immunomodulatory properties of the parent polysaccharide. The material's polymer backbone undergoes enzymatic degradation by lysozyme and other proteases present in the [[tumor-microenvironment]], enabling sustained and controlled release of co-loaded therapeutic agents.¹² When incorporated into composite hydrogel systems, CMCS participates in sequential cross-linking mechanisms—first through physical interactions with counter-ions and later through covalent chemical cross-linking catalyzed by genipin and other natural cross-linkers—to form high-strength double-network hydrogels with enhanced mechanical stability and prolonged residence time at the site of action.²

---

## Oral targeted delivery for colorectal cancer

In oral delivery applications, CMCS-based formulations leverage pH-dependent properties to enable precise targeting of the lower gastrointestinal tract. When integrated into gellan gum (GG) hydrogels, CMCS protects [[nano-prodrugs|OE/CMCS-based nano-prodrugs]] from gastric acid-induced degradation while facilitating progressive pH-triggered pore expansion in the alkaline environment of the small intestine and colon.¹ The dynamic release kinetics of this system ensure that therapeutic payloads reach their intended tissue site while minimizing systemic absorption and off-target toxicity. Beyond drug delivery, CMCS-based oral hydrogels alter the composition of the gastrointestinal microbiota, establishing a microenvironment conducive to enhanced therapeutic efficacy—a finding with implications for [[colorectal-cancer|CRC]] immunotherapy and overall treatment outcomes.¹

---

## Injectable thermoresponsive systems and tumor targeting

CMCS demonstrates thermoresponsive gelation behavior when combined with nanocomposite components and chemical cross-linkers, enabling in situ hydrogel formation at physiological temperature (37°C) upon parenteral injection. This property is exploited in advanced therapeutic systems where CMCS, β-glycerophosphate, and genipin are co-injected as a liquid formulation that rapidly polymerizes to form a stable depot at the tumor site.² The resulting double-network hydrogel retains a high local drug concentration while resisting premature leaching, creating a durable reservoir for long-term immunotherapy. Within the [[tumor-microenvironment]], enzymatic cleavage of the CMCS backbone sustains the gradual release of co-loaded immunotherapeutic agents, including photothermal effectors and reactive oxygen species generators, enabling synergistic mechanisms of tumor cell apoptosis and immune cell recruitment.²

---

## Immunomodulatory and microbiota effects

CMCS possesses intrinsic immunomodulatory properties that amplify therapeutic efficacy beyond passive drug delivery. The polysaccharide modulates immune feedback pathways to enhance T cell infiltration into tumors and promote repolarization of tumor-associated macrophages toward pro-inflammatory phenotypes favorable to tumor rejection.² Additionally, CMCS-based oral formulations alter gut microbiota composition—an effect complementary to the immunological activity of co-loaded nanoparticles and observable both in the context of microbiota-immune crosstalk and in enhanced intestinal absorption and transport of loaded [[nano-prodrugs]].¹ These dual effects—direct immunomodulation and microbiota-mediated enhancement—position CMCS as a functional rather than merely inert matrix component in precision oncology.
