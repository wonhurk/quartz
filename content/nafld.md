**Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)** is a spectrum of metabolic liver disease characterized by excessive hepatic lipid accumulation (steatosis) that can progress to nonalcoholic steatohepatitis ([[nash|NASH]]) with chronic inflammation and hepatocellular injury.¹ Two interconnected pathogenic mechanisms drive disease progression: dysregulation of hepatic lipid metabolism controlled by [[ctsg|cathepsin G (CTSG)]], and aberrant macrophage activation initiated by the [[cd1d]]-[[pira2]] signaling axis.¹²

---

## Mechanism

[[ctsg|Cathepsin G]] emerges as a critical regulator of hepatic lipid homeostasis in NAFLD. In steatotic livers and metabolic disease states, [[ctsg|CTSG]] expression is significantly elevated and positively correlates with disease severity in [[nash|NASH]] patients.² The protease drives hepatic lipid accumulation by upregulating key lipogenesis genes including [[acc|acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC)]] and [[scd1|stearoyl-CoA desaturase-1 (SCD1)]], while simultaneously suppressing genes required for lipid oxidation ([[ppar-alpha|PPARα]], [[lcad|LCAD]]) and lipoprotein export (MTTP).² This transcriptional reprogramming channels hepatic lipid flux toward accumulation, establishing the steatotic state. Mechanistically, [[ctsg|CTSG]] mediates these metabolic changes by suppressing [[akt|Akt]] signaling; importantly, [[akt|Akt]] activation directly alleviates [[ctsg|CTSG]]-induced lipid deposition and ameliorates disease progression.²

Progression from simple steatosis to [[nash|nonalcoholic steatohepatitis]] is driven by macrophage infiltration and activation. The [[cd1d]]-[[pira2]] signaling axis has been identified as a fundamental mechanism controlling this immune transition. [[cd1d|CD1d]] functions as a cell surface ligand for [[pira2|PIRA2]] (paired immunoglobulin-like receptor A2), and their direct interaction potently activates hepatic macrophages both in vitro and in vivo.¹ The engagement involves specific domains—the α1 and α2 domains of [[cd1d|CD1d]] and the D1 and D2 domains of [[pira2|PIRA2]]—which trigger [[fcr-gamma|FcRγ]]-ITAM tyrosine phosphorylation and recruit downstream inflammatory signaling cascades.¹

Genetic evidence demonstrates that disrupting the [[cd1d]]-[[pira2]] axis substantially ameliorates NAFLD phenotypes. [[pira2|Pira2]]-deficient mice exhibit significantly reduced hepatic inflammation and lipid accumulation compared to wild-type littermates.¹ Macrophage-specific [[pira2|Pira2]] conditional knockout mice similarly show impaired macrophage activation and reduced inflammatory cytokine production in vivo, confirming a macrophage-cell-autonomous requirement for [[pira2|PIRA2]] in this activation pathway.¹ In NAFLD patients, both [[cd1d|CD1d]] and [[lilra2|LILRA2]] protein levels are elevated; furthermore, [[cd1d|CD1d]] stimulation induces proinflammatory cytokine expression in human macrophages, which is attenuated by [[lilra2|LILRA2]] blockade.¹ These findings position the [[cd1d]]-[[pira2]] axis and [[lilra2|LILRA2]] as tractable therapeutic targets.

---

## Disease progression and pathogenic integration

The coupled dysregulation of hepatic lipid metabolism and macrophage-mediated inflammation establishes a pathogenic cycle in NAFLD. [[ctsg|CTSG]] elevation drives steatosis through enhanced lipogenesis and suppressed lipid catabolism, simultaneously creating a lipid-rich microenvironment that facilitates macrophage infiltration and [[cd1d]]-[[pira2]]-mediated activation. Once activated, hepatic macrophages produce inflammatory cytokines that reciprocally amplify [[ctsg|CTSG]] expression and further suppress [[akt|Akt]] signaling, perpetuating hepatic lipid accumulation and advancing disease toward fibrotic [[nash|NASH]].¹² This integrated model explains why [[nash|NASH]] patients exhibit concurrent elevation of both [[ctsg|CTSG]] and the [[cd1d]]-[[pira2]] pathway components, and why targeting either mechanism alone may be insufficient for complete disease control.

---

## Therapeutic implications

The mechanistic insights into NAFLD pathogenesis delineate multiple therapeutic strategies. Suppressing [[ctsg|CTSG]] expression or directly enhancing [[akt|Akt]] signaling could reduce hepatic lipid accumulation and slow disease progression. Blocking the [[cd1d]]-[[pira2]] interaction or targeting macrophage-specific [[pira2|PIRA2]] signaling offers a complementary approach to dampen hepatic inflammation and prevent transition to cirrhosis. The observation that [[lilra2|LILRA2]] blockade attenuates [[cd1d]]-induced cytokine expression in human macrophages suggests that [[lilra2|LILRA2]] antagonism represents a clinically tractable approach to selectively suppress pathogenic macrophage activation without broadly disabling innate immunity.¹
