**Poly(ADP-ribose) Polymerase (PARP)** is a nuclear enzyme essential for [[dna-damage-response|DNA damage response]] and apoptosis regulation in cancer cells, serving dual roles as a therapeutic target and an apoptotic biomarker. The cleavage of PARP by caspases represents a canonical hallmark of [[apoptosis|apoptotic cell death]], establishing PARP proteolysis as both a diagnostic indicator and a mechanistic confirmation of anticancer drug efficacy. [[parp-inhibitors|PARP inhibitors]] have emerged as a major therapeutic strategy exploiting synthetic lethality in tumors deficient in homologous recombination repair, particularly those harboring loss-of-function mutations in [[brca1|BRCA1]], [[brca2|BRCA2]], [[atm|ATM]], PALB2, and CHEK2.¹ By blocking poly(ADP-ribose) synthesis and impairing DNA repair, PARP inhibitors force DNA repair-deficient cancer cells into irreversible cell death while sparing proficient cells, positioning PARP as a critical nexus between DNA damage signaling, cell survival, and precision oncology.

---

## Mechanism

PARP catalyzes the synthesis of poly(ADP-ribose) polymers in response to DNA damage, functioning as a master orchestrator of DNA repair protein recruitment and activation at sites of genomic injury. This ADP-ribosylation process is essential for cellular defense against genotoxic stress, including double-strand breaks, single-strand breaks, and other DNA lesions. The catalytic inhibition of PARP creates a synthetic lethal context in cancer cells bearing mutations in homologous recombination repair genes: while PARP-proficient cells retain compensatory repair capacity, PARP-deficient repair cells cannot tolerate the accumulation of unrepaired DNA damage and succumb to cell death.¹ Beyond DNA repair inhibition, PARP activation participates directly in apoptotic signaling; when cancer cells are treated with apoptosis-inducing compounds, activated caspase-3 and caspase-7 cleave PARP at a canonical Asp-Glu-Val-Asp (DEVD) consensus site, releasing the catalytic domain and generating an 89 kDa N-terminal fragment that serves as a definitive marker of caspase activation.

## Pancreatic cancer and PARP inhibitor efficacy

PARP inhibitors have transformed the treatment landscape for advanced pancreatic cancer, particularly in precision-defined patient populations. The PARP inhibitor [[niraparib]] demonstrates substantial efficacy in platinum-sensitive pancreatic cancers harboring germline [[brca1|BRCA1]] or [[brca2|BRCA2]] pathogenic variants, representing a molecular-guided therapeutic approach.¹ The therapeutic window of PARP inhibition extends beyond classical BRCA mutations to include patients with pathogenic variants in [[atm|ATM]], PALB2, and CHEK2, reflecting the shared dependence of these genes on homologous recombination pathways and expanding the patient population eligible for PARP-directed therapy.¹ Current Phase II investigations are examining whether PARP inhibitors offer efficacy in broader pancreatic cancer populations beyond germline mutation carriers, potentially identifying additional genomic or phenotypic contexts—such as somatic mutations, epigenetic silencing, or functional repair deficiency—where PARP inhibition provides durable clinical benefit.¹

## Apoptosis induction and biomarking

PARP cleavage functions as a canonical biomarker of apoptotic execution in drug discovery and mechanistic studies, particularly in non-small-cell lung cancer and other malignancies. The homoisoflavonoid [[liriopein-c|liriopein C]], a natural product scaffold optimized through chemical synthesis, induces apoptosis in non-small-cell lung cancer cells with sub-micromolar potency; apoptosis is confirmed through Annexin V/7-AAD staining and characteristic PARP cleavage by activated caspases.² The synthetic (S)-enantiomer of liriopein C exhibits markedly superior cytotoxicity compared to the naturally occurring (R)-enantiomer, demonstrating that stereochemical refinement can substantially enhance apoptosis induction. This capacity to reliably trigger PARP proteolysis establishes liriopein C as a valuable lead compound for developing apoptosis-based anticancer agents, with translatable potential across malignancies dependent on caspase-driven cell death pathways.

---

## Immunotherapy synergy

PARP-targeting compounds engage the innate immune system beyond direct apoptosis induction, creating opportunities for combination immunotherapy. Treatment with [[liriopein-c|liriopein C]] enhances macrophage-mediated tumor cell clearance in vivo, substantially increasing the phagocytic uptake of cancer cells by F4/80⁺CD11b⁺ peritoneal macrophages.² This dual mechanism—simultaneous induction of apoptosis and activation of macrophage-mediated tumor recognition—positions PARP-targeted therapies as natural partners for [[immune-checkpoint-blockade|immune checkpoint blockade]] strategies. By synchronizing apoptotic cell death with enhanced innate immune surveillance, PARP inhibitors may overcome immune evasion mechanisms that limit the efficacy of single-agent immunotherapy, providing a mechanistic rationale for clinical evaluation of PARP-based combination approaches in immunotherapy-resistant malignancies.
