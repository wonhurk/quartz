**CCL2** (C-C motif chemokine ligand 2), also known as monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1), is a pleiotropic chemokine that orchestrates inflammation, tissue remodeling, and intercellular communication across multiple disease and tissue contexts. As a critical signaling hub, CCL2 engages diverse cell populations through the atypical chemokine receptor [[ackr1|ACKR1]] and classical chemokine receptors, coordinating immune polarization, angiogenesis, and extracellular matrix remodeling. Recent single-cell and radiogenomic studies have established CCL2 as a central mediator linking adipose tissue dysfunction, diabetic complications, obesity-associated inflammation, and tumor microenvironment composition, making it a pivotal nexus between metabolic disease and tissue remodeling.¹

---

## Mechanism

CCL2 functions as a communication node in tissue remodeling through a network of ligand-receptor interactions and exosome-mediated delivery. The chemokine is enriched in exosomes secreted by CCL2-expressing adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs), where it travels alongside functionally synergistic factors including [[tgfb1|TGFB1]] and [[il34|IL34]], establishing a multicellular signaling axis.¹ CCL2 activation of [[ackr1|ACKR1]], combined with TGFB1-TGFBR1 and IL34-CSF1R engagement, orchestrates cross-talk between fibroblasts, macrophages, and endothelial cells.¹ Downstream of ligand binding, CCL2 triggers the PI3K/AKT/mTOR/HIF-1α signaling cascade, which enhances angiogenic potential, endothelial cell proliferation, and tissue regenerative capacity in high-glucose and inflammatory microenvironments.¹

---

## Diabetic wound healing

CCL2 has emerged as a critical therapeutic factor in diabetic foot ulcers and wounds complicated by metabolic dysfunction. Single-cell RNA sequencing of human adipose tissue revealed a distinct, functionally specialized CCL2-expressing ADSC subpopulation that is significantly enriched in obese individuals.¹ This subpopulation exhibits elevated stemness markers, distinct metabolic profiles, and selective pathway enrichment in extracellular matrix organization and tissue development — characteristics that distinguish it from conventional ADSCs.¹ Exosomes derived from CCL2-positive ADSCs dramatically accelerated wound closure in diabetic mice compared with conventional ADSC exosomes, promoting angiogenesis, collagen deposition, and M2-macrophage polarization while simultaneously suppressing pro-inflammatory cytokine production.¹ In vitro, CCL2-enriched exosomes reversed the suppression of endothelial cell proliferation, migration, and tube formation induced by chronic hyperglycemia, indicating functional restoration of angiogenic capacity in diabetic conditions.¹ The [[ackr1|ACKR1]]-mediated signaling axis emerged as the primary driver of these therapeutic effects, positioning the CCL2-ACKR1 axis as a critical target for accelerating resolution of diabetic wounds.

---

## Obesity and systemic inflammation

Beyond its regenerative roles, CCL2 serves as a pro-inflammatory marker in obesity-associated metabolic dysfunction. In the obese state, CCL2 levels are markedly elevated in adipose tissue and serum, contributing to systemic inflammation and metabolic complications.² Therapeutic modulation of adipose tissue through targeted intervention of CCL2 and inflammatory pathways — achieved using novel hypoxia-responsive phototheranostic approaches — significantly reduced pro-inflammatory markers including CCL2 and [[il6|IL-6]] while increasing anti-inflammatory [[il10|IL-10]] levels.² These interventions resulted in substantial metabolic improvement, including reductions in serum cholesterol (49.1%), triglycerides (30.3%), and insulin (75.7%), alongside decreases in adipose tissue mass and increases in white-to-brown adipose tissue conversion.² These findings establish CCL2 as both a sensitive biomarker of obesity-associated inflammation and a targetable mediator for therapeutic intervention in metabolic diseases.

---

## Extracellular matrix remodeling in glioma

CCL2 functions as a hub gene coordinating extracellular matrix remodeling in glioma, acting as part of a seven-gene regulatory network alongside [[mmp2|MMP2]], [[mmp9|MMP9]], [[cxcl8|CXCL8]], [[timp1|TIMP1]], [[il6|IL-6]], and [[col1a2|COL1A2]].³ Through radiogenomic integration — combining MRI radiomics signatures with transcriptomic profiling across 891 glioma patients — CCL2 expression was identified as significantly associated with radiomics scores that reflect ECM remodeling intensity.³ This association enables non-invasive prediction of patient survival and quantification of ECM remodeling burden from MRI imaging alone, without the need for tissue biopsy.³ Experimental validation in orthotopic mouse glioma models confirmed that silencing core ECM-remodeling genes, including direct CCL2-network members, reduces radiomics scores and tumor infiltration, establishing CCL2 as a key molecular node linking imaging phenotypes to tumor biology and prognosis.³
