**Coronary atherosclerosis** is the progressive accumulation of lipid-rich plaques within the coronary arteries, representing a leading cause of cardiovascular morbidity and mortality. The pathophysiology involves multiple lipoprotein species with distinct and independent roles in plaque initiation, development, and destabilization.¹ Beyond classical low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, triglyceride-rich remnant lipoproteins and [[lipoprotein-a]], an inherited genetic risk factor, drive heterogeneous pathogenic pathways that predispose to coronary plaque formation and vulnerable plaque phenotypes.²

---

## Mechanism

Coronary atherosclerosis develops through a complex multifactorial process in which apolipoprotein B-containing lipoproteins accumulate within the coronary arterial intima, undergo oxidative modification, and trigger inflammatory cascades that drive lesion progression.¹ Among apoB-containing particles, triglyceride-rich remnant lipoproteins—characterized by elevated triglyceride content and reduced particle size—represent a particularly important pathogenic species. These remnants penetrate the arterial wall with high efficiency and are retained through interactions with arterial proteoglycans, contributing directly to plaque lipid burden and inflammatory activation. The production and clearance of remnant lipoproteins is regulated by [[apoc3]], an inhibitor of lipoprotein lipase, making the APOC3 pathway a tractable therapeutic target.

The fundamental heterogeneity of coronary atherosclerosis is further illustrated by the selective pathogenic properties of [[lipoprotein-a]], an inherited cardiovascular risk factor distinct from traditional lipid risk factors. Lipoprotein(a) shows direct preferential association with morphological and compositional features of vulnerable coronary plaques, including elevated lipid core size and reduced fibrous cap thickness—characteristics that predict acute coronary events and plaque rupture.²

---

## Remnant lipoproteins and APOC3 inhibition

Triglyceride-rich remnant cholesterol represents a significant and independent modifiable risk factor for coronary atherosclerosis. Olezarsen, an antisense oligonucleotide targeting [[apoc3]], achieves potent lowering of remnant cholesterol while demonstrating a favorable neutral effect on low-density lipoprotein cholesterol. In adults with moderate [[hypertriglyceridemia]], olezarsen reduces triglycerides by approximately 60% and remnant cholesterol by approximately 70%, while reducing apolipoprotein B by approximately 15%.¹ The Essence-TIMI 73b imaging trial directly investigated whether this pharmacological reduction of remnant lipoproteins favorably modifies coronary plaque burden, establishing APOC3 inhibition as a precision targeting approach for remnant-driven atherosclerosis.

---

## Lipoprotein(a) and vulnerable plaque phenotypes

[[Lipoprotein-a]] is a genetically inherited cardiovascular risk factor that operates independently of traditional lipid metabolism. Among asymptomatic primary prevention patients without prior coronary events, elevated lipoprotein(a) levels show selective association with vulnerable coronary plaque phenotypes characterized by larger lipid cores and thinner fibrous caps compared to stable atherosclerotic lesions.² Importantly, lipoprotein(a) demonstrates superior predictive value for identifying vulnerable plaque characteristics in comparison with established risk markers including [[ldl-particle-concentration]], [[high-sensitivity-crp]], and [[coronary-artery-calcium]], suggesting that genetic lipoprotein(a) burden captures pathogenic plaque processes distinct from traditional atherosclerotic risk factors.²
