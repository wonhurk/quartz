**Chronic diabetic wounds**, including [[diabetic-foot-ulcer|diabetic foot ulcers]], are characterized by severely delayed healing driven by excessive [[ros|reactive oxygen species (ROS)]], persistent inflammation, impaired [[angiogenesis]], neuropathy, bacterial infection, immune dysfunction, and dysfunctional extracellular matrix remodeling.¹²³ The pathological microenvironment disrupts normal wound healing cascades, creating a self-reinforcing cycle of oxidative stress and inflammatory signaling that prevents tissue regeneration. Current therapeutic strategies center on smart hydrogel platforms that respond to multiple biological signals—including pH, ROS, and glutathione—to deliver antibacterial agents, antioxidants, and pro-angiogenic factors in situ.¹³⁴ Cell-based approaches utilizing [[pdlscs|periodontal ligament stem cells (PDLSCs)]] and [[adipose-derived-stem-cells|adipose-derived stem cells (ADSCs)]], particularly CCL2-positive subpopulations, have emerged as powerful regenerative modalities that accelerate healing through [[macrophage-polarization|macrophage reprogramming]], enhanced vascularization, and exosome-mediated tissue communication.¹⁵

---

## Pathophysiology

The diabetic wound microenvironment is defined by a pathological imbalance in redox homeostasis, with chronic hyperglycemia driving overproduction of reactive oxygen species that overwhelm endogenous antioxidant systems.³⁶ This oxidative stress impairs fibroblast and [[endothelial-cells|endothelial cell]] function, suppresses angiogenic signaling pathways, and sustains pro-inflammatory macrophage phenotypes that perpetuate tissue damage rather than repair.¹⁵ Neuropathy compounds the problem by disrupting neuro-vascular coupling, further limiting the oxygen and nutrient supply necessary for wound closure.² Bacterial colonization is facilitated by compromised immune surveillance, with common pathogens such as *Staphylococcus aureus* and *Escherichia coli* establishing biofilms that resist conventional antimicrobial therapy.⁴ The extracellular matrix fails to undergo normal remodeling, with dysregulated collagen deposition leading to either deficient granulation tissue or excessive scarring through upregulation of profibrotic genes including YAP and En1.¹

## Smart hydrogel platforms

Advanced hydrogel systems have been engineered to sense and respond to the pathological signatures of diabetic wounds, enabling targeted therapeutic delivery without systemic exposure. The [[gt-pba-egcg-opu-tob-pdlscs-hydrogel|GT-PBA/EGCG/OPU/TOB-PDLSCs hydrogel]] exemplifies this strategy through dual pH/ROS-responsive degradation: dynamic Schiff base crosslinking between phenylboric acid-modified gelatin and oxidized pullulan forms an interpenetrating polymer network, while phenylborate ester linkages with epigallocatechin-3-gallate (EGCG) confer ROS-triggered release of both EGCG and tobramycin in response to elevated oxidative stress and acidic pH.¹ The [[fp-hydrogel|FP-hydrogel]] employs semiclosed cyclic hemiaminal structures synthesized from polyethylene glycol diamines and polyformaldehyde, releasing formaldehyde at controlled, sub-toxic concentrations under weakly acidic conditions (pH 6.5) to achieve antibacterial efficacy exceeding 99% against common wound pathogens while simultaneously scavenging ROS and modulating the inflammatory microenvironment.³ These platforms demonstrate mechanical strength, self-healing capability, and biodegradability essential for practical wound dressing applications, achieving sustained release over 10 days or more.⁴

## Cell-based regenerative approaches

Stem cell therapies leverage the paracrine and differentiation potential of progenitor populations to restore tissue function in the ischemic, inflammatory diabetic wound bed. Periodontal ligament stem cells encapsulated within hydrogel matrices maintain high viability and proliferative capacity while exerting multifaceted regenerative effects: they scavenge intracellular ROS, reprogram macrophages toward the pro-regenerative M2 phenotype, and enhance angiogenesis, resulting in significantly accelerated wound closure with reduced scar formation.¹ Single-cell RNA sequencing has identified a distinct CCL2-expressing subpopulation of adipose-derived stem cells enriched in obese individuals that exhibits elevated stemness markers, unique metabolic profiles, and preferential engagement with fibroblasts, macrophages, and endothelial cells through ligand-receptor interactions including CCL2-ACKR1, TGFB1-TGFBR1, and IL34-CSF1R.⁵ Exosomes secreted by these CCL2-positive ADSCs are enriched in CCL2, TGFB1, and IL34, and when applied to diabetic mouse wounds, they significantly outperform conventional exosomes in promoting angiogenesis, collagen deposition, and M2-macrophage polarization while reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine expression.⁵ Mechanistically, CCL2 carried by these exosomes activates the PI3K/AKT/mTOR/HIF-1α signaling axis in endothelial cells, reversing high-glucose-induced suppression of proliferation, migration, and tube formation.⁵

## Nanozyme and nanoparticle delivery systems

Cascade nanozyme platforms integrate enzymatic catalysis with controlled release to address the complex, multifactorial pathology of diabetic wounds. The [[cascade-nanozyme-hydrogel-dressing|NO/O₂-releasing cascade nanozyme hydrogel]] incorporates Cu/Mg-tannic acid nanozymes loaded with the nitric oxide donor BNN6 within a dopamine-modified gelatin and quaternary ammonium-lipoic acid-modified chitosan matrix, providing tissue adhesion and inherent antibacterial capability.² Near-infrared irradiation triggers simultaneous nitric oxide release and catalase-like decomposition of endogenous hydrogen peroxide for sustained oxygen generation, synergistically promoting macrophage polarization toward the M2 phenotype, enhancing endothelial cell proliferation and angiogenic gene expression, and facilitating neurite outgrowth to restore neuro-vascular coupling.² The [[sf-zns-hydrogel|SF/ZnS hydrogel]] employs pH-responsive zinc sulfide nanoparticles synthesized via green aqueous-phase hydrothermal methods, encapsulated within methacrylated silk fibroin to achieve controlled release of therapeutic hydrogen sulfide and zinc ions under acidic wound conditions.⁶ This system demonstrates potent ROS-scavenging capacity, restores fibroblast and endothelial cell function under oxidative stress, and promotes macrophage polarization toward the reparative M2 phenotype, significantly accelerating wound closure and enhancing re-epithelialization in streptozotocin-induced diabetic mouse models.⁶

## Natural product therapeutics

[[glabridin|Glabridin]], a natural product with wound-repair potential but limited by poor solubility and photostability, has been successfully formulated within a cyclodextrin-based deep eutectic solvent-constructed chitosan eutectogel that addresses both pharmacokinetic challenges simultaneously.⁴ The deep eutectic solvent formed from sulfobutyl ether-β-cyclodextrin and levulinic acid acts as both solubilizer and dynamic crosslinker within the gel network, enabling sustained glabridin release for over 10 days while protecting it from photodegradation.⁴ The resulting flexible patch demonstrates greater than 99% antibacterial efficacy against *S. aureus* and *E. coli* through disruption of bacterial cell membrane integrity, and in infected diabetic wound models achieves 94.3% wound closure by day 21 by ameliorating the immune microenvironment, scavenging reactive oxygen species, promoting angiogenesis, and supporting tissue regeneration.⁴
