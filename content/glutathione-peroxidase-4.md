**Glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4)** is a glutathione-dependent antioxidant enzyme that serves as the central molecular brake against [[ferroptosis]], catalyzing the reduction of lipid and hydrogen peroxides to prevent iron-catalyzed lipid peroxidation and ferroptotic cell death.¹ Its anti-ferroptotic function is sustained through deubiquitination by [[usp20|USP20]], which prevents proteasomal degradation and sustains cellular antioxidant capacity, particularly in cancer cells adapting to therapy-induced oxidative stress.² GPX4 operates as a core node within the [[nrf2]]/[[ho-1]]/GPX4/[[sod1]] antioxidant pathway and functions collaboratively with [[prdx6]] and [[sephs2]] to enhance cellular redox homeostasis.

---

## Mechanism of ferroptosis suppression

GPX4 executes its anti-ferroptotic function by catalyzing the glutathione-dependent reduction of lipid hydroperoxides generated through iron-catalyzed oxidation of polyunsaturated fatty acids in cell membranes. When GPX4 is deactivated—either through proteasomal degradation, direct inhibition, or enzymatic inactivation—intracellular glutathione becomes depleted and lipid peroxides accumulate unchecked, triggering the hallmark cellular rupture of ferroptotic death.¹ The enzyme's antioxidant activity is sustained by a pool of reduced glutathione (GSH), making GPX4 directly coupled to the cellular cystine/glutamate antiporter [[slc7a11|xCT (SLC7A11)]], which imports cystine for GSH synthesis. Inhibition of either GPX4 or xCT/SLC7A11 depletes the GSH pool and rapidly precipitates ferroptosis.

## Cancer drug resistance and GPX4 stabilization

The [[usp20|deubiquitinating enzyme USP20]] has emerged as a critical regulator of GPX4 abundance in cancer cells, selectively removing K48-linked polyubiquitin chains and preventing GPX4 degradation. In tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI)-resistant renal cell carcinoma and lung cancer, upregulation of USP20 sustains GPX4 protein levels despite oxidative stress, allowing cancer cells to maintain antioxidant capacity and evade TKI-induced ferroptosis.² This USP20-GPX4 axis correlates clinically with poor prognosis in both malignancies; patients with co-overexpression of USP20 and GPX4 exhibit acquired resistance to sunitinib and sorafenib. Genetic ablation of USP20 or pharmacological inhibition of the deubiquitinase resensitizes TKI-resistant tumors to ferroptotic death, establishing this protein-interaction network as a druggable target for overcoming metabolic resistance.²

## Antioxidant pathway integration

GPX4 operates as a central node within the broader [[nrf2]]/[[ho-1]]/GPX4/[[sod1]] antioxidant pathway, which collectively alleviates oxidative stress and suppresses apoptosis in multiple tissues. In the kidney, for example, targeted delivery of cerium dioxide nanoparticles to injured tissue activates this pathway, restoring GPX4 activity alongside heme oxygenase-1 ([[ho-1]]) and superoxide dismutase ([[sod1]]), thereby reducing ischemia-reperfusion injury and improving renal function.³ The pathway also incorporates [[prdx6|peroxiredoxin 6 (PRDX6)]] and [[sephs2|selenophosphate synthetase 2 (SEPHS2)]], which strengthen GPX4 enzymatic activity through direct protein-protein interactions and enhance the cellular capacity to neutralize lipid and hydrogen peroxides.⁴

---

## Ferroptosis-immunotherapy synergy in triple-negative breast cancer

In triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), direct inactivation of GPX4 by platinum-based prodrugs targeting thioredoxin reductase (TrxR) simultaneously triggers ferroptosis and immunogenic cell death. The [[complex-6b|platinum compound complex 6b]] deactivates GPX4 through thioredoxin inhibition, depleting glutathione and accumulating intracellular lipid peroxides to induce ferroptotic cell death. Concurrently, this process generates excessive reactive oxygen species and endoplasmic reticulum stress, which activate downstream immunogenic cell death pathways that recruit dendritic cells and prime CD8+ T cell responses.¹ This dual mechanism overcomes cisplatin resistance in both sensitive and resistant TNBC cell lines, offering a chemoimmunotherapeutic strategy in malignancies deficient in conventional chemotherapy targets.

---

## Nanoparticle-mediated GPX4 modulation in infection

[[selenium-nanoparticles|Selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs)]] represent an alternative therapeutic approach targeting GPX4, upregulating its expression and activity to suppress ferroptosis in infectious contexts. In Klebsiella pneumoniae-induced mastitis, selenium nanoparticle supplementation restores mammary tissue GPX4 levels and strengthens the PRDX6-SEPHS2 protein complex, which cooperatively enhance antioxidant capacity.⁴ Concurrently, SeNPs suppress ferroptosis by inhibiting NCOA4-mediated ferritinophagy, reducing iron overload and lipid peroxidation, thereby breaking the pathological cycle of infection-driven ferroptosis that compromises innate immune function. This ferroptosis-suppressive strategy contrasts with ferroptosis-inducing therapeutic approaches in cancer, highlighting GPX4 as a context-dependent therapeutic target whose manipulation must be tailored to disease pathophysiology.

---

## Non-cancer disease applications

Beyond oncology, GPX4 dysfunction contributes to ferroptosis-driven pathology in acute kidney injury (AKI), where oxidative stress and renal tubular epithelial cell ferroptosis underlie ischemia-reperfusion injury. Restoring GPX4 activity through targeted nanoplatforms that activate the [[nrf2]]/[[ho-1]] signaling axis improves renal function and reduces inflammatory infiltration.³ In infectious diseases, bacterial mastitis-induced ferroptosis is suppressed through GPX4 upregulation, highlighting the importance of this enzyme across diverse pathological contexts. The therapeutic principle remains consistent: maintaining or restoring GPX4 activity suppresses ferroptosis and protects tissue function.
