**Wound healing** is a complex physiological process of tissue repair and regeneration following injury, involving the coordinated migration and proliferation of [[keratinocyte]]s and [[fibroblast]]s, modulation of [[macrophage]] polarization, and restoration of tissue integrity.¹ The process unfolds through overlapping phases of hemostasis, inflammation, proliferation, and remodeling, driven by growth factor signaling, immune cell recruitment, and the synthesis of extracellular matrix proteins including [[collagen]]. Contemporary therapeutic strategies leverage three complementary approaches: electroactive smart dressings that harness wound exudate chemistry for drug delivery and bioelectrical stimulation, natural marine-derived compounds that enhance cell motility through growth factor pathway modulation, and biocompatible nanoparticles engineered with natural biopolymers that provide antioxidant support and accelerate tissue regeneration.

---

## Mechanism

Wound healing progression involves the recruitment of [[macrophage]]s and immune cells during the inflammatory phase, which simultaneously clear debris and release growth factors that orchestrate the subsequent proliferative phase.¹² During proliferation, [[keratinocyte]]s migrate to re-epithelialize the wound bed—a process dependent on cytoskeletal remodeling, adhesion molecule expression, and growth factor signaling—while [[fibroblast]]s synthesize [[collagen]] and other matrix components to restore structural integrity.¹ The remodeling phase, extending over weeks to months, involves [[macrophage]] polarization, collagen cross-linking, and tissue maturation. Wound exudate, traditionally viewed as a pathological overflow, has emerged as a repository of biochemical signals and ions that can be strategically harnessed for therapeutic benefit.

## Electroactive dressing systems

A transformative approach combines salt-responsive polymers with yarn-based bioelectrical systems to create self-powered wound healing platforms that convert wound exudate into a therapeutic resource. These electroactive dressings integrate zwitterionic polymers (PVBIPS) grafted onto cotton yarns, embedded with the antibacterial agent [[carvacrol]], and woven with anode and cathode materials to form a self-powered electric fabric.¹ When the dressing contacts wound exudate, salt ions trigger dissociation of the PVBIPS polymer coating, enhancing moisture retention and enabling controlled release of [[carvacrol]]. Concurrently, discharge of the yarn-based battery generates [[magnesium-oxide-nanoparticles|Mg(OH)₂]], which synergistically amplifies the antimicrobial efficacy of [[carvacrol]].¹ The bioelectric field generated by the dressing directly promotes [[macrophage]] polarization toward pro-healing phenotypes and guides [[fibroblast]] migration and proliferation, thereby accelerating tissue regeneration. In both rat and porcine wound models, this integrated electromechanical and electrochemical system significantly enhanced healing outcomes, demonstrating that exudate-responsive technologies can repurpose wound chemistry into a therapeutic advantage.

## Marine-derived bioactive compounds

Natural bioactive compounds isolated from marine actinomycetes offer multi-target mechanisms for wound healing acceleration. [[monaprenylindole-a|Monaprenylindole A]], a prenylated indole derivative from a marine-derived [[streptomyces|Streptomyces]] species, enhances [[keratinocyte]] motility through coordinated modulation of cytoskeletal remodeling and growth factor signaling pathways.² Rather than engaging a single molecular target, this compound modulates multiple wound healing-associated gene networks, conferring a polyvalent therapeutic mechanism suited to the inherent complexity of tissue repair.² Pharmacokinetic analyses indicate favorable bioavailability and safety profiles. In vitro studies demonstrate robust enhancement of [[keratinocyte]] invasion and migration, with monaprenylindole A upregulating key genes involved in cell-cell adhesion, cytoskeletal remodeling, growth factor signaling, and spheroid formation.² This multi-target approach to activating pro-regenerative transcriptional programs provides a conceptually distinct strategy from single-pathway wound healing therapies.

## Biocompatible nanoparticle formulations

Green synthesis methodologies employing natural biopolymers have enabled development of sustainable, biocompatible nanoparticles for wound healing. Konjac glucomannan (KGM), a natural polysaccharide, serves as an effective capping and stabilizing agent for magnesium oxide nanoparticles, yielding uniformly spherical particles with an average diameter of 13.34 nanometers.³ The resulting KGM-stabilized [[magnesium-oxide-nanoparticles|MgO nanoparticles]] demonstrate moderate antioxidant activity and excellent hemocompatibility, remaining non-hemolytic at concentrations up to 100 μg/mL.³ In vivo wound healing experiments revealed substantial efficacy: a 1 wt% KGM-MgO nanoparticle formulation achieved 82% wound contraction by day 14, accompanied by accelerated [[collagen]] deposition and complete re-epithelialization.³ The nanoparticles exhibited no adverse effects on vascular architecture in angiogenesis models and caused no phytotoxic effects in plant toxicity assays, establishing KGM-MgO as a biocompatible, environmentally sustainable platform for wound healing and regenerative medicine applications.

## Integration and future directions

The three therapeutic strategies converge on shared mechanistic outcomes: acceleration of [[keratinocyte]] and [[fibroblast]] function, strategic modulation of immune responses through [[macrophage]] polarization, and enhanced tissue regeneration and re-epithelialization. The electroactive dressing approach uniquely exploits the chemical composition of wound exudate itself, transforming pathological overflow into therapeutic advantage. Marine-derived compounds provide multi-target cell motility enhancement without single-point-of-failure resistance mechanisms. Biocompatible nanoparticles offer sustained antioxidant support and biocompatible matrix scaffolding. Together, these complementary strategies exemplify a broader paradigm shift in wound healing research toward rationally designed multifunctional therapeutics and green synthesis methods that simultaneously advance clinical efficacy and environmental sustainability.
