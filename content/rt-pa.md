**Recombinant tissue plasminogen activator** (rt-PA, commercially [[alteplase]]) is a [[serine protease]] that catalyzes the degradation of [[fibrin]] to dissolve intravascular [[thrombus|blood clots]] in acute ischemic stroke and thromboembolic diseases.¹ As a standard thrombolytic agent, it converts plasminogen to plasmin, which then enzymatically cleaves the cross-linked fibrin polymer that comprises the structural scaffold of pathological clots and restores vascular perfusion.¹ However, clinical efficacy is constrained by a short plasma half-life, systemic off-target fibrinolysis, and insufficient penetration into fibrin-dense thrombus tissue.¹ Recent therapeutic innovations have focused on advanced delivery formulations and combination pharmacotherapies to enhance both safety and efficacy.

---

## Mechanism

rt-PA is a recombinant form of the natural [[serine protease]] tissue plasminogen activator.¹ The enzyme exhibits fibrin-selective activity, preferentially converting plasminogen to the broad-spectrum protease plasmin in the presence of [[fibrin]].¹ Plasmin then enzymatically cleaves the cross-linked fibrin polymer that forms the structural basis of pathological [[thrombus|thrombi]], leading to clot dissolution and restoration of vascular perfusion.¹ This fibrin-specific mechanism of action is a cornerstone of thrombolytic therapy, particularly in acute ischemic stroke where rapid clot lysis minimizes ischemic neuronal injury and preserves tissue viability. The standard dosing for stroke involves an initial intravenous bolus followed by a 90-minute infusion to achieve sustained fibrinolysis.

---

## Clinical applications

rt-PA is the standard intravenous thrombolytic agent for acute ischemic stroke, typically administered within a narrow therapeutic window of 4.5 hours from symptom onset.² The drug is also used to treat various thromboembolic conditions including deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism. The clinical benefit of rt-PA-based thrombolysis derives from rapid restoration of vascular perfusion, which directly correlates with reduced neurological disability and improved functional recovery. However, the short plasma half-life necessitates continuous infusion, and systemic activation of the fibrinolytic cascade can trigger unwanted bleeding complications, including symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage.

---

## Advanced delivery formulations

To overcome the inherent limitations of free rt-PA, researchers have developed [[chitosan-microcapsules|chitosan microcapsules]] (CSMs) engineered with fibrin-targeting CREKA peptides as an encapsulation system for rt-PA, designated as rt-PA@CSMs-CREKA.¹ This formulation employs a self-sacrificing template strategy: calcium carbonate microparticles preloaded with rt-PA are coated with water-soluble chitosan, triggering template degradation while preserving rt-PA within the resulting hollow microcapsule structure.¹ The microcapsules are then functionalized with the fibrin-homing CREKA peptide sequence to enable active targeting to [[thrombus|thrombus]] tissue.¹

rt-PA@CSMs-CREKA demonstrates several critical advantages over free rt-PA. The microscale size overcomes margination effects—the tendency of particles to concentrate near vessel walls—enabling more uniform drug distribution within the vasculature and superior penetration into fibrin-rich thrombus tissue.¹ The encapsulation format achieves high rt-PA loading efficiency and dramatically extends circulating drug half-life by shielding the protease from systemic clearance and proteolytic degradation.¹ In both in vitro and in vivo preclinical models, rt-PA@CSMs-CREKA exhibits enhanced fibrinolytic activity and more efficient [[thrombus]] dissolution compared to equimolar doses of free rt-PA.¹ Biocompatibility assessments confirm that chitosan microcapsules are well-tolerated with minimal systemic toxicity.¹

---

## Combination with antiplatelet agents

Clinical evidence demonstrates the efficacy of combining rt-PA ([[alteplase]]) with [[glenzocimab]], a humanized monoclonal antibody fragment targeting human platelet glycoprotein VI.² Glenzocimab inhibits platelet activation and aggregation through glycoprotein VI blockade while preserving hemostatic function, thereby reducing the bleeding risk associated with systemic fibrinolysis.² In the GREEN (Glenzocimab for REperfusion in the setting of Endovascular therapy for brain infarctioN) study, rt-PA combined with glenzocimab during endovascular intervention for acute ischemic stroke achieved significantly higher reperfusion rates compared to rt-PA monotherapy and reduced the incidence of symptomatic hemorrhagic transformation.² This synergistic approach enhances thrombolytic efficacy while mitigating hemorrhagic complications, representing an important advancement in acute stroke management.

---

## Modulation of coagulation factors

rt-PA@CSMs-CREKA formulations suppress key coagulation factors including [[fxa|Factor Xa (FXa)]] and [[pai-1|plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1)]].¹ Factor Xa is a central prothrombotic serine protease in the amplification phase of coagulation, while PAI-1 is the physiological inhibitor of tissue plasminogen activator activity and a major negative regulator of fibrinolysis.¹ Suppression of these factors by the encapsulated rt-PA formulation reduces residual thrombotic risk and may contribute to enhanced therapeutic efficacy in preventing clot reformation after thrombolytic intervention.
