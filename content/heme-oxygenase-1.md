**Heme Oxygenase-1** (HO-1) is a cytoprotective antioxidant enzyme that catalyzes heme degradation into biliverdin and carbon monoxide, thereby reducing intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) and protecting cells from oxidative stress-induced apoptosis. HO-1 functions as a central effector of the [[nrf2\|Nrf2]] signaling pathway, coordinating with the peroxidase [[gpx4\|GPX4]] and dismutase [[sod1\|SOD1]] to establish integrated redox homeostasis.¹ In cancer contexts, HO-1 activation via [[bmp4\|BMP4]]-containing exosomes from cancer-associated fibroblasts drives treatment resistance while simultaneously suppressing cuproptosis regulators; conversely, in kidney injury and vital pulp disease, the Nrf2-HO-1 axis alleviates oxidative damage and promotes tissue regeneration through coordinated antioxidant and anti-inflammatory signaling.

---

## Mechanism

HO-1 is transcriptionally activated through the [[nrf2\|Nrf2]] pathway in response to cellular oxidative stress or growth factor signaling.¹ Once upregulated, HO-1 catalyzes the first committed step of heme catabolism, generating biliverdin—a potent antioxidant metabolite—and carbon monoxide, both of which reduce intracellular ROS accumulation. The enzyme functions coordinately with the glutathione peroxidase [[gpx4\|GPX4]] and superoxide dismutase [[sod1\|SOD1]] within an integrated antioxidant defense system that simultaneously detoxifies lipid hydroperoxides and superoxide radicals.¹ This coordinated regulation of the Nrf2/HO-1/GPX4/SOD1 pathway is essential for protection against oxidative stress-induced apoptosis in epithelial and immune cells, establishing HO-1 as a central hub for cellular survival under pathological stress.

---

## Cancer treatment resistance

In cervical cancer, HO-1 upregulation drives radiotherapy resistance through a sophisticated intercellular communication mechanism mediated by cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs). CAF-derived exosomes (CAF-Exo) transfer [[bmp4\|BMP4]] as their principal molecular cargo to tumor cells, where it activates the Nrf2-HO-1 signaling axis upon cellular uptake.² Critically, the same exosomal signaling simultaneously suppresses key regulators of cuproptosis, a copper-dependent cell death pathway, thereby establishing a dual-protective mechanism that shields tumor cells from radiotherapy-induced oxidative stress and death. Functional studies demonstrate that exosomal [[bmp4\|BMP4]] knockdown effectively restores tumor radiosensitivity both in vitro and in vivo, indicating that the CAF-Exo/BMP4/Nrf2-HO-1/cuproptosis axis is essential for treatment resistance.² This exosome-mediated communication represents a previously unrecognized therapeutic target, as disrupting the [[bmp4\|BMP4]]-driven HO-1 activation pathway could enhance radiotherapy efficacy in cervical cancer.

## Non-cancer applications

Beyond oncology, HO-1 exerts critical protective roles in acute kidney injury and vital pulp therapy. In acute kidney injury (AKI) induced by ischemia-reperfusion, a targeted nanoplatform accumulates in injured kidneys through integrin αvβ3 receptor binding and activates the Nrf2/HO-1/GPX4/SOD1 pathway to suppress ROS overload, prevent apoptosis of renal tubular epithelial cells, and promote mitophagy-mediated mitochondrial quality control.¹ This coordinated activation reduces inflammatory infiltration and restores renal function in AKI mouse models. In vital pulp therapy for treating pulpitis, gallium-doped mesoporous bioactive glass nanoparticles (Ga-MBGNs) activate HO-1 through the Nrf2 pathway while suppressing [[nf-kb\|NF-κB]]-dependent inflammatory signaling, thereby attenuating ROS accumulation, promoting odontoblastic differentiation, and enabling reparative dentin formation.³ In both contexts, HO-1's coordinated antioxidant and anti-inflammatory functions appear inseparable from its capacity to promote cell survival and tissue regeneration.
