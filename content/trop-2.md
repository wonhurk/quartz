**Trop-2** is a cell-surface antigen serving dual roles as a circulating biomarker and antibody-drug conjugate target in solid malignancies. In [[castration-resistant-prostate-cancer|metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC)]], elevated levels of Trop-2 present on plasma-derived [[extracellular-vesicles|extracellular vesicles]] correlate with worse overall survival and molecular tumor burden.¹ As a therapeutic target, Trop-2 is recognized by [[immu132|IMMU132]], an antibody-drug conjugate that delivers the topoisomerase I inhibitor [[sn38|SN-38]] to [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer]] cells, inducing DNA damage and triggering multiple death pathways.² Beyond direct cytotoxicity, IMMU132 simultaneously suppresses the [[perk|PERK-eIF2α-ATF4]] axis of the unfolded protein response, disrupting adaptive survival mechanisms that would otherwise confer resistance. Rational combination of anti-Trop-2 ADCs with [[perk|PERK inhibitors]] yields synergistic effects by co-targeting endoplasmic reticulum stress and [[wnt-beta-catenin|Wnt/β-catenin]] signaling, establishing Trop-2-directed therapy as a multi-pathway approach to overcome treatment resistance.

---

## Mechanism

Trop-2 is presented on the surface of circulating [[extracellular-vesicles|extracellular vesicles (EVs)]] derived from tumor cells, where it can be quantified by liquid biopsy proteomics. The protein's presence on plasma EVs correlates closely with imaging metrics of tumor burden measured by PSMA-PET, as well as with serum levels of PSA and alkaline phosphatase, integrating proteomic biomarkers with traditional oncologic surrogates.¹ Trop-2 is one of four cell-surface antigens enriched on EV preparations from mCRPC patients, alongside [[psma|PSMA]], [[b7h3|B7-H3]], and [[steap1|STEAP1]], forming a multi-analyte signature of tumor-derived material with prognostic significance.¹

As a therapeutic target, Trop-2 is recognized by [[immu132|IMMU132]], which binds the cell-surface antigen, undergoes receptor-mediated endocytosis, and releases its payload of [[sn38|SN-38]] into the cytoplasm. [[sn38|SN-38]] inhibits topoisomerase I, blocking DNA religation and generating DNA breaks that trigger apoptosis.² Beyond this direct mechanism, IMMU132 treatment suppresses the [[perk|PERK-eIF2α-ATF4]] pathway, a central arm of the unfolded protein response that is typically activated by therapy-induced endoplasmic reticulum stress. This dual inhibition of DNA repair and adaptive stress responses represents a rational strategy to dismantle resistance mechanisms.

## Prostate cancer biomarker discovery

Plasma EV-derived Trop-2 emerged as a prognostic biomarker through comprehensive proteomics profiling of [[castration-resistant-prostate-cancer|mCRPC]] patients treated with 177Lu-PSMA-617.¹ Among 5,137 proteins identified in EV preparations, high Trop-2 levels were independently associated with worse overall survival, and the protein correlated with molecular tumor volume as measured by PSMA-PET imaging as well as with serum PSA and alkaline phosphatase concentrations.¹ Similarly, [[circulating-tumor-cells|circulating tumor cells]] expressing PSMA and EpCAM showed associations with progression-free and overall survival, suggesting that integrated liquid biopsy combining proteomic and cell-based analytes may enhance biomarker-driven patient stratification.¹

---

## Colorectal cancer ADC applications

[[immu132|IMMU132]], the first-in-class anti-Trop-2 antibody-drug conjugate, delivers [[sn38|SN-38]] to [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer]] cells and demonstrates potent cytotoxic activity in preclinical models.² The mechanism of cell death involves topoisomerase I inhibition and DNA damage, yet [[colorectal-cancer|CRC]] cells develop resistance through upregulation of adaptive survival pathways. Notably, the [[perk|PERK-eIF2α-ATF4]] axis of the unfolded protein response is activated in response to therapy-induced endoplasmic reticulum stress, allowing surviving cells to resume protein synthesis and restore metabolic homeostasis.² IMMU132 treatment itself suppresses this adaptive axis, yet residual pathway activity permits escape. Additionally, resistant tumors exhibit elevated [[wnt-beta-catenin|Wnt/β-catenin]] signaling, an oncogenic transcriptional program that drives proliferation and survival.² These observations suggest that multiple adaptive mechanisms collaborate to confer ADC resistance.

---

## Synergistic pathway targeting to overcome resistance

Combining [[immu132|IMMU132]] with the [[perk|PERK inhibitor]] GSK2606414 yields robust synergistic cytotoxicity across multiple [[colorectal-cancer|CRC]] preclinical models.² The mechanistic basis of this synergy involves enhanced suppression of both endoplasmic reticulum stress signatures and [[wnt-beta-catenin|Wnt/β-catenin]] pathway activity.² By simultaneously addressing three critical nodes—DNA damage induction via [[sn38|SN-38]], disruption of ER stress adaptation via [[perk]] inhibition, and suppression of an oncogenic transcriptional program via [[wnt-beta-catenin|Wnt/β-catenin]] pathway downregulation—this dual-targeting strategy disables multiple resistance axes simultaneously. These findings establish a rational framework for advancing [[immu132|Trop-2-directed ADC]] therapy into combination regimens in treatment-refractory [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer]], where multi-pathway targeting may overcome the limitations of monotherapy and achieve durable disease control.
