**Hydrogel scaffold** is an implantable biocompatible material engineered to encapsulate and confine [[engineered-bacteria|genetically engineered microbes]] for autonomous therapeutic production and delivery within physiological environments. The scaffold is distinguished by a dual mechanical architecture combining high stiffness to regulate bacterial proliferation with high toughness to resist fracture under physiological stress, enabling complete microbial containment for extended periods while maintaining structural integrity.¹ This design transforms the hydrogel into a living bioreactor capable of environmental sensing and on-demand therapeutic release, overcoming the critical clinical challenge of bacterial dissemination during systemic treatment.

---

## Mechanical Design and Bacterial Containment

The engineering of hydrogel scaffolds centers on achieving mechanical properties that simultaneously address two opposing demands: constraining microbial growth while resisting material failure under physiological stress. The dual stiffness-toughness architecture accomplishes this by combining high stiffness — which suppresses bacterial proliferation through mechanical regulation — with high toughness to prevent catastrophic fracture when exposed to compressive, tensile, and shear forces encountered in vivo.¹ This design has demonstrated complete bacterial confinement for six months and withstood multiple forms of mechanical loading that would otherwise cause material failure in conventional hydrogels.¹ The ability to sustain long-term bacterial viability while maintaining physical containment represents a fundamental advancement in living therapeutic systems, enabling the hydrogel to function as a self-contained biofactory.

---

## Prosthetic Joint Infection Treatment

Hydrogel scaffolds have been successfully applied to treat prosthetic joint infections through genetically engineered bacteria integrated into the material matrix. By embedding bacteria that produce antimicrobial compounds or immune modulators, the scaffold delivers therapy autonomously at the infection site without systemic dissemination.¹ This approach was validated in murine models of prosthetic joint infection, where the engineered bacteria within the hydrogel demonstrated efficacy in clearing infection while the material's containment prevented bacterial escape. The autonomous release mechanism relies on environmental sensing — bacteria detect local cues such as pH, nutrient availability, or inflammatory signals and respond by modulating therapeutic production, enabling context-appropriate treatment intensity.¹

## Cancer Therapy Applications

Hydrogel scaffolds have emerged as versatile platforms for combined chemo-immunotherapy in cancer. Thermo-sensitive hydrogel variants enable sustained peritumoral delivery of therapeutic payloads with localized activity and minimal systemic exposure.² When integrated with [[paclitaxel]]-loaded [[plga-nanoparticles|poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) nanoparticles]], hydrogels enable dual-modality treatment combining direct chemotherapy with bacteriotherapy.² This biohybrid approach was exemplified through the conjugation of [[lactobacillus-rhamnosus-gg|Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG]] — a probiotic bacterium with demonstrated acid-environment tropism — to paclitaxel-loaded PLGA nanoparticles and embedded within the hydrogel matrix.² The temperature-responsive nature of the scaffold allows for controlled release kinetics, ensuring sustained therapeutic concentration at the tumor while reducing off-target toxicity.

---

## Immunotherapy and Bacterial Cytotoxicity

Hydrogel-embedded [[lactobacillus-rhamnosus-gg|Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG]] activates anti-tumor immunity through multiple mechanisms. The bacterium exhibits strong adhesion to tumor cells and active penetration into deep tumor tissues, where it displays potent cytotoxicity associated with mitochondrial dysfunction and apoptosis, likely driven by localized acidification of the tumor microenvironment.² Additionally, the presence of embedded bacteria promotes M1-type macrophage polarization and dendritic cell maturation, establishing a pro-inflammatory immune milieu hostile to tumor growth.² The hydrogel's sustained-release capacity extends the temporal window of immune activation, enabling durable immunological responses. Competitive inhibition of tumor-promoting bacterial species further reshapes the tumor microbiota toward anti-tumor phenotypes, reinforcing the immunotherapeutic effect.²

---

## Clinical Validation and Safety

The combined chemo-immunotherapeutic potential of hydrogel-embedded bacteriotherapy has been validated in two preclinical tumor models. In the 4T1 breast tumor lung metastasis model and the B16F10 tumor postoperative recurrence model, paclitaxel-loaded nanoparticles conjugated to [[lactobacillus-rhamnosus-gg|Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG]] and delivered via thermo-sensitive hydrogel demonstrated superior anti-tumor efficacy with favorable safety profiles.² The material's confinement capability ensures long-term bacterial viability while preventing systemic bacterial dissemination, addressing the primary regulatory and safety concern limiting clinical translation of cell-based and microbial therapeutics.
