**Selenium nanoparticles (SeNPs)** are biocompatible nanomaterials that function as potent antioxidant and immunomodulatory agents through coordinated activation of cellular redox defense pathways. SeNPs operate principally by restoring the activity of [[gpx4|glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4)]] and [[prdx6|peroxiredoxin 6 (PRDX6)]], while simultaneously suppressing [[ferroptosis]]—an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death—via inhibition of [[ncoa4|nuclear receptor coactivator 4 (NCOA4)]]-mediated ferritinophagy. These dual mechanisms enable SeNPs to restore cellular glutathione pools, attenuate pro-inflammatory signaling, and protect against oxidative injury and lipid peroxidation.¹

---

## Mechanism

Selenium nanoparticles exert their protective effects through a multi-layered antioxidant defense system centered on the [[prdx6|PRDX6]]/[[sephs2|selenophosphate synthetase 2 (SEPHS2)]] regulatory axis. Upon oral administration, SeNPs increase serum selenium bioavailability and enhance the activity of tissue-resident [[gpx4|GPX4]], the critical selenoprotein responsible for detoxifying lipid hydroperoxides and restoring glutathione pools. Immunofluorescence analysis reveals that SeNPs physically strengthen the PRDX6-SEPHS2 protein interaction, thereby amplifying GPX4 enzymatic activity and reinforcing the cellular antioxidant defense network.¹ Crucially, SeNPs suppress ferroptotic cell death by inhibiting NCOA4-driven ferritinophagy—a selective autophagy pathway that liberates iron from ferritin stores. By blocking this pathway, SeNPs reduce iron overload and prevent iron-catalyzed lipid peroxidation, protecting cellular membranes from ferroptotic collapse and maintaining tissue integrity.¹

## Mastitis treatment

Klebsiella pneumoniae-induced mastitis represents a significant threat to livestock health and milk production, with limited therapeutic alternatives to conventional antibiotics. In an in vivo goat model of experimental mastitis, oral SeNP administration at 20 μg/kg body weight substantially attenuated histopathological damage and suppressed neutrophil infiltration in affected mammary tissue.¹ At the molecular level, SeNP treatment restored anti-inflammatory [[interleukin-10|interleukin-10 (IL-10)]] mRNA expression while simultaneously suppressing pro-inflammatory [[il-1b|IL-1β]] and [[il-6|IL-6]] cytokine transcription, thereby dampening the destructive inflammatory cascade characteristic of bacterial infection.¹ Beyond inflammation control, hemodynamic assessments and ultrasonographic evaluation confirmed that SeNPs improved mammary blood flow dynamics and restored echotextural normality in infected tissue, indicating functional restoration of the organ beyond mere dampening of inflammation.

## Sepsis and immune restoration

Sepsis frequently precipitates profound immunosuppression that impedes pathogen clearance and increases mortality risk, especially in critically ill populations. To address this clinical challenge, a pilot randomized clinical trial enrolling seventy sepsis patients evaluated SeNP supplementation as an adjunct to standard critical care.² Patients receiving SeNPs at 400 μg selenium daily demonstrated significantly enhanced immune function compared to control subjects receiving standard care alone, with improvements evident across days 4, 7, and 10 post-enrollment.² Specifically, SeNP therapy increased total lymphocyte counts and expanded absolute numbers of [[cd3-t-cells|CD3+]], [[cd4-t-cells|CD4+]], and [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+]] T cell subsets, indicating restoration of adaptive immune capacity.² Concurrently, SeNP supplementation reduced systemic inflammation as measured by decreased plasma levels of pro-inflammatory [[il-6|IL-6]] and high-mobility group box 1 (HMGB1), markers of endotoxemia and widespread tissue damage. Furthermore, SeNP therapy conferred protective effects across multiple organ systems, decreased biomarkers of systemic infection, and was associated with reduced intensive care unit and hospital mortality.² Notably, the treatment exhibited an excellent safety profile with no adverse events reported during the trial, demonstrating clinical tolerability in the acute care setting.

---

## Clinical translation and perspectives

The successful translation of SeNPs from veterinary bacterial infection models to human sepsis represents a significant milestone in nanomedicine and infectious disease therapeutics. The biocompatibility, tolerability, and multitarget mechanism of SeNPs—engaging both antioxidant and ferroptosis-suppressive pathways simultaneously—position them as a rational adjunctive strategy for immune dysfunction in sepsis and other immunocompromised states. Future investigations are likely to expand SeNP applications to other sepsis-associated complications, diverse bacterial infections, and immunocompromised populations where ferroptosis and oxidative stress contribute to organ failure and mortality.
