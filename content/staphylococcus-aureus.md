**Staphylococcus aureus** is a gram-positive pathogenic bacterium and leading causative agent of wound infections, presenting particular clinical severity in diabetic patients who experience delayed wound healing and heightened infection susceptibility.¹ The organism establishes persistent infections through biofilm formation and virulence mechanisms centered on disruption of host tissue and bacterial cell membrane integrity. Recent advances in biomaterial engineering have identified potent antimicrobial strategies employing [[chitosan]]-based formulations and conductive graphene-polymer composites, offering promising alternatives to conventional antibiotic therapies and addressing the challenge of [[antiretroviral-therapy|antimicrobial resistance]].

---

## Pathogenesis in diabetic wounds

Diabetic wound infections caused by S. aureus represent a major clinical challenge due to the compromised immune response and impaired tissue regeneration capacity inherent to diabetes.¹ The bacterium colonizes wound sites and establishes persistent infections that delay epithelialization and promote a hostile microenvironment enriched with inflammatory mediators and reactive oxygen species. Traditional antibiotic therapies increasingly face resistance, necessitating development of alternative antimicrobial modalities. The pathogenic mechanism of S. aureus exploits disruption of bacterial cell membrane integrity as a key vulnerability point amenable to novel therapeutic intervention.

## Novel biomaterial antimicrobial strategies

Engineered biomaterials have emerged as promising therapeutic platforms against S. aureus infections in diabetic wound contexts. A dopamine-functionalized [[conductive-multifunctional-hydrogel|reduced graphene oxide/alginate hydrogel]] loaded with thymol and [[chitosan]] nanoparticles demonstrates potent antimicrobial activity, producing inhibition zones of approximately 20 mm against S. aureus while maintaining exceptional biocompatibility (>90% cell viability).² The system achieves skin-equivalent electrical conductivity (3.95 mS·cm⁻¹), enabling electroactive acceleration of wound healing alongside direct antimicrobial action. The hydrogel exhibits robust mechanical properties (1.03 MPa compressive strength) and simultaneously promotes high fibroblast migration rates (71.41%), both essential for productive wound repair.

Complementary [[chitosan]]-based approaches utilizing [[glabridin]] delivery achieve superior antimicrobial potency. Cyclodextrin-based deep eutectic solvent-[[chitosan]] eutectogels demonstrate over 99% in vitro antibacterial efficacy against S. aureus through cell membrane disruption mechanisms.¹ In infected diabetic wound models, these eutectogels significantly accelerated wound closure to 94.3% by day 21, accomplishing this outcome through multiple synergistic mechanisms: direct antimicrobial action, reactive oxygen species scavenging, immune microenvironment amelioration, and promotion of angiogenesis and tissue regeneration. The integration of a deep eutectic solvent with [[chitosan]] imparts favorable mechanical properties while enabling sustained bioactive compound release over extended periods, providing both therapeutic antimicrobial efficacy and enhanced biocompatibility.
