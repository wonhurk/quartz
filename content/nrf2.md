**Nuclear Factor Erythroid 2-Related Factor 2 (Nrf2)** is a master transcription factor and central regulator of cellular antioxidant defense, activated through the Keap1-Nrf2 signaling axis and serving as a critical therapeutic hub for redox homeostasis across diverse pathological contexts. Upon dissociation from its cytoplasmic repressor Keap1, Nrf2 translocates to the nucleus and upregulates antioxidant response elements including [[heme-oxygenase-1|heme oxygenase-1 (HO-1)]], [[gpx4|glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4)]], superoxide dismutase (SOD1), and [[gclc|glutamate-cysteine ligase catalytic subunit (GCLc)]], collectively reducing [[reactive-oxygen-species|reactive oxygen species]] accumulation and restoring mitochondrial function.¹ ² ³ Concurrently, Nrf2 activation suppresses NF-κB-mediated inflammatory signaling, providing dual protection against oxidative stress and inflammation-driven pathology.⁴ ⁵ However, aberrant Nrf2 activation in malignant cells creates a therapeutic paradox: enhanced antioxidant defenses confer resistance to radiotherapy and chemotherapy by suppressing pro-death pathways including [[ferroptosis]] and [[cuproptosis]].⁶ ⁷

---

## Mechanism

The Keap1-Nrf2 axis functions as a redox-sensitive switch governing cellular antioxidant capacity. Under basal conditions, Keap1 sequesters Nrf2 in the cytoplasm and promotes its proteasomal degradation. Oxidative stress or electrophilic compounds modify cysteine residues on Keap1, disrupting this interaction and allowing Nrf2 nuclear translocation.⁵ Once in the nucleus, Nrf2 binds to antioxidant response elements in the promoter regions of cytoprotective genes, orchestrating a coordinated transcriptional program that includes glutathione synthesis enzymes and redox-active proteins.⁸ In pulmonary endothelial cells subjected to hypoxia-reoxygenation injury, Nrf2 nuclear translocation regulates [[gpx4|GPX4]] expression and modulates downstream pathways including ERK1/2 and NF-κB phosphorylation, ultimately suppressing neutrophil extracellular trap formation and [[ferroptosis]].⁴ The pathway's therapeutic relevance extends to mitochondrial quality control, where Nrf2 activation promotes mitophagy and preserves respiratory chain function in renal tubular epithelial cells subjected to ischemia-reperfusion stress.⁷

## Protective applications

Nrf2 activation has demonstrated broad renoprotective, pulmonary-protective, and barrier-protective effects across multiple organ systems. In diabetic nephropathy, [[cafestol|Cafestol]] administration at 5–10 mg/kg for eight weeks activated the Keap1-Nrf2 axis independent of glycemic control, restoring antioxidant defenses (HO-1, SOD, GSH) while reducing glomerular and tubular injury, inflammatory mediators (IL-6, TNF-α, ICAM-1), and advanced glycation end-products.⁵ In acute kidney injury induced by ischemia-reperfusion, a multifunctional nanoplatform (RGD-CeO₂@Que) targeted injured renal tissues via integrin αvβ3 binding and activated the Nrf2/HO-1/GPX4/SOD1 pathway, alleviating oxidative stress, reducing apoptosis, and promoting mitophagy in HK-2 renal tubular cells.⁷ In idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, inhalable biomimetic nanoparticles (Mul-siRNA@MM) coated with M2 macrophage membranes delivered [[mulberrin|Mulberrin]] and suppressed [[reactive-oxygen-species|ROS]] production via Nrf2 pathway activation, reducing M2 macrophage infiltration and downstream profibrotic cytokine release including transforming growth factor-β.⁹ Similarly, gallium-doped mesoporous bioactive glass nanoparticles ([[ga-mbgn|Ga-MBGNs]]) attenuated dental pulp inflammation by activating the NRF2-HO-1 pathway while suppressing NF-κB signaling, inducing reparative dentin formation and maintaining pulp vitality.⁸ In [[intestinal-barrier-dysfunction|intestinal barrier dysfunction]] induced by sleep deprivation, [[nootkatone|Nootkatone]] activated the Nrf2 pathway, restoring mitochondrial redox homeostasis, upregulating epithelial tight-junction proteins, inducing mucin production, and suppressing proinflammatory cytokine expression.¹

## Cancer resistance

In malignant contexts, constitutive or adaptive Nrf2 activation establishes a cytoprotective environment that enables therapy evasion. In [[lung-squamous-cell-carcinoma|lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC)]] arising within idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, tumors exhibited enrichment of Nrf2-linked antioxidant response pathways alongside Wnt3 activation, creating a stress-adapted phenotype resistant to oxidative damage.² Restoration of redox sensitivity required targeting Wnt/β-catenin signaling upstream of Nrf2. In cervical cancer, cancer-associated fibroblast-derived exosomes delivered [[bone-morphogenetic-protein-4|bone morphogenetic protein 4 (BMP4)]], which activated the Nrf2-HO-1 signaling axis while simultaneously suppressing key [[cuproptosis]] regulators, establishing a dual-pathway mechanism for radiotherapy resistance.³ BMP4 knockdown effectively restored radiosensitivity both in vitro and in vivo. In glioblastoma, high-throughput screening using a blood-brain barrier-permeable probe identified Baicalin as a β3-adrenergic receptor inhibitor that depleted glutathione and triggered oxidative stress-mediated apoptosis by disrupting the Gαi/o-ERK-Nrf2-[[gclc|GCLc]] signaling cascade.⁶ These findings establish Nrf2 as a context-dependent therapeutic vulnerability in treatment-refractory cancers, where its antioxidant program must be pharmacologically suppressed rather than activated.

## Novel activators and delivery systems

Pharmacological Nrf2 modulation has been achieved through both small-molecule natural compounds and engineered nanoplatforms. [[Nootkatone|Nootkatone]], a sesquiterpenoid from grapefruit, demonstrated oral bioavailability and activated Nrf2 in colonic tissues, restoring gut barrier integrity and microbial composition in sleep-deprived mice.¹ [[Cafestol|Cafestol]], a diterpene from coffee, exhibited renoprotective effects at 5–10 mg/kg with no adverse effects in normal physiology, selectively activating Nrf2 in diabetic kidney injury.⁵ [[Mulberrin|Mulberrin]], delivered via inhalable M2 macrophage membrane-coated nanoparticles, achieved lung-specific Nrf2 activation and suppressed macrophage-driven fibrosis in bleomycin-induced pulmonary injury.⁹ Baicalin, a flavonoid, functioned as a β3-adrenergic receptor antagonist that paradoxically induced oxidative stress in glioblastoma by inhibiting Nrf2-dependent glutathione synthesis.⁶ Engineered cerium dioxide nanoplatforms (RGD-CeO₂@Que) combined intrinsic ROS-scavenging properties with quercetin cargo and RGD-mediated renal targeting, activating Nrf2 downstream pathways including HO-1, GPX4, and SOD1 in acute kidney injury models.⁷ Gallium-doped mesoporous bioactive glass nanoparticles provided sustained Ga³⁺ release, activating NRF2-HO-1 while simultaneously suppressing NF-κB in inflamed dental pulp.⁸
