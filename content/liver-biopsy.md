**Liver biopsy** is an invasive diagnostic procedure that remains the reference standard for detecting [[metabolic-dysfunction-associated-steatohepatitis|metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH)]] and grading hepatic steatosis in patients with [[masld|metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD)]].¹ As the gold standard for assessing advanced fibrosis — the major determinant of liver-related morbidity and mortality in MASLD — liver biopsy provides definitive histological evaluation of inflammation, lipid accumulation, and fibrous tissue remodeling.¹ However, its invasive nature and unsuitability for repeated monitoring have catalyzed a paradigm shift in clinical practice toward noninvasive diagnostic modalities, relegating liver biopsy to a secondary role for risk stratification and specialist consultation in select patients.

---

## Diagnostic role in MASLD and MASH

Liver biopsy establishes the definitive diagnosis of MASH by revealing the histological triad of steatosis, hepatocyte ballooning, and inflammation.¹ While biopsy is no longer routinely required for initial MASLD diagnosis — which can be inferred from imaging and biochemical markers — it remains essential for identifying and characterizing at-risk MASH in patients at high risk for progression to cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma.¹ The procedure enables simultaneous grading of hepatic steatosis alongside assessment of necroinflammatory activity and fibrosis stage, providing comprehensive pathological characterization unavailable through less invasive means.

---

## Fibrosis assessment and prognosis

Advanced fibrosis represents the strongest prognostic indicator of liver-related morbidity and mortality in MASLD populations. Liver biopsy remains the definitive method for staging fibrosis by direct histological visualization of collagen deposition and architectural distortion.¹ The accuracy of biopsy-based fibrosis staging is particularly valuable in patients with discordant noninvasive findings, such as those with elevated [[fib-4|FIB-4 index]] scores but contradictory imaging results, or those presenting with clinical features suggestive of progressive liver injury requiring precise risk quantification.

---

## Application in obesity and bariatric populations

In patients with obesity preparing for bariatric surgery, liver biopsy has historically served as the critical reference standard for assessing MASH severity and steatosis grade before intervention.² However, CT-based composite biomarkers have been increasingly developed and validated to identify MASH and accurately grade hepatic steatosis in obese populations without the need for invasive biopsy, substantially reducing the procedural burden in this high-risk group.²

---

## Limitations and shift toward noninvasive alternatives

The invasive nature of liver biopsy — requiring percutaneous needle insertion or transjugular access — limits its utility for repeated monitoring of treatment efficacy and longitudinal disease progression.² Current evidence-based clinical guidelines now recommend first-line use of noninvasive fibrosis assessment tools including the [[fib-4]] index, elastography, imaging modalities, and emerging CT-based biomarkers for initial risk stratification and patient selection.¹ Liver biopsy is increasingly reserved for cases with equivocal noninvasive findings, persistent diagnostic uncertainty, or when specialist hepatology evaluation is clinically indicated for patients with high-risk features suggestive of advanced fibrosis or early cirrhosis.
