**Vitamin D3** is an essential bioactive steroid hormone regulating immune function and metabolic health, yet its therapeutic efficacy is severely constrained by poor chemical stability and low intestinal bioavailability in conventional formulations.¹ Recent advances employ [[high-internal-phase-pickering-emulsions|high internal phase Pickering emulsions (HIPPEs)]] stabilized by egg white peptide–konjac glucomannan co-assemblies to overcome these limitations, achieving 3.74-fold enhanced emulsifying activity and 53-fold increased emulsifying stability compared to single-component systems.¹ These optimized carriers enable simultaneous co-delivery of vitamin D3 with [[riboflavin]] sodium phosphate while maintaining long-term storage stability and favorable rheological properties.

---

## Mechanism and immunological roles

Vitamin D3 exerts critical functions through the active metabolite 1α,25-dihydroxyvitamin D [1α,25-(OH)₂D], which modulates immune cell differentiation, inflammatory responses, and calcium-phosphate homeostasis. Pre-vaccination serum concentrations of this bioactive form have been identified as an independent predictor of immune response magnitude and quality, particularly in the context of [[covid-19-vaccine|COVID-19 mRNA vaccination]].² Blood vitamin D levels are additionally associated with disease severity in acute viral infections, underscoring its relevance as a preventive and immunocompetence factor.²

---

## Bioavailability limitation and advanced emulsion delivery

The primary barrier to vitamin D3 therapeutics is rapid degradation and minimal intestinal absorption. EWP@KGM co-assemblies overcome this through enhanced interfacial properties: the konjac glucomannan polymer network increases interfacial thickness and steric hindrance, protecting encapsulated vitamin D3 against oxidative and hydrolytic breakdown.¹ Under simulated gastrointestinal digestion, these HIPPEs preserve encapsulated vitamin D3 and improve its bioaccessibility, with release kinetics governed by anomalous transport driven jointly by diffusion and matrix relaxation.¹ This performance substantially exceeds that of conventional oil-in-water emulsions or single-polymer stabilizers.

---

## Co-encapsulation and multifunctional delivery

The HIPPE platform enables simultaneous stabilization and controlled release of multiple bioactive compounds. Vitamin D3 and riboflavin sodium phosphate co-encapsulated within EWP@KGM-stabilized emulsions maintain integrity of both payloads during gastrointestinal transit, achieving coordinated bioaccessibility that would be impossible in separate formulations.¹ This co-delivery strategy leverages the tunable microstructure of high internal phase emulsions to compartmentalize distinct components while preserving their stability.

---

## Formulation properties and pharmaceutical suitability

Vitamin D3-loaded HIPPEs exhibit shear-thinning (pseudoplastic) behavior coupled with gel-like viscoelasticity (G′ > G″), properties essential for food-grade and pharmaceutical applications.¹ These rheological characteristics enable facile processing and dispensing while ensuring adequate intestinal residence time for absorption. Long-term storage stability is maintained without phase separation or payload degradation, satisfying shelf-life and commercial viability requirements for nutraceutical and clinical products.¹
