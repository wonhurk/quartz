**Diabetes mellitus** is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by dysregulated glucose metabolism and impaired [[insulin]] signaling that drives multiple systemic complications including [[diabetic-retinopathy|diabetic retinopathy]], periodontitis, [[intervertebral-disc-degeneration|intervertebral disc degeneration]], and increased cardiovascular and cancer risk.¹ The pathophysiology involves reactive oxygen species (ROS) overproduction, elevated cholesterol biosynthesis, and endothelial vascular dysfunction.² Environmental factors such as [[blue-light|blue-enriched]] [[artificial-light-at-night|artificial light at night]] exposure contribute to diabetes prevalence, with each ten-fold increase in light intensity raising diabetes odds by 18%.³

---

## Pathophysiology

The molecular pathogenesis of diabetes involves disruption of core metabolic and vascular regulatory pathways. In type 2 diabetes, the classical Chinese herbal formula [[xiehuang-san|Xiehuang San]] restores [[insulin]] signaling by targeting the CLCF1-STAT3 pathway, demonstrating that this axis is critically dysregulated in diabetic states.¹ At the microvascular level, VE-cadherin Y685 phosphorylation mediates early retinal vascular barrier breakdown and pericyte loss, serving as an initiating molecular event in [[diabetic-retinopathy|diabetic and prediabetic retinopathy]].⁴ High glucose and nucleoside diphosphate kinase B (NDPKB) deficiency induce VE-cadherin Y685 phosphorylation, promoting its junctional internalization and activating the hexosamine biosynthesis pathway, which increases angiopoietin 2 (Ang2) and impairs endothelial barrier function.⁴ Pharmacological studies reveal that protein O-linked β-N-acetyl glucosamine (O-GlcNAc) modification mediates Y685-dependent Ang2 upregulation, and preventing Y685 phosphorylation through VE-cadherin Y685F mutation blocks these pathological effects both in vitro and in vivo.⁴

The hyperglycemic state drives ROS overproduction that inhibits osteogenic differentiation in periodontal tissues, contributing to accelerated bone destruction in diabetic periodontitis.⁵ Diabetes-induced ROS dysregulation is countered by thioredoxin reductase 1 (TrxR1) synthesis, which activates the TrxR1/ROS/β-catenin cascade to modulate the periodontal osteogenic microenvironment.⁵

## Cancer and metabolic complications

Diabetic patients with [[pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma|pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)]] exhibit worse clinical outcomes, with higher tumor stages, poorer differentiation, and significantly worse survival than non-diabetic patients.² Integrated spatially resolved multiomics studies reveal that diabetes influences interactions between tumor cells and immune cells, accelerating PDAC growth through [[srebp2|SREBP2]]-dependent cholesterol biosynthesis.² Increased cholesterol biosynthesis promotes neutrophil recruitment and accelerates the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) by stimulating the CXCL1/CXCR1/CXCR2 signaling axis, ultimately driving PDAC progression.² Inhibition of [[srebp2|SREBP2]], pharmacologic blockade of CXCL1, or perturbation of NETs markedly reduces PDAC growth in diabetic mouse models, demonstrating that this cholesterol-cancer nexus is therapeutically actionable.²

Beyond pancreatic malignancy, diabetes contributes to hepatic steatosis and metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), which are precursors to liver cirrhosis and cancer.⁶ Circulating plasminogen activator inhibitor 1 (PAI-1) levels increase with age and diabetes, promoting thrombosis and contributing to diet-induced obesity and age-associated cardiovascular morbidity.⁷

## Immunological and infectious complications

Diabetes mellitus causes systemic immunosuppression, increasing susceptibility to persistent infections from multidrug-resistant (MDR) pathogens including methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA).⁸ This immunocompromised state elevates the risk of severe complications in diabetic wound infections and keratitis. Engineered copper-based nanoassemblies (DC/Cu) address this challenge through peroxidase-like catalytic activity that induces membrane lipid peroxidation and facilitates copper influx, triggering cuproptosis-like bacterial death characterized by inhibition of Fe-S cluster proteins and disruption of the tricarboxylic acid cycle.⁸ Transcriptomic profiling confirms that Cu²⁺-mediated metabolic disruption provides an alternative antibacterial mechanism effective against MRSA in diabetic wound and keratitis models.⁸

Diabetic periodontitis represents another infectious complication driven by disordered glucose metabolism, which accelerates bone destruction and increases dental plaque reproduction.⁵ Selenium-albumin corona rinse (H@Se NPs) addresses this through tri-modal action: promoting macrophage polarization from M1 to M2 phenotype for anti-inflammatory effects, direct antibacterial activity against subgingival microbial colonies, and ROS scavenging to enhance osteogenic differentiation via the TrxR1/ROS/β-catenin cascade.⁵

## Environmental risk factors

[[Blue-light|Blue-enriched]] [[artificial-light-at-night|artificial light at night]] exposure is positively associated with diabetes prevalence, with urban nighttime environments rapidly shifting from traditional sodium lamps to high-intensity [[white-leds|white LEDs]].³ Cross-sectional analysis leveraging multispectral nighttime imagery from the SDGSAT-1 Glimmer Imager demonstrates that higher artificial light at night (ALAN) intensity corresponds to increased diabetes odds, with the association most pronounced for blue light wavelengths (450-490 nm).³ The highest quartile of blue light exposure shows 61% higher odds of diabetes compared to the lowest quartile, and associations are stronger in areas dominated by [[white-leds|white LEDs]] than in those dominated by high-pressure sodium lamps.³

## Therapeutic strategies

Emerging therapeutic approaches span traditional herbal medicine, glucose-lowering agents, multidisciplinary management protocols, and digital health technologies. [[Xiehuang-san|Xiehuang San]], a classical Chinese herbal formula with analgesic, anti-inflammatory, gastrointestinal-regulating, and hypoglycemic effects, restores [[insulin]] signaling by targeting the CLCF1-STAT3 pathway in type 2 diabetes.¹ DPP4 inhibitors, particularly sitagliptin, attenuate [[intervertebral-disc-degeneration|intervertebral disc degeneration]] progression through macrophage-nucleus pulposus cell crosstalk mechanisms, addressing a frequently overlooked musculoskeletal complication of diabetes.⁹

Structured [[multidisciplinary-case-management|multidisciplinary case management]] significantly improves cardiac function and glycemic control in elderly diabetic patients undergoing off-pump [[coronary-heart-disease|coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG)]].¹⁰ A six-month intervention including individualized cardiac rehabilitation, modified Mediterranean diet, psychological support, and intensified follow-up demonstrates significantly higher left ventricular ejection fraction (mean difference 6.2%) and lower HbA1c (mean difference 1.3%) compared to routine care.¹⁰ The intervention also improves secondary endpoints including reduced left ventricular diameters, enhanced lipid profiles, and longer six-minute walk test distances.¹⁰

[[Cgm-integrated-digital-health-app|Continuous glucose monitoring (CGM)]]-integrated digital health apps represent a personalized nutrition approach, combining real-time glucose biofeedback with self-monitored behaviors to optimize metabolic health in individuals with and without diabetes.¹¹ These platforms enable on-demand, personalized care through synchronization with continuous glucose monitors, although user characteristics, engagement patterns, and sustained-use factors require further characterization in real-world settings.¹¹
