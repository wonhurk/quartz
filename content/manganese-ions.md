**Manganese ions (Mn²⁺)** are transition metal cations deployed as active pharmaceutical ingredients in nanoparticulate delivery systems to overcome immunosuppressive tumor microenvironments and drive robust antitumor immunity. Their core mechanism centers on dual action: generation of hydroxyl radicals and reactive oxygen species (ROS) that induce immunogenic cell death, coupled with potent activation of the [[sting|stimulator of interferon genes (STING)]] pathway that amplifies dendritic cell and T-cell responses.¹ When incorporated into biomineralized nanoadjuvants or self-amplifying nanocatalytic platforms, Mn²⁺ exhibits pH-responsive release kinetics ideally suited to the acidic tumor microenvironment, enabling sustained redox disruption while orchestrating cascade immune activation through cooperation with [[cgas|cGAS]] signaling and checkpoint inhibition.

---

## Mechanism

Manganese ions exert their therapeutic effect through a coordinated redox mechanism that simultaneously generates cytotoxic ROS and activates innate immune signaling. Within nanoparticulate formulations, Mn²⁺ catalyzes the generation of hydroxyl radicals and other ROS species that induce immunogenic cell death in tumor cells, liberating damage-associated molecular patterns that prime dendritic cells for antigen presentation.¹ The released Mn²⁺ preferentially accumulates in endosomal compartments where it encounters dsDNA, a scenario particularly favorable when Mn²⁺ is co-delivered with DNA-loaded carriers. In this configuration, Mn²⁺ enhances recognition by [[cgas|cytoplasmic DNA sensor cGAS]], dramatically amplifying cGAS-catalyzed generation of cyclic GMP-AMP (cGAMP), which activates [[sting|STING]] on endosomal membranes.² This cascade amplification—driven cooperatively by both the metal ion and nucleic acid—produces substantially more robust interferon-β production than either component alone, effectively bridging innate and adaptive immunity.

The pH-responsive release of Mn²⁺ is a critical design feature. In formulations such as DNA@MnP (DNA-loaded manganese phosphate), the cumulative release of Mn²⁺ reaches 72% within 12 hours at pH 5.5, precisely matching the acidic microenvironment of tumors and inflamed tissues, whereas physiological pH (7.4) yields minimal leakage.² This controlled release prevents off-target ROS generation in healthy tissue while maximizing local immunological activation at the tumor site.

## Cancer immunotherapy

Manganese ion-based nanoparticles address the dual barrier problem facing solid tumors: both physical stromal barriers and immunosuppressive microenvironments. In the carrier-free [[qn-np|QN nanoparticle]] platform, Mn²⁺ is coordinated with quercetin and the indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1 inhibitor NLG919 to create a system in which each component targets a distinct impediment to immunotherapy.¹ The Mn²⁺ component triggers robust immune responses by generating hydroxyl radicals for immunogenic cell death induction and by activating the STING pathway, collectively working to restore anti-tumor immune function that was previously suppressed. When combined with anti-PD-L1 checkpoint blockade, Mn²⁺-containing nanoparticles achieve synergistic suppression of both primary tumors and pulmonary metastases, demonstrating that ionized metal catalysis can be seamlessly integrated into the contemporary immunotherapy landscape.¹ The immunological mechanism operates through coordinated promotion of dendritic cell maturation, with a 2.10-fold elevation in bone marrow-derived dendritic cell maturation rate, and profound macrophage polarization toward pro-inflammatory M1 phenotypes, with an 8.31-fold increase in the M1/M2 ratio.²

## Vaccine adjuvant application

Beyond therapeutic application in established tumors, Mn²⁺-based biomineralized nanoadjuvants function as superior cancer vaccine components. The [[dna-mnp|DNA@MnP]] formulation—in which dsDNA is templated onto manganese phosphate through biomimetic mineralization—achieves a 16-fold increase in antigen-specific IgG titers compared with conventional aluminum salt adjuvants, while simultaneously eliciting a balanced Th1/Th2 response characteristic of protective anti-tumor immunity.² The platform was further advanced through personalization: tumor lysate-loaded formulations (TLs@MnP) enable patient-specific vaccination that harnesses the individual tumor's antigenic repertoire, amplified by the synergistic cGAS-STING cascade. When co-administered with anti-TIGIT checkpoint inhibitors, these personalized Mn²⁺-based vaccines activate both innate immunity—through STING-driven interferon production—and adaptive immunity—through T-cell and B-cell priming—providing a comprehensive immunological shield against recurrent disease.

## Phototherapy synergy

A particularly promising evolution of Mn²⁺-based immunotherapy integrates near-infrared II (NIR-II) phototherapy to achieve self-amplifying nanocatalytic activity. In the MVP platform (manganese-in-situ-mineralized violet phosphorus nanosheets), violet phosphorus nanosheets serve dual roles: they donate electrons to sustain active Mn²⁺ concentrations in the tumor microenvironment, amplifying [[chemodynamic-therapy|Mn²⁺-mediated chemodynamic therapy]], while simultaneously functioning as NIR-II photocatalysts that elevate ROS generation through photon-driven electron transfer.³ This synergistic elevation of ROS disrupts tumor cell redox homeostasis irreversibly while the NIR-II photothermal effect enhances Mn²⁺-mediated STING pathway activation, collectively driving immunological reawakening. In preclinical female tumor-bearing models, MVPs achieved an 85.24% tumor inhibition rate through integrated catalytic-photothermal-immunological synergy, establishing that ionized metal chemistry merges naturally with photodynamic approaches.³
