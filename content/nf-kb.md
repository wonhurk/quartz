**Nuclear Factor Kappa-Light-Chain-Enhancer of Activated B Cells (NF-κB)** is a central transcription factor that orchestrates pro-inflammatory gene expression in response to cellular stress, infection, and tissue injury. Upon activation, NF-κB translocates to the nucleus and drives transcription of inflammatory mediators including IL-6, IL-1β, TNF-α, [[inos|inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS)]], and [[cox-2|cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2)]].¹ The pathway is principally activated downstream of pattern recognition receptors such as [[tlr4|Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)]] and is tightly modulated by metabolic sensors including [[sirt1|sirtuin 1 (SIRT1)]] and the [[ahr|aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR)]].¹⁻³ Aberrant NF-κB activation constitutes a pathological nexus across multiple organ systems, driving excessive cytokine production, pro-inflammatory immune cell polarization, and tissue destruction in neurological disorders, inflammatory bowel disease, sepsis-induced organ failure, invasive carcinomas, and hereditary kidney diseases.

---

## Mechanism and regulation

NF-κB activation is initiated when extracellular danger signals—including pathogen-associated molecular patterns and damage-associated molecular patterns—engage surface receptors such as [[tlr4|TLR4]], triggering phosphorylation cascades that liberate NF-κB from its cytoplasmic inhibitor IκB.⁴ Once in the nucleus, NF-κB binds κB response elements in the promoters of inflammatory target genes, amplifying cytokine production and recruiting immune effector cells. The pathway is negatively regulated by [[sirt1|SIRT1]], a NAD⁺-dependent deacetylase that suppresses NF-κB phosphorylation and nuclear translocation, thereby dampening inflammatory signaling.³ [[ahr|AhR]] activation by tryptophan-derived metabolites such as [[indole-3-propionic-acid|indole-3-propionic acid (IPA)]] similarly inhibits NF-κB through suppression of MAPK cascades, establishing a gut microbiota-brain-immune axis that modulates neuroinflammation.¹ Oxidative stress signals and metabolic perturbations further sustain NF-κB activity, creating a self-reinforcing inflammatory loop that underlies chronic disease pathology.⁵

## Neurological diseases

In spinal cord injury, aberrant astrocyte activation perpetuates neuroinflammation through sustained NF-κB signaling. [[indole-3-propionic-acid|IPA]], a gut microbiota-derived tryptophan metabolite, suppresses NF-κB-driven expression of inflammatory mediators (IL-6, IL-1β, [[inos|iNOS]], [[cox-2|COX-2]], CCL2, CXCL2, CXCL10) by activating the [[ahr|AhR]], reducing glial scar formation and enhancing motor function recovery.¹ In Parkinson disease, [[astragaloside-iv|Astragaloside IV]] directly binds [[tlr4|TLR4]] to inhibit the TLR4/NF-κB signaling axis in microglia within the substantia nigra, ventral tegmental area, and hippocampus, attenuating both motor deficits and anxiety-like behaviors—a dual therapeutic effect absent in TLR4-deficient mice, confirming TLR4 as the essential target.⁴ In cerebral ischemia-reperfusion injury, electroacupuncture activates [[sirt1|SIRT1]] to suppress NF-κB phosphorylation, driving a phenotypic switch in astrocytes from neurotoxic A1 (C3⁺) to neuroprotective A2 (S100A10⁺) states, thereby restoring synaptic density and cerebral blood flow.³ This same modulation of [[sirt1|SIRT1]]-NF-κB signaling improves neurological outcomes and reduces pro-inflammatory cytokine levels in stroke models.

In Alzheimer disease, NF-κB exhibits a paradoxical role beyond canonical inflammation. Piezoelectric nanoparticle-driven ultrasound neuromodulation at gamma frequency activates NF-κB during rhythmic stimulation to regulate [[ampar|AMPAR]] trafficking, balancing synaptic internalization and delivery to restore hippocampal gamma oscillations and memory-related network connectivity.⁹ This highlights NF-κB as a context-dependent regulator of synaptic plasticity, not solely a pathological driver.

## Inflammatory and metabolic diseases

In inflammatory bowel disease, pharmacologic PTPRO inhibition using [[compound-10j|compound 10j]] suppresses IL-6 production via NF-κB pathway modulation in macrophages, attenuating disease severity in DSS-induced [[colitis|colitis]] models and restoring colon length while reducing IFN-γ and IL-2 levels.⁶ In sepsis-induced multi-organ dysfunction, bioengineered hybrid spheroids integrating mesenchymal stem cells with metformin-loaded microspheres—termed [[mscargo|MSCargo]]—suppress NF-κB signaling downstream of [[tlr4|TLR4]], reducing oxidative stress and inflammatory cytokine-induced hepatocyte damage while enhancing hepatic, renal, and pulmonary regeneration.⁵ In diabetic wound healing, [[glabridin|glabridin]]-loaded eutectogels scavenge reactive oxygen species to suppress NF-κB activation, ameliorating the immune microenvironment and achieving 94.3% wound closure by day 21 with over 99% antibacterial efficacy against S. aureus and E. coli.⁷

In autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease caused by UMOD mutations, the p.His36Tyr variant activates an amyloid precursor protein-[[cd74|CD74]] axis that sustains NF-κB pathway activation in macrophages, initiating pyroptosis and pro-inflammatory cytokine release that drives progressive renal interstitial inflammation and fibrosis—a cascade pharmacologically interrupted by App inhibitor ARN2966.¹²

## Cancer applications

In invasive lobular carcinoma, the pan-lysyl-oxidase inhibitor PXS-5505 downregulates NF-κB transcriptional programs alongside MYC and [[ap-1|AP-1]], disrupting a collagen-integrin-MYC axis that sustains tumor expansion and metastatic seeding in estrogen receptor-positive and triple-negative models.² In diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, malignant B cells enriched with NF-κB signaling follow a developmental trajectory mirroring germ layer progression, with NF-κB activation defining a progenitor state that differentiates into organotrophic metastatic branches through either sustained T-cell activation or upregulated B-cell receptor signaling.¹¹ This germ layer-dependent specification of extranodal invasion correlates with inferior progression-free survival and overall survival following R-CHOP treatment, particularly in mesoderm-originating extranodal invasion.

## Non-inflammatory contexts

Beyond canonical inflammatory signaling, NF-κB directly regulates synaptic protein expression and [[ampar|AMPAR]] trafficking in hippocampal neurons. In Alzheimer disease models, rhythmic ultrasound neuromodulation using piezoelectric nanoparticles activates NF-κB transcription factor function during gamma-frequency stimulation, upregulating P300-mediated histone acetylation and rebalancing synaptic internalization and delivery mechanisms.⁹ This restores impaired gamma oscillations and memory-related network connectivity, revealing NF-κB as a bidirectional modulator of synaptic plasticity rather than a unilateral inflammatory driver.

---

## Therapeutic approaches

Multiple therapeutic modalities effectively suppress NF-κB signaling across disease contexts. Natural bioactive compounds—including [[indole-3-propionic-acid|indole-3-propionic acid]], [[astragaloside-iv|Astragaloside IV]], and [[glabridin|glabridin]]—exploit metabolic and immunomodulatory pathways to inhibit NF-κB activation.¹⁴⁷ Small-molecule PTPRO inhibitors such as [[compound-10j|compound 10j]] provide oral bioavailability and enhanced in vivo efficacy with favorable safety profiles for inflammatory bowel disease.⁶ Nanotherapeutics represent an emerging class of NF-κB modulators: [[molybdenum-nanodots|molybdenum nanodots]] scavenge reactive oxygen species and curb NF-κB-mediated M1 macrophage polarization to attenuate implant-associated osteolysis,⁸ while [[ga-mbgn|gallium-doped mesoporous bioactive glass nanoparticles]] suppress NF-κB signaling to promote reparative dentin formation in vital pulp therapy.¹⁰ Neuromodulation strategies, including electroacupuncture and piezoelectric nanoparticle-driven ultrasound, exploit the [[sirt1|SIRT1]]-NF-κB axis to enhance tissue repair and synaptic plasticity in central nervous system disorders.³⁹ The NF-κB inhibitor [[pdtc|PDTC]] serves as a pharmacological tool to dissect pathway contributions in experimental models.³
