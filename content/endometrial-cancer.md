**Endometrial cancer** is a malignancy of the uterine lining traditionally managed through surgical hysterectomy with pelvic lymph node dissection, the gold-standard approach for staging and primary tumor control.¹ Over the past decade, robotic-assisted surgical platforms have substantially transformed the surgical landscape, reducing operative time and improving procedural efficiency while maintaining oncologic outcomes.² The advent of DNA methylation-based epigenetic biomarkers, particularly the [[wid-qec|WID-qEC test]] applied to cervico-vaginal samples, now enables non-invasive detection with validated accuracy for endometrial and cervical malignancies.¹ However, this molecular test exhibits clinically important cross-reactivity with [[ovarian-cancer|ovarian cancer]], requiring integrated diagnostic triage strategies.

---

## Surgical management

Hysterectomy with pelvic lymph node dissection represents the standard surgical treatment for endometrial cancer, permitting both tumor extirpation and accurate pathological staging to guide adjuvant therapy decisions. The choice of surgical approach—open, laparoscopic, or robotic-assisted—has been historically driven by surgeon training, patient factors, and institutional resources. Recent advances in minimally invasive techniques have expanded the options available for safe and effective management of endometrial carcinoma across diverse surgical settings.

---

## Robotic-assisted surgery adoption

Robotic-assisted hysterectomy for endometrial cancer with pelvic lymph node dissection has become increasingly prevalent, with adoption rising from 6.1% to 38.2% of all benign and malignant hysterectomies over the past decade at major medical centers.² For endometrial carcinoma specifically, robotic approaches reduced operative time by 48.5 minutes compared to open procedures, a clinically meaningful improvement in surgical efficiency.² While robotic and laparoscopic techniques yield comparable perioperative morbidity and complication rates, robotic surgery incurs additional costs of approximately 2.79 thousand USD for benign procedures. However, for the malignant setting with lymphadenectomy, the operative time savings may offset such costs through reduced operating room utilization. Notably, robotic surgery adoption has expanded even more dramatically for [[cervical-cancer|cervical cancer]] hysterectomy, increasing from 12.5% to 55.9%, establishing robotic-assisted approaches as co-dominant with open surgery in the cervical cancer therapeutic repertoire.²

---

## Molecular detection via DNA methylation

The [[wid-qec|WID-qEC test]], a DNA methylation-based molecular assay applied to cervico-vaginal samples, has been validated for accurate detection of endometrial and cervical cancers without direct tissue sampling.¹ The test identifies aberrant epigenetic patterns characteristic of malignant transformation, offering a non-invasive screening or diagnostic modality. Among 1,269 cervico-vaginal samples from women without endometrial or cervical cancer—comprising healthy controls, women with benign gynecological conditions, and those with ovarian cancer—80 women tested WID-qEC positive despite absence of endometrial or cervical pathology.¹ This finding prompted investigation into the clinical significance of discordant positive results and the utility of the test in broader malignancy screening contexts.

---

## Cross-reactivity with ovarian cancer

Strikingly, 54% of WID-qEC-positive results in the absence of endometrial or cervical cancer (43 of 80 cases) originated from women with [[ovarian-cancer|ovarian cancer]], revealing an unexpected association between the endometrial-cancer-designed methylation signature and ovarian malignancy.¹ WID-qEC positivity was independently associated with ovarian cancer presence (adjusted odds ratio 2.93; 95% CI 1.75–4.95) and with a higher lifetime number of ovulatory cycles (adjusted odds ratio 2.67; 95% CI 1.06–7.50), a recognized ovarian cancer risk factor.¹ These associations persisted regardless of age, menopausal status, hormone replacement therapy use, or family history of breast or ovarian cancer, suggesting a biologically coherent mechanism linking ovarian cancer risk to the methylation patterns detected by WID-qEC. While the standalone positive predictive value for ovarian cancer in the general population remains modest, the findings outline a two-step triage paradigm whereby WID-qEC positivity absent endometrial or cervical malignancy prompts investigation for occult ovarian neoplasia or elevated ovarian cancer risk, potentially informing both diagnostic workup and preventive strategies in high-risk populations.
