**Acute lung injury (ALI)** is a severe, often life-threatening inflammatory lung disease characterized by excessive production of [[ros|reactive oxygen species]], disruption of the endothelial and epithelial barriers, and accumulation of pro-inflammatory mediators in pulmonary tissue. ALI represents both the most prevalent and most fatal manifestation of [[sepsis]], occurring when dysregulated immune responses overwhelm protective mechanisms and drive multi-organ dysfunction.¹ The disease is initiated by bacterial products such as [[lipopolysaccharide|lipopolysaccharide (LPS)]], which activate pattern recognition receptors on immune and stromal cells, triggering cascades of oxidative damage and inflammatory amplification. Recent advances in multi-mechanistic nanoparticle therapeutics—combining antimicrobial, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory functions—have demonstrated substantial improvements in survival outcomes in preclinical sepsis models.

---

## Mechanism

The pathophysiology of ALI centers on uncontrolled [[ros|reactive oxygen species]] generation coupled with dysregulation of antioxidant defenses. Bacterial [[lipopolysaccharide|endotoxin (LPS)]] activates toll-like receptors on resident lung macrophages and circulating immune cells, initiating a pro-inflammatory cascade that releases tumor necrosis factor-α, interleukins, and other pro-inflammatory mediators into the pulmonary microenvironment.¹ These mediators simultaneously increase vascular permeability, trigger [[ros|ROS]]-producing NADPH oxidases, and suppress epithelial tight junction proteins, enabling protein-rich fluid to leak into the alveolar space. The resultant oxidative stress damages lipid membranes of endothelial and epithelial cells, intensifying barrier dysfunction and promoting recruitment of additional neutrophils and macrophages. This self-amplifying loop of microbial products, immune activation, and oxidative damage leads to progressive respiratory failure and, without intervention, death.

---

## ALI in Sepsis

[[Sepsis]]-induced ALI occurs when systemic infection triggers pulmonary inflammation disproportionate to local microbial burden, reflecting dysregulated host immune responses. Polymicrobial infection—such as in abdominal trauma or peritonitis—drives particularly severe ALI through continuous release of bacterial endotoxins and pathogen-associated molecular patterns that sustain the inflammatory cascade. The combination of high [[ros|ROS]] production, endothelial and epithelial cell necrosis, and impaired gas exchange leads to profound hypoxemia and mechanical ventilation dependence. Traditional supportive care addresses symptoms but does not target the underlying pathogenic mechanisms of endotoxin-driven inflammation and oxidative injury, explaining the persistently high mortality in ALI and [[sepsis]]-associated acute respiratory distress syndrome.

## Multi-mechanistic nanoparticle therapeutics

Recent advances employ composite nanoparticles targeting multiple pathogenic pathways simultaneously. [[Pv-nps|Polymyxin B-antioxidant nanoassemblies (PV NPs)]] integrate bactericidal activity of [[polymyxin-b|polymyxin B]], direct neutralization of bacterial [[lipopolysaccharide|LPS]], and broad-spectrum [[ros|ROS]] scavenging via a conjugated α-tocopherol-gallic acid moiety.¹ In murine models of LPS-induced ALI, PV NPs potently attenuate lung inflammation and oxidative stress. Most strikingly, in the cecal ligation-and-puncture (CLP) model of polymicrobial sepsis, PV NPs achieve 75% survival rates, substantially outperforming either component alone and significantly exceeding standard antimicrobial or antioxidant monotherapies.¹ This exceptional efficacy reflects complementary actions of pathogen elimination and ROS scavenging working in concert to restore barrier function and dampen inflammatory amplification.

An additional advantage of PV NPs is their improved safety profile relative to [[polymyxin-b|polymyxin B]] monotherapy, which exhibits dose-limiting nephrotoxicity. By conjugating the antioxidant into the nanoparticle assembly and optimizing surface properties, PV NPs reduce accumulation in the kidney while prolonging pulmonary retention, enabling higher effective doses without renal injury.¹ This combination of superior efficacy and reduced systemic toxicity positions PV NPs as a clinically translatable strategy addressing ALI's multi-mechanistic pathogenesis.

## mRNA therapeutics and lung-targeted delivery

Emerging [[mrna|mRNA]] delivery platforms offer complementary anti-inflammatory approaches by encoding therapeutic proteins directly within target cells. Lung-targeted [[lipid-nanoparticles|lipid nanoparticles (LNPs)]] formulated with ionizable cationic lipids achieve exceptional pulmonary specificity—up to 99% targeting efficiency—enabling direct mRNA delivery to endothelial and epithelial cells.² These optimized LNPs efficiently transduce target cells, achieving gene editing efficiencies of approximately 70% while demonstrating remarkable tolerability even at high doses (12 mg/kg) and retention of activity across repeated administrations.² The sustained transduction efficiency across multiple doses positions repeated mRNA therapeutics as viable for prolonged treatment protocols.

In ALI models, delivery of [[il-10|IL-10 mRNA]] via lung-targeted LNPs substantially reduces the inflammatory response through suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokine production and promotion of regulatory immune cell populations.² The mRNA platform's modularity enables co-delivery of gene-editing machinery—adenine base editor mRNA with guide RNAs—or silencing RNAs targeting pro-inflammatory transcripts, suggesting broad potential for rational reprogramming of the inflammatory lung environment in ALI. These advances represent a paradigm shift from single-mechanism interventions toward simultaneous targeting of multiple pathogenic pathways.
