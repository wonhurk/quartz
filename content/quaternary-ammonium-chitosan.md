**Quaternary ammonium chitosan (QCS)**, also designated HACC, is a permanently cationic [[chitosan]] derivative that retains stable positive charge across physiological and near-neutral pH ranges. This property overcomes a critical limitation of conventional [[chitosan]], which exhibits pH-dependent charge and exhibits reduced functionality outside acidic environments.¹ QCS has emerged as a versatile material platform spanning two distinct applications: flexible wearable sensors fabricated through dual-network composite hydrogels, and mucoadhesive coatings for intranasal pharmaceutical delivery systems.

---

## Mechanism and pH Independence

Quaternization of chitosan introduces permanent ammonium functional groups that maintain positive charge independent of environmental pH. While unmodified [[chitosan]] relies on protonation of free amino groups for charge and loses effectiveness at physiological pH, QCS carries an intrinsic cationic character across the full spectrum of physiological conditions.¹ This pH-independent charge is critical for physiological applications where conventional chitosan would suffer loss of bioactivity. The permanent positive charge drives strong electrostatic interactions with negatively charged biological surfaces, including [[mucin]] and tissue membranes, enabling mucoadhesion that remains effective without reliance on pH adjustment.

---

## Flexible Wearable Sensors

QCS has been successfully integrated into dual-network composite hydrogels through photopolymerization, combining [[poly-vinyl-alcohol]] (PVA), acrylic acid, [[gelatin]], and conductive fillers including acetylene black.² The resulting material demonstrates exceptional mechanical robustness: a tensile strength of 5.49 MPa with 736% elongation at break, coupled with electrical conductivity of 0.83 S/m.² These properties enable fabrication of flexible strain sensors capable of linear response across a broad sensing range of 0–500% strain with outstanding cyclic stability exceeding 2000 cycles and rapid dynamic response of 336 milliseconds.² The sensors maintain functionality even under frigid conditions, with excellent freeze resistance and long-term water retention, making them suitable for continuous real-time monitoring of human motion including finger flexion, wrist rotation, elbow extension, knee articulation, and dynamic writing trajectory capture.²

## Intranasal Drug Delivery

QCS-coated [[liposomes]] represent a major advance in intranasal pharmaceutical delivery, particularly for treating [[allergic-rhinitis]]. In this application, the permanent positive charge of QCS-modified liposomes creates superior mucoadhesion compared to unmodified carriers, exhibiting 5.26-fold higher mucin-binding affinity.¹ Liposomes coated with QCS encapsulating mometasone furoate (QCS-MF-Lip) achieve approximately 4.7-fold greater ex vivo mucosal retention (19.59 μg·cm⁻²) compared with the commercial formulation Nasonex®, establishing a sustained local depot at the site of nasal administration.¹ In rat models of [[allergic-rhinitis]], QCS-MF-Lip significantly outperformed the commercial spray, reducing histamine levels and enhancing anti-inflammatory effects to achieve superior therapeutic control of allergic symptoms.¹ The particle size optimization to 170 ± 1 nm with a narrow polydispersity index of 0.232 ± 0.011 further supports efficient nasal deposition and bioavailability.

---

## Biocompatibility and Stability

As a chitosan derivative, QCS inherits the excellent biocompatibility and biodegradability of its parent polymer. Its enhanced mechanical and thermal stability in composite hydrogel systems — including freeze resistance and sustained water retention over extended periods — makes it suitable for both implantable and topical biomedical devices. The synergy between QCS's biological tolerance and its persistent cationic functionality positions it as a platform technology for next-generation drug delivery and sensing applications.
