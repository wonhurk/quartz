**Heart failure** is a clinical syndrome of cardiac dysfunction characterized by [[adverse cardiac remodeling|cardiac remodeling]] and reduced cardiac output, arising from diverse etiologies including [[acute-myocardial-infarction|myocardial infarction]], metabolic disturbances, kidney disease, and hypertension. The pathophysiology is fundamentally linked to dysregulated innate immunity; specifically, abnormal [[macrophage]] polarization in the post-infarction milieu drives fibrotic remodeling and subsequent ventricular dysfunction. Recent mechanistic insights identify [[siglec-e|Siglec-E]], an immunoregulatory receptor on myeloid cells, as a critical modulator of this process, offering both preventive targets and safety considerations for emerging therapies.¹ Heart failure stratification by left ventricular ejection fraction — preserved (HFpEF), mildly reduced (HFmrEF), and reduced (HFrEF) — defines distinct pathophysiological phenotypes and treatment responses.

---

## Mechanism

Adverse [[cardiac-remodeling|cardiac remodeling]] after [[acute-myocardial-infarction|myocardial infarction]] initiates through macrophage-driven inflammatory and fibrotic cascades. [[siglec-e|Siglec-E]], a transmembrane glycoprotein expressed on myeloid cells, regulates macrophage phenotype by engaging inhibitory signaling pathways that promote anti-inflammatory polarization. In murine models of permanent coronary artery occlusion, deletion of Siglec-E dramatically worsens post-infarction outcomes: Siglec-E–deficient mice exhibit impaired left ventricular ejection fraction, increased infarct size, exacerbated fibrosis, larger ventricular volumes, and reduced survival compared to wild-type littermates.¹ Conversely, intact Siglec-E signaling licenses a reparative macrophage phenotype that attenuates inflammatory damage and preserves cardiac function. Notably, the human homolog [[siglec-e|Siglec-9]] shows parallel upregulation in monocytes during acute myocardial infarction, demonstrating conserved immunoregulatory mechanisms across species and validating findings in human cohorts.¹

## Classification by ejection fraction

Heart failure phenotypes are stratified by left ventricular ejection fraction, reflecting distinct pathophysiological substrates. Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) comprises roughly half of all clinical heart failure cases and is particularly prevalent in older women with hypertension and metabolic syndrome. Mildly reduced ejection fraction (HFmrEF) represents an intermediate category, while reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF) involves severe systolic dysfunction and carries the highest mortality risk. This phenotypic stratification has emerged as critical for treatment selection, as many therapies — particularly novel mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists — demonstrate efficacy differentially across these categories.

## Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonism

[[finerenone|Finerenone]], a non-steroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA), has demonstrated efficacy in reducing heart failure hospitalizations and cardiovascular mortality in populations with mildly reduced and preserved ejection fractions. In the FINEARTS-HF trial, finerenone reduced the composite endpoint of heart failure events and cardiovascular death in patients with HFmrEF and HFpEF, populations historically underserved by cardioprotective therapies.² Bayesian analysis of the trial data provides probabilistic estimates of treatment benefit, enabling clinicians to assess the magnitude and credibility of finerenone's protective effect in these phenotypes.² The drug targets aldosterone-driven inflammation and fibrosis without the hyperkalaemia and renal dysfunction risks that limit traditional steroidal MRAs, expanding therapeutic options in older and metabolically complex patients.

## Risk stratification: cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic syndrome

Suboptimal [[ckm-syndrome|cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic (CKM) health]] is highly prevalent in older adults and emerges as a powerful predictor of incident heart failure. The CKM syndrome staging framework integrates cardiometabolic, renal, and metabolic parameters to stratify individuals into risk categories. In the Atherosclerosis Risk in Communities (ARIC) study, CKM staging demonstrated strong discriminatory value for predicting incident heart failure in aging populations, particularly those with concurrent hypertension, diabetes, obesity, and reduced glomerular filtration rate.³ This multidimensional approach moves beyond single biomarkers, recognizing that heart failure arises at the intersection of cardiovascular, renal, and metabolic dysfunction — a paradigm shift toward integrated prevention strategies targeting all three domains simultaneously.

## Acute heart failure detection: artificial intelligence

Acute heart failure with [[pulmonary-congestion|pulmonary congestion]] presents diagnostic challenges in emergency settings, where rapid discrimination from other causes of dyspnea is critical. [[artificial-intelligence|Artificial intelligence]] models trained on chest computed tomography (CT) images automatically detect imaging signatures of acute heart failure, including [[pulmonary-congestion|pulmonary congestion]], cardiomegaly, and signs of cardiac decompensation.⁴ In prospective validation studies, AI-based imaging analysis demonstrates performance competitive with expert radiologist and cardiologist interpretations, identifying patients who would benefit from advanced diagnostic imaging and cardiology consultation without requiring radiologist availability.⁴ This approach addresses the bottleneck of radiologist reporting capacity and enables rapid risk stratification in high-volume emergency departments, particularly valuable in resource-limited settings.
