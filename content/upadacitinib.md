**Upadacitinib** is an oral selective [[janus-kinase|Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor]] approved by the FDA for the treatment of moderate-to-severe [[atopic-dermatitis|atopic dermatitis (AD)]], a chronic immune-mediated inflammatory skin disease characterized by intense pruritus, eczematous rash, and skin pain. The drug selectively inhibits JAK signaling pathways that drive Th2 and Th17 immune responses, suppressing the inflammatory cascades underlying disease pathophysiology.¹ Long-term clinical data spanning 140 weeks from the Measure Up 1 and Measure Up 2 pivotal trials demonstrate sustained therapeutic efficacy with comprehensive improvements across patient-reported outcomes, extending therapeutic benefit beyond symptom control to meaningful gains in quality of life, sleep quality, and mental health.

---

## Mechanism of action

Upadacitinib operates through selective inhibition of [[janus-kinase|JAK]] signaling, disrupting the intracellular cascade that propagates immune-mediated inflammation in atopic dermatitis. The drug targets the IL-4/STAT6/Th2 and IL-6/STAT3/Th17 inflammatory axes that are pathogenic in AD, preventing activated immune cells from transmitting inflammatory signals that would otherwise perpetuate the chronic itch-scratch cycle and skin barrier dysfunction.¹ As a once-daily oral agent, upadacitinib offers patients improved adherence and convenience compared to injectable or topical alternatives, addressing the considerable treatment burden in a chronically relapsing disease.

---

## Clinical efficacy in atopic dermatitis

Upadacitinib has emerged as an established therapeutic standard for moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis, supported by rigorous long-term clinical trial data. The Measure Up 1 and Measure Up 2 trials tracked patient outcomes over 140 weeks, demonstrating sustained therapeutic benefit and deep clinical responses in disease control.¹ Beyond traditional clinical endpoints, the trials specifically measured patient-reported outcomes across multiple domains reflecting the patient's lived experience with the disease.

---

## Patient-reported outcomes

A cornerstone of upadacitinib's clinical value is its comprehensive impact on patients' functional well-being and psychological health. Treatment significantly reduced pruritus—the defining symptom of atopic dermatitis and primary driver of disease burden—alongside improvements in skin pain, a frequently overlooked but clinically significant symptom causing patient distress.¹ Quality of life improvements were substantial and sustained, with patients reporting meaningful gains in sleep quality, a domain severely compromised by nocturnal itch in untreated disease. Notably, upadacitinib reduced symptoms of [[anxiety]] and depression, which are elevated in AD populations and often persist despite clinical resolution of skin lesions.¹ These mental health benefits reflect both direct anti-inflammatory effects on neuroinflammation and indirect benefits of successful itch control, highlighting the holistic therapeutic impact of effective AD treatment on patients' overall well-being.

---

## Comparative efficacy and emerging alternatives

Upadacitinib has become the established comparator standard in clinical trials evaluating newer anti-inflammatory agents for atopic dermatitis. Emerging [[parp14|PARP14 inhibitors]] represent a novel pharmacological approach to AD, targeting mono-ADP-ribosyltransferase activity within the same IL-4/STAT6 and IL-6/STAT3 inflammatory pathways central to disease pathogenesis.² Novel [[parp14|PARP14 inhibitor]] compounds have demonstrated superior efficacy in preclinical models of atopic dermatitis relative to upadacitinib, positioning JAK and PARP14 inhibition as complementary strategies within the expanding therapeutic landscape of AD.² As JAK inhibition becomes increasingly established as a standard-of-care approach, comparative trials continue to define the relative roles of distinct anti-inflammatory targets in optimizing patient outcomes.
