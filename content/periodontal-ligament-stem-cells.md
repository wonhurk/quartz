**Periodontal Ligament Stem Cells (PDLSCs)** are mesenchymal stem cells derived from periodontal ligament tissue that function as a critical cellular resource for regenerative medicine, capable of sustaining high viability and proliferative capacity within responsive biomaterial matrices.¹ These cells orchestrate tissue repair through multiple synergistic mechanisms: intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavenging that protects healing tissues from oxidative stress, reprogramming of tissue macrophages toward pro-regenerative phenotypes that dampen chronic inflammation, and direct support of [[angiogenesis]] to establish functional blood supply in regenerating tissues.¹² PDLSCs have emerged as particularly valuable in two distinct therapeutic contexts — scarless diabetic wound repair and guided periodontal bone regeneration — where their biological activities can be harnessed directly or through a cell-free exosome-based approach.

---

## Mechanism

PDLSCs maintain regenerative capacity through active metabolic and immunomodulatory functions. When encapsulated within interpenetrating polymer networks or loaded onto electrospun scaffolds, these cells preserve high viability while simultaneously activating intracellular ROS-scavenging pathways that counter oxidative stress in damaged microenvironments.¹ A critical function of encapsulated PDLSCs is their ability to reprogram tissue-resident [[macrophage]]s away from pro-inflammatory M1 phenotypes toward pro-regenerative M2 phenotypes, thereby reducing chronic inflammation and creating permissive conditions for tissue remodeling.¹ PDLSCs also support revascularization by promoting [[angiogenesis]], establishing functional microvascular networks essential for sustained tissue regeneration.² Additionally, PDLSCs secrete bioactive [[exosome]]s — nano-sized extracellular vesicles that encapsulate regenerative signaling molecules — which can function as cell-free alternatives for tissue engineering applications.

---

## Diabetic wound healing

Chronic diabetic wounds present a formidable therapeutic challenge due to pathological elevation of reactive oxygen species, chronic inflammatory infiltrates, and impaired angiogenesis that together derail the normal wound-healing cascade. PDLSCs address this multi-factorial pathology when integrated into dual-responsive smart hydrogels that dynamically sense the pathological microenvironment. An interpenetrating polymer network composed of phenylboric acid-modified gelatin, oxidized pullulan, and bioactive polyphenols creates a matrix that responds to pathologically elevated pH and ROS levels — enabling on-demand release of antimicrobial and antioxidant payloads.¹ Within this engineered microenvironment, encapsulated PDLSCs maintain high proliferative capacity while simultaneously scavenging intracellular ROS and reprogramming infiltrating macrophages toward regenerative phenotypes.¹ The result is multi-modal tissue repair: accelerated wound closure, enhanced angiogenesis, reduced scar formation, and downregulation of pro-fibrotic gene signatures — delivering scarless healing in diabetic wound models.¹

## Periodontal bone regeneration

Periodontitis, surgical trauma, and congenital defects result in irreversible loss of periodontal bone and ligament attachment, leading to progressive tooth mobility and tooth loss. PDLSCs represent a crucial cellular resource for reconstituting lost periodontal tissues, with two distinct therapeutic strategies emerging. The direct cellular approach uses PDLSCs seeded onto supportive scaffolds; however, a cell-free exosome-derived strategy has proven equally efficacious and operationally simpler.² PDLSC-derived [[exosome]]s encapsulate bioactive regenerative cargo that directly instructs bone progenitor cell recruitment, proliferation, and osteogenic differentiation, representing a promising cell-free therapeutic alternative to cell transplantation.²

Recent advances employ PDLSC-derived exosomes loaded onto biodegradable piezoelectric fibrous membranes composed of poly(L-lactide) and gelatin, fabricated by electrospinning and coated with polydopamine for exosome binding and controlled release.² The piezoelectric properties of the polarized polymer matrix create biomimetic electromechanical stimulation mimicking native bone's electrophysiological environment, while the PDLSC-derived exosomes supply soluble regenerative signals. This synergy — combining material-based electromechanical cues with bioactive exosome signaling — creates a microenvironment optimized for cell recruitment, proliferation, angiogenic differentiation, and bone matrix deposition.² In rat periodontal defect models, piezoelectric membranes functionalized with PDLSC-derived exosomes significantly accelerated bone regeneration compared to membranes lacking either exosomes or piezoelectric properties, demonstrating the power of mechanistic synergy in guided tissue regeneration.

---

## Cell-free therapeutics and platform innovation

The adoption of PDLSC-derived [[exosome]]s rather than whole cells offers multiple practical advantages: enhanced shelf-life, reduced immunogenicity, simplified manufacturing, and scaled therapeutic potential without cell maintenance overhead.² [[Exosome]]-based platforms are particularly well-suited for integration into advanced biomaterial systems that provide both structural support and bioactive signaling, such as piezoelectric membranes for bone regeneration. Future refinement may expand exosome loading onto other responsive or electromechanical matrices, broadening the therapeutic scope of this cell-free approach across multiple regenerative medicine applications.
