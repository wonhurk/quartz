**CD44** is a multifunctional cell surface receptor that promotes tumor progression and immune suppression across multiple cancer types through ligand-mediated signaling cascades. The protein binds tumor-secreted ligands including [[serglycin]] and [[endotrophin]], which activate intracellular pathways involving [[stat3|STAT3]], [[smad3|SMAD3]], and TGF-β receptor signaling to drive malignant phenotypes.¹ ² CD44 is highly expressed on T lymphocytes—particularly regulatory T cells—and on cancer cells including triple-negative breast cancer, where it sustains immunosuppression, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and chemotherapy resistance. Beyond its protumoral functions, CD44 serves as a therapeutic targeting ligand exploited by [[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid]]-functionalized nanoparticles to enhance intratumoral drug delivery.³

---

## Ligand-receptor signaling

CD44 transduces extracellular signals from the tumor microenvironment through binding of tumor-derived ligands. In [[gastric-adenocarcinoma|gastric cancer]], tumor cell-secreted [[serglycin]] binds CD44 on regulatory T cells, activating the CD44–TGF-βRI–[[smad3|SMAD3]] signaling pathway and triggering a metabolic shift toward glycolysis while facilitating reactive oxygen species clearance.¹ This signaling cascade reduces oxidative modification at the [[lag3|LAG3]] methionine 171 site, enhancing LAG3 dimerization and suppressing its K48-linked ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation via disrupted PELI1 interaction. In [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma]], hepatic stellate cell-derived [[endotrophin]]—a cleavage fragment of collagen VI α3—binds CD44 on pericentral tumor cells, activating [[stat3|STAT3]] signaling to promote epithelial-mesenchymal transition, proliferation, and sorafenib resistance.² This ETP-CD44-STAT3 axis establishes a feed-forward loop in which STAT3-driven COL6A3 expression sustains endotrophin production, linking fibroinflammation to malignant progression.

---

## Immune suppression in gastric cancer

The [[serglycin]]-CD44 axis drives differentiation of a specialized subset of [[lag3|lymphocyte activation gene 3 (LAG3)]]-positive regulatory T cells and maintains their immunosuppressive function in [[gastric-adenocarcinoma|gastric cancer]].¹ Single-cell RNA sequencing of paired tumor and adjacent normal gastric mucosa revealed that Tregs were enriched in tumor tissues and their abundance correlated with [[serglycin]] expression levels. Functionally, serglycin-CD44 signaling promoted LAG3 upregulation on Tregs by attenuating oxidative modification and subsequent degradation, resulting in sustained suppressive activity. Blocking serglycin-CD44 signaling suppressed LAG3+ Treg differentiation and function, relieved immunosuppression in the tumor microenvironment, and inhibited tumor progression in preclinical models, establishing this axis as a target for restoring antitumor immunity.

---

## Tumor-stroma cross-talk in liver cancer

In [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma]], the [[endotrophin]]-CD44-[[stat3|STAT3]] axis mediates heterotypic signaling between stromal and cancer cells, facilitating malignant progression.² Endotrophin produced by hepatic stellate cells targets CD44-expressing tumor cells, driving epithelial-mesenchymal transition, proliferation, and resistance to sorafenib. This signaling induces a STAT3-dependent feedback loop in which tumor cells upregulate COL6A3 expression, sustaining endotrophin production and perpetuating tumor-stroma cross-talk. Disruption of this axis by CD44 knockout, STAT3 inhibition, or CD44 binding-deficient endotrophin mutants suppressed malignant phenotypes in vitro. In metabolic dysfunction-associated HCC induced by diethylnitrosamine plus high-fat diet, dual knockout of Col6a3 and Cd44 markedly reduced tumor burden, restored sorafenib sensitivity, and attenuated fibrosis and steatotic-fibrotic niche formation, validating the therapeutic relevance of targeting this pathway in obesity-associated liver cancer.

## Targeted drug delivery via hyaluronic acid

CD44 overexpression on cancer cells enables [[hyaluronic-acid|hyaluronic acid (HA)]]-mediated active targeting for nanoparticle-based drug delivery systems. In colorectal cancer, [[ha-pgc|HA-functionalized copper metal-organic frameworks]] exploit CD44-mediated endocytosis to enhance tumor accumulation and enable intratumoral activation of ferroptosis-inducing agents.³ The HA shell facilitates CD44 binding, promoting nanoparticle uptake and localized delivery of xCT inhibitors that deplete glutathione and amplify lipid peroxidation, resulting in ferroptotic cell death and enhanced CD8+ T-cell infiltration. Similarly, in triple-negative breast cancer, biodegradable HA-coated polymersomes encapsulating gemcitabine and copper peroxide nanoparticles target CD44-overexpressing tumor cells, generating reactive oxygen species that suppress cytidine deaminase, enhance gemcitabine activation, and promote immunogenic cell death.⁴ This converts immunologically "cold" tumors into "hot" tumors, significantly improving anti-PD-L1 immunotherapy efficacy. Additional platforms including HA-shelled nanoparticles for mitochondrial-targeted cuproptosis and hydrogen sulfide gas therapy further exploit CD44 for tumor-selective delivery and enhanced therapeutic outcomes.⁵

---

## Therapeutic disruption strategies

CD44 knockout or pharmacological blockade suppresses tumor growth, restores chemotherapy sensitivity, and reduces disease burden across multiple preclinical models. In [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|HCC]], genetic ablation of Cd44 combined with Col6a3 deletion markedly reduced tumor burden and reversed sorafenib resistance, demonstrating that disruption of the endotrophin-CD44 axis can overcome therapeutic resistance mechanisms.² Antibody-mediated blockade of serglycin-CD44 signaling in [[gastric-adenocarcinoma|gastric cancer]] suppressed LAG3+ regulatory T cell differentiation, relieved immunosuppression, and inhibited tumor progression, highlighting CD44 as a node for immune checkpoint intervention.¹ These findings establish CD44 as both a protumoral oncogenic driver and an attractive therapeutic target whose inhibition can simultaneously disrupt tumor cell signaling, restore drug sensitivity, and enhance antitumor immune responses.
