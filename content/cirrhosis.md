**Cirrhosis** is the final stage of [[chronic-liver-disease|chronic liver disease]], characterized by extensive hepatic fibrosis and structural remodeling that represent the major determinant of liver-related morbidity and mortality.¹ [[masld|Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD)]] has emerged as the most prevalent chronic liver disease worldwide, with an increasing proportion of patients progressing through advanced fibrosis to cirrhosis and its most serious complication, [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]].¹ Early identification of individuals at high risk for advanced fibrosis and cirrhosis is essential for preventing liver-related events, halting disease progression, and improving long-term patient outcomes through timely specialist intervention and targeted therapeutic management.

---

## Mechanism

Cirrhosis develops through progressive accumulation of fibrous tissue within the liver parenchyma, ultimately leading to architectural distortion and loss of normal hepatic function. The transition from simple steatosis to advanced fibrosis and cirrhosis is driven by metabolic dysfunction, characterized by abnormal glucose and lipid metabolism coupled with lipid accumulation within hepatocytes. This process is accelerated in the setting of [[masld|MASLD]], where metabolic abnormalities trigger hepatocellular injury, inflammation, and the activation of hepatic stellate cells—the primary drivers of collagen deposition and fibril formation. Once fibrotic tissue extensively replaces normal liver parenchyma and architectural distortion occurs, the disease enters the cirrhotic stage, at which point hepatic function becomes severely compromised and the risk of life-threatening complications, including portal hypertension, variceal bleeding, hepatic encephalopathy, and malignant transformation, increases substantially.

## Clinical diagnosis and risk stratification

Accurate identification of patients with advanced fibrosis and cirrhosis relies on a tiered diagnostic approach combining noninvasive assessment tools, biochemical markers, imaging modalities, and clinical evaluation. The [[fib-4-index|Fibrosis-4 (FIB-4) Index]] is the widely accessible and reliable first-line noninvasive tool for initial fibrosis risk stratification in both primary care and specialist settings.¹ The FIB-4 Index, calculated from patient age, aspartate aminotransferase, alanine aminotransferase, and platelet count, provides rapid assessment without the need for specialized equipment or radiological intervention. Patients with elevated FIB-4 values or clinical features suggestive of progressive liver injury, including jaundice, ascites, or stigmata of portal hypertension, should undergo secondary assessment with elastography—a quantitative method for measuring liver stiffness—and be referred to a hepatology specialist when appropriate.¹ Liver biopsy, while historically the gold standard for fibrosis staging, is no longer routinely required for diagnosis in most patients with [[masld|MASLD]] but remains an important adjunct when establishing a definitive diagnosis of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), assessing inflammatory activity, or clarifying ambiguous noninvasive findings.

## Ultrasound imaging and quality enhancement

Ultrasound is the first-line imaging modality for assessing the cirrhotic liver and detecting associated complications, particularly [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinomas]] in surveillance programs.² However, image quality limitations—arising from aging ultrasound equipment, suboptimal operator technique, or challenging patient anatomy—can significantly impair diagnostic accuracy and clinical decision-making. Deep learning approaches, specifically switchable cycle generative adversarial networks (CycleGAN) trained in an unsupervised manner, have demonstrated remarkable potential for enhancing liver ultrasound image quality without requiring paired reference images.² When trained on images from older, lower-performing ultrasound devices as inputs and high-quality images from newer devices as targets, CycleGAN-based algorithms significantly improve brightness, contrast, and overall diagnostic quality of reconstructed liver ultrasound images.² This technological advancement enables healthcare systems to extend the diagnostic lifespan of aging imaging equipment and overcome resource constraints, particularly in resource-limited settings, thereby enhancing the accessibility and accuracy of ultrasound-based cirrhosis assessment and hepatocellular carcinoma surveillance.

---

## Complications and progression to HCC

The development of cirrhosis substantially increases the risk of malignant transformation to [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]], which represents one of the most serious complications of end-stage liver disease.¹ Once cirrhosis is diagnosed, patients require lifelong hepatologic surveillance and screening for HCC using imaging-based approaches, including ultrasound combined with contrast-enhanced computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging. The structural derangement and portal hypertension that characterize cirrhosis create an environment of chronic hepatocellular proliferation and inflammation conducive to malignant transformation. Preventing progression to HCC requires aggressive management of the underlying [[masld|MASLD]], optimization of metabolic parameters, and potentially targeted pharmacotherapies that address metabolic dysfunction and reduce the inflammatory and fibrotic burden of advanced liver disease.
