**Castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC)** represents disease progression despite androgen deprivation therapy, persisting through multiple mechanisms of continued or alternative androgen receptor (AR) signaling and downstream transcriptional pathways. Despite castrate levels of circulating testosterone, tumor growth continues via intratumoral androgen synthesis, AR amplification or mutation, and constitutively active AR splice variants including AR-V7.¹ Current therapeutic strategies include second-generation AR inhibitors such as [[enzalutamide]] and [[abiraterone]], PARP inhibitors in homologous recombination repair-deficient tumors, [[cdk9|CDK9]] inhibitors targeting AR-driven transcription, and prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)-directed radioligand therapies including [[lnth-1095|131I-LNTH-1095]] and [[177lu-psma-617|177Lu-PSMA-617]].²⁻⁴

---

## Androgen receptor-driven transcription

CRPC progression fundamentally relies on AR-driven oncogenic transcription, a process requiring [[cdk9|cyclin-dependent kinase 9 (CDK9)]] as a critical cofactor. CDK9 phosphorylates RNA polymerase II at the C-terminal domain, enabling transcriptional elongation of AR target genes.² Structure-based drug design targeting a distinct CDK9 subpocket has yielded selective inhibitors including [[compound-91]], which demonstrates robust antiproliferative activity against CRPC cells expressing AR-V7. [[compound-91|Compound 91]] disrupts Pol II phosphorylation and downregulates short-lived oncoproteins including AR-V7 and Myc, achieving 66% tumor growth inhibition in orthotopic CRPC models with favorable oral bioavailability (F = 41%).²

## Therapeutic strategies

Second-generation AR pathway inhibitors — [[enzalutamide]], a competitive AR antagonist, and [[abiraterone]], a CYP17A1 inhibitor blocking androgen synthesis — represent the backbone of CRPC therapy following progression on initial androgen deprivation. However, resistance inevitably develops through diverse molecular mechanisms.¹ PARP inhibitors including olaparib have demonstrated efficacy in CRPC patients harboring mutations in homologous recombination repair genes such as BRCA1/2, ATM, and PALB2. When combined with [[abiraterone]] and [[prednisone]], olaparib significantly prolongs radiographic progression-free survival in both metastatic castration-resistant and hormone-sensitive disease, with HRR gene mutations predicting enhanced response.³

PSMA-directed radioligand therapy has emerged as a transformative treatment modality. [[lnth-1095|131I-LNTH-1095]], an iodine-131-labeled small molecule targeting PSMA, demonstrates superior efficacy when combined with [[enzalutamide]] compared to enzalutamide monotherapy in patients who progressed on prior [[abiraterone]].⁴ Similarly, [[177lu-psma-617|177Lu-PSMA-617]] represents a clinically validated radioligand therapy for metastatic CRPC, with significant overall survival benefit in PSMA-positive disease.⁵

## Resistance mechanisms

Resistance to [[enzalutamide]] develops through suppression of ferroptosis mediated by decorin downregulation. [[decorin|Decorin (DCN)]], a suppressor of prostate cancer progression, is significantly reduced in enzalutamide-resistant cells, high-grade tumors, and CRPC tissues associated with poor survival outcomes. Loss of decorin confers resistance to ferroptosis, an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death. Mechanistically, decorin activates calcium-dependent Protein Kinase C Beta II (PKCβII), which enhances phosphorylation of [[acsl4|long-chain acyl-CoA synthetase 4 (ACSL4)]] to regulate lipid remodeling and promote ferroptosis. Enzalutamide-mediated AR nuclear translocation negatively regulates decorin transcription, creating a feedback loop that promotes resistance. Restoring decorin expression re-sensitizes resistant cells to enzalutamide and significantly inhibits tumor growth and metastasis in vivo through ferroptosis-dependent mechanisms.¹

In PARP inhibitor-treated CRPC cells, the oncogenic M1C protein drives a distinct resistance mechanism through activation of retrotransposons. Olaparib treatment induces M1C expression in homologous recombination-competent cells, driving ATM expression, KAP1(S824) phosphorylation, and STING pathway activation. This cascade derepresses LINE-1 retrotransposons, inducing L1-5'UTR, L1-ORF1, and L1-ORF2 transcripts along with the encoded ORF1p RNA binding protein. M1C also activates HERV-K102/108 gag, pol, and env genes, producing HERV-K ENV protein. Retrotransposon activation drives genomic instability and drug resistance while simultaneously inducing APOBEC3 genes that evolved to restrain retrotransposon-mediated instability. M1C is essential for LINE-1, HERV-K, and APOBEC3 expression in olaparib-treated cells, representing a therapeutic target for advancing PARP inhibitor efficacy in homologous recombination-competent CRPC.⁶

## Liquid biopsy biomarkers

Plasma extracellular vesicle (EV) proteomics has identified candidate biomarkers predictive of [[177lu-psma-617|177Lu-PSMA-617]] outcomes in metastatic CRPC. Shotgun proteomics of 100 patients receiving radioligand therapy identified 5,137 EV-derived proteins, including the cell-surface targets PSMA, B7-H3, [[trop-2|Trop-2]], and [[steap1|STEAP1]]. High levels of all four proteins associate with worse overall survival and positively correlate with molecular tumor volume on PSMA-PET imaging, serum PSA, and alkaline phosphatase, serving as markers of molecular tumor burden.⁵

Circulating tumor cell (CTC) enumeration provides complementary prognostic information. PSMA+/EpCAM+ and PSMA-/EpCAM+ CTC subpopulations associate with worse progression-free survival and overall survival in patients receiving [[177lu-psma-617|177Lu-PSMA-617]]. Pathway analysis reveals that p53 upregulation associates with poor progression-free and overall survival, while activated [[e2f|E2F]] pathway activity unexpectedly portends better outcomes, suggesting cell-cycle-active tumors may be more radiosensitive.⁵

## Combination approaches

Combination therapeutic strategies demonstrate superior efficacy compared to monotherapy across multiple CRPC treatment paradigms. Radioligand therapy with [[lnth-1095|131I-LNTH-1095]] combined with [[enzalutamide]] overcomes resistance observed with enzalutamide alone in patients progressing on [[abiraterone]].⁴ Similarly, olaparib combined with [[abiraterone]] and [[prednisone]] extends radiographic progression-free survival beyond that achieved with hormonal therapy alone, particularly in patients harboring HRR gene mutations.³ [[cdk9|CDK9]] inhibition combined with AR suppression addresses resistance driven by AR-V7 and other constitutively active AR variants, targeting both ligand-dependent and ligand-independent AR transcriptional programs.² These data collectively support an evolving treatment paradigm prioritizing rational combination strategies that address complementary mechanisms of resistance and tumor cell survival in advanced CRPC.
