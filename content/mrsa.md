**Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA)** is a multidrug-resistant gram-positive bacterium with high mortality rates, distinguished by its capacity to form impenetrable drug-resistant [[biofilm]] structures in chronic infections.¹² MRSA causes severe pathological manifestations in diabetic patients, colonizing wounds and driving progressive tissue destruction, while establishing life-threatening pneumonia in respiratory infections.¹ The organism's intrinsic antibiotic resistance coupled with protective [[biofilm]] formation has motivated development of novel nanotechnology-based therapeutic approaches that bypass traditional antimicrobial mechanisms through distinct pathways: [[cuproptosis]]-like programmed cell death, [[reactive-oxygen-species]] generation, multivalent carbohydrate-derived bactericidal polymers, and [[bacteriophage]]-enabled targeting combined with photothermia.

---

## Pathogenesis and biofilm formation

MRSA establishes chronic infections through construction of structured, matrix-enclosed [[biofilm]] communities that confer high-level resistance to conventional antibiotics and host immune surveillance.²³ In diabetic foot ulcers and other chronic wounds, MRSA colonization exacerbates tissue necrosis and systemic inflammatory burden, resulting in persistent infection refractory to standard antimicrobial therapy.¹ The phenotypic and genotypic heterogeneity of bacteria within biofilms generates multiple resistance layers, effectively rendering last-line antibiotics insufficient for infection control. This resistance phenotype has catalyzed exploration of fundamentally non-antibiotic bactericidal mechanisms—particularly strategies that exploit metabolic collapse and catastrophic membrane disruption through rationally engineered nanomaterial platforms.

---

## Cuproptosis-induced bacterial death

Recent advances have leveraged [[cuproptosis]], an iron-independent form of regulated cell death, to selectively eliminate MRSA and dismantle its biofilm structures. Diclofenac-copper nanoassemblies (DC/Cu) achieve this integration through engineered peroxidase-like catalytic activity that metabolizes endogenous hydrogen peroxide, inducing acute lipid peroxidation and progressive destabilization of the bacterial cell membrane.¹ This membrane damage increases permeability and facilitates rapid copper influx into the cytoplasm, where Cu²⁺ accumulates to lethal concentrations. Transcriptomic profiling demonstrates that copper overload specifically inhibits iron-sulfur cluster proteins and disrupts the tricarboxylic acid cycle, directly triggering the cuproptosis-like death pathway.¹ Simultaneously, the released diclofenac anti-inflammatory payload suppresses the local inflammatory microenvironment while liberated Cu²⁺ stimulates tissue regeneration, thereby integrating antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and tissue-reparative functions within a single platform.¹

Bimetallic copper-manganese nanoparticles (Cu-Mn NPs) extend this cuproptosis-based strategy to respiratory infections with engineered lung retention and mucus-penetrating properties optimized for pneumonia treatment.³ Cu-Mn NPs synergistically induce bacterial cuproptosis-like death while simultaneously generating abundant [[reactive-oxygen-species]] that directly disrupt bacterial membrane integrity and interfere with electron transport chain function.³ The particles additionally drive macrophage polarization toward the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype, enhancing immune-mediated bacterial killing. In a methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus-infected pneumonia mouse model, Cu-Mn NP treatment substantially improved survival rates and reduced inflammatory lung injury without detectable systemic toxicity.³

## Glycopolymer antimicrobials

Multivalent glycopolymer scaffolds have converted benign carbohydrate moieties into potent bactericidal agents. Poly2DG, a 2-deoxyglucose-displaying polymer synthesized via ring-opening metathesis polymerization, exhibits broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity against MRSA and other multidrug-resistant pathogens, achieving minimum inhibitory concentrations as low as 0.2 µg/mL.⁴ Notably, neither free 2-deoxyglucose nor the polymer backbone alone confer antimicrobial activity, establishing that multivalent presentation is essential for bactericidal efficacy.⁴ This approach provides a chemically straightforward alternative to direct copper-based or phage-mediated strategies, operating through carbohydrate-based mechanisms orthogonal to traditional antibiotic pathways and resistance mechanisms.

## Phage-quantum dot nanoarchitectonics

Bacteriophage-quantum dot nanoarchitectonics (QDs-Phage) represent an integrated approach combining [[bacteriophage]] precision targeting with photothermally-mediated bacterial killing for chronic wound infections. MRSA-specific [[bacteriophage]] conjugated to photothermally efficient lead sulfide quantum dots create a "target-first, activate-later" therapeutic platform that concentrates phage delivery at bacterial biofilm sites.² Under near-infrared laser irradiation, the system exhibits potent synergistic bactericidal activity against both planktonic MRSA and biofilm-embedded cells.² In a murine model of full-thickness skin wounds infected with MRSA, topical application of QDs-Phage combined with near-infrared light irradiation substantially accelerated wound closure and tissue repair without observable systemic toxicity.² The integration of bacteriophage lytic activity with photothermally-induced killing addresses the therapeutic limitation of phage monotherapy, which is often insufficient for eradicating large biofilm burdens in chronic infection contexts.

---

## Clinical applications and future directions

DC/Cu nanoassemblies have demonstrated efficacy in diabetic MRSA infection models, where they simultaneously combat pathogenic bacteria, suppress inflammatory damage, and promote tissue regeneration in diabetic foot ulcers and diabetic keratitis.¹ This multifunctional capability addresses a fundamental therapeutic gap: conventional antibiotics alone cannot restore tissue homeostasis or reverse the immunosuppression characteristic of diabetes mellitus.¹ Cu-Mn nanoparticles specifically target acute lung infections, where MRSA-induced pneumonia carries substantial mortality risk.³ The engineered mucus-penetrating and lung-retention properties enable effective delivery to infected respiratory epithelium, where integrated cuproptosis induction and reactive oxygen species generation eliminate both planktonic and biofilm-resident bacteria.³ Bacteriophage-quantum dot platforms similarly address chronic wound infections where single-modality approaches prove insufficient; the combination of phage-mediated targeting, photothermally-induced bacterial killing, and direct quantum dot-enabled tissue regeneration substantially improves healing kinetics compared to conventional phage therapy or antibiotic monotherapy.²
