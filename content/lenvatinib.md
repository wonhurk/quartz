**Lenvatinib** is a multi-tyrosine kinase inhibitor with potent activity against [[flt1|FLT1]] and angiogenic pathway receptors, approved for treatment of [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma (uHCC)]] when combined with chemotherapy and immune checkpoint blockade. Its mechanism of action extends beyond classical angiogenesis inhibition to encompass the suppression of [[flt1|FLT1]]-mediated epithelial-endothelial crosstalk and cellular senescence, pathological processes that drive immunosuppression and tumor progression across multiple malignancies including [[clear-cell-renal-cell-carcinoma|clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC)]].¹ The landmark phase 2 HILL trial established lenvatinib combined with hepatic arterial infusion chemotherapy (FOLFOX-HAIC) and the [[pd-l1|PD-L1]] inhibitor [[durvalumab]] as a transformative triple-combination regimen for uHCC, achieving exceptional survival outcomes and redefining the therapeutic landscape for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma.

---

## Mechanism

Lenvatinib exerts its antitumor effects through complementary molecular pathways. The drug potently inhibits [[flt1|FLT1]], a receptor expressed on endothelial cells and tumor-infiltrating immune cells, thereby disrupting the [[flt1|FLT1]]-mediated epithelial-endothelial crosstalk that ordinarily sustains both tumor vascularization and microenvironmental immune suppression.¹ Beyond its classical anti-angiogenic activity against VEGFR and FGFR, lenvatinib exhibits the unique property of suppressing cellular senescence — a state of permanent cell-cycle arrest that accumulates in the ccRCC tumor microenvironment and enforces an immunologically "cold" phenotype with reduced [[cd8-t-cells|CD8+ T cell]] infiltration.¹ This senescence suppression appears mechanistically linked to disruption of the FLT1-[[vegfa|VEGFA]]-[[akt1|AKT1]] regulatory network, restoring immune competence and sensitizing tumors to checkpoint blockade.¹

## Hepatocellular carcinoma

The HILL trial (n=40, median follow-up 23.1 months) established the clinical superiority of the lenvatinib–FOLFOX-HAIC–[[durvalumab]] triple combination as first-line therapy for uHCC.² The regimen achieved a median progression-free survival of 15.8 months, with a 6-month PFS rate of 77.5%, and demonstrated exceptionally durable overall survival outcomes: 97.5% 1-year OS and 94.0% 2-year OS.² By modified RECIST criteria, the objective response rate was 75.0% (9 complete/pathologic complete responses, 21 partial responses) with a disease control rate of 95.0%.² Response kinetics were rapid: the median time to response was 2.2 months with a median duration of response of 10.4 months.² The triple combination was remarkable not only for efficacy but also for tolerability, with 85.0% of patients experiencing only grade 1–2 adverse events, grade 3 hepatotoxicity and thrombocytopenia each occurring in only 2 patients (5.0%), and no grade 4 toxicity observed.²

Beyond systemic control, the triple regimen demonstrated substantial capacity for surgical conversion: 7 patients (17.5%) underwent curative R0 resection after combination therapy, with 3 of these (42.9%) achieving pathologic complete response.² This surgical conversion rate highlights lenvatinib's capacity to render initially unresectable disease amenable to curative-intent surgery, expanding therapeutic options for patients with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma.

## Clear cell renal cell carcinoma

The mechanisms by which lenvatinib targets [[flt1|FLT1]]-driven cellular senescence establish its therapeutic relevance in ccRCC, where elevated senescence burden is associated with immune suppression and poor clinical outcomes.¹ Comprehensive multi-omics analysis and machine learning algorithms identified [[flt1|FLT1]] as the pivotal single gene driving ccRCC progression through epithelial-endothelial crosstalk.¹ In this disease context, [[flt1|FLT1]] functions as a hub within a regulatory network encompassing [[vegfa|VEGFA]] and [[akt1|AKT1]], coordinating endothelial-epithelial cell communication that simultaneously elevates tumor aggressiveness while creating an immunologically hostile microenvironment.¹ Lenvatinib's capacity to disrupt this axis positions it as a rationally targeted therapeutic option for ccRCC patients, particularly those with elevated senescence burden and immune-suppressed tumor phenotypes.
