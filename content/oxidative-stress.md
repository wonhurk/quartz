**Oxidative stress** is a pathophysiological state characterized by excessive accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) relative to endogenous antioxidant defense capacity, resulting in cellular damage across multiple organ systems. This redox imbalance drives critical disease mechanisms including [[ferroptosis]], mitochondrial dysfunction, neuroinflammation, and barrier integrity disruption.¹ [[h2o2|Hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂)]] serves as both a signaling molecule and a quantifiable biomarker of oxidative stress in pathological processes ranging from cancer to acute organ injury.² Counterregulatory pathways—principally nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 ([[nrf2|Nrf2]]) and glutathione (GSH)-dependent antioxidant systems—normally suppress ROS accumulation, but their failure precipitates oxidative tissue damage across the lifespan.³

---

## Mechanism

Oxidative stress arises when ROS generation—from mitochondrial respiration, NADPH oxidases, and inflammatory cells—overwhelms cellular antioxidant defenses including superoxide dismutase ([[sod1|SOD1]]), catalase, glutathione peroxidase, and the thioredoxin system. Excessive ROS oxidize lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids, compromising membrane integrity, enzyme function, and genomic stability.⁴ Mitochondria are both source and target: respiratory chain dysfunction amplifies ROS production while oxidative damage to mitochondrial membranes and DNA creates a self-perpetuating pathological amplification loop.⁵ 

In iron-replete environments, oxidative stress triggers [[ferroptosis]]—an iron-dependent form of regulated cell death driven by lipid peroxidation.⁶ [[h2o2|H₂O₂]] oxidizes polyunsaturated fatty acids in phospholipid membranes, and when glutathione peroxidase 4 ([[gpx4|GPX4]]) activity is exhausted, lethal lipid hydroperoxides accumulate.⁷ This ferroptotic pathway is particularly relevant in neurodegeneration, obesity-related cardiomyopathy, and age-related retinal disease.⁸ Detection and quantification of [[h2o2|H₂O₂]] in living systems has been enabled by bioorthogonal CRISPR/Cas12a biosensing platforms ([[a-bo-crispr|A-BO-CRISPR]]) that achieve micromolar sensitivity and real-time imaging in tumor-bearing mice.²

## Neurological diseases

Oxidative stress and neuroinflammation form a self-perpetuating pathological cycle in central nervous system disorders, necessitating concurrent dual-targeted therapeutic approaches. In Alzheimer's disease, NADPH oxidase 4 ([[nox4|NOX4]]) is upregulated in astrocytes via the miR-10b-3p axis, driving both oxidative damage and neuronal [[ferroptosis]]; the Ang-(1-7) agonist diminazene (DIZE) attenuates this cascade by suppressing astrocytic NOX4 overactivation.⁹ Traumatic brain injury similarly exhibits a vicious cycle of neuroinflammation and oxidative stress, which a streptavidin-condensed bifunctional nucleic acid nanoplatform (DZ-G4/H NPs) disrupts by integrating C1qa-cleaving DNAzymes with peroxidase-mimetic G-quadruplex/hemin complexes, thereby alleviating cerebral edema and cognitive deficits.¹⁰

Chronic antipsychotic therapy induces gut microbial dysbiosis and profound depletion of the microbiota-derived metabolite [[ergothioneine]] in blood and brain, which correlates with hippocampal oxidative stress and cognitive impairment.¹¹ [[ergothioneine|Ergothioneine]] supplementation mitigates these deficits by inhibiting the redox-sensitive phosphatase PTP1B, demonstrating that microbiome-oxidative stress interactions are therapeutically targetable in iatrogenic neurological injury.¹¹ In ischemic stroke, a layered double hydroxide nanohybrid (S-E@LDH) with broad-spectrum enzyme mimetic activity rationally integrates calcium chelation, ROS scavenging, and anti-inflammatory drug release to disrupt the calcium-ROS amplification loop and reprogram microglia toward tissue-reparative phenotypes.¹²

## Cancer

In triple-negative breast cancer ([[triple-negative-breast-cancer|TNBC]]), oxidative stress-ferroptosis synergy has been exploited therapeutically through GSH-responsive nanoprodrugs. The self-assembling prodrug RSSF—engineered from rhein (chemotherapeutic) and [[ferrocene]] (Fenton-active ferroptosis booster) via disulfide linkage—co-delivers [[sorafenib]] to synchronously deplete GSH, generate hydroxyl radicals, and disable cellular ferroptosis defenses, triggering combined ferroptotic and apoptotic cell death.¹³ This multi-modal approach addresses the adaptive resistance mechanisms that limit ferroptosis monotherapy in aggressive malignancies.

## Cardiovascular and metabolic disease

Obesity is associated with low circulating IGF-1, mitochondrial injury, and myocardial dysfunction. Transcriptomic analyses of obese human and murine hearts reveal metabolic reprogramming—a shift from TCA cycle toward glycolysis—and identify [[ferroptosis]] as a key regulatory node in obesity cardiomyopathy.⁵ Cardiac-specific IGF-1 overexpression mitigates high-fat diet-induced cardiac hypertrophy, contractile dysfunction, oxidative stress, and mitochondrial injury via arachidylcarnitine-dependent suppression of [[ferroptosis]], establishing a mechanistic link between growth factor signaling, lipid metabolism, and iron-dependent cell death in metabolic heart disease.⁵

## Retinal degeneration

Oxidative stress-induced [[retinal-pigment-epithelium|retinal pigment epithelium (RPE)]] damage and [[ferroptosis]] are central to [[age-related-macular-degeneration|age-related macular degeneration (AMD)]], a leading cause of irreversible blindness. Current anti-VEGF therapies target only late-stage choroidal neovascularization (CNV) and exhibit incomplete response. A polyamidoamine dendrimer platform (Nuci@PAM-BR) modified with antioxidant [[bilirubin]] and loaded with antiferroptotic nuciferine achieves iron homeostasis restoration via triple-pool-targeted scavenging—simultaneously addressing oxidative stress, iron dysregulation, and lipid peroxidation.¹⁴ This approach reduced CNV leakage area by over 80% and neovascular lesions by over 85%, demonstrating that multi-targeted redox intervention can halt AMD progression upstream of angiogenesis.¹⁴

---

## Acute organ injury

In sepsis-induced acute lung injury (ALI), dysregulated oxidative stress amplifies inflammatory lung damage and drives mortality. Polymyxin B-antioxidant nanoassemblies (PV NPs) integrate antibacterial activity, endotoxin neutralization, and broad-spectrum ROS scavenging via an ester-linked α-tocopherol-gallic acid conjugate, while mitigating polymyxin B's dose-limiting nephrotoxicity.¹⁵ In murine cecal ligation-and-puncture sepsis, PV NPs conferred 75% survival by attenuating lung inflammation and oxidative stress, significantly outperforming either component alone.¹⁵ This multi-mechanistic strategy couples synergistic pharmacodynamics with an improved safety profile for life-threatening infectious syndromes.

## Wound healing and barrier function

Diabetic wounds, characterized by delayed healing and chronic infection, exhibit elevated ROS that impair angiogenesis and tissue regeneration. A cyclodextrin-based deep eutectic solvent chitosan eutectogel delivering [[glabridin]]—a natural product with antioxidant and antibacterial properties—achieved 94.3% wound closure by day 21 in an infected diabetic wound model by scavenging ROS, inhibiting inflammation, and promoting angiogenesis.¹⁶ Similarly, a functionally graded bilayer polyurethane sponge featuring polydopamine-coated silver nanoparticles alleviated local oxidative stress during the inflammatory phase while providing sequential antibacterial and pro-angiogenic functions across the full healing cascade.¹⁷

Sleep deprivation is strongly associated with intestinal barrier dysfunction driven by oxidative stress. The grapefruit sesquiterpenoid nootkatone activates the [[nrf2|Nrf2]] pathway, reducing ROS accumulation and restoring mitochondrial redox homeostasis in colonic tissues, thereby ameliorating sleep-deprivation-induced gut barrier disruption, inflammation, and microbial dysbiosis.³ This work highlights [[nrf2|Nrf2]] as a master regulator coordinating antioxidant defenses, mitochondrial function, and barrier integrity across organ systems.

## Environmental induction

Atmospheric fine particulate matter from desert dust aerosols exhibits oxidative potential comparable to urban pollution, challenging the presumed low toxicity of natural aerosols. Mechanical processes during dust entrainment—saltation and sandblasting—induce formation of mineral free radicals on particle surfaces, serving as an important source of reactive species.¹⁸ Furthermore, photoaging chemically enhances oxidative potential in airborne dust, explaining why urban-observed dust exhibits greater toxicity than desert-source dust.¹⁸ This paradigm shift establishes desert dust as a major exogenous contributor to oxidative stress-related disease across more than half the world's countries.

## Therapeutic strategies

Emerging oxidative stress therapies integrate multiple mechanisms: ROS scavenging antioxidants ([[bilirubin]], [[glabridin]], α-tocopherol), [[nrf2|Nrf2]] pathway activators (nootkatone), iron homeostasis modulators targeting the ferroptosis axis, and microbial metabolites ([[ergothioneine]], nootkatone) that restore redox balance. Nanomedicine platforms enable spatiotemporally controlled, multi-targeted interventions—exemplified by enzyme-mimetic layered double hydroxides, stimuli-responsive prodrugs, and triple-pool-targeted dendrimers—that address the complex, dynamic pathophysiology of oxidative stress across disease contexts.¹²¹⁴¹⁷ 

Biomarker-guided therapy is enabled by [[a-bo-crispr|A-BO-CRISPR]] and related platforms that provide real-time [[h2o2|H₂O₂]] imaging in living systems, supporting precision dosing and therapeutic monitoring in oncology and inflammatory diseases.² In infectious disease, disruption of fungal iron homeostasis by pyrazolone derivatives ([[compound-d2]]) demonstrates that oxidative stress induction—when selectively targeted to pathogens—can serve as a therapeutic mechanism rather than a pathological outcome.¹⁹
