**Prostate-Specific Antigen (PSA)** is a classical serum protein biomarker for [[prostate-cancer|prostate cancer]] detection and diagnosis, yet conventional blood tests lack sufficient specificity, producing frequent false positives that lead to unnecessary biopsies.¹ The cell-surface glycoprotein [[psma|prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)]] represents a superior non-invasive diagnostic biomarker detectable in urine and plasma-derived extracellular vesicles with substantially improved diagnostic accuracy. In metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC), serum PSA levels strongly correlate with molecular tumor burden and are powerful predictors of disease progression and overall survival.² Advanced electrochemical biosensors enable ultrasensitive PSA and [[psma|PSMA]] detection from biological fluids, while [[psma|PSMA]]-directed radionuclide therapy has transformed treatment outcomes for advanced disease.

---

## Mechanism

PSA is a serine protease enzyme constitutively produced by prostate epithelial cells and normally present in low concentrations in serum. Its elevation in blood has traditionally been used as a surrogate marker for prostate carcinogenesis, yet PSA lacks cancer specificity — elevated levels also occur in benign prostatic hyperplasia and prostatitis. The fundamental limitation of conventional blood PSA testing is its inability to differentiate aggressive cancers from indolent tumors, resulting in false-positive rates exceeding 75% and driving unnecessary diagnostic biopsies and overtreatment in the vast majority of screened men.¹

---

## Diagnostic Applications and Limitations

PSA measurement remains the most widely implemented [[prostate-cancer|prostate cancer]] screening test globally, yet its clinical utility is substantially compromised by poor specificity. Traditional cutoff values (>4 ng/mL) generate false-positive rates that necessitate biopsy confirmation in men without significant disease.¹ In contrast, [[psma|prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA)]] — a cell-surface transmembrane glycoprotein markedly overexpressed in prostate cancer cells — represents a far more specific diagnostic target. Detection of [[psma|PSMA]] in patient urine provides a non-invasive alternative with superior diagnostic accuracy compared to serum PSA, avoiding venipuncture and reducing the psychological burden of false-positive screening.¹

---

## Prognostic Value in Advanced Disease

In metastatic castration-resistant [[prostate-cancer|prostate cancer]], serum PSA levels demonstrate substantial predictive utility beyond their diagnostic role. PSA concentrations positively correlate with molecular tumor volume as quantified by [[psma|PSMA]]-PET imaging, as well as with serum alkaline phosphatase and circulating [[psma|PSMA]]-positive tumor cells.² These correlations establish serum PSA as a robust prognostic marker, with elevated baseline PSA predicting worse progression-free survival and overall survival in patients receiving systemic therapy.²

---

## Liquid Biopsy and Extracellular Vesicle Proteomics

Plasma-derived extracellular vesicles (EVs) represent an emerging liquid biopsy platform that carries PSA alongside [[psma|PSMA]] and other tumor-associated surface proteins including B7-H3, Trop-2, and STEAP1.² Comprehensive proteomics profiling of circulating EVs provides a multi-dimensional assessment of tumor burden without invasive tissue sampling. Elevated levels of [[psma|PSMA]] and related surface proteins in plasma-derived EVs strongly associate with worse overall survival and progression-free survival in mCRPC patients.² Circulating [[psma|PSMA]]-positive and [[psma|PSMA]]-negative tumor cells enumerated alongside EV proteomics further refine patient stratification and enable prediction of treatment response.²

---

## [[psma|PSMA]]-Directed Therapy and Biomarker Integration

The development of [[psma|PSMA]]-targeted radiotherapy, particularly lutetium-177-labeled [[psma|PSMA]]-617 (177Lu-PSMA-617), has transformed outcomes in metastatic castration-resistant [[prostate-cancer|prostate cancer]].² Serum PSA levels prior to and during [[psma|PSMA]]-targeted radionuclide therapy correlate with therapeutic response and predict clinical benefit, establishing PSA measurement as a complementary biomarker for patient selection and response monitoring. Integration of PSA with plasma extracellular vesicle proteomics — including [[psma|PSMA]], B7-H3, Trop-2, and STEAP1 levels — enables multi-dimensional patient stratification and precision medicine approaches tailored to individual tumor biology.²

---

## Advanced Electrochemical Sensing Platforms

Recent advances in biosensor engineering have enabled ultrasensitive detection of PSA and [[psma|PSMA]] from biological fluids at clinically relevant concentrations. Multiplexed three-electrode electrochemical aptasensors incorporating three-dimensional reduced graphene oxide (3D-rGO) and gold nanoparticles (AuNPs) achieve detection limits of 5 pg/mL for urinary [[psma|PSMA]], with linear quantification across the 15–2000 pg/mL range.¹ Cyclic voltammetry and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy confirm stepwise sensor construction and selective target recognition. Novel catalytic amplification strategies employing glucose oxidase-functionalized covalent organic frameworks paired with ferrocene-modified electrodes further enhance signal amplification and long-term stability for PSA measurement, enabling continuous monitoring with near-zero background noise.³ These emerging platforms promise to translate laboratory biomarker discoveries into point-of-care clinical diagnostics.
