**Cereblon (CRBN)** is an E3 ubiquitin ligase that serves as the essential protein-recruitment component in [[proteolysis-targeting-chimera|PROTAC (proteolysis-targeting chimera)]] molecules for targeted protein degradation. In PROTACs, CRBN functions as the ubiquitin ligase recruiter that brings substrate proteins into proximity with the [[ubiquitin-proteasome-system|ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS)]] for polyubiquitination and subsequent proteasomal elimination.¹ The field has increasingly focused on optimizing CRBN ligands to enhance PROTAC potency, selectivity, and pharmacokinetic properties. Recent CRBN-recruiting degraders have demonstrated substantial therapeutic potential, including [[sh-26|SH-26]] for protecting against acetaminophen-induced acute liver injury via [[phd1|PHD1]] degradation, and [[pd-305|PD-305]] for cancer immunotherapy through [[ptpn2|PTPN2]] degradation, with advanced compounds achieving subnanomolar degradation potency.

---

## Mechanism

CRBN mediates targeted protein degradation through a concentration-, time-, and [[ubiquitin-proteasome-system|UPS]]-dependent mechanism within PROTAC molecules. When a PROTAC brings a substrate protein and CRBN into spatial proximity, CRBN's intrinsic E3 ubiquitin ligase activity catalyzes the transfer of ubiquitin chains onto the substrate. This polyubiquitination marks the protein for recognition by the 26S proteasome, leading to unidirectional degradation. The efficiency of CRBN-mediated degradation is governed by the affinity of the PROTAC's CRBN ligand moiety, the linker architecture connecting the substrate binder and ligand, and the cellular availability of substrate and CRBN. Rational optimization of CRBN ligands — particularly through scaffold hopping strategies and chemical derivatization — can achieve degradation potencies in the subnanomolar range, far exceeding earlier iterations.

## Acute liver injury protection

[[sh-26|SH-26]], a cereblon-recruiting PROTAC, enables targeted degradation of [[phd1|prolyl hydroxylase domain enzyme 1 (PHD1)]] with protective efficacy in acetaminophen-induced acute liver injury models. PHD1 is a key regulator of hypoxic adaptation and metabolic homeostasis; pathological PHD1 activity exacerbates APAP-triggered hepatotoxicity through reactive oxygen species accumulation and [[nlrp3-inflammasome|NLRP3 inflammasome]] activation. SH-26-mediated PHD1 degradation attenuated hepatic inflammatory responses and necrosis without detectable cytotoxicity to hepatocytes.¹ Mechanistically, PHD1 elimination reduced ROS production, prevented mitochondrial dysfunction, and suppressed NLRP3-driven inflammasome signaling, collectively affording robust in vivo protection against acetaminophen-induced liver damage.¹ This work established SH-26 as a chemical tool for dissecting pathological PHD1 functions and validated CRBN-based PROTACs as a precision degradation strategy for drug-induced organ injury.

## Cancer immunotherapy

[[pd-305|PD-305]], an optimized CRBN-recruiting PROTAC, targets [[ptpn2|protein tyrosine phosphatase N2 (PTPN2)]] for cancer immunotherapy. PTPN2 suppresses interferon-gamma signaling and CD8+ T cell activation, making it an attractive oncology target; however, earlier PTPN2 degraders suffered from unfavorable physicochemical properties and suboptimal potency. PD-305 employs a rigid linker and naphtholactam-based CRBN ligand, achieving subnanomolar degradation potency with a DC₅₀ of 0.25 nM — approximately 868-fold superior to the previous clinical candidate PVD-06.² The compound exhibits excellent subtype selectivity, sparing [[ptpn1|PTPN1]] with a greater than 20-fold preference for PTPN2. Compared to earlier PTPN2 degraders, PD-305 displays lower molecular weight, fewer hydrogen bond donors and acceptors, and reduced lipophilicity — properties conducive to improved oral bioavailability and in vivo tolerability.² In interferon-stimulated colorectal cancer cell lines, PD-305 demonstrated potent antiproliferation activity, nearly 80-fold more effective than the clinical stage agent AC484, and achieved excellent in vivo PTPN2 degradation with effective tumor growth suppression in mouse models.

## Advanced CRBN ligand optimization

The discovery and development of next-generation CRBN ligands has become a primary focus for enhancing PROTAC efficacy and selectivity. Ring-opening scaffold hopping — exemplified by the identification of alkyne-containing PTPN2 ligands — has yielded new chemical scaffolds compatible with subnanomolar degradation performance.² Systematic linker optimization, combined with iterative refinement of the CRBN-binding moiety, enables simultaneous achievement of high potency, reduced molecular complexity, improved selectivity, and favorable drug-like properties. These advances establish CRBN as a highly druggable and versatile component in precision protein degradation therapeutics, with each new ligand variant expanding the palette of targetable proteins and disease indications addressable by PROTAC technology.
