**Glucocorticoids** are corticosteroid hormones that modulate immune and inflammatory responses, functioning as key regulators of both malignant and autoimmune disease microenvironments. Beyond their classical roles in metabolism and stress response, glucocorticoids reshape immune cell phenotypes with profound implications for immunotherapy strategies. In [[adrenocortical-carcinoma|adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC)]] — where systemic cortisol overproduction occurs in approximately 60% of cases — glucocorticoids act as powerful drivers of [[macrophages|macrophage]] polarization toward a C1Q+ CD163+ phagocytic phenotype primed for interferon-gamma-dependent CXCL9 secretion, thereby reshaping the tumor microenvironment.¹ In autoimmune kidney disease, particularly [[iga-nephropathy|immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgA nephropathy)]], glucocorticoids synergize with [[telitacicept|B cell-targeted immunotherapy]], achieving superior proteinuria reduction compared to monotherapy.² These findings position glucocorticoids as understudied but clinically important modulators of immune microenvironments across oncology and autoimmune disease.

---

## Mechanism

Glucocorticoids exert their immunomodulatory effects through glucocorticoid receptor signaling on myeloid cells, with particularly pronounced effects on [[macrophages|macrophage]] differentiation and polarization. In vitro exposure to glucocorticoids polarizes peripheral blood monocytes toward a C1Q+ CD163+ phenotype characterized by elevated expression of C1Q and the scavenger receptor CD163, hallmarks of a pro-phagocytic, tissue-remodeling [[macrophages|macrophage]] profile.¹ These glucocorticoid-polarized [[macrophages]] display enhanced capacity for antigen presentation and are specifically primed to secrete the T cell chemoattractant CXCL9 in response to interferon-gamma stimulation. Indeed, upon IFN-γ treatment, C1Q+ [[macrophages]] produce higher CXCL9 levels than classical M1 pro-inflammatory [[macrophages]], establishing glucocorticoids as inducers of a chemokine-secreting phenotype with potential to recruit adaptive immune cells into tissues and tumor microenvironments.¹

## Adrenocortical carcinoma

[[adrenocortical-carcinoma|Adrenocortical carcinoma]] is a rare endocrine malignancy uniquely characterized by systemic cortisol excess in approximately 60% of cases, creating an intrinsic, hormone-driven immunomodulatory tumor microenvironment. ACC tumors are highly infiltrated with CD68+/CD163+ [[macrophages]], and notably, this [[macrophages|macrophage]] infiltration occurs independently of cortisol overproduction, suggesting both autocrine and paracrine drivers of myeloid recruitment and retention.¹ Responses to immune checkpoint inhibitors vary considerably among ACC patients, yet no reliable biomarkers currently exist to predict therapeutic outcomes or to distinguish responders from non-responders. The glucocorticoid-driven [[macrophages|macrophage]] polarization toward CXCL9-secreting phenotypes may contribute to this heterogeneity, potentially dampening or promoting checkpoint immunotherapy responses depending on the predominant [[macrophages|macrophage]] phenotype within each tumor.¹

In preclinical ACC-bearing mouse models, blockade of the glucocorticoid receptor with mifepristone — a competitive antagonist of the glucocorticoid receptor — combined with immune checkpoint inhibitor treatment suppressed intratumoral CXCL9 expression and altered tumor microenvironment composition.¹ These findings suggest that glucocorticoid-mediated immune remodeling represents a targetable vulnerability in ACC, with potential to enhance checkpoint immunotherapy by rebalancing [[macrophages|macrophage]] polarization away from phagocytic, pro-fibrotic phenotypes.

## IgA Nephropathy

In [[iga-nephropathy|immunoglobulin A nephropathy (IgA nephropathy)]], a primary glomerulonephritis characterized by IgA-dominant immune complex deposition, glucocorticoids amplify the efficacy of [[telitacicept]], a dual inhibitor of B lymphocyte stimulator (BLyS) and a proliferation-inducing ligand (APRIL). In a real-world cohort of IgA nephropathy patients, those receiving [[telitacicept]] combined with glucocorticoids achieved a mean proteinuria reduction of 84.1% (interquartile range: −90.4% to −74.4%) at six months, significantly superior to the 71.4% reduction (IQR: −88.5% to −33.5%) observed with [[telitacicept]] monotherapy.² Both treatment arms demonstrated comparable estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) improvements (~8%) and reductions in serum IgA and IgG levels, indicating that the enhanced proteinuria reduction with combination therapy was not offset by deteriorating renal function.² Urinary soluble CD163, a putative biomarker of disease activity and [[macrophages|macrophage]] activation, significantly decreased with [[telitacicept]] treatment, consistent with downstream suppression of myeloid-driven inflammation.² No serious adverse events were reported in either group, establishing the safety of combination glucocorticoid and [[telitacicept]] therapy in IgA nephropathy.
