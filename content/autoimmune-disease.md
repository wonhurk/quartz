**Autoimmune disease** is a chronic systemic condition characterized by dysregulated adaptive immunity in which the body's immune system loses self-tolerance and attacks its own tissues and organs. The pathological hallmark involves failure of peripheral immune tolerance — normally maintained through homeostatic maturation of [[dendritic-cells|dendritic cells]] that promote regulatory T cell development via [[tslp|TSLP]] signaling — combined with excessive production of the pro-inflammatory cytokine [[il-17a|IL-17A]] by [[th17|Th17 cells]]. The susceptibility to autoimmune disease is significantly shaped by genetic and demographic factors linked to ancestry, ethnicity, and sex, which modulate immune regulation genes and the production of pathogenic effector cytokines across different populations.¹²³

---

## Mechanism

Peripheral immune tolerance is established through a homeostatic maturation program in type 2 conventional dendritic cells ([[cdc2|cDC2s]]), during which the cells coordinate upregulation of tolerance-associated transcripts including tissue-restricted self-antigens, regulatory T cell-polarization machinery, and the [[tslp|TSLP]] receptor complex consisting of IL-7Rα and TSLP receptor (TSLPR).¹ The homeostatically matured cDC2s (DC2hm) possess potent capacity to convert naive CD4+ T cells into [[foxp3|FOXP3+]] regulatory T cells through TSLP-dependent signaling, establishing this cytokine as a critical regulator of dendritic cell-mediated tolerance. Disruption of this program — whether through impaired DC maturation, defective [[tslp|TSLP]] signaling, or reduced [[regulatory-t-cells|Treg]] differentiation — compromises peripheral immune tolerance and permits autoreactive T cell responses to develop.

In contrast to this homeostatic tolerance program, [[th17|Th17 cells]] represent a critical source of pathogenic inflammation in autoimmune disease. [[il-17a|IL-17A]], the signature cytokine produced by these cells, drives chronic autoimmune inflammation and is emerging as both an important biomarker and therapeutic target across multiple autoimmune, cardiovascular, and infectious disease contexts.² The balance between tolerogenic [[cdc2|cDC2]]-derived [[regulatory-t-cells|Treg]] development and pathogenic Th17 differentiation determines whether immune homeostasis is maintained or autoimmune inflammation develops.

## Ethnicity and genetic susceptibility

Ethnicity-associated molecular features significantly influence autoimmune disease susceptibility through multiple layers of genetic and metabolic variation. Comprehensive multi-omics profiling across European, East Asian, and South Asian ancestry groups has revealed ethnicity-linked differences in host metabolism, immune regulation gene expression, and lipid metabolism pathways.² Specific genetic variants associated with immune regulation differ between ancestral populations, modulating both the baseline state of immune activation and the capacity to mount regulatory responses. These ancestry-related molecular variations contribute to population-level differences in autoimmune disease incidence and prevalence, and inform precision medicine approaches to diagnosis and treatment.

---

## Sex-dependent IL-17A regulation

[[il-17a|IL-17A]] regulation exhibits striking sexual dimorphism that influences disease susceptibility and treatment response. In males, inflammatory determinants predominantly drive IL-17A production, whereas in females, metabolic pathways linked to potassium homeostasis govern IL-17A levels.³ This sex-specific biology affects not only circulating IL-17A concentrations but also the validity of IL-17A as a biomarker across sex groups and the efficacy of therapeutic IL-17A targeting strategies. Recognition of these sex-dependent mechanisms is essential for interpreting autoimmune disease presentations and optimizing therapeutic responses across both sexes.

---

## Geographic and ancestry-related variation

Geographic location influences immune function and biological aging through ancestry-related molecular changes that modulate autoimmune disease risk. Individuals of the same ancestry exhibit different biological ages and immune profiles depending on their geographic location, suggesting that environment-ancestry interactions shape immune aging trajectories. East Asians showed lower biological age in their ancestral regions, whereas individuals of European ancestry exhibited lower biological age in the US and Canada compared to Europe.² These geographic variations in immune aging and metabolic function contribute to population-level differences in autoimmune disease burden and may inform strategies for risk stratification and prevention.
