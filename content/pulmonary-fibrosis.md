**Pulmonary fibrosis (PF)** is a progressive chronic lung disease characterized by excessive fibrotic remodeling and irreversible scarring of pulmonary tissue, with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) representing the most severe and lethal form. The pathogenesis is driven by multiple interconnected mechanisms: [[senescent-fibroblasts|senescent fibroblasts]] evade immune clearance by expressing [[hla-e|HLA-E]], which engages the inhibitory checkpoint receptor [[nkg2a|NKG2A]] on exhausted [[nk-cells|natural killer (NK) cells]], creating immune-privileged niches within fibrotic foci.¹ Aberrant oxidative stress responses involving impaired [[nrf2|NRF2]]-mediated antioxidant pathways and M2 macrophage polarization further propagate fibrosis through sustained secretion of profibrotic mediators including [[tgf-beta|transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β)]].⁶

---

## Pathogenic mechanisms

The immune evasion axis central to pulmonary fibrosis involves selective expression of HLA-E on senescent HAS1⁺ fibroblast subsets, which suppresses NK cell cytotoxicity by engaging NKG2A, the predominant inhibitory checkpoint receptor identified in fibrotic lung tissue by single-cell RNA sequencing and spectral flow cytometry.¹ Spatial transcriptomics and multiplex immunofluorescence reveal that HLA-E⁺ fibroblasts localize to the periphery of fibroblastic foci adjacent to NKG2A⁺ NK cells, forming immune-privileged microenvironments, whereas extracellular matrix-producing myofibroblasts at the fibrotic core lack HLA-E expression and exhibit minimal NK engagement.¹

Oxidative stress represents a parallel pathogenic driver. Dysregulated NRF2 signaling impairs the cellular antioxidant response, allowing reactive oxygen species accumulation that propagates tissue injury.⁶ Concurrently, M2 macrophage infiltration and upregulation of the adenosine A2B receptor ([[adora2b|ADORA2B]]) promote fibrosis progression by driving TGF-β secretion and sustaining profibrotic cytokine release.⁶

---

## NK cell immunotherapy

Therapeutic blockade of the NKG2A–HLA-E checkpoint restores NK cell function and promotes clearance of senescent fibroblasts, reversing established fibrosis in preclinical bleomycin-induced mouse models.¹ [[monalizumab|Monalizumab]], a clinical-grade humanized anti-NKG2A monoclonal antibody, reactivates patient-derived NK cells and enhances lysis of human senescent fibroblasts in vitro, positioning NK cell immunotherapy as a potentially transformative approach to eliminate the cellular drivers of fibrotic remodeling.¹

## Cancer association

IPF significantly elevates the risk of [[lung-squamous-cell-carcinoma|lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC)]] through mechanisms linking fibrosis to carcinogenesis. Multi-omics analysis and digital spatial profiling of LUSC specimens arising within usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) demonstrate that tumors originate from metaplastic basal cells derived from transdifferentiated alveolar type 2 (AT2) cells.² Both UIP-affected tissue and associated LUSC exhibit aberrant activation of [[wnt3|Wnt3]]/β-catenin signaling alongside enrichment of NRF2-linked antioxidant responses, creating stress-adapted cellular states.² Targeting Wnt/β-catenin signaling restores oxidative damage sensitivity in these cancer cells, highlighting a therapeutic vulnerability at the fibrosis–cancer interface.²

## Occupational silicosis

Silicosis induced by occupational exposure to [[nano-silica|nano-silica (nano-SiO₂)]] particles represents a distinct form of progressive pulmonary fibrosis and remains a major global occupational health threat, particularly with the expanding industrial use of nanoscale silica.⁴ [[cd163|CD163]], a scavenger receptor associated with M2 macrophage polarization, has been identified as a potential diagnostic biomarker for nano-silica-induced fibrosis, offering a molecular signature to track disease progression and macrophage-mediated pathology.⁴

---

## Novel therapeutic delivery systems

Pulmonary-targeted nanoparticle platforms address the limitations of systemic antifibrotic therapy. Oral [[pirfenidone]], though clinically established for IPF, exhibits poor lung accumulation and dose-limiting systemic adverse effects.⁵ Nebulizable pirfenidone-loaded human serum albumin nanoparticles (PFD-HNPs) achieve superior aerosol performance with preferential deposition in deep lung regions, high entrapment efficiency (63–68%), and physicochemical stability post-nebulization.⁵ In vitro, PFD-HNPs demonstrate efficient macrophage uptake, cytoplasmic retention for sustained drug release, and potent inhibition of TGF-β1-induced profibrotic marker expression in lung fibroblasts, with favorable safety profiles in vivo.⁵

An alternative biomimetic strategy employs M2 macrophage membrane-coated nanoparticles (Mul-siRNA@MM) for codelivery of [[mulberrin]] and ADORA2B-targeted siRNA.⁶ Inhaled Mul-siRNA@MM nanoparticles activate the NRF2 antioxidant pathway to suppress reactive oxygen species production while simultaneously silencing ADORA2B expression, thereby reducing M2 macrophage infiltration and downstream TGF-β secretion.⁶ This dual-mechanism approach markedly ameliorates bleomycin-induced lung fibrosis in preclinical models, demonstrating the potential of inhaled biomimetic nanodelivery for lung-specific targeting with minimal systemic toxicity.⁶
