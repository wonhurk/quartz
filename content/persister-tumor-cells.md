**Persister tumor cells (PTCs)** are a quiescent population of cancer cells that survive [[neoadjuvant-chemotherapy|neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC)]] and represent a critical barrier to long-term treatment success in [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer (CRC)]].¹ Despite initial chemotherapy-induced tumor regression, PTCs persist within the residual microenvironment where they actively orchestrate immune suppression through elevated [[cd155|CD155]] expression, which engages [[cd96|CD96]] receptors on infiltrating [[cd8|CD8+]] T cells to drive T cell exhaustion and dysfunction. Elevated PTC abundance independently predicts poor long-term clinical outcomes and correlates with therapeutic failure, even in patients exhibiting initial chemotherapy responsiveness, highlighting their prognostic importance as a determinant of recurrence and treatment resistance.

---

## Mechanism of persistence

PTCs are enriched in post-NAC tumor samples and maintain a quiescent state that confers resistance to chemotherapy-induced cell death. These cells possess aggressive stem-like traits that enable long-term survival in the tumor microenvironment.¹ The central mechanism of PTC-driven immune suppression operates through the [[cd155]]-[[cd96]] axis. PTCs express elevated levels of [[cd155|CD155]], which serves as a ligand for the inhibitory [[cd96|CD96]] receptor expressed on tumor-infiltrating [[cd8|CD8+]] T cells.² Engagement of CD155 with CD96 diverts CD8+ T cells toward an exhaustion trajectory, characterized by reduced mitochondrial function and loss of effector T cell memory phenotypes.¹ This CD96-mediated exhaustion drives progressive T cell dysfunction, enabling PTC survival despite the presence of antitumor-primed immune cells.

---

## Clinical significance in colorectal cancer

PTCs represent a major determinant of therapeutic failure in CRC, independent of initial chemotherapy response. Patients exhibiting elevated PTC abundance within residual tumors exhibit significantly worse long-term prognosis compared to those with minimal PTCs, even when both groups show comparable initial chemotherapy-induced tumor regression.¹ This prognostic independence underscores PTCs as a distinct biological entity whose elimination is necessary for durable therapeutic outcomes. The presence of stem-like characteristics within the PTC population enables persistence, adaptation to the post-chemotherapy microenvironment, and establishment of the immunosuppressive niche that perpetuates treatment failure.

---

## CD96-targeted immunotherapy

Therapeutic targeting of the [[cd96]] axis effectively restores anti-tumor immunity and eliminates PTCs. Anti-[[cd96|CD96]] monoclonal antibody treatment blocks the CD155-CD96 interaction, thereby redirecting CD8+ T cells away from exhaustion and toward memory-like phenotypes with enhanced mitochondrial function and proliferative capacity.¹ This CD96 blockade effectively eradicates PTCs in preclinical orthotopic CRC models, demonstrating the vulnerability of the PTC-driven immunosuppressive microenvironment to CD96-directed therapy.

Engineered [[car-t-cells|CAR-T cell]] approaches targeting PTCs have been developed by engineering [[epcam|EpCAM]]-targeted [[car-t-cells|CAR-T cells]] with complete [[cd96|CD96]] knockout.¹ These dual-engineered CAR-T cells simultaneously target PTC surface antigen expression while removing the exhaustion-promoting CD96 receptor from the T cell compartment, resulting in robust PTC elimination and remarkable therapeutic potential against CRC.² The combination of antigen targeting (via [[epcam|EpCAM]]) and CD96 disruption creates a synthetic immunological advantage for destroying PTCs while restoring durable T cell-mediated anti-tumor immunity.

---

## Future therapeutic directions

The identification of PTCs as a major barrier to CRC treatment success has opened new therapeutic avenues beyond conventional chemotherapy. Rational combinations of anti-[[cd96|CD96]] therapy with standard NAC regimens may enhance long-term outcomes by simultaneously eliminating chemotherapy-sensitive bulk tumors and preventing PTC-driven immune escape. [[epcam|EpCAM]]-targeted [[car-t-cells|CAR-T cells]] with CD96 depletion represent a personalized immunotherapeutic strategy to target the heterogeneous PTC compartment, with potential applicability to other epithelial cancers expressing both EpCAM and CD155 in their therapy-resistant subpopulations.
