**Interleukin-4 (IL-4)** is a pleiotropic cytokine mediating opposing immunological outcomes depending on context—driving pathogenic Th2 inflammation in allergic diseases while simultaneously enabling reparative tissue regeneration when therapeutically delivered. IL-4 exerts its pro-inflammatory functions through the [[stat6|IL-4/STAT6/Th2 signaling axis]], promoting Th2 cell differentiation and type 2 immune responses in conditions such as [[atopic-dermatitis|atopic dermatitis]], where suppression of IL-4 signaling constitutes a validated therapeutic strategy. Conversely, intracellular IL-4 expression induced via [[lipid-nanoparticles|lipid nanoparticle (LNP)-mediated mRNA delivery]] reprograms macrophages toward a pro-reparative phenotype capable of sustaining tissue-supportive functions even within inflammatory microenvironments. This dual therapeutic potential—as a suppression target in inflammatory pathology and as a regenerative agent in tissue repair—establishes IL-4 as a cytokine whose activity context determines clinical utility.

---

## Mechanism

IL-4 exerts its pleiotropic immunological effects through receptor-mediated activation of the transcription factor [[stat6|STAT6]], initiating phosphorylation cascades that promote Th2 cell differentiation and the generation of type 2 inflammatory responses.¹ In disease contexts, the mono-ADP-ribosyltransferase [[parp14|PARP14]] amplifies IL-4/STAT6/Th2 signaling, serving as a critical regulatory node that sustains pathogenic Th2 polarization and inflammatory cytokine production. Pharmacological inhibition of [[parp14|PARP14]] attenuates the capacity of IL-4 to drive pro-inflammatory Th2 responses, providing a mechanistic intervention point for allergic and inflammatory disorders.¹ The pathway's plasticity extends beyond inflammation: under appropriate delivery conditions, IL-4 can be supplied exogenously to reprogram immune cells toward reparative phenotypes that actively promote tissue healing and regeneration.

---

## Atopic dermatitis and inflammatory suppression

[[Atopic-dermatitis|Atopic dermatitis]] is characterized by elevated IL-4 expression and aberrant Th2 skewing, driven in part through [[parp14|PARP14]]-augmented IL-4/STAT6 signaling. In a dinitrochlorobenzene-induced mouse model of atopic dermatitis, selective PARP14 inhibition via the compound XW-17 (IC₅₀ = 3.03 nM) significantly attenuated skin lesions and reduced expression of key inflammatory mediators including IL-4, IL-13, IgE, and IL-17A.¹ This simultaneous suppression of both Th2 (IL-4, IL-13) and Th17 (IL-17A) responses demonstrates that blocking [[parp14|PARP14]] disrupts multiple inflammatory pathways in parallel, validating [[parp14|PARP14]] inhibition as a disease-modifying approach for allergic inflammation.¹ The superior efficacy of PARP14 inhibition compared to existing therapies suggests that targeting IL-4 upstream regulatory nodes may provide clinical advantages over direct IL-4 neutralization strategies.

## Macrophage reprogramming and tissue regeneration

In contrast to its pro-inflammatory role in allergic disease, IL-4 can be harnessed as a regenerative agent through [[lipid-nanoparticles|lipid nanoparticle (LNP)-mediated mRNA delivery]], enabling sustained IL-4 expression within adoptively transferred macrophages. This engineered approach overcomes the inherent phenotypic plasticity of macrophages, allowing cells to maintain a pro-reparative state despite exposure to inflammatory cytokines in the tissue microenvironment. In a murine model of [[volumetric-muscle-loss|volumetric muscle loss]], macrophages pre-treated with [[lipid-nanoparticles|IL-4-loaded LNPs]] retained their reparative phenotype for at least one week post-transplantation, actively promoting tissue healing outcomes including increased vascularization, enhanced fiber size distribution, and remodeling of the tissue scaffold.² Remarkably, [[lipid-nanoparticles|IL-4-LNP-treated macrophages]] also educated endogenous resident macrophages toward reparative phenotypes, amplifying the regenerative response beyond the transplanted cell population.² The phenotypic stability achieved through LNP-mediated IL-4 expression opens novel opportunities for macrophage-based cell therapies across regenerative medicine applications.
