**Microglia** are resident immune cells of the central nervous system that serve as primary mediators of neuroinflammatory responses following brain injury and pathological insults. These specialized glial cells undergo rapid morphological and functional transformation upon sensing injury-associated signals, adopting a pro-inflammatory phenotype characterized by the upregulation of [[ccr5|C-C chemokine receptor 5 (CCR5)]] and massive production of pro-inflammatory cytokines that perpetuate secondary brain injury, neuronal apoptosis, and cognitive dysfunction.¹² The discovery that [[ccr5|CCR5]] functions as a critical upstream regulator of microglial activation has established pharmacological modulation of this pathway as a therapeutically viable strategy for mitigating neuroinflammation-driven brain injury across diverse pathological contexts.²

---

## Mechanism of Microglial Activation

Microglia exist in a quiescent surveillance state under physiological conditions but undergo explosive activation upon sensing danger-associated molecular patterns and injury signals released by damaged neural tissue. This activation response is mediated through pattern recognition receptors including [[tlr4|TLR4]] and represents a critical component of innate immune defense within the CNS. Upon activation, microglia undergo dramatic morphological changes—from ramified resting cells to activated amoeboid forms—accompanied by upregulation of pro-inflammatory gene programs that drive the production of TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and other inflammatory mediators. These cytokines amplify neuroinflammatory cascades through paracrine signaling to neurons and other glial cells, ultimately resulting in neuronal apoptosis, synaptic dysfunction, and impaired cognitive function.

The temporal dynamics of microglial activation depend critically on the nature and severity of the precipitating brain injury. In [[radiotherapy|cranial radiation]], microglia remain persistently activated for remarkably extended periods—up to 84 days following exposure—creating a sustained pro-inflammatory microenvironment that continuously drives secondary neurodegeneration and progressive cognitive decline.¹ This protracted activation underlies the high incidence of radiation-induced cognitive impairment, a major dose-limiting toxicity that restricts the therapeutic doses that can be safely delivered to patients requiring brain tumor radiotherapy.

## Microglial Activation in Subarachnoid Hemorrhage

[[subarachnoid-hemorrhage|Subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH)]] represents an acute and catastrophic form of brain injury that triggers explosive microglial activation within hours of the hemorrhagic event, substantially exacerbating primary neuronal damage through secondary injury mechanisms. Molecular expression profiling reveals that [[ccr5|CCR5]] is rapidly upregulated following SAH, with prominent expression localized to microglia within the cortex and perilesional regions surrounding the hemorrhagic site.² This upregulation of [[ccr5|CCR5]] on activated microglia serves as a critical node controlling the intensity and duration of pro-inflammatory microglial polarization, thereby linking innate immune activation to the perpetuation of secondary brain injury.

The role of activated microglia in SAH pathophysiology extends beyond simple bystander inflammation. Activated microglia directly mediate neuronal injury through multiple interconnected mechanisms: release of pro-inflammatory cytokines that prime neurons for death, disruption of the blood-brain barrier and cerebral edema formation, production of reactive oxygen species, and direct contact-dependent neurotoxicity. The temporal correlation between robust microglial activation and progressive neurological deterioration over hours to days after SAH establishes these cells as a primary driver of secondary injury and an attractive therapeutic target.

## Therapeutic Modulation through CCR5 Antagonism

The identification of [[ccr5|CCR5]] as an upstream driver of pathological microglial activation in SAH has proven directly therapeutically actionable. Intranasal treatment with [[maraviroc]] (MVC), a selective [[ccr5|CCR5]] antagonist originally developed for HIV therapy, successfully shifts activated microglia toward an anti-inflammatory phenotype and substantially suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokine production.² In experimental SAH models, [[maraviroc]] administration attenuates the formation of brain edema, suppresses neuronal apoptosis, and produces dramatic improvements in both acute neurological deficits and long-term functional recovery.² Mechanistic studies confirm that [[maraviroc]] directly reverses the pro-inflammatory polarization of microglia induced by oxyhemoglobin (the major toxic hemoglobin species released during intracerebral hemorrhage), revealing a cell-autonomous neuroprotective mechanism independent of peripheral immune effects.

The intranasal route of [[maraviroc]] delivery is particularly advantageous for targeting the inflamed CNS while minimizing systemic immune effects. This approach exemplifies the broader therapeutic principle that pharmacological phenotype switching of microglia—rather than complete cellular ablation—represents a sustainable and physiologically sound strategy for interrupting neuroinflammatory cascades that drive secondary brain injury.

## Neuroprotection through Carbon-Based Nanomaterials

An emerging complementary approach to microglial-targeted neuroprotection leverages carbon-based nanomaterials with intrinsic antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties. The water-soluble [[c60-ser|C60 fullerene derivative (C60-ser)]]—a [60]fullerene nanoparticle engineered for enhanced aqueous solubility—effectively suppresses microglial activation and prevents neurodegeneration in the context of [[radiotherapy|cranial radiation-induced brain injury]].¹ In mice subjected to whole-brain irradiation, oral administration of [[c60-ser]] initiated 24 hours after radiation exposure restored spatial working memory performance to near-normal levels by day 45 post-irradiation, as demonstrated by Y-maze testing.¹ Immunohistochemical quantification of CD68 (a marker of microglial activation) revealed that while cranial [[radiotherapy|irradiation alone]] sustained high microglial activation for the full 84-day observation period, [[c60-ser]] treatment markedly suppressed this activation, reducing CD68 reactivity to levels indistinguishable from unirradiated controls.¹

The neuroprotective mechanism of [[c60-ser]] appears to involve suppression of the sustained pro-inflammatory microenvironment that drives radiation-induced neurodegeneration. By interrupting chronic microglial activation and the accompanying pro-inflammatory cytokine production, [[c60-ser]] preserves cognitive function and synaptic integrity. This approach illustrates the therapeutic potential of nanostructured materials in specifically targeting pathological neuroinflammation.

## Microglial Engagement by CNS-Targeted Delivery Platforms

Emerging delivery platforms engineered to penetrate the blood-brain barrier via the olfactory route are beginning to directly engage microglia as part of their mechanistic action. The [[nanoinbrain|NanoInBrain platform]], a rationally designed polypeptide-based hybrid system combining a star-shaped poly-L-glutamate nanocarrier with a hyaluronic acid-based depot-forming hydrogel matrix, achieves robust intranasal CNS delivery through the olfactory mucosa.³ Intracellular imaging studies confirm that [[nanoinbrain|NanoInBrain]] is actively taken up by both neurons and microglia within brain parenchyma, suggesting significant therapeutic potential for future microglial-targeted drug delivery.³ Such platforms may enable direct intracellular delivery of anti-inflammatory payloads, gene-silencing therapeutics, or metabolic modulators capable of shifting microglial phenotype from pro-inflammatory to anti-inflammatory states, potentially enhancing the efficacy of microglial-targeted neuroprotection.
