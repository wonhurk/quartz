**TCF1 (TCF7)** is a transcription factor that marks and sustains stem-like, self-renewing CD8+ T cell populations with enhanced persistence and anti-tumor potential, particularly within [[tumor-infiltrating-lymphocytes|tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs)]] and tissue-resident memory cells (TRMs).¹ TCF1 expression is dynamically regulated during T cell differentiation, remaining high in progenitor and memory states but progressively lost during differentiation toward terminal exhaustion — a transition mechanistically driven by the [[irf4]]-[[blimp1]] transcriptional axis.¹ This TCF1 loss can be prevented through alternative [[cd28]]-driven signaling that bypasses canonical [[tcr]] engagement while preserving metabolic fitness and exhaustion resistance.² Enforced preservation of TCF1+ populations through proteostasis maintenance enhances TIL function and improves cancer immunotherapy outcomes, establishing TCF1 as a key therapeutic target for durable anti-tumor immunity.¹

---

## Mechanism

TCF1 functions as a central transcriptional hub controlling T cell fate decisions between self-renewal and terminal differentiation. In the absence of exhaustion signals, TCF1 sustains a transcriptional program characteristic of stem-like memory cells marked by metabolic flexibility, multipotency, and resistance to activation-induced cell death.¹ The primary endogenous driver of TCF1 loss is the [[irf4]]-[[blimp1]] transcriptional axis activated during conventional [[tcr]]/[[cd3]] stimulation: [[irf4]] induction promotes [[blimp1]] upregulation, which in turn suppresses TCF1 expression and locks cells into a glycolytic, terminally differentiated state.² Critically, this differentiation trajectory is not obligatory. Alternative signaling through [[cd28]] alone — delivered via artificial antigen-presenting cells engineered with membrane-bound [[cd28]] superagonists — sustains high TCF1 expression and reprograms naive CD8+ T cells toward a stem-like memory phenotype without activating [[irf4]].² This [[cd28]]-driven pathway preserves metabolic fitness and prevents the exhaustion-associated transcriptional remodeling that occurs through conventional TCR engagement, revealing an underappreciated role for [[cd28]] in guiding stem-like T cell fate.

---

## T cell stem cells and memory

TCF1 expression marks the apex of T cell hierarchy, identifying stem cell-like memory CD8+ T cells (Tscm) with superior self-renewal capacity, multipotency across effector functions, and intrinsic resistance to differentiation and exhaustion.² High TCF1 levels are retained in tissue-resident memory populations in both healthy tissues and tumors, where they correlate with superior anti-tumor function and patient prognosis.¹ In stark contrast, terminally exhausted TILs lack TCF1 expression entirely, having undergone progressive downregulation during the transition from progenitor-exhausted to terminal states.¹ This distinction is functionally critical: progenitor-exhausted TILs that retain TCF1 expression remain reversible and capable of reinvigoration by immunotherapy, whereas TCF1-negative terminally exhausted cells have largely lost this capacity. Tumors enriched for TCF1+ TILs with tissue-resident memory features are associated with improved patient prognosis, highlighting the prognostic value of the TCF1+ population as a predictor of immunotherapy response.¹

## Adoptive cell therapy and immunotherapy synergy

Preserving and expanding TCF1+ populations has emerged as a rational strategy to improve adoptive T cell therapy and overcome the limitations of conventional ex vivo stimulation protocols. Traditional [[cd3]]/[[cd28]] stimulation, while effective at generating effector T cells, paradoxically drives [[irf4]]-dependent TCF1 downregulation and premature differentiation, limiting persistence and anti-tumor durability of transferred cells.² A novel approach using [[cd28]]-driven ex vivo expansion with artificial antigen-presenting cells bypasses [[tcr]] signaling entirely and prevents [[irf4]] induction, generating CD8+ T cells with sustained TCF1 expression, superior metabolic fitness, and enhanced resistance to exhaustion.² Upon subsequent tumor encounter and [[tcr]] engagement, these [[cd28]]-expanded T cells mount robust effector responses while maintaining superior persistence and anti-tumor activity compared to conventionally stimulated cells.² This approach prioritizes preservation of stem-like potential over immediate effector potency, representing a paradigm shift in adoptive cell therapy design toward durable, functionally potent anti-tumor immunity.

## Proteostasis-dependent TCF1 preservation

Recent proteomic and transcriptomic profiling identified proteostasis — the cellular balance of protein synthesis, folding, and degradation — as a critical distinguishing feature separating TCF1+ stem-like TILs and tissue-resident memory populations from terminally exhausted cells.¹ Terminally exhausted TILs accumulate unfolded proteins and exhibit downregulation of E3 ubiquitin ligases [[neurl3]], [[rnf149]], and [[wsb1]], despite intact proteasome function, suggesting dysregulated protein quality control.¹ Enforced expression of these ubiquitin ligases in T cells preserved stem-like TCF1+ populations, improved TIL function in tumors, enhanced responses in chronic infection models, and significantly improved cancer immunotherapy outcomes.¹ The mechanism involves proteostasis-dependent maintenance of TCF1 protein stability and suppression of the protein aggregation that drives terminal exhaustion.¹ Sustained ligase expression in preclinical models rescued the proteostatic collapse associated with terminal exhaustion and substantially improved immunotherapy efficacy, implicating proteostasis maintenance as a therapeutically targetable axis for enhancing TCF1+ population persistence and anti-tumor function.

---

## Clinical significance and therapeutic leverage

The identification of TCF1 status as a prognostic marker in tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte populations has important implications for biomarker-driven patient stratification and therapy selection.¹ TCF1+ TILs with tissue-resident memory features may identify patients most likely to benefit from immune checkpoint blockade or adoptive cell therapy, while TCF1-low tumors might require combinatorial approaches to reinvigorate exhausted populations or proteostasis-enhancing interventions. Multiple intervention points now exist to therapeutically enhance TCF1+ population persistence: proteostasis enhancement via E3 ligase stabilization, optimized [[cd28]]-driven stimulation protocols in adoptive cell therapy, or direct TCF1 stabilization through protein quality control pathways. These complementary approaches offer flexible leverage for enhancing durable anti-tumor immunity across diverse cancer types and patient populations.
