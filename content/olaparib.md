**Olaparib** is a poly-ADP ribose polymerase (PARP) inhibitor that impairs cellular DNA damage repair and is approved for treatment of multiple cancer types, particularly those harboring homologous recombination repair (HRR) gene deficiencies. By trapping PARP at DNA lesions and blocking single-strand break repair, olaparib induces synthetic lethality in tumors that depend on HRR pathways for genomic survival.¹ The drug achieves enhanced efficacy when combined with hormone-directed therapies in prostate cancer, DNA damage response modulators in breast cancer, and metabolic inhibitors in colorectal cancer, though emerging resistance mechanisms via oncogenic protein activation limit broader applicability.²³

---

## Mechanism

Olaparib functions as a catalytic inhibitor of PARP, preventing the transfer of ADP-ribose groups to target proteins and thereby impairing the repair of DNA single-strand breaks. In cells with defective HRR pathways — such as those bearing [[brca1]], [[brca2]], or other HRR gene mutations — the accumulation of unrepaired DNA lesions exceeds cellular tolerance, triggering apoptosis. This synthetic lethality approach has proven transformative in precision oncology, as HRR-deficient tumors become exquisitely dependent on PARP inhibition for cell death.¹ The efficacy of olaparib is particularly pronounced in genomically unstable malignancies, and recent studies reveal that the drug's toxicity can be substantially augmented when combined with agents targeting complementary DNA damage response pathways.

## Prostate cancer applications

In [[metastatic-hormone-sensitive-prostate-cancer|metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC)]], olaparib combined with [[abiraterone|abiraterone and prednisone]] has emerged as a standard-of-care approach, particularly for patients harboring HRR gene mutations including [[brca1|BRCA1]], [[brca2|BRCA2]], and other homologous recombination repair deficiencies. Phase II clinical data confirm that this combination significantly prolongs radiographic progression-free survival in HRR-mutated disease, where the synergistic blockade of both androgen signaling and DNA repair pathways drives superior tumor control.² The drug's mechanism in prostate cancer is exquisitely dependent on HRR competence; as detailed below, [[hr]]-competent castration-resistant prostate cancer cells activate alternative resistance pathways upon olaparib exposure.

## Triple-negative breast cancer synergy

[[Triple-negative-breast-cancer|Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC)]] cells demonstrate marked sensitivity to olaparib when combined with [[usp1]] inhibitors. Ubiquitin-specific protease 1 (USP1) regulates the DNA damage response by deubiquitinating [[pcna|PCNA]], a critical factor in DNA repair; inhibition of USP1 therefore potentiates olaparib efficacy by impairing damage tolerance mechanisms. Preclinical xenograft studies with compound 57, a novel USP1-UAF1 inhibitor, demonstrate synergistic antitumor activity: the combination substantially enhanced tumor growth inhibition compared to olaparib monotherapy, driven by elevated DNA damage accumulation and suppression of tumor survival pathways.³ This work validates USP1-PARP inhibitor synergy as a promising therapeutic strategy for TNBC.

## Colorectal cancer and lipid metabolism

In [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer]], olaparib achieves synthetic lethality when preceded by inhibition of [[fasn|fatty acid synthase (FASN)]]. FASN inhibition induces DNA damage while simultaneously impairing the [[ddr|DNA damage response]] through decreased [[atm|ATM]] expression and reduced [[chk2|CHK2]] phosphorylation; mechanistically, FASN inhibition attenuates [[brca1|BRCA1]] and ATM recruitment to γH2AX foci in an acetylation-dependent manner. When FASN inhibitors such as [[tvb-2640]] are combined with chemotherapy (irinotecan), the resulting DNA damage is substantially potentiated, and olaparib serves as a highly effective maintenance therapy by exploiting the FASN inhibition-induced repair deficiency.⁴ This multi-agent approach — FASN inhibition, chemotherapy, and PARP inhibition in sequence — represents a precision combination strategy for metabolically-driven colorectal cancers.

---

## M1C-mediated resistance

Despite its efficacy in HRR-deficient tumors, olaparib treatment in HR-competent castration-resistant prostate cancer cells induces expression of the oncogenic [[m1c|M1C]] protein, activating a compensatory genomic instability pathway that confers drug resistance. M1C drives the expression of [[atm|ATM]], phosphorylation of [[kap1|KAP1(S824)]], and activation of the [[sting|STING]] pathway, which collectively derepress the [[line-1|LINE-1]] retrotransposon.² M1C further activates [[herv-k|HERV-K]] gag, pol, and env genes, inducing expression of HERV-K ENV protein and simultaneously upregulating [[apobec3|APOBEC3]] genes that have evolved to restrain retrotransposon-mediated genomic instability. This M1C-driven feedback loop — integrating retrotransposon activation with APOBEC3 upregulation — serves as a critical resistance mechanism in HR-competent cells, enabling escape from PARP inhibition despite sustained DNA damage. Targeting M1C alongside PARP inhibition represents a rational approach to overcome this resistance pathway in patients whose tumors retain homologous recombination capacity.
