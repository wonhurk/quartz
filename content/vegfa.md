**VEGFA** (vascular endothelial growth factor A) is a critical hypoxia-responsive growth factor that drives angiogenesis and vascular remodeling through coordinated regulation of endothelial cell migration and tube formation. Its expression is tightly controlled by [[hif-1-alpha|HIF-1α]] under hypoxic conditions, making it a central node linking cellular oxygen status to angiogenic gene expression. While VEGFA-driven vascular regeneration is neuroprotective in ischemic stroke when coupled with [[nos3|NOS3]] activation, supporting functional recovery and endothelial repair, the same signaling axis drives pathological neovascularization supporting malignant progression in solid tumors including [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]] and [[clear-cell-renal-cell-carcinoma|clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC)]].¹ The pathway signals through [[flt1|FLT1]] (VEGF receptor 1) as a pivotal mediator of epithelial-endothelial crosstalk networks implicated in cellular senescence and immune dysfunction. Suppression of the HIF-1α/VEGF axis represents a promising therapeutic strategy to simultaneously inhibit tumor angiogenesis, restore vascular normalization, and enhance immunotherapy efficacy.

---

## Mechanism

VEGFA signaling is initiated under hypoxic conditions when [[hif-1-alpha|HIF-1α]] stabilizes and transactivates the VEGFA promoter, establishing the functional link between cellular oxygen depletion and angiogenic gene expression. Once secreted, VEGFA binds to its cognate receptor [[flt1|FLT1]] on vascular endothelial cells, triggering downstream signaling cascades that promote endothelial cell migration and tube formation essential for vascular neovascularization. The pathway is functionally coupled to metabolic reprogramming, where VEGFA-driven angiogenesis coincides with glycolytic metabolism and ATP biosynthesis, creating an integrated metabolic-vascular axis that supports both tissue repair and malignant progression depending on cellular and tissue context. Co-expression of [[nos3|NOS3]] (endothelial nitric oxide synthase) amplifies VEGFA-driven vascular responses by enhancing endothelial function and promoting vascular regeneration, a relationship particularly critical in recovery from ischemic injury.

The VEGFA/FLT1 axis operates within broader epithelial-endothelial crosstalk networks where FLT1 expression on endothelial cells mediates bidirectional communication with epithelial cells expressing VEGFA. In this network, VEGFA-expressing epithelial cells drive endothelial activation, and this crosstalk is associated with both cellular senescence markers and enhanced malignant phenotypes when dysregulated. The signaling specificity is further refined through interaction with additional nodes including [[akt1|AKT1]], which modulates the proliferative and survival responses downstream of FLT1 activation.

## Tumor angiogenesis and malignant progression

In hepatocellular carcinoma, VEGFA-mediated neovascularization is a hallmark of tumor progression, supporting both the structural growth of malignant lesions and metabolic reprogramming toward aerobic glycolysis and biosynthetic substrate accumulation. Post-embolization hypoxia paradoxically triggers HIF-1α-dependent VEGFA upregulation, creating a cycle of therapeutic resistance where transarterial embolization-induced ischemia drives compensatory angiogenesis through increased VEGFA expression that supplies oxygen to residual tumor tissue.² This hypoxia-driven angiogenic rebound limits the durability of embolization-based therapies and necessitates combination approaches that directly target the HIF-1α/VEGF axis rather than relying on ischemic stress alone.

In clear cell renal cell carcinoma, VEGFA emerges from integrated multi-omics and machine learning analysis as a core component of a FLT1-centered regulatory network driving aggressive malignant behavior through epithelial-endothelial crosstalk. High VEGFA expression in proximal tubular epithelial cells synergizes with endothelial cell expression of [[flt1|FLT1]] and [[akt1|AKT1]] to establish a senescence-rich, immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment that contributes to the poor prognosis and therapy resistance characteristic of this disease.³ Targeting this FLT1/VEGFA network and the underlying cellular senescence programs it sustains represents a rational approach to restore immunological control and improve outcomes in ccRCC.

## Ischemic stroke recovery and vascular regeneration

In stark contrast to its tumor-promoting role, VEGFA upregulation constitutes a neuroprotective mechanism in ischemic stroke when coordinated with [[nos3|NOS3]] activation. Following ischemic insult, HIF-1α-driven VEGFA expression initiates vascular regeneration and endothelial repair of the compromised neurovascular unit, supporting functional recovery of motor coordination and reduction of anxiety-like behaviors in animal stroke models.¹ The VEGFA/NOS3 pathway restores tissue perfusion through promotion of endothelial migration and vascular tube formation, physically repairing stroke-damaged vasculature while simultaneously stabilizing metabolic homeostasis through coordinated reprogramming of key metabolic pathways including arginine-proline, methionine, purine, and tyrosine metabolism.¹ This metabolic restoration is critical for mitigating ammonia toxicity and oxidative stress that accumulate during ischemic tissue damage and reperfusion.

Natural activation of VEGFA/NOS3 signaling can be achieved through plant-derived extracellular vesicles enriched in bioactive phthalides, which efficiently cross the blood-brain barrier and accumulate in ischemic brain tissue at peak concentrations within 12 hours of systemic administration. These [[cxev|CXEVs]] activate VEGFA and NOS3 expression while normalizing downstream effectors including KDR and MAPK1, driving endothelial migration and tube formation in vitro and vascular regeneration in vivo.¹ The capacity of plant-derived nanoparticles to naturally activate VEGFA/NOS3 signaling provides a template for biomimetic therapeutic approaches that harness context-dependent angiogenic activation specifically for tissue repair.

## Metabolic coupling and hypoxic programming

VEGFA-driven angiogenesis is functionally inseparable from metabolic reprogramming, particularly the upregulation of glycolytic enzymes [[ldha|LDHA]] and the glucose transporter [[glut1|GLUT1]] under [[hif-1-alpha|HIF-1α]] regulation.⁴ In tumors, this metabolic-vascular coupling creates a self-reinforcing cycle where increased angiogenesis supplies oxygen while simultaneously priming endothelial and epithelial cells for aerobic glycolysis, ATP depletion resistance, and accumulation of biosynthetic precursors supporting rapid proliferation. In ischemic tissues, the same metabolic repriming through VEGFA/NOS3 signaling serves a reparative function by restoring energy homeostasis and substrate pools depleted during ischemic injury and metabolic crisis. The fundamental distinction between pathological and reparative outcomes hinges on tissue context and the sustainability of the metabolic-angiogenic program: sustained pathological angiogenesis in tumors supports vascularization and malignant progression, while transient angiogenic activation in stroke supports tissue recovery and functional restoration.

## Therapeutic targeting of the HIF-1α/VEGF axis

Suppression of [[hif-1-alpha|HIF-1α]] and its downstream VEGFA target represents a rational strategy to simultaneously inhibit tumor angiogenesis, block metabolic reprogramming, restore vascular normalization, and enhance immune activation against malignancies. Indirect targeting approaches using [[zns-encapsulated-gelatin-microspheres|ZnS-encapsulated gelatin microspheres (ZnS@GMs)]] destabilize HIF-1α itself through hydrogen sulfide-mediated disruption of mitochondrial respiration and zinc-mediated inhibition of glycolytic enzymes hexokinase and lactate dehydrogenase.² This dual mechanism offers complementary benefit: suppression of VEGFA expression through HIF-1α destabilization combined with direct inhibition of glycolytic metabolism through enzymatic targeting.

Vascular normalization following HIF-1α/VEGF suppression reduces the hypoxic tumor microenvironment and improves perfusion of oxygen and immunotherapeutic agents, complementing immune checkpoint inhibition with [[anti-pd-1-antibody|anti-PD-1 therapy]] by restoring dendritic cell maturation, cytotoxic T cell infiltration, and macrophage repolarization within the tumor microenvironment.² These synergistic effects position HIF-1α/VEGF axis inhibition as a cornerstone of combination immunotherapy strategies in hypoxia-driven malignancies.

## Natural VEGFA activators

Plant-derived extracellular vesicles from Ligusticum sinense chuanxiong (commonly termed [[cxev|CXEVs]]) are naturally enriched in phthalide metabolites including ligustilide and butylphthalide derivatives that efficiently cross the blood-brain barrier and accumulate in ischemic brain tissue. CXEVs activate VEGFA and [[nos3|NOS3]] signaling while normalizing downstream effectors KDR and MAPK1 expression, driving endothelial migration and tube formation in vitro and promoting vascular regeneration in vivo.¹ These natural bioactive molecules can be engineered into sophisticated drug delivery platforms with optimized loading efficiency and sustained release kinetics to enhance therapeutic potency in ischemic stroke through co-delivery with neuroprotective compounds.¹ The capacity of plant-derived nanoparticles to naturally activate VEGFA/NOS3 signaling provides a powerful template for biomimetic therapeutic approaches that harness context-dependent angiogenic activation for tissue repair while avoiding the pathological sequelae of VEGFA activation in malignancy.
