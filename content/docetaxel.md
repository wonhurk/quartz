**Docetaxel** is a semisynthetic taxane-class chemotherapy agent with established clinical utility in conventional combination regimens and emerging applications in sophisticated nanomedical platforms. Mechanistically, docetaxel stabilizes microtubule bundles and inhibits their depolymerization, disrupting cell division and triggering apoptosis across multiple cancer types. Its therapeutic benefit extends beyond direct cytotoxicity when integrated into engineered nanoassemblies, where molecular design principles govern assembly kinetics, drug release, and the critical balance between antitumor efficacy and systemic toxicity.¹ Recent advances demonstrate that docetaxel's activity in combination therapy and nanomedical formulations can be substantially optimized through coordinated targeting of both malignant cells and the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment.

---

## Clinical applications in gastric cancer

In locally advanced gastric cancer, docetaxel has emerged as a potent addition to standard chemotherapy, improving outcomes for patients with HER2-negative disease. The DOS regimen — combining docetaxel, [[oxaliplatin]], and [[s-1]] — achieves a major pathological response rate of 25.7% in HER2-negative locally advanced gastric adenocarcinoma, significantly surpassing the 17.8% response rate observed with standard [[oxaliplatin]] and S-1 (SOX) therapy alone.² This improvement translates to reduced disease progression risk, with multivariable analysis demonstrating a hazard ratio of 0.74 for disease-free survival when docetaxel is added to the regimen.² Critically, the addition of docetaxel does not increase severe perioperative complications, maintaining a safety profile equivalent to standard chemotherapy with 2.2% severe adverse events in both arms.² The progression-reduction benefit of DOS was robust across prespecified subgroups, particularly benefiting elderly patients (age ≥65 years) and those with earlier T-stage or lower nodal burden disease, establishing DOS as a new neoadjuvant standard for appropriately selected populations.

## Prodrug nanoassemblies and topology-guided design

Docetaxel's therapeutic profile has been substantially refined through engineered self-assembling nanoparticles where the drug is conjugated to fatty acid-based modification modules. The molecular topology of these modification modules — whether linear, branched, or cyclic — fundamentally dictates assembly kinetics, drug release profiles, and the critical balance between antitumor efficacy and systemic toxicity. Linear-structured nanoassembly modules demonstrate superior performance across key functional metrics: they accelerate assembly kinetics, enhance predictable and controlled drug release, and maximize antitumor efficacy in both in vitro and in vivo models.¹ Branched variants show intermediate performance characteristics, while cyclic-topology nanoassemblies achieve optimized safety profiles despite reduced potency.¹ This finding demonstrates that molecular design can be strategically tuned to prioritize either therapeutic intensity or tolerability depending on clinical context. The topology-to-therapy mapping approach provides systematic evidence that iterative modification of the nanoassembly scaffold — independent of docetaxel's intrinsic cytotoxicity — represents a powerful and generalizable design parameter for cancer nanotherapeutics.

## P-selectin targeting and tumor microenvironment remodeling

Docetaxel has been integrated into advanced immunochemotherapy platforms that simultaneously address malignant cells and the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment through coordinated molecular targeting. The [[FINAL]] nanoplatform — Fucoidan-docetaxel Immunomodulatory Nanoparticles as an Antitumoral Lancer — couples docetaxel payload with surface modification by [[fucoidan]] to engage [[p-selectin]] expressed on both cancer cells and [[tumor-associated-macrophages]].³ Beyond targeting selectivity, this dual-cell orchestration achieves comprehensive tumor microenvironment remodeling: fucoidan's intrinsic bioactivities reduce reactive oxygen species accumulation within cancer cells, promote M1 macrophage polarization (shifting the immunosuppressive landscape toward antitumor immunity), and suppress [[vegf-a|VEGF-A]]-mediated angiogenesis.³ P-selectin-mediated targeting simultaneously enhances cellular uptake and disrupts tumor-macrophage adhesion, reducing circulating hybrid cell formation and constraining protumoral cell-cell communication.³ Concurrent transcriptomic profiling reveals that [[FINAL]] activates synergistic immune-stimulatory pathways while repressing tumor progression signatures, indicating that the integration of docetaxel into this immunochemotherapy platform achieves system-level therapeutic coordination rather than simple additive effects.³
