**Renal fibrosis** is a pathological process characterized by excessive accumulation of extracellular matrix (ECM) in kidney tissue, representing a common outcome of [[chronic-kidney-disease|chronic kidney disease (CKD)]] with multiple etiologies. The condition manifests prominently in systemic lupus erythematosus ([[systemic-lupus-erythematosus|SLE]]) and diabetic kidney disease ([[dkd|DKD]]), where distinct mechanistic pathways drive progressive fibrotic injury. Central to renal fibrosis pathogenesis are impaired neuroimmune communication through the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway and dysregulated renal iron metabolism that promotes fibrotic signaling responses.¹² Recent advances have identified these mechanisms as therapeutically tractable targets, offering interventions that can arrest or reverse renal fibrotic progression through both immunomodulatory and metabolic approaches.

---

## Mechanism

Renal fibrosis develops through multifactorial pathways involving immune dysregulation, metabolic dysfunction, and aberrant fibrotic signaling cascades. In [[systemic-lupus-erythematosus|SLE]], chronic inflammation is linked to elevated blood pressure and impaired neuroimmune communication, particularly through reduced activity of the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway, which normally resolves inflammation via [[acetylcholinesterase]] activity. In [[dkd|DKD]], a distinct but equally critical mechanism involves pathological iron deposition specifically within proximal renal tubules that correlates directly with fibrotic markers. This aberrant iron distribution drives renal fibrosis progression through activation of [[tgf-beta|TGF-β signaling]], a central fibrotic pathway in kidney disease pathogenesis. Both mechanisms ultimately converge on excessive ECM accumulation and progressive loss of renal function, though their upstream drivers differ markedly.

## Renal fibrosis in systemic lupus erythematosus

Renal involvement is a hallmark of [[systemic-lupus-erythematosus|SLE]], where immune dysregulation and hypertension converge to drive renal injury and progressive fibrosis. Long-term systemic treatment with the [[acetylcholinesterase]] inhibitor [[galantamine]] significantly ameliorates renal fibrosis in SLE mice through restoration and enhancement of neuroimmune communication.¹ Galantamine-treated SLE mice exhibited marked reductions in renal injury markers, including urinary albumin, kidney injury molecule-1 (KIM-1), and neutrophil gelatinase-associated lipocalin (NGAL), alongside decreased renal fibrosis.¹ The therapeutic benefits extend beyond the kidney: galantamine treatment improved overall survival and lowered blood pressure in SLE mice, indicating that restoration of cholinergic anti-inflammatory tone provides systemic cardiovascular and immunological protection. Mechanistically, [[galantamine]] reduces circulating soluble TNFR1 — a marker of systemic inflammation implicated in the cardiovascular complications of SLE — and suppresses kidney-infiltrating CD8+ T cells and splenic B cells.¹ These findings establish that impaired neuroimmune communication is a therapeutically addressable driver of both systemic and renal manifestations of lupus nephritis.

## Renal fibrosis in diabetic kidney disease

Diabetic kidney disease represents one of the leading causes of end-stage renal disease, with renal fibrosis marking the transition to irreversible renal failure. A critical mechanistic link between iron dysmetabolism and renal fibrosis has recently been established: although total renal iron content remains unchanged in [[dkd|DKD]], pronounced pathological iron deposition occurs specifically within proximal renal tubules, where it correlates directly with elevated fibrotic markers.² This aberrant tubular iron distribution drives [[tgf-beta|TGF-β]]-dependent fibrotic responses and ECM accumulation. The iron ionophore [[beta-thujaplicin]] (hinokitiol) effectively mobilizes pathologically deposited tubular iron and significantly attenuates renal fibrosis in DKD models.² Mechanistically, [[beta-thujaplicin]] operates through multiple complementary pathways: it reduces oxidative stress, decreases intracellular iron levels, and directly inhibits [[tgf-beta|TGF-β]] signaling in renal tubular cells exposed to the metabolic stress of high glucose and lipid-rich conditions.² These findings establish iron metabolic reprogramming as a primary driver of [[dkd|DKD]] progression and identify pharmacological targeting of tubular iron deposition as a novel therapeutic strategy for preventing renal fibrosis.

## Therapeutic approaches

Current therapeutic strategies for renal fibrosis target distinct mechanistic pathways depending on disease context. In [[systemic-lupus-erythematosus|SLE]]-associated renal fibrosis, enhancement of the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway via [[acetylcholinesterase]] inhibition with [[galantamine]] reduces fibrotic progression and improves renal function markers, while also providing systemic cardiovascular and immunological benefits.¹ In [[dkd|DKD]]-associated renal fibrosis, mobilization of pathologically deposited tubular iron using the iron ionophore [[beta-thujaplicin]] attenuates [[tgf-beta|TGF-β]]-driven fibrotic responses and ECM accumulation.² These divergent but complementary approaches underscore that renal fibrosis is a fundamentally multifactorial process amenable to targeted intervention through both immunomodulatory and metabolic mechanisms, opening avenues for future combination therapies and patient-stratified treatment strategies based on underlying disease etiology.
