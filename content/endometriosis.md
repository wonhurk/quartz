**Endometriosis** is a chronic gynecologic disease characterized by the ectopic growth of endometrial-like tissue within the peritoneal cavity, affecting approximately 10% of women globally.¹² The disease is fundamentally driven by estrogen dysregulation and encompasses dysregulated immune function, aberrant tissue remodeling, and progressive fibrosis.¹ Recent multi-ancestry genome-wide association studies identified 80 genomic regions associated with endometriosis risk across diverse populations, including 37 novel loci, of which 5 are shared with [[adenomyosis]], revealing convergent pathways in cell differentiation, immune regulation, hormonal signaling, and inflammation.² The [[leptin-receptor|leptin receptor (LEPR)]] emerges as a disease-specific therapeutic target independent of clinical phenotype,¹ while the central driver of pathogenesis is [[estrogen-receptor-beta|estrogen receptor-beta (ER-β)]], whose dysregulation sustains the ectopic lesion microenvironment.¹ Progressive fibrosis mediated by disease-associated fibroblasts represents a major pathological feature, with [[aebp1|AEBP1]] linked to cuproptosis-related mechanisms offering new intervention opportunities.³

---

## Genetic architecture and molecular pathways

Endometriosis exhibits a complex polygenic basis, with 80 genomic regions spanning multiple functional categories and ancestry backgrounds. These loci converge on dysregulation of cell differentiation, immune and hormonal regulation, tissue remodeling, and inflammatory responses.² The disease is characterized by aberrant activation of the NF-κB pathway and dysregulation of PI3K-AKT signaling, which drive the expansion and perpetuation of ectopic lesions.⁴ MicroRNA-mediated regulation acts as a key coordinating mechanism, with hub genes [[nfkb1|NFKB1]] and IL6 representing focal points of network dysregulation in endometriosis pathogenesis.⁴ These molecular alterations create a permissive microenvironment supporting the survival, growth, and angiogenic remodeling of ectopic endometrial tissue.

---

## Fibrosis, cuproptosis, and fibroblast heterogeneity

Progressive fibrosis emerges as a defining pathological feature of endometriosis, driven by specialized disease-associated fibroblast subpopulations. Single-cell profiling has revealed that cuproptosis—a copper-dependent form of regulated cell death—is enriched in fibroblasts with profibrotic transcriptional signatures and elevated cuproptosis-related gene activity.³ Through machine learning-guided network analysis, [[aebp1|AEBP1]] was identified as a fibrosis-related hub gene directly linked to cuproptosis pathways, positioning it as a critical effector of fibroblast-mediated fibrogenic progression.³ In functional studies, copper-dependent induction of cuproptosis markers increased [[aebp1|AEBP1]] expression and fibrosis-related proteins via β-catenin pathway engagement, whereas genetic knockdown of [[aebp1|AEBP1]] or the cuproptosis effector FDX1 attenuated these fibrotic phenotypes.³ [[Tetrathiomolybdate|Tetrathiomolybdate (TTM)]], a copper chelator, suppresses cuproptosis-driven fibrosis in vivo, offering a tractable pharmacological approach to target this fibroblast-mediated mechanism.³

## Environmental risk factors and endocrine disruption

Environmental exposure to plastic-derived additives represents a significant modifiable risk factor for endometriosis pathogenesis. [[Phthalate-esters|Phthalate esters (PAEs)]] and [[organophosphate-esters|organophosphate esters (OPEs)]] act as endocrine-disrupting chemicals that perturb female reproductive health through multiple molecular mechanisms.⁴ Network toxicology analyses demonstrated that PAE and OPE compounds interact with disease-specific hub targets—particularly IL6 and [[nfkb1|NFKB1]] in endometriosis—converging on NF-κB activation and PI3K-AKT dysregulation pathways.⁴ Direct molecular docking studies confirmed binding of DEHP, MEHP, and TPHP to key hub proteins, establishing plausible causality between plasticizer exposure and endometriosis risk.⁴ These findings underscore the role of environmental pollutants in driving reproductive pathology and support mechanistic-based prevention strategies targeting exposure reduction.

## Leptin receptor-targeted drug delivery and metformin therapy

The [[leptin-receptor|leptin receptor (LEPR)]] has emerged from clinical analyses as a robust, disease-associated marker strongly linked to endometriosis independent of age, menstrual cycle phase, pelvic pain, infertility history, and disease stage.¹ Leveraging this clinical insight, researchers developed a liposomal delivery platform (HLipo) decorated with the HY7 peptide to specifically target LEPR-expressing ectopic lesions.¹ Genetic blockade or mutation of [[leptin-receptor|LEPR]] abolished lesion-specific liposomal accumulation, confirming the specificity of this targeting strategy.¹ Building on this platform, [[metformin]]-loaded liposomes (Met@HLipo) demonstrated markedly superior antiendometriotic efficacy compared with free [[metformin]], without detectable toxicity.¹ Mechanistically, Met@HLipo induces autophagy-mediated degradation of [[estrogen-receptor-beta|ER-β]], directly targeting the central driver of endometriosis pathogenesis and breaking the estrogen-dependent feedback loop that perpetuates the disease.¹

## Comorbid clinical symptoms and polygenic interactions

Endometriosis frequently presents with multiple comorbid symptoms, including [[abdominal-pain|abdominal pain]], [[anxiety]], [[migraine]], and [[nausea]], reflecting systemic aspects of the disease beyond localized ectopic lesion growth.² Polygenic risk scores for endometriosis show significant statistical interaction with each of these symptom domains, suggesting shared genetic architecture underlying both the disease phenotype and its clinical manifestations.² The intersection of genetic vulnerability, molecular dysregulation, and symptom burden indicates that endometriosis is not solely a local gynecologic condition but a systemic disorder with pleiotropic genetic and molecular effects.

---

## Drug repurposing and future therapeutic strategies

Multi-omic analyses integrated with clinical and pharmacological databases have identified several existing drugs as candidates for endometriosis management, including therapeutic agents currently deployed in breast cancer treatment and hormonal contraception.² These repurposing opportunities span distinct mechanistic categories: agents targeting estrogen signaling (given the [[estrogen-receptor-beta|ER-β]]-driven pathogenesis), immunomodulatory drugs addressing dysregulated immune tolerance, and emerging cuproptosis-targeting compounds such as [[tetrathiomolybdate|tetrathiomolybdate (TTM)]] that interrupt fibroblast-mediated progression.³ The convergence of leptin receptor-targeted delivery and autophagy-directed ER-β degradation with cuproptosis modulation represents a multi-modal therapeutic landscape offering improved efficacy and reduced adverse effects compared with current hormonal and surgical approaches.
