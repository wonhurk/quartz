**Tenecteplase (TNK)** is a third-generation [[thrombolytic]] agent engineered with enhanced [[fibrin]] selectivity and superior capability in breaking down platelet-rich [[thrombus|clots]].¹ Developed to overcome limitations of earlier thrombolytic generations, tenecteplase demonstrates improved fibrin specificity while maintaining potent clot-lysing activity across diverse clinical presentations, from acute [[ischemic-stroke]] to minimally invasive management of [[intracerebral-hemorrhage]]. Its enhanced pharmacokinetic profile positions it as an evolving tool in acute cerebrovascular emergency care, particularly for time-sensitive interventions in older populations.

---

## Mechanism

Tenecteplase functions as a [[fibrin]]-selective thrombolytic agent with engineered modifications that enhance its binding to [[fibrin]] within [[thrombus|clots]] and reduce systemic activation. The drug's enhanced [[fibrin]] selectivity and superior capability in breaking down platelet-rich clots distinguish it from earlier thrombolytic generations.¹ This mechanistic advantage reduces off-target fibrinogenolysis and systemic coagulation disturbances, thereby improving the therapeutic window while maintaining efficacy in lysing resistant thrombi. The enhanced capacity for platelet-rich clot breakdown is particularly relevant in stroke presentations where clot composition varies widely, and in minimally invasive surgical approaches where localized fibrinolytic activity can be targeted to the lesion site.

---

## Ischemic stroke

The therapeutic potential of tenecteplase in acute [[ischemic-stroke]] is being evaluated in the Tenecteplase Versus Standard of Care for Minor Ischaemic Stroke With Proven Occlusion (TEMPO-2) trial, which examines safety and efficacy outcomes in patients receiving intravenous thrombolysis.² The trial specifically focuses on minor [[ischemic-stroke]] with angiographically proven [[thrombus|occlusion]], a population often undertreated due to perceived low disability risk. Notably, the TEMPO-2 trial includes examination of outcomes across age groups, with dedicated analysis of patients older than 80 years compared to those aged 80 years or younger.² The age-stratified design addresses a critical gap in thrombolysis safety literature, as elderly populations have been traditionally underrepresented in thrombolysis trials despite bearing high stroke burden. This evaluation may expand the therapeutic utility of tenecteplase across age groups in acute [[ischemic-stroke]] care.

## Intracerebral hemorrhage

Tenecteplase is also being evaluated as a component of minimally invasive surgical management for acute spontaneous deep [[intracerebral-hemorrhage]]. In the Neuronavigation-Assisted Stereotactic minimally invasive puncture with Tenecteplase (NAS-TNK) protocol, tenecteplase is combined with neuronavigation-assisted stereotactic (NAS) guidance to enhance the precision of catheter placement during minimally invasive hematoma evacuation.¹ The integration of NAS technology provides real-time stereotactic navigation, substantially improving accuracy of catheter positioning to the hematoma core, while tenecteplase's superior platelet-rich clot breakdown capability enables more effective intraoperative lysis of organized hemorrhagic material. This dual approach targets two critical limitations of conventional minimally invasive puncture: imprecision in catheter placement and inadequate hematoma fragmentation. The NAS-TNK protocol is designed to evaluate whether this combination can reduce disability and mortality in patients with acute spontaneous deep [[intracerebral-hemorrhage]], particularly within the [[hypertensive-intracerebral-hemorrhage]] population, where hemorrhage burden and outcome disability are substantial.¹
