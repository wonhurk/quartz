**Tumor-associated macrophages (TAMs)** are immunosuppressive myeloid cells within the tumor microenvironment that represent a major barrier to effective cancer immunotherapy, particularly in metastatic non-small cell lung cancer.¹ TAM accumulation correlates strongly with resistance to [[immune-checkpoint-inhibitors|immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)]] targeting the PD-1/PD-L1 axis, and high TAM infiltration inversely correlates with CD8+ T cell infiltration and antitumor immunity.¹ Molecular regulators including [[cbx4|chromobox 4 (CBX4)]], protein kinase C iota (PKCι), and [[ccl7|CCL7]] drive TAM infiltration and maintain their immunosuppressive phenotype through epigenetic and transcriptional mechanisms.²³

---

## Mechanism of immunosuppression

TAMs exert immunosuppressive activity through multiple coordinated mechanisms that suppress adaptive antitumor immunity. The immunosuppressive M2 phenotype predominates in nonresponders to immunotherapy, characterized by secretion of anti-inflammatory cytokines and expression of immunoregulatory surface molecules that actively suppress CD8+ T cell cytotoxic function.¹ Epigenetic regulation by [[cbx4|CBX4]]—which targets H3K9me3- and H3K27me3-marked endogenous retroelements such as RLTR4-Mm-int—silences retrotransposons and suppresses type I interferon responses, preventing the establishment of an inflamed tumor microenvironment.³ Single-cell RNA-seq and spatial transcriptomics analyses reveal that CBX4 is preferentially expressed in immunosuppressive TAM subpopulations within tumors of patients who fail to respond to anti-PD-1 therapy.³

The PKCι-[[ccl7|CCL7]] signaling axis represents a second critical pathway driving TAM-mediated immunosuppression. PKCι regulates Yap1-dependent transcription of the chemokine CCL7, which actively recruits macrophages to the tumor microenvironment and fosters an immunosuppressive milieu characterized by reduced CD8+ T cell infiltration.² High PKCι expression correlates with resistance to anti-PD-1 antibody therapy, and pharmacological inhibition with auranofin reduces TAM accumulation while promoting CD8+ T cell infiltration.² The inverse relationship between TAM density and CD8+ T cell numbers underscores the direct antagonism between myeloid immunosuppression and adaptive antitumor immunity.¹

---

## Immune checkpoint inhibitor resistance

TAMs constitute independent unfavorable determinants of ICI efficacy across multiple cancer types, with particularly prominent roles in metastatic non-small cell lung cancer.¹ Spatial single-cell proteotyping of pretreatment tumor tissue demonstrates that immunosuppressive TAMs—independent of other immune cell subsets including Th1, Treg, and NK cells—predict poor response to PD-1 axis blockade.¹ High [[cd73|CD73]] expression on cancer cells confers tolerance to ICIs potentially through M2-TAM accumulation and aberrant angiogenesis, representing a convergent mechanism linking metabolic and cellular immunosuppression.¹

In hepatocellular carcinoma, CBX4 expression levels inversely correlate with immune responses and retrotransposon activity, enabling prognostic stratification of patients undergoing ICB therapy.³ Loss of CBX4 in either macrophages or tumor cells derepresses retrotransposons, activating cytosolic RNA-sensing pathways and triggering type I interferon responses that increase infiltration and cytotoxic activity of CD8+ T cells and NK cells, thereby restoring ICB sensitivity.³ This epigenetic immune checkpoint mechanism positions CBX4 as both a predictive biomarker and therapeutic target for overcoming ICI resistance.

---

## Therapeutic targeting and repolarization

TAMs can be therapeutically repolarized from the immunosuppressive M2 phenotype toward immune-activating M1 macrophages through multiple complementary strategies. Pharmacological inhibition of PKCι with auranofin disrupts the PKCι-Yap1-CCL7 axis, reducing TAM accumulation and triggering a CD8+ T cell-dependent antitumor immune response when combined with anti-PD-1 therapy.² The combination controls tumor growth through restored adaptive immunity rather than direct cytotoxicity.²

P-selectin-targeted nanoplatforms achieve dual-cell orchestration by simultaneously engaging P-selectin-expressing cancer cells and TAMs. The FINAL (Fucoidan-[[docetaxel|docetaxel]] Immunomodulatory Nanoparticles as an Antitumoral Lancer) platform exploits [[fucoidan|fucoidan]] surface modification to deliver both receptor-dependent targeting signals and receptor-independent bioactivities.⁴ P-selectin-mediated targeting enhances cellular uptake and disrupts tumor-TAM adhesion, reducing circulating hybrid cell formation, while fucoidan's intrinsic bioactivity promotes M1 macrophage polarization and suppresses VEGF-A-mediated angiogenesis.⁴ RNA-seq transcriptomic profiling demonstrates that FINAL drives synergistic immune activation pathways while simultaneously repressing tumor progression signatures, doubling survival duration in triple-negative breast cancer models.⁴

Thermoresponsive [[chitosan-based-composite-hydrogel|chitosan-based composite hydrogels]] provide sustained tumor-microenvironment-responsive drug release for long-term immunotherapy. These systems integrate indocyanine green-loaded gold nanorods within boronic acid-modified mesoporous silica, forming double-network hydrogels that enable synergistic photothermal and ROS-mediated tumor cell apoptosis coupled with TAM repolarization.⁵ [[chitosan-based-composite-hydrogel|Chitosan]] itself modulates immune feedback to amplify immune activation and infiltration, markedly suppressing tumor growth and metastasis through sustained immunomodulation.⁵
