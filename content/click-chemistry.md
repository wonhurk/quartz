**Click chemistry** is a class of efficient, biocompatible covalent coupling reactions enabling rapid formation of stable bonds under mild, physiological conditions. The technology is characterized by its modularity, high specificity, and minimal generation of toxic byproducts, making it an enabling platform for constructing complex multifunctional biomedical systems.¹ At the mechanistic core is the [[azide-alkyne-cycloaddition]], which catalytically produces stable carbon-carbon bonds with exquisite selectivity in complex biological environments, permitting precise molecular modifications across diverse applications from [[wound-healing|therapeutic wound management]] to [[cancer-detection|liquid biopsy diagnostics]].

---

## Mechanism

Click chemistry encompasses a family of reactions unified by criteria of high efficiency, broad substrate scope, and mild reaction conditions conducive to biological applications. The copper(I)-catalyzed azide-alkyne cycloaddition (CuAAC) rapidly produces stable 1,4-triazole linkages, while copper-free variants employ ring strain or strain-promoted mechanisms to circumvent heavy metal catalysts and their associated toxicity.² The catalytic efficiency of these reactions permits covalent labeling of complex biomacromolecules and assembly of intricate nanostructures without compromising biological activity or generating harmful byproducts. The reaction's orthogonality—its ability to proceed in the presence of multiple other functional groups without interference—renders click chemistry uniquely suited for precision modification of multivalent systems and bioconjugation in living cells and tissues.

## Peptide dendritic nanoparticles

Click chemistry has enabled rational design of [[peptide]]-based nanoparticles through dendritic polymerization strategies that harness efficient azide-alkyne coupling. Peptide monomers engineered with complementary azide and alkyne functional groups undergo covalent dendritic assembly, generating stable nanoparticles with precisely modifiable surfaces independent of peptide sequence variations.¹ These peptide nanoparticles integrate structural stability, inherent biocompatibility, and facile surface engineering, positioning them as versatile building blocks for advanced functional biomaterials. The modular nature of dendritic assembly permits rapid iterative optimization of particle properties, enabling tuning of size, charge, and surface chemistry to match specific therapeutic or diagnostic requirements.

---

## Wound healing

Click chemistry-derived peptide dendritic nanoparticles have been incorporated into polyethylene glycol (PEG) hydrogels as multifunctional crosslinkers for infected [[wound-healing|wound management]]. The resulting hybrid hydrogels exhibit exceptional mechanical stability, resistance to swelling, and intrinsic antibacterial activity derived from the cationic peptide scaffolds.¹ In full-thickness infected wound models, these hydrogels markedly accelerated healing by simultaneously eradicating bacterial bioburden, attenuating inflammatory responses, and promoting angiogenesis. This application demonstrates the utility of click chemistry as a platform for assembling therapeutically active nanomaterials that combine structural robustness with antimicrobial function, addressing the clinical challenge of facilitating wound closure in contaminated tissue environments.

## Oncolytic virus delivery

Click chemistry has been instrumental in engineering an inhalable microbe-oncolytic virus consortium for [[lung-cancer|lung cancer]] immunotherapy. Tumor-responsive PEGylated adenoviruses are conjugated to motile algae (Synechococcus WH8102) via click chemistry, creating a synergistic delivery system that leverages both viral oncolytic activity and algal motility.² The consortium penetrates respiratory mucus and epithelial barriers through algal propulsion, while intratumoral calcium ion deprivation by the algae destabilizes cellular tight junctions and enables deep penetration into solid tumors. This architecture potentiates adenovirus-mediated immunogenic cell death and activates synergistic microbial-viral antitumor immunity. Inhalation administration of the microbe-oncolytic virus consortium demonstrated robust antitumor efficacy in murine lung tumor models, establishing click chemistry-mediated bioconjugation as a strategy for constructing multifunctional biotherapeutics that overcome biological barriers to tumor eradication.

## Cancer detection and classification

Click chemistry enables bioorthogonal tagging of tumor-derived [[extracellular-vesicles|extracellular vesicles]] (EVs) for noninvasive [[cancer-detection|cancer detection]] and invasiveness classification. An in vivo metabolic engineering strategy employs tumor-targeting reactive oxygen species (ROS)-responsive micelle probes that accumulate selectively in [[bladder-cancer|bladder tumors]] and release azide-choline in response to the oxidative microenvironment.³ The azide moieties are covalently incorporated into nascent bladder cancer-derived EV membranes, chemically marking them for selective enrichment via click chemistry upon secretion into urine. This bioorthogonal approach effectively eliminates background interference from nontumor EVs, enabling precise bladder cancer diagnosis through liquid biopsy. Critically, the platform distinguishes nonmuscle-invasive from muscle-invasive bladder cancer based on profiled EV biomarkers, demonstrating that click chemistry provides not only detection but also functional classification of cancer invasiveness. The modularity of this metabolic programming strategy permits extension to other malignancies or disease states by altering targeting and recognition moieties.

## General bioconjugation

Beyond these disease-specific applications, click chemistry has become the standard method for bioconjugation across biomedical research and drug development. The efficiency, biocompatibility, and orthogonality of azide-alkyne coupling have rendered it the reaction of choice for protein labeling, small molecule drug conjugation, and assembly of complex multi-component systems. Click chemistry maintains its advantages even in complex biological matrices, enabling selective covalent modification of specific molecular targets amid millions of non-reactive species, establishing it as a foundational technology enabling precision biomolecular engineering.
