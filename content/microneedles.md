**Microneedles** (MNs) are transdermal drug delivery patches engineered with precisely-shaped needle microstructures that painlessly penetrate the skin barrier to enable efficient percutaneous administration of therapeutic agents.¹ Distinct from conventional topical and oral routes, microneedles leverage their micrometer-scale needle geometries to bypass the stratum corneum while avoiding systemic side effects associated with intramuscular injection. The technology is composed of natural polymers—including mungbean starch, polyvinyl alcohol, hyaluronic acid, and fucoidan—combined with [[nanoparticles|drug-loaded nanoparticles]] to achieve multifunctional therapeutic delivery with sustained-release kinetics and rapid transdermal penetration.

---

## Mechanism and design

Microneedles operate by creating microchannels through the skin's outermost barrier layer, establishing a direct transdermal pathway for both hydrophilic and lipophilic drugs. The pyramidal square geometry (620–640 μm in length) ensures efficient mechanical penetration with compression forces ranging from 1.30 to 4.80 N per needle, providing sufficient strength for skin insertion without causing pain or excessive tissue damage.¹ Upon insertion, the microneedle tips dissolve within two minutes, releasing therapeutic cargo into the dermal layers through [[fickian-diffusion|Fickian diffusion mechanisms]], which govern sustained and predictable drug bioavailability across multiple tissue compartments. The natural polymer matrix biodegrades gradually, reducing foreign body reactions and supporting tissue biocompatibility—a critical advantage over synthetic polymeric systems that may trigger inflammatory responses.

## Herpes simplex virus therapy

Acyclovir-loaded starch-based microneedles represent a major advance in transdermal [[herpes-simplex-virus|HSV]] therapy, addressing the poor oral bioavailability and systemic side effects of conventional acyclovir formulations. Starch-based patches prepared with mungbean starch, polyvinyl alcohol, and plasticizers (arginine and mannitol) achieve superior drug release kinetics compared to film-type formulations, with [[acyclovir]] bioavailability 2.50 to 4.32 times higher than conventional biomaterial films.¹ Notably, over 98% of acyclovir was released from the microneedle patches within 80 minutes, enabling rapid therapeutic response at the dermal level where [[herpes-simplex-virus|HSV]] replicates. Intradermal visualization studies using riboflavin-labeled microneedles on porcine ear and agar block models confirmed efficient and localized drug deposition, establishing the feasibility of precise percutaneous delivery. The microneedle system demonstrated strong antiviral efficacy alongside excellent biodegradability, cell viability, and antimicrobial activity, positioning acyclovir-loaded patches as a promising clinical candidate for transdermal HSV therapy with improved patient compliance and reduced systemic exposure.

## Acne treatment and photothermal synergy

A polysaccharide-based soluble microneedle system has emerged as an innovative approach to [[acne-vulgaris|acne vulgaris treatment]], addressing the challenge of delivering multiple therapeutic modalities—antimicrobial, antioxidant, and photothermal—in a single patient-friendly application. The system integrates [[curcumin]]-loaded mesoporous polydopamine [[nanoparticles]] (MPDA) into a matrix of hyaluronic acid and fucoidan, creating a multifunctional patch that combines sustained-release kinetics with reactive oxygen species scavenging and antibacterial properties.² The microneedles exhibit robust mechanical strength and rapid tip dissolution within 2 minutes, enabling efficient transdermal delivery of curcumin directly to the pilosebaceous units where [[propionibacterium-acnes|Propionibacterium acnes]] colonizes.

The antimicrobial efficacy of the curcumin-MPDA microneedle system is substantial: 70.9% bacterial growth inhibition against P. acnes is achieved through the intrinsic antimicrobial and reactive oxygen species-generating properties of curcumin and polydopamine.² Integration with near-infrared photothermal irradiation dramatically amplifies therapeutic outcomes, boosting antibacterial activity to 99.4% and restoring dermal thickness to near-normal levels in acne-affected skin. This synergistic effect—combining chemical (curcumin, reactive oxygen species), biological (reactive oxygen species scavenging), and physical (photothermal) mechanisms—eliminates the need for conventional systemic antibiotics, reducing antibiotic resistance risk while improving patient tolerability and safety.

## Multifunctional design and biocompatibility

The design versatility of microneedle systems enables simultaneous engineering of multiple therapeutic functions within a single transdermal patch. By tuning the natural polymer composition and incorporating drug-loaded [[nanoparticles]], microneedles can deliver a spectrum of active agents—from small-molecule antivirals like [[acyclovir]] to curcumin-based natural products—while maintaining excellent biocompatibility and biodegradability. The pyramidal needle geometry balances mechanical resilience with rapid dissolution, achieving 2-minute tip dissolution without compromising structural integrity during insertion. Cell viability assays and biodegradation studies confirm the biocompatibility of both starch-based and polysaccharide-based systems, supporting safe transdermal application for chronic inflammatory and infectious skin conditions.² The natural polymer matrix also supports sustained-release kinetics through [[fickian-diffusion|Fickian diffusion]], enabling prolonged therapeutic drug concentrations within target tissues while minimizing peak-level systemic exposure—a pharmacokinetic advantage particularly important for patients requiring long-term or repeated dosing.
