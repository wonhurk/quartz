**UDP-D-Glucose** (uridine diphosphate D-glucose) is a nucleotide sugar cofactor that serves as the activated glucose donor in glycosylation reactions catalyzed by [[glycosyltransferase]] enzymes. The molecule's central biochemical role stems from its highly reactive diphosphate moiety, which functions as an excellent leaving group in phosphoryl transfer reactions, enabling the efficient transfer of glucose to diverse substrates ranging from cyclolipopeptides to natural products.¹ Industrial applications demand continuous regeneration of UDP-D-glucose to sustain catalytic cycling, achievable through coupled enzymatic systems such as [[sucrose-synthase]]-mediated regeneration.²

---

## Mechanism

UDP-D-glucose participates in glycosyltransferase-catalyzed reactions through a nucleophilic substitution mechanism in which the diphosphate moiety departs as the leaving group. The phosphate leaving group is stabilized through electrostatic interactions with critical enzyme residues, ensuring efficient catalysis. In the glycosyltransferase AcbI from *Pantoea agglomerans*, the arginine residue at position 75 (R75) plays an essential role in electrostatically stabilizing the reactive phosphate of UDP-D-glucose during the glycosylation reaction.¹ This stabilization mechanism resembles that of canonical type A glycosyltransferases, enabling efficient and regiospecific glucose transfer to acceptor substrates.

---

## Antifungal biosynthesis

UDP-D-Glucose serves as the sugar donor substrate in the biosynthesis of herbicolin A, a cyclolipopeptide antibiotic with potent antifungal activity. The glycosyltransferase AcbI catalyzes the final glycosylation step, transferring glucose from UDP-D-glucose onto the herbicolin A backbone to yield the fully modified product.¹ This final glycosylation step is critical for achieving enhanced antifungal potency, demonstrating that UDP-D-glucose-dependent post-biosynthetic modification of peptidic scaffolds can significantly augment bioactivity. The identification and characterization of AcbI provides a biochemical foundation for engineering and optimizing glycosylation of other cyclolipopeptidic natural products.

---

## Sweetener production

UDP-D-Glucose is essential for the enzymatic synthesis of rebaudioside D, a high-value, non-caloric natural sweetener derived from stevia. The UDP-glycosyltransferase UGT94B1M0 catalyzes the conversion of rebaudioside A to rebaudioside D by transferring glucose from UDP-D-glucose to the stevia backbone.² Engineering efforts have focused on enhancing the thermostability and catalytic efficiency of UGT94B1M0 to improve industrial viability. The optimal engineered variant, UGT94B1M3, exhibits an 8.40 °C higher melting temperature and a 20.65-fold extended half-life compared to the wild-type enzyme, alongside a 1.45-fold improvement in catalytic efficiency.² These enhancements are attributable to increased structural rigidity and favorable electrostatic interactions, as revealed by molecular dynamics simulations.

---

## Industrial UDP-glucose regeneration

The high molar cost of UDP-D-glucose necessitates efficient regeneration systems for economically viable industrial biosynthesis. When coupled with the recombinant sucrose synthase from *Arabidopsis thaliana*, engineered UGT94B1M3 achieves remarkable synthetic performance: producing 33.87 mM rebaudioside D within one hour at a molar conversion rate of 84.67%, representing a 2.81-fold improvement over the wild-type enzyme coupled system.² This coupled regeneration approach transforms UDP-D-glucose from a stoichiometric cost liability into a catalytically recycled cofactor, enabling sustainable large-scale biomanufacturing of glycosylated natural products. The generalizability of this strategy suggests that UDP-D-glucose regeneration systems, when paired with thermostability-engineered glycosyltransferases, can unlock industrial deployment of nucleotide sugar-dependent biosynthetic pathways.
