**Radioresistance** is an adaptive resistance phenotype that emerges in cancer cells following [[ionizing-radiation|ionizing radiation (IR)]] therapy, fundamentally limiting radiotherapy efficacy across multiple malignancies.¹ The phenotype arises through three interconnected mechanisms: abnormal accumulation of [[lysosomal-cholesterol|lysosomal cholesterol]] coupled with [[cellular-senescence|cellular senescence]], aberrant signaling through the oncogenic kinase [[aurka|AURKA (Aurora Kinase A)]], and the survival of [[polyploid-giant-cancer-cells|polyploid giant cancer cells (PGCCs)]] that adopt a mechanically dormant yet invasive phenotype. These distinct pathways converge to suppress apoptosis and enhance cellular proliferation and migration, establishing a therapy-refractory state that drives cancer recurrence.

---

## Mechanism

Radioresistance operates through multiple, often interconnected molecular pathways that collectively reprogram cancer cells to survive lethal radiation doses. Lysosomal cholesterol deposition represents a critical upstream driver of the radioresistant phenotype. Following [[ionizing-radiation|ionizing radiation]] exposure, cancer cells exhibit aberrant accumulation of cholesterol within lysosomes, which simultaneously triggers [[cellular-senescence|cellular senescence]] and amplification of the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP).¹ This cholesterol-senescence axis creates a self-perpetuating survival niche that suppresses apoptotic signaling while promoting a secretory profile that supports continued tumor growth.

At the kinase level, the oncogenic protein [[aurka|AURKA (Aurora Kinase A)]] orchestrates a signaling cascade that fundamentally blocks the tumor suppressor [[pten|PTEN]]. AURKA physically interacts with [[gsk3b|GSK3β]] to catalyze inhibitory phosphorylation of PTEN at threonine 366, thereby impairing PTEN's phosphatase activity and unleashing sustained activation of the pro-survival [[pi3k|PI3K]]/[[akt|AKT]]/mTOR axis.² This pathway enhancement markedly accelerates cellular proliferation and migration while simultaneously extinguishing apoptotic responses.

A third mechanism of radioresistance arises from the survival and expansion of [[polyploid-giant-cancer-cells|polyploid giant cancer cells (PGCCs)]], a distinct therapy-resistant subpopulation characterized by multinucleation, genetic instability, and stem-like properties. Following radiation exposure, PGCCs adopt a unique [[mechanical-dormancy|mechanical dormancy]] state marked by cortical actin remodeling, nuclear enlargement, and biomechanical stiffening.³ This mechanobiological dormancy couples suppressed proliferation with sustained expression of invasion-associated markers, establishing a latent yet clinically significant threat to long-term cancer control.

---

## Pancreatic cancer

[[aurka|AURKA]] overexpression stands as a dominant driver of radioresistance in [[pancreatic-cancer|pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)]], and its elevated levels robustly correlate with poor patient prognosis.² The kinase confers potent radioresistance in PDAC cell lines, yielding survival enhancement ratio (SER) values of 0.847 ± 0.086 in PANC-1 cells and 0.824 ± 0.073 in SW1990 cells, indicating substantial protection from radiation-induced cell death.² Functionally, AURKA-mediated PTEN inhibition not only confers radioresistance but also drives enhanced cellular proliferation, migration, and apoptosis suppression — a combination particularly dangerous in PDAC, which already exhibits high baseline aggressiveness.

---

## Breast cancer and polyploid giant cancer cells

In [[breast-cancer|breast cancer]], [[polyploid-giant-cancer-cells|polyploid giant cancer cells (PGCCs)]] emerge as a major contributor to radiotherapy failure and disease recurrence.³ These multinucleated cells represent a mechanically distinct subpopulation that survives radiation through a combination of genetic instability and stem-like features. The discovery that radiation-induced PGCCs exhibit [[mechanical-dormancy|mechanical dormancy]] — a state coupling suppressed proliferation with sustained invasion-associated gene expression — reveals a latent therapeutic vulnerability. The dormant mechanotype, characterized by cortical actin remodeling and biomechanical stiffening, enables these cells to evade immediate growth constraints while preserving their capacity for subsequent invasion and metastatic dissemination.

## Therapeutic approaches

Emerging strategies to overcome radioresistance target the underlying molecular vulnerabilities. Sequential drug delivery via nanomedicine, exemplified by [[u-at-hc|U@HC]], exploits the acidic tumor microenvironment and elevated glutathione levels to sequentially release U18666A (a cholesterol transport inhibitor) and β-cyclodextrin.¹ Pretreatment with this nanomedicine inhibits the aberrant accumulation of [[lysosomal-cholesterol|lysosomal cholesterol]] during radiotherapy, simultaneously reducing [[cellular-senescence|cellular senescence]] and SASP amplification, thereby potentiating radiosensitization and extending survival in cancer models.

Direct kinase inhibition targeting [[aurka|AURKA]] or its interaction with [[gsk3b|GSK3β]] represents an alternative therapeutic avenue for [[pancreatic-cancer|pancreatic cancer]] and other AURKA-dependent malignancies. The reversibility of the radioresistant phenotype through either [[gsk3b|GSK3β]] knockdown or expression of a phosphorylation-deficient [[pten|PTEN-T366A]] mutant confirms the essentiality of the AURKA-GSK3β-PTEN axis and identifies it as a viable target for radiosensitization.²
