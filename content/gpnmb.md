**GPNMB** (glycoprotein non-metastatic B) is a surface protein marking distinct macrophage and microglial subsets with heightened immunological and metabolic activity. In the context of [[neurodegenerative-disease|neurodegenerative disease]], GPNMB represents a shared molecular alteration associated with microglial activation and lysosomal dysfunction across six major disorders.¹ In [[metabolic-dysfunction-associated-steatohepatitis|metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH)]], GPNMB+ macrophages emerge as disease progresses and exhibit antigen-presenting, phagocytic functions coupled with adaptive metabolic and pro-inflammatory phenotypes that are exquisitely regulated by spatial tissue context and disease stage.² These cells interact functionally with [[il-32]]-producing hepatocytes, highlighting cell-cell cooperation in driving disease pathogenesis.

---

## Mechanism and expression

GPNMB serves as a reliable marker for identifying macrophage and microglial subsets undergoing activation in disease contexts. As a transmembrane glycoprotein, GPNMB marks cells that have undergone metabolic and transcriptional reprogramming. In the liver, GPNMB+ macrophages display a phenotype characterized by antigen presentation machinery, heightened phagocytic capacity, and upregulation of pro-inflammatory mediators. The expression and phenotype of GPNMB+ cells are not constitutive but rather dynamically regulated by their tissue microenvironment and the underlying disease state. This spatial and temporal regulation makes GPNMB a powerful biomarker for disease stratification across patient cohorts.

## Neurodegeneration

GPNMB was identified as a shared proteomic alteration across six major neurodegenerative diseases through a comprehensive pan-neurodegeneration atlas integrating multilayer analysis of 2,279 brain samples.¹ The six diseases — [[alzheimer-disease|Alzheimer's disease (AD)]], [[lewy-body-dementia|Lewy body dementia (LBD)]], [[frontotemporal-lobar-degeneration|frontotemporal lobar degeneration with TDP-43 pathology]], progressive supranuclear palsy with tau pathology, [[vascular-dementia|vascular dementia]], and [[parkinson-disease|Parkinson's disease (PD)]] — all showed dysregulation of GPNMB linked to microglial and lysosomal activation. The consistent appearance of GPNMB across this heterogeneous collection of diseases suggests that microglial dysfunction and lysosomal stress represent converging pathogenic mechanisms in neurodegeneration. This pan-disease signature positions GPNMB as a candidate biomarker for microglial activation state and disease severity across neurodegenerative pathology, though longitudinal studies are needed to establish its predictive value.

---

## Metabolic disease and macrophage phenotyping

In [[mash|MASH]], GPNMB+ macrophages accumulate progressively as hepatic disease advances from simple steatosis toward steatohepatitis. These macrophages represent a distinct immunological and metabolic identity characterized by enhanced antigen-presentation and phagocytic activity, coupled with adaptive metabolic reprogramming and pro-inflammatory signature. Critically, the phenotype and abundance of GPNMB+ cells are not fixed but are tightly regulated by the local tissue spatial context and the disease stage, enabling their use as robust markers for patient stratification by disease activity and progression stage.²

The functional role of GPNMB+ hepatic macrophages involves crosstalk with hepatocytes themselves. Specifically, GPNMB+ macrophage accumulation is supported and amplified by [[il-32]]-producing hepatocytes, establishing a cell-cell interaction axis that drives disease progression.² This paracrine cooperation highlights how stromal cell populations in metabolic liver disease actively contribute to macrophage differentiation and function, rather than macrophage phenotypes arising in isolation.
