**Aptamers** are short, single-stranded DNA or RNA molecules that fold into specific three-dimensional structures, enabling high-affinity and high-specificity binding to molecular targets ranging from small molecules to cell-surface proteins. Unlike [[monoclonal-antibodies|monoclonal antibodies]], aptamers exhibit low immunogenicity, rational design capacity, and stability across diverse chemical environments, positioning them as versatile molecular recognition elements for both diagnostic biosensing and targeted therapeutics.¹ Their clinical utility spans oncology—including [[prostate-specific-membrane-antigen|PSMA]] detection in prostate cancer and HER2 assessment in breast cancer—infectious disease treatment through [[malate-synthase|Malate Synthase]] inhibition in tuberculosis, and real-time biomarker monitoring in intensive care settings.²³⁴

---

## Mechanism and molecular properties

Aptamers achieve target specificity through conformational selection, wherein their three-dimensional folding creates binding pockets complementary to the target molecule's surface topology. This target-induced conformational change can be harnessed as a transduction mechanism in biosensing platforms. When immobilized on nanostructured surfaces such as three-dimensional reduced graphene oxide or gold nanoparticles, aptamers maintain accessibility while enhancing electron transfer efficiency and signal amplification.⁵ Oriented immobilization strategies, including aptamer-mediated antibody capture on Au@Pt nanozyme surfaces, preserve both aptamer binding domains and catalytic activity of the underlying nanomaterial, overcoming limitations of conventional random conjugation methods.⁶ The programmability of nucleic acid aptamers further enables integration into functional DNA nanostructures, such as [[gelatin-methacryloyl|GelMA]]-based DNA hydrogels synthesized via rapid photoinitiated grafting, where aptamer conformational switching triggers network cleavage for signal readout.¹

## Cancer diagnostics

In breast cancer, aptamer-based platforms address critical limitations of conventional immunohistochemistry, particularly in detecting glycosylated biomarkers. The [[cap-g-bioplatform|CAP-g bioplatform]] employs a dual-recognition strategy combining a PD-L1 aptamer with a glycan probe on a three-dimensional CF/DPEDOT:PSS/Au nanoflexible scaffold, enabling detection of glycosylated PD-L1 on small extracellular vesicles with an ultralow limit of detection of 52 particles/mL—one to two orders of magnitude more sensitive than existing methods.⁷ This approach overcomes glycan-mediated epitope shielding that renders conventional immunoassays ineffective for predicting immune checkpoint blockade response. For HER2 assessment, the proximity-driven, spatially matched [[3d-dna-aptasensor|3D DNA aptasensor]] integrates tetrahedral framework nucleic acids functionalized with cholesterol for membrane anchoring, overcoming electrostatic repulsion between negatively charged aptamers and cell membranes.² Unlike chromogenic immunohistochemistry's "one-signal-per-protein" model, this platform implements on-membrane catalytic hairpin assembly for isothermal amplification, achieving robust signal enhancement and effectively distinguishing HER2-positive from HER2-negative cancer cells.

In prostate cancer, a multiplexed electrochemical aptasensor based on three-dimensional reduced graphene oxide and gold nanoparticles enables ultrasensitive detection of urinary [[prostate-specific-membrane-antigen|PSMA]], achieving a detection limit of 5 pg/mL across a linear range of 15–2000 pg/mL.⁵ The three-working-electrode screen-printed platform permits simultaneous triplicate measurements from a single urine sample, reducing variability and offering a non-invasive alternative to the prostate-specific antigen blood test, which suffers from false positives and unnecessary biopsies.

## Therapeutic applications

Aptamer therapeutics face distinct challenges compared to diagnostic applications, principally rapid in vivo degradation and poor cellular membrane penetration. In tuberculosis treatment, a single-stranded DNA aptamer (MS10-Trunc, 5'-GGTGGTGGTGG-3') targets [[malate-synthase|Malate Synthase]], a key enzyme in the glyoxylate pathway essential for persistent Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection and a surface adhesin facilitating host cell invasion.⁴ Encapsulation within monoolein-based cationic [[cubosome|cubosomes]] lowered the minimum inhibitory concentration by approximately 3.5-fold against actively replicating MTB-H37Ra and shortened the bacterial lifecycle from five to three days compared to unencapsulated aptamer. This represents the first investigation of cubosome lipid nanoparticles for aptamer therapeutic delivery, addressing both stability and membrane penetration barriers that have hindered clinical translation of nucleic acid therapeutics in infectious disease.

## Non-cancer applications

Beyond oncology, aptamers enable real-time monitoring in critical care and food safety applications. The [[neurosense|NeuroSense]] platform integrates glucose and lactate aptamer-based electrochemical biosensors, a polydopamine pH sensor, and an impedance-based flow sensor into standard external ventricular drainage systems for near real-time cerebrospinal fluid monitoring.³ By providing bedside assessment of key biomarkers essential for detecting infection and drain dysfunction, NeuroSense improves temporal resolution compared to intermittent laboratory-based sampling, enabling timely intervention in neurocritical care patients. In food safety, a light-based DNA hydrogel integrated with a paper substrate employs an aptamer for [[aflatoxin-b1|aflatoxin B1]] detection.¹ Target-induced conformational change triggers cleavage of the hydrogel cross-linked network, altering fluid flow velocity in the paper substrate for naked-eye signal readout, with a detection limit of 34.22 pM—below European Union regulatory limits—and spike-recovery rates of 81–98% in real samples.

## Novel platforms and signal amplification

Contemporary aptasensor design increasingly leverages nanomaterial scaffolds and nucleic acid amplification cascades to achieve picomolar sensitivity. The integration of aptamers with lateral flow immunoassay formats using Au@Pt nanozyme labels combines catalytic amplification with molecular recognition, where oriented antibody immobilization via a fragment crystallizable-specific aptamer ensures full accessibility of antigen-binding domains while preserving peroxidase-mimicking activity.⁶ Hybridization chain reaction coupled with dual-recognition-driven proximity cascades amplifies signals from membrane-bound glycoprotein targets, achieving detection limits unattainable by conventional single-binding-event sensors.⁷ Three-dimensional nanostructured electrodes maximize effective reaction area and minimize charge-transfer resistance through π-π stacking interactions, synergistically enhancing electron transfer efficiency and analytical precision.⁵ These integrated design strategies position aptamers as increasingly important molecular tools in precision medicine, where their capacity to overcome immunological detection limitations and enable rational surface engineering addresses unmet needs in biomarker discovery and therapeutic monitoring.
