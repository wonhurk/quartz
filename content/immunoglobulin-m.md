**Immunoglobulin M (IgM)** is the largest antibody molecule and the first antibody class produced during the primary immune response to both pathogens and autoantigens.¹² Its pentameric structure and high avidity make it particularly effective as an early-stage immune defense mechanism. However, IgM also plays a dual pathogenic and diagnostic role across disease contexts: in autoimmunity, elevated IgM titers serve as early biomarkers of disease processes and are significantly influenced by the in utero environment; in infectious disease, quantitative IgM measurements provide critical diagnostic information and prognostic value for predicting disease severity.¹²

---

## Role in autoimmune disease

In [[type-1-diabetes|type 1 diabetes (T1D)]], IgM autoantibodies emerge as sensitive early markers of disease susceptibility and progression. Using the non-obese diabetic (NOD) mouse model, studies have demonstrated significantly elevated IgM titers against key autoantigens including [[insulin]], [[gad65|GAD65]], and DNA in NOD/B6 offspring compared to NOD controls, despite these offspring displaying a ~50% reduction in cumulative diabetes incidence.¹ Notably, elevated IgM autoantibody patterns do not strictly correlate with disease development, suggesting that IgM serves as an indicator of autoreactive B cell activation rather than a determinant of diabetes manifestation. The presence of elevated anti-insulin and anti-GAD65 IgM titers reflects the early activation of autoreactive B cell populations and may represent a stage of autoimmunity that precedes or is distinct from the pathological progression to overt disease.

---

## Maternal immune imprinting

The maternal environment exerts profound effects on IgM autoantibody patterns during fetal development, with consequences that persist into adulthood. Embryo transfer experiments in which NOD fetuses developed in C57BL/6 (B6) dams generated NOD/B6 offspring with dramatically altered IgM and IgG subclass distributions compared to mice gestated in NOD mothers.¹ NOD/B6 mice exhibited significantly increased IgM titers against insulin, DNA, and GAD65, alongside elevated IgG1 and IgG2c levels — alterations driven by the maternal immunological environment rather than intrinsic genetic factors. This maternal immune imprinting extended to lymphocyte populations, with NOD/B6 mice displaying an intermediate phenotype in B cell subset distribution and a notable increase in CD4⁺CD25⁺ regulatory T cells (Tregs), particularly with restored CD25 expression. These findings underscore how the in utero milieu shapes autoimmune susceptibility and autoantigen-specific immune responses, independent of the offspring's own genetic background.

---

## Diagnostic applications in dengue infection

In [[dengue]] viral infection, IgM serology provides essential diagnostic information for distinguishing primary from secondary infection and for predicting clinical severity.² The IgM/IgG antibody ratio reliably differentiates primary dengue from secondary dengue infection, with a ratio of less than 0.85 demonstrating superior sensitivity and specificity (AUROC 0.793) for identifying secondary infection — a clinically important distinction as secondary dengue carries greater risk of hemorrhagic manifestations.² Point-of-care quantitative measurement of IgM titers enables rapid assessment of infection stage and severity risk stratification.

Quantitative IgM titers correlate strongly with specific dengue severity manifestations across multiple organ systems. Low IgM titers (less than 4) show a striking 82.1% correlation with third spacing, a marker of vascular leakage and dengue severity, and predict additional complications including thrombocytopenia and elevated transaminases.² By contrast, elevated IgG titers (greater than or equal to 35) predict third spacing and secondary complications. The combination of quantitative IgM and IgG measurements, together with NS1 antigen levels, enables clinicians to rapidly assess dengue severity indices including bleeding manifestations, renal dysfunction, hepatic involvement, and shock. This multi-parameter serological approach supports early clinical decision-making and outcome prediction in pediatric dengue, where timely identification of severe cases is critical for management and intervention.

---

## Point-of-care diagnostics

The accessibility of quantitative IgM measurement at point-of-care represents a significant advance in rapid disease diagnosis and staging. In dengue-endemic regions, point-of-care IgM/IgG ratio determination combined with NS1 antigen detection enables same-encounter classification of infection type (primary versus secondary) and severity risk assessment, improving clinical management efficiency.² This point-of-care approach is particularly valuable in resource-limited settings where rapid assessment is essential. The dual diagnostic and pathogenic roles of IgM — functioning simultaneously as a pathogenic mediator in autoimmune disease and as a critical diagnostic biomarker in infectious disease — underscore its central importance for understanding immune responses and disease progression across diverse pathological conditions.
