**RUNX2** (RUNT-related transcription factor 2) is a master regulator of [[osteogenic-differentiation|osteogenic differentiation]] and bone formation in bone marrow-derived mesenchymal stem cells (BMSCs), serving as a principal marker of osteogenic commitment alongside alkaline phosphatase, [[ocn|osteocalcin (OCN)]], and [[opn|osteopontin (OPN)]].¹ Its expression undergoes dramatic upregulation—4–5 fold—during osteogenic activation, particularly following senescence-mitigation pathways in aged BMSCs, establishing it as a key indicator of successful bone regeneration.² Beyond bone biology, RUNX2 paradoxically suppresses [[ferroptosis]] in [[ovarian-cancer|ovarian cancer]], enhancing cisplatin chemoresistance through transcriptional control of the [[yap1|YAP1]]/[[gls1|GLS1]] metabolic axis; this cancer-promoting function is critically enhanced by [[zdhhc14|ZDHHC14]]-mediated S-palmitoylation.³

---

## Mechanism

RUNX2 functions as a DNA-binding transcription factor that drives the committed osteogenic gene expression program in BMSCs. Within bone tissue, RUNX2 orchestrates the expression of structural and mineralization genes required for extracellular matrix deposition and calcium phosphate precipitation. Its activity is tightly integrated with broader skeletal remodeling signals, including the Hippo pathway; suppression of Hippo-axis components enhances RUNX2 expression and promotes coupled osteogenic and angiogenic activity.¹ In the context of age-related bone dysfunction, RUNX2 upregulation is accompanied by concurrent suppression of senescence markers (p16, p21) and pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-6, TNF-α), indicating that RUNX2 activation reverses senescent, pro-inflammatory states in aged stem cells.²

In cancer, RUNX2 transcriptionally activates [[yap1|YAP1]], which in turn engages the [[gls1|GLS1]]-driven glutamine metabolism axis, a critical node supporting cancer cell survival and drug resistance. This YAP1/GLS1 signaling cascade simultaneously suppresses ferroptosis execution, allowing cancer cells to evade iron-catalyzed lipid peroxidation that would otherwise trigger regulated cell death.³

## Osteogenic bone formation and regeneration

RUNX2 is essential for restoring osteogenic capacity in aged bone marrow stem cells and directing bone regeneration in critical-sized defects. Activation of the [[a20|A20]] senescence-mitigation pathway through [[crispr-based-gene-editing-microspheres|CRISPRa-mediated gene activation]] specifically upregulates RUNX2 and osteogenic gene expression (alkaline phosphatase) by 4–5 fold in aged BMSCs, while simultaneously reducing p16 and p21 senescence markers by over 30%.² In vivo, such RUNX2 elevation drives rapid bone regeneration and vascularization in aged mice bearing critical-sized bone defects, achieving approximately 80% defect healing compared to untreated controls and promoting CD31+ vascularization 3.1 times faster than controls.² RUNX2 upregulation is similarly achieved through pharmacological modulation by [[dendrobine]], a natural alkaloid that promotes coupled osteogenesis and angiogenic factor secretion (VEGF, MMP2, MMP9) in BMSCs, amplifying the regenerative capacity of the bone microenvironment.¹

## Cancer suppression via RUNX2-targeted chemoresistance

Therapeutic resistance to platinum-based chemotherapy and ferroptosis represent major clinical barriers in [[ovarian-cancer|ovarian cancer]]. RUNX2 accumulation in chemoresistant ovarian cancer cells suppresses ferroptosis by reducing the cellular machinery required for iron-catalyzed lipid peroxidation—including downregulation of [[acsl4|ACSL4]], elevation of [[gpx4|GPX4]], and suppression of reactive oxygen species and lipid peroxidation markers.³ Blockade of RUNX2 restores sensitivity to cisplatin and ferroptosis induction, as RUNX2 loss de-represses the ferroptosis program and prevents YAP1/GLS1-axis-mediated metabolic adaptation.³ This mechanism positions RUNX2 as a targetable node in acquired chemoresistance, particularly in settings where resistant ovarian tumors have accumulated RUNX2 through unknown transcriptional or epigenetic mechanisms.

---

## Regulation and post-translational modification

RUNX2 function is dynamically regulated at the post-translational level through S-palmitoylation mediated by the palmitoyl acyltransferase [[zdhhc14|ZDHHC14]].³ Palmitoylation enhances RUNX2 stability and transcriptional activity, amplifying its ferroptosis-suppressing and chemoresistance-promoting functions in ovarian cancer cells.³ In contrast, in non-transformed BMSCs, RUNX2 stability and osteogenic activity are regulated by the balance between its expression level and post-translational modifications; precise control of RUNX2 by transcriptional activators (such as A20 signaling) rather than by pathological palmitoylation drives physiological osteogenesis.

---

## Therapeutic modulation

Therapeutic upregulation of RUNX2 has emerged as a strategy to counteract age-related osteogenic failure and postmenopausal bone loss. [[Dendrobine]] directly enhances osteogenic differentiation with elevated RUNX2 protein expression, promoting both BMSCs osteogenesis and HUVEC-BMSC coculture angiogenic capacity in models of [[postmenopausal-osteoporosis|postmenopausal osteoporosis]].¹ CRISPR-based [[crispr-based-gene-editing-microspheres|gene-editing microspheres]] targeting the [[a20|A20]]-senescence axis provide an injectable, spatiotemporally controlled approach to RUNX2 upregulation in aged bone defects, demonstrating clinically relevant bone regeneration rates in vivo.² Conversely, in the context of ovarian cancer, RUNX2 suppression or inhibition of its palmitoylation by [[zdhhc14|ZDHHC14]] antagonists represents a complementary strategy to restore ferroptosis sensitivity and overcome acquired chemoresistance.
