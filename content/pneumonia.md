**Pneumonia** is a serious global respiratory infection characterized by high contagion and mortality, commonly caused by multidrug-resistant bacterial pathogens including carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae ([[crkp|CRKP]]), methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus ([[mrsa|MRSA]]), and carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales.¹ Pneumonia represents the predominant infection source in approximately 55% of multidrug-resistant gram-negative infections in contemporary clinical practice, with Pseudomonas aeruginosa (36.1%) and carbapenem-resistant organisms (34.7%) being the leading causative agents.² The emergence of widespread multidrug resistance has necessitated parallel therapeutic strategies: conventional antimicrobial agents such as [[ceftazidime-avibactam]] combined with innovative nanoparticle-based delivery systems that integrate targeted drug accumulation, direct bactericidal mechanisms, and immunomodulatory activity.³

---

## Epidemiology and pathogenesis

Bacterial pneumonia arises from infection of the lower respiratory tract by gram-negative and gram-positive organisms, triggering inflammatory cascades that damage host tissues, promote sepsis, and can culminate in organ dysfunction. Among multidrug-resistant gram-negative infections, Pseudomonas aeruginosa dominates at 36.1% prevalence, while carbapenem-resistant Enterobacterales constitute 34.7%, establishing these organisms as the most common respiratory pathogens in diverse clinical settings.² [[crkp|Carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae]] exemplifies the virulence problem: combining broad antibiotic resistance with high mortality rates and severely limited treatment options. The severity of multidrug-resistant pneumonia is amplified by the dual challenge of delayed diagnosis and excessive systemic inflammation, which damage host tissues even as pathogens are being cleared by antimicrobial therapy.

---

## Conventional antimicrobial therapy

Ceftazidime-avibactam, a broad-spectrum beta-lactam/beta-lactamase inhibitor combination, has become a cornerstone therapy for multidrug-resistant gram-negative pneumonia. Across 22 U.S. medical centers, ceftazidime-avibactam treatment achieved composite clinical success—defined as 30-day survival, microbiological clearance, and resolution of fever and leukocytosis within 72 hours—in 64.8% of patients with pneumonia caused by multidrug-resistant organisms.² Among patients who had not received prior active antimicrobial therapy, treatment initiation within 42 hours was associated with higher success rates (73.3% versus 63.4%), demonstrating the critical importance of early antimicrobial coverage. Despite these improvements, the moderate overall success rate underscores the continuing clinical need for complementary therapeutic strategies and more effective delivery mechanisms.

## Macrophage-mediated nanoparticle delivery

A cell-based nano-bionic drug delivery system designated PT@M2 addresses two fundamental barriers in treating [[crkp|CRKP]] pneumonia: inadequate drug accumulation at infection sites and excessive systemic inflammation. PT@M2 comprises poly(lactic-co-glycolic acid) nanoparticles loaded with tigecycline, encapsulated within M2-type [[macrophage|macrophages]], enabling stable systemic circulation, targeted lung homing, and acid-triggered drug release within the acidic infection microenvironment.¹ In vitro characterization demonstrated that PT@M2 substantially reduced the minimum inhibitory concentration against [[crkp|CRKP]], disrupted bacterial membranes, and accumulated reactive oxygen species at high concentrations. In murine pneumonia models, PT@M2 reduced bacterial colony burden, suppressed inflammatory responses, and restored normal pulmonary histopathology. Mechanistically, PT@M2 inhibits calcium ion influx by downregulating [[cacng6|CACNG6]], which indirectly modulates the [[mapk|MAPK signaling pathway]], thereby simultaneously remodeling the immune microenvironment away from pathologic inflammation.¹ This dual mechanism—combining direct bactericidal activity with controlled immune system remodeling—represents a novel paradigm for treating multidrug-resistant respiratory infections.

## Bimetallic nanoparticle approach and cuproptosis induction

Bimetallic copper-manganese composite nanoparticles (Cu-Mn NPs) functionalized with PEGylated chitosan provide an alternative nanoparticle strategy combining superior mucus penetration and sustained lung retention. These particles synergistically trigger [[cuproptosis|cuproptosis]]-like bacterial cell death, disrupt bacterial membrane integrity, and interfere with respiratory chain function through abundant reactive oxygen species generation.³ Cu-Mn NPs demonstrate efficacy against drug-resistant [[biofilm|biofilms]], bypassing a major mechanism of treatment failure in recurrent or chronic infections. In a [[mrsa|methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus]] (MRSA)-infected pneumonia model, Cu-Mn NP therapy significantly improved survival rates and reduced inflammatory pulmonary injury without causing systemic toxicity, indicating a favorable therapeutic window.³ The particles simultaneously promote [[macrophage|macrophage]] polarization toward the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype, enhancing pathogen clearance while maintaining controlled immune activation to limit collateral tissue damage.

---

## Immunomodulation and therapeutic integration

Both emerging nanoparticle approaches operate through coordinated immunomodulatory mechanisms that extend beyond direct antimicrobial activity. PT@M2 remodels the immune microenvironment through M2 [[macrophage|macrophage]] engagement and [[mapk|MAPK]] pathway modulation, whereas Cu-Mn NPs drive macrophage polarization toward the M1 phenotype, establishing complementary immune activation patterns optimized for pathogen elimination. The convergence of targeted drug delivery, direct bactericidal mechanisms, reactive oxygen species generation, and immune system engagement represents a fundamental shift in treating multidrug-resistant respiratory infections. As prevalence of multidrug-resistant gram-negative pathogens continues to rise globally and conventional antibiotics face escalating resistance, nanoparticle-based therapies combined with appropriate conventional agents offer promising avenues for substantially improving clinical outcomes in this serious infectious disease.
