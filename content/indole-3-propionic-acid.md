**Indole-3-propionic acid (IPA)** is a tryptophan-derived small-molecule metabolite synthesized by [[gut-microbiota|commensal gut bacteria]] that functions as a potent anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective agent in the central nervous system. IPA exerts its therapeutic effects by activating the [[ahr|aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR)]], a ligand-gated transcriptional regulator that suppresses pro-inflammatory signaling through inhibition of [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] and [[mapk|MAPK]] cascades.¹ The discovery of IPA as a microbiota-derived neuromodulator demonstrates the functional importance of bacterial metabolites in maintaining neural homeostasis and protecting against neuroinflammatory pathology.

---

## Mechanism

IPA suppresses [[astrocyte]]-mediated neuroinflammation through [[ahr|AhR]]-dependent transcriptional signaling. In vitro studies using TNF-α-stimulated [[astrocyte]]s demonstrate that IPA activates [[ahr|AhR]], which in turn inhibits phosphorylation and nuclear translocation of the transcription factors [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] and [[mapk|MAPK]].¹ This upstream suppression prevents the expression of a broad spectrum of pro-inflammatory mediators, including the cytokines [[il-6|interleukin-6 (IL-6)]] and [[il-1b|interleukin-1β (IL-1β)]], the oxidative stress enzymes [[inos|inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS)]] and [[cox-2|cyclooxygenase-2 (COX-2)]], and the chemotactic factors [[ccl2|CCL2]], [[cxcl2|CXCL2]], and [[cxcl10|CXCL10]].¹ The [[ahr|AhR]] pathway represents a cell-intrinsic regulatory node in neuroimmune homeostasis; IPA's ability to engage this receptor at physiologically relevant concentrations identifies it as a specific endogenous ligand that distinguishes its anti-inflammatory action from non-selective inhibitors.

---

## Spinal cord injury recovery

The therapeutic efficacy of IPA has been established in both in vitro and in vivo models of [[spinal-cord-injury|spinal cord injury (SCI)]], where IPA treatment promotes functional motor recovery and tissue preservation. In mouse [[spinal-cord-injury|SCI]] models, IPA administration significantly reduced glial scar formation—a major physical and chemical barrier to axonal regeneration in traumatic central nervous system injury.¹ Beyond scar reduction, IPA enhanced neuronal cell survival and preserved neural tissue integrity, as confirmed by magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showing reduced lesion volume and histopathological analysis documenting preserved tissue architecture. Functionally, IPA-treated mice demonstrated substantial improvements in motor recovery, reflected in increased Basso Mouse Scale (BMS) scores, enhanced inclined plane test performance, and restoration of coordinated gait patterns.¹ These pre-clinical findings indicate that IPA's anti-inflammatory mechanism translates into meaningful neurological benefit and suggest that therapeutic strategies targeting the IPA/[[ahr|AhR]] axis warrant clinical investigation in traumatic [[spinal-cord-injury|spinal cord injury]].

---

## Microbiota-brain axis

IPA exemplifies the emerging recognition that commensal bacteria produce bioactive metabolites with direct effects on host neural function and immune homeostasis. The conversion of [[tryptophan|tryptophan]] to IPA by specific bacterial taxa represents a quantifiable link between microbiota composition and neuroimmune outcomes. Dysbiosis or selective loss of IPA-producing bacterial populations may contribute to aberrant neuroinflammation and secondary neurodegeneration in [[spinal-cord-injury|spinal cord injury]] and other neurological conditions. These observations suggest that therapeutic approaches targeting microbial composition or [[tryptophan|tryptophan]] metabolism to enhance IPA production could provide a microbiota-based strategy for neuroprotection, potentially complementing or enhancing conventional pharmacological interventions for neuroinflammatory diseases.
