**Ulcerative colitis (UC)** is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease characterized by continuous mucosal inflammation of the colon and rectum, epithelial barrier dysfunction, and dysregulated immune responses. The disease manifests through progressive erosion of the intestinal mucus layer, depletion of [[lactobacillus-rhamnosus-gg|goblet cells]], and accumulation of proinflammatory cytokines including TNF-α and [[tl1a|TL1A]].¹ Emerging therapeutic paradigms target multiple converging pathways: restoration of the protective mucus barrier through [[lactobacillus-rhamnosus-gg|probiotic-mediated]] goblet cell regeneration, inhibition of [[acsl4|ACSL4]]-dependent ferroptosis in colonic epithelium, and selective immunomodulation via [[s1pr1|sphingosine-1-phosphate receptor 1 (S1PR1)]] agonism and [[tl1a|TL1A]] blockade.¹⁻³

---

## Pathogenesis and molecular mechanisms

UC pathogenesis involves three interconnected mechanisms: breakdown of the epithelial mucus barrier, ferroptotic cell death driven by iron-catalyzed lipid peroxidation, and sustained activation of proinflammatory signaling cascades. The mucus layer, normally maintained by abundant goblet cells secreting gel-forming mucins, becomes depleted during active disease, exposing the underlying epithelium to luminal antigens and bacterial translocation.¹ Concurrently, [[acsl4|acyl-CoA synthetase long-chain family member 4 (ACSL4)]] mediates incorporation of polyunsaturated fatty acids into membrane phospholipids, rendering colonic epithelial cells vulnerable to ferroptosis through the metadherin (MTDH)/ACSL4/NF-κB signaling axis.⁷ This ferroptotic cascade is amplified by iron dysregulation and depletion of the glutathione-GPX4 antioxidant system, creating a self-perpetuating cycle of epithelial injury and barrier compromise.

The TNF superfamily cytokine [[tl1a|TL1A]] signals through death receptor 3 (DR3) on T helper cells, innate lymphoid cells, and fibroblasts, driving chronic inflammation and tissue fibrosis.⁵ Dysregulated TL1A/DR3 signaling emerges as a central node connecting adaptive immunity to tissue destruction, with clinical proof of concept established in both UC and Crohn's disease.⁵

## Targeted immunomodulation

[[s1pr1|Sphingosine-1-phosphate receptor 1]] has been clinically validated as a therapeutic target for UC through its role in regulating lymphocyte trafficking and immune cell egress from lymphoid tissues. The novel 1,2,4-oxadiazole derivative [[tyy-31|TYY-31]] exhibits single-digit picomolar potency for S1PR1 (EC₅₀ = 1.13 pM) with exceptional selectivity, demonstrating minimal activity against S1PR2/3/5 (EC₅₀ > 10 μM) and over 30,000-fold selectivity versus S1PR4.³ In preclinical [[dextran-sulfate-sodium|dextran sulfate sodium]]-induced colitis models, [[tyy-31|TYY-31]] achieved therapeutic efficacy comparable to the approved agent [[ozanimod|Ozanimod]] at 30-fold lower doses (0.3 mg/kg versus 1 mg/kg), while efficiently inducing receptor internalization and blocking S1PR1 recycling with excellent cardiac safety characteristics.³ This S1PR3-sparing selectivity profile addresses safety concerns associated with earlier-generation agonists, as substantial clinical evidence indicates that highly selective S1PR1 agonists exhibit improved cardiovascular tolerability.³

The extended half-life monoclonal antibodies SPY002 and SPY072 represent a second-generation approach to [[tl1a|TL1A]] neutralization. Both antibodies demonstrate selective, high-affinity binding to human TL1A (K_D ≈ 31–35 pM) and potent functional inhibition of DR3 signaling.⁵ Through strategic Fc modifications that attenuate effector function while enhancing neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn) binding at acidic pH, SPY002 and SPY072 achieve predicted human half-lives supporting quarterly or biannual dosing intervals.⁵ In preclinical toxicity studies, no drug-related adverse effects were observed at exposures exceeding 10-fold anticipated clinical levels, and anti-TL1A treatment demonstrated efficacy comparable to TNF antagonists and anti-IL-23 antibodies in multiple inflammatory disease models.⁵

## Ferroptosis-targeted therapy

Targeting [[acsl4|ACSL4]]-mediated ferroptosis has emerged as a disease-modifying strategy in UC, addressing both epithelial injury and inflammatory amplification. [[emodin|Emodin]], a natural anthraquinone derivative, suppresses ferroptosis through coordinated modulation of iron homeostasis, mitochondrial stabilization, and activation of the glutathione-GPX4 antioxidant axis.¹ When formulated as lactoferrin-coated nanoparticles (LENs) and incorporated into the [[leg-pda|LEG@PDA]] probiotic delivery system, [[emodin|emodin]] achieves targeted delivery to colonic epithelial cells while simultaneously preserving colonic epithelial integrity and expanding goblet cell populations.¹

An alternative approach employs dexamethasone-loaded hollow mesoporous silica nanoparticles (HMSN@DEX) combined with ultrasound-targeted microbubble destruction (UTMD) to enhance site-specific drug accumulation in inflamed tissue.⁷ This system regulates the MTDH/[[acsl4|ACSL4]]/NF-κB pathway, interrupting the ferroptotic cascade while simultaneously delivering potent anti-inflammatory corticosteroid therapy directly to diseased colonic segments.⁷ The dual mechanism—ferroptosis inhibition coupled with localized immunosuppression—addresses both epithelial pathology and immune dysregulation through a single therapeutic platform.

## Mucosal barrier restoration

Restoration of goblet cell-dependent mucus layer integrity represents a foundational therapeutic objective in UC, as current clinical treatments including conventional probiotics and small-molecule agents fail to achieve sustained mucosal repair. The [[leg-pda|LEG@PDA]] system—an orally delivered probiotics-nanotherapeutic platform composed of [[lactobacillus-rhamnosus-gg|Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG (LGG)]] backpacked with [[emodin|emodin]]-loaded lactoferrin nanoparticles and surface-coated with polydopamine—addresses this unmet need through complementary mechanisms.¹ The outer polydopamine layer provides gastric protection and colon-targeted delivery, while lactoferrin-mediated targeting directs nanoparticles to colonic epithelial cells.¹ Concurrently, [[lactobacillus-rhamnosus-gg|LGG]] is delivered to colonic lymphoid patches through M cell-mediated transcytosis, where it orchestrates goblet cell mucus extrusion via the calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP)-RAMP1 signaling axis.¹ This coordinated restoration of goblet cell populations and mucus secretory function, validated in murine models, demonstrates both acute therapeutic efficacy and prevention of disease relapse.¹

## Advanced drug delivery platforms

The harsh chemical and enzymatic environment of the gastrointestinal tract necessitates sophisticated delivery systems to achieve colon-specific therapeutic release while protecting cargo molecules during oral transit. Multiple platform technologies leverage site-specific triggers inherent to the diseased colon, including pH transitions, microbial enzyme activity, and inflammatory microenvironment cues.

Dual-crosslinked polysaccharide hydrogel microspheres exemplify this engineering paradigm. The [[adcxy-cur-sa-cs|ADCXy-Cur-SA-CS]] system encapsulates [[curcumin|curcumin]] within glycated collagen matrices crosslinked with sodium alginate and calcium ions, then coated with chitosan to confer colon-specific enzymolysis targeting, mucosal adhesion, and pH-responsive release characteristics.² This architecture maintains high stability in simulated gastric conditions while enabling controlled [[curcumin|curcumin]] release in the colonic pH environment, achieving therapeutic effects through a "one stone, four birds" strategy of antioxidant activity, anti-inflammatory signaling, mucosal barrier repair, and gut microbiota modulation.²

Enzyme-responsive systems utilize the elevated expression of bacterial and host enzymes in the inflamed colon. The [[ua-pb-gel|UA@PB/Gel]] platform embeds butyrate-conjugated poly(vinyl alcohol) nanoparticles within an inulin hydrogel, responding to colonic esterase and inulinase activity to release both ursolic acid and the short-chain fatty acid butyrate.⁴ This Generally Recognized As Safe (GRAS) materials-based system demonstrates efficient colon-targeting, prolonged retention, and minimal systemic toxicity while restoring epithelial barrier integrity and modulating gut short-chain fatty acid levels.⁴

Gene therapy approaches require even more stringent protection for nucleic acid cargo. The modular [[ig-sp-fk-lnps-sitnf-alpha|IG@SP@FK LNPs@siTNFα]] platform integrates three functional components: cathepsin B/glutathione dual-responsive gemini-like cationic lipid nanoparticles encapsulating TNF-α siRNA, a negatively charged Spirulina intermediate layer providing enhanced mucosal adhesion through intrinsic helical architecture, and an outer inulin-derived hydrogel coating that undergoes colonic degradation to trigger site-specific release.⁶ This nano-in-microalgae integrated system demonstrates efficient cellular internalization, proton-sponge effect-driven endosomal escape, and dual-responsive siRNA release, ultimately silencing TNF-α expression and significantly attenuating UC progression.⁶

## Spatial transcriptomics and translational research

Single-cell-resolved spatial transcriptomics platforms have emerged as essential tools for mapping UC pathogenesis and validating therapeutic mechanisms in patient tissues. A comprehensive atlas constructed from over 100 intestinal tissue sections across non-diseased controls, UC, and Crohn's disease samples—encompassing over three million cells—systematically evaluated the [[cosmx|CosMx]] and [[xenium|Xenium]] imaging-based platforms.⁸ [[cosmx|CosMx]] achieves higher detection efficiency than [[xenium|Xenium]] across commercially available gene panels in both UC and Crohn's disease, with [[xenium|Xenium]] showing reduced performance associated with tissue type, block quality, and panel size.⁸ Critically, [[cosmx|CosMx]] identified regulatory T cell-associated biology in both disease subtypes, validated through independent laboratory experiments and multiplex spatial multi-omics, while maintaining stable data quality across variations in fixation time and sectioning procedures that support operational feasibility for multi-center clinical trials.⁸ This technical and biological benchmarking establishes foundational infrastructure for translational inflammatory bowel disease research, enabling reliable application of spatial technologies to stratify patient populations, predict therapeutic responses, and guide precision medicine implementation.
