**Mixotrophic Partial Denitrification-Anammox System (MPDA)** is a single-stage [[wastewater-treatment|wastewater treatment]] technology that integrates sulfur-driven [[partial-denitrification|partial denitrification]] with [[anammox|anammox]] (anaerobic ammonia oxidation) to achieve simultaneous nitrogen removal from organic-rich wastewaters.¹ Operating as a sustainable alternative to conventional [[nitrogen-removal|nitrogen removal]] pathways, the MPDA system harnesses the metabolic complementarity of facultative mixotrophic [[sulfur-oxidizing-bacteria|sulfur-oxidizing bacteria (SOB)]] and core [[anammox|anammox bacteria]] to generate robust performance under fluctuating [[organic-loading|organic loading rates]]. Over 292 days of continuous operation on real domestic wastewater, the system achieved average ammonia removal of 93.3% and total nitrogen removal of 90.9%, while isotopic tracing demonstrated that thiosulfate-driven and organic-carbon-driven pathways contributed 51.3% and 27.7% of total nitrogen removal, respectively.¹

---

## Mechanism

The MPDA system operates through two complementary nitrogen removal pathways that proceed simultaneously within a single bioreactor. The [[sulfur-oxidizing-bacteria|sulfur-oxidizing]] pathway is initiated when facultative mixotrophic bacteria — primarily [[thiobacillus|Thiobacillus]] and members of [[hydrogenophilaceae|Hydrogenophilaceae]] — oxidize thiosulfate derived from domestic wastewater, generating [[nitrite|nitrite]] as the primary electron-accepting product. This thiosulfate oxidation serves as the energy source for these organisms, enabling chemolithoautotrophic growth while simultaneously producing nitrite. Concurrently, the [[organic-carbon|organic-carbon-driven]] pathway involves similar bacterial populations utilizing acetate and other organic substrates from the wastewater as both carbon and energy sources, further contributing to nitrite generation.¹

The core [[anammox|anammox]] bacteria, dominated by [[candidatus-brocadia|Candidatus Brocadia]], catalyze the coupling of ammonia oxidation with nitrite reduction, converting ammonia and nitrite directly to dinitrogen gas in an anaerobic process that requires no external organic carbon input. The {{spda|SPDA (sulfur-driven partial denitrification-anammox)}} concept upon which MPDA is based established this principle; the MPDA system expands applicability by demonstrating that concurrent organic carbon availability, rather than inhibiting anammox activity, can be integrated through metabolic plasticity of the core microbial community.¹

## Stable Performance Under Fluctuating Conditions

The MPDA system maintained robust and consistent performance throughout 292 days of continuous operation despite variable [[organic-loading|organic loading rates]] of 0.35–0.45 kg chemical oxygen demand (COD) per cubic meter per day. Average ammonia removal efficiency reached 93.3%, while total nitrogen removal achieved 90.9%, demonstrating that the system's integrated metabolic design is resilient to the dynamic conditions characteristic of real domestic wastewater treatment.¹ This stability contrasts with conventional [[nitrification|nitrification]]-based systems, which often suffer performance degradation when exposed to elevated organic substrates that promote heterotrophic growth at the expense of nitrifier enrichment.

---

## Microbial Populations and Metabolic Partitioning

DNA-stable isotope probing (DNA-SIP) experiments using ¹³C-labeled thiosulfate and acetate identified the specific metabolic roles of key bacterial populations. [[thiobacillus|Thiobacillus]] species and [[hydrogenophilaceae|Hydrogenophilaceae]] members were confirmed as primary contributors to nitrite supply, with metagenomic analysis revealing that Thiobacillaceae encode a complete [[calvin-cycle|Calvin cycle]] for autotrophic carbon fixation and possess a full [[pentose-phosphate-pathway|pentose phosphate pathway]], enabling both energy generation and biosynthetic capacity.¹

[[candidatus-brocadia|Candidatus Brocadia]], the dominant [[anammox|anammox]] taxon, showed a striking positive correlation with organic loading rates, a finding unexpected from conventional understanding of anammox metabolism. DNA-SIP analysis using organic ¹³C labeling directly implicated Brocadia in [[heterotrophic|heterotrophic]] carbon assimilation, suggesting previously unrecognized organoautotrophic metabolic capacity. Metagenomic reconstruction of the Brocadiaceae genome revealed a near-complete [[tca-cycle|citric acid cycle (TCA cycle)]] alongside the [[wood-ljungdahl-pathway|Wood-Ljungdahl pathway]], a combination that enables both autotrophic fixation of ammonia-derived nitrogen and direct utilization of ambient organic substrates for growth and biosynthesis.¹ This metabolic versatility appears central to the system's stability under organic-rich conditions.

---

## Pathway Contributions and System Synergy

Quantitative ¹⁵N isotopic tracing demonstrated precise apportionment of nitrogen removal between the two driving pathways. Thiosulfate-driven [[partial-denitrification|partial denitrification]] accounted for 51.3% of total nitrogen removal, establishing sulfur-driven metabolism as the dominant removal mechanism. The organic-carbon-driven pathway contributed 27.7%, with the remainder attributable to heterotrophic denitrification and other minor pathways. This metabolic partitioning reveals that the system functions as a genuinely integrated process in which both sulfur and organic substrates are simultaneously metabolized by overlapping but distinct bacterial populations, each reinforcing the capacity for ammonia oxidation and nitrogen gas production.¹
