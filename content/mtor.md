**Mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR)** is a serine/threonine protein kinase that serves as a central metabolic and signaling hub integrating nutrient availability, growth factor signaling, and cellular stress responses across multiple malignancies. mTOR functions downstream of oncogenic pathways including [[kit|KIT receptor tyrosine kinase]], the PI3K/AKT axis, and [[fra-2|AP-1 transcription factors]], and its activation is marked by phosphorylation of [[p-s6|ribosomal protein S6 (pS6)]].¹ Constitutive mTOR signaling drives glucose dependency, coordinates de novo pyrimidine nucleotide biosynthesis, and confers adaptive resistance to targeted therapies in [[b-cell-acute-lymphoblastic-leukemia|B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia (B-ALL)]], [[acute-myeloid-leukemia|acute myeloid leukemia (AML)]], [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)]], [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer (CRC)]], and [[pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma|pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)]].¹²⁵

---

## Mechanism

mTOR orchestrates cellular metabolism by activating the [[cad|carbamoyl phosphate synthetase 2, aspartate transcarbamylase, and dihydroorotase (CAD) complex]], which catalyzes the rate-limiting initial steps of de novo pyrimidine synthesis.¹ In B-ALL cells with active mTOR signaling — identified by high [[p-s6|pS6]] phosphorylation — glucose is shunted preferentially into glycolysis and nucleotide biosynthesis. Isotope tracing experiments confirm that pS6+ cells are highly glycolytic and depend on glucose-derived carbon for uridine production.¹ When glucose is withdrawn, only exogenous uridine, not other purines or pyrimidines, rescues pS6+ cell viability, establishing uridine as the essential metabolic output of mTOR-driven glucose consumption.¹ Pharmacological inhibition of mTOR signaling abolishes both glucose dependency and CAD phosphorylation, confirming the direct mechanistic link.¹

In [[acute-myeloid-leukemia|AML]], mTOR operates downstream of [[kit|KIT receptor signaling]] through the ERK/mTOR axis. When the histone demethylase [[lsd1|LSD1 (KDM1A)]] is inhibited, KIT transcription is repressed, leading to downstream mTOR pathway suppression in sensitive cells.² Conversely, in LSD1 inhibitor–partially responsive or tolerant AML lines, mTOR signaling is reactivated following drug treatment, representing a key adaptive resistance mechanism.²

## Cancer contexts

### B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemia

In [[b-cell-acute-lymphoblastic-leukemia|B-ALL]], mTOR activation defines a metabolically distinct, high-risk subpopulation characterized by phosphorylated [[p-s6|ribosomal protein S6]]. Primary pS6+ B-ALL cells express elevated levels of pyrimidine synthesis enzymes, including [[dhodh|dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH)]], the rate-limiting mitochondrial enzyme in the pathway.¹ High DHODH expression correlates with relapse and poor event-free survival across most B-ALL molecular subtypes.¹ The DHODH inhibitor BAY-2402234 selectively kills pS6+ cells in vitro, with potency directly correlating to the strength of pS6 signaling across 14 patient-derived xenografts.¹

### Acute myeloid leukemia

mTOR signaling status predicts therapeutic response to [[lsd1|LSD1]] inhibitors in [[acute-myeloid-leukemia|AML]]. Genome-wide transcriptomic profiling reveals that [[kit|KIT receptor]] is preferentially overexpressed in LSD1 inhibitor–sensitive AMLs and suppressed in partially responsive or tolerant lines.² Chromatin immunoprecipitation studies demonstrate that LSD1 binds near the KIT transcription start site and modulates repressive H3K9me2 marks, thereby controlling KIT expression and downstream mTOR pathway activity.² KIT mRNA levels positively correlate with LSD1 transcript levels in primary human AML biospecimens, and pharmacological LSD1 inhibition represses KIT transcription ex vivo.² Co-targeting LSD1 and c-Kit augments anti-leukemic activity in KIT-dependent AML cells.²

### Hepatocellular carcinoma

In [[hepatocellular-carcinoma|HBV-associated HCC]], mTOR pathway activation is linked to cancer stem cell features and poor prognosis. Integrated multi-omics analysis using single-cell and bulk RNA sequencing identified [[ssr2|SSR2]] and [[ube2d3|UBE2D3]] as high-confidence stemness biomarkers, with pathway enrichment revealing that both genes converge on PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling.³ High [[ssr2|SSR2]] expression and low [[ube2d3|UBE2D3]] expression are associated with worse clinical outcomes, and mechanistic modeling prioritizes the PI3K/AKT/mTOR axis as a core regulatory circuit underlying stemness in HBV-HCC.³

### Colorectal cancer

In [[colorectal-cancer|colorectal cancer]], mTOR signaling through the PI3K/AKT/mTOR axis stabilizes the partial epithelial-mesenchymal transition (partial EMT) state, a hybrid phenotype that confers metastatic plasticity and treatment resistance.⁴ The calcium-activated chloride channel [[ano1|ANO1 (TMEM16A)]] maintains this state by sustaining PI3K/AKT/mTOR and ERK pathway activity.⁴ When [[ano1|ANO1]] is depleted, CRC cells lose expression of mesenchymal markers (N-cadherin, vimentin, α-SMA) and EMT transcription factors (Zeb1, Snail, Twist), dismantle their hybrid subcellular architecture, and selectively lose the E-cadherin⁺/vimentin⁺ partial EMT subpopulation.⁴ These findings position mTOR as a central effector linking ion channel signaling to metastatic phenotype.

### Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma

In [[pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma|PDAC]], mTOR upregulation is a primary mechanism of adaptive resistance to [[kras|KRAS^G12D^]]-selective inhibitors. Treatment with the KRAS^G12D^ inhibitor MRTX-1133 consistently induces expression of [[fra-2|Fos-related antigen-2 (Fra-2)]], a stress-responsive AP-1 transcription factor that directly enhances mTOR transcription and signaling.⁵ [[fra-2|Fra-2]] overexpression promotes resistance by reprogramming the transcriptional landscape, and FRA2 and MTOR transcript levels strongly correlate in PDAC patient samples.⁵ Functional assays in established cell lines, xenograft models, and patient-derived organoids confirm that [[fra-2|Fra-2]]-driven mTOR reactivation bypasses KRAS^G12D^ inhibition, underscoring mTOR as a convergent resistance node.⁵

## Drug resistance mechanisms

Activation of mTOR signaling is a recurrent adaptive resistance mechanism across multiple targeted therapies. In [[acute-myeloid-leukemia|AML]], mTOR pathway reactivation following [[lsd1|LSD1]] inhibitor treatment defines partially responsive and tolerant cell populations, whereas sensitive cells exhibit sustained mTOR suppression.² This dichotomy establishes mTOR signaling status as a predictive biomarker for LSD1 inhibitor efficacy.² In [[pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma|PDAC]], [[fra-2|Fra-2]]-mediated transcriptional upregulation of mTOR bypasses [[kras|KRAS^G12D^]] inhibition, demonstrating how stress-responsive transcription factors can restore proliferative signaling even when the primary oncogenic driver is blocked.⁵ These findings position mTOR as a central node that integrates diverse resistance signals, and suggest that co-targeting mTOR alongside primary therapies may prevent or delay resistance.

---

## Therapeutic implications

mTOR inhibition represents a mechanistically rational strategy to suppress proliferation, induce apoptosis, and sensitize cancer cells to co-therapies across multiple malignancies. In [[b-cell-acute-lymphoblastic-leukemia|B-ALL]], pharmacological mTOR inhibition abolishes glucose dependency and CAD phosphorylation, rendering cells vulnerable to metabolic stress.¹ Targeting the mTOR-regulated enzyme [[dhodh|DHODH]] selectively kills high-pS6 cells and may overcome chemoresistance in relapsed disease.¹ In [[acute-myeloid-leukemia|AML]], combining mTOR inhibitors with [[lsd1|LSD1]] or [[kit|c-Kit]] inhibitors may prevent adaptive pathway reactivation.² In [[pancreatic-ductal-adenocarcinoma|PDAC]], co-targeting [[fra-2|Fra-2]] and mTOR may enhance the efficacy of [[kras|KRAS]] inhibitors by blocking the primary resistance circuit.⁵ Across contexts, [[p-s6|pS6]] phosphorylation and mTOR expression levels serve as biomarkers for treatment sensitivity, pathway activation, and stemness features.¹³
