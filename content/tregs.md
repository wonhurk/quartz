**Regulatory T cells (Tregs)** are a specialized subset of CD4+ T lymphocytes characterized by the expression of [[cd25|CD25]] (the high-affinity IL-2 receptor α-chain) and the lineage-defining transcription factor [[foxp3|FOXP3]]. These cells actively suppress immune responses and maintain immune homeostasis, preventing autoimmunity while modulating inflammatory responses in infection, transplantation, and cancer.¹ Impaired Treg numbers or function drive pathology in [[chronic-graft-versus-host-disease|chronic graft-versus-host disease (cGVHD)]], [[type-1-diabetes|type 1 diabetes]], [[immune-thrombocytopenia|immune thrombocytopenia]], and tumor-associated immunosuppression, while therapeutic augmentation of Treg populations offers disease control across these conditions.

---

## Mechanism

Treg development and function are governed by the master transcription factor [[foxp3|FOXP3]], whose expression is induced and stabilized by [[stat5|STAT5]] signaling downstream of [[il-2|IL-2]] and maintained through epigenetic modifications at the Foxp3 locus.⁸ Tregs suppress effector T cell proliferation and cytokine production through multiple mechanisms: competitive consumption of IL-2, secretion of inhibitory cytokines including [[il-10|IL-10]] and [[tgfb|TGF-β]], expression of cytotoxic molecules such as granzyme B, and metabolic disruption of target cells.¹ In peripheral tissues, type 2 conventional dendritic cells ([[cdc2|cDC2s]]) promote de novo Treg differentiation from naive [[cd4-t-cells|CD4+ T cells]] through homeostatic maturation programs that upregulate [[tslp|thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP)]] receptor complexes, including [[il-7r|IL-7Rα]] and TSLPR, enabling TSLP-mediated tolerance induction.²

## Chronic graft-versus-host disease

Impaired Treg recovery following [[hematopoietic-stem-cell-transplantation|hematopoietic stem cell transplantation]] is a central mechanism underlying [[chronic-graft-versus-host-disease|chronic graft-versus-host disease]], a debilitating complication characterized by autoimmune-like tissue damage.¹ Phase 1/2 clinical trials of donor-derived Treg infusions in steroid-refractory or -dependent cGVHD demonstrated feasibility, safety, and clinical efficacy: 71% of patients achieved global responses, and 52% experienced at least a 2-point improvement on the cGVHD severity scale.¹ These responses enabled immunosuppression reduction, with 58% decreasing corticosteroids, 83% reducing ruxolitinib, 33% lowering mycophenolate, and 75% discontinuing calcineurin inhibitors entirely. Infused Treg clonotypes remained detectable in circulation up to 12 months post-infusion, accompanied by increases in serum [[il-7|IL-7]] and [[ifn-gamma|IFN-γ]] and decreases in soluble CD13 and [[st2|sST2]], consistent with sustained immune modulation.¹

## Cancer immunosuppression

Tumors actively recruit and expand Tregs to establish local immunosuppression and evade immune surveillance. In gastric cancer, tumor cell-derived [[serglycin|serglycin (SRGN)]] drives differentiation of a specialized [[lag3|LAG3]]-positive Treg subset through [[cd44|CD44]]-[[tgfbr1|TGF-βRI]]-[[smad3|SMAD3]] signaling.³ SRGN-CD44 engagement triggers glycolytic reprogramming and reactive oxygen species clearance, which reduces oxidative modification at LAG3 methionine 171, enhancing LAG3 dimerization while disrupting interaction with the E3 ubiquitin ligase [[peli1|PELI1]], thereby stabilizing LAG3 protein and maintaining Treg suppressive function.³ Blocking SRGN-CD44 signaling suppresses LAG3+ Treg differentiation, relieves immunosuppression, and inhibits tumor progression. In cholangiocarcinoma, high [[s100a12|S100A12]] expression correlates with reduced Treg infiltration and increased M0 and M2 macrophage polarization, suggesting tumor-specific reprogramming of the immune microenvironment.⁵

Paradoxically, elevated baseline Treg frequencies adjacent to [[cd3|CD3]]+ T cells predict improved responses to pembrolizumab-based immunotherapy in non-small cell lung cancer.⁶ Integration of Treg spatial relationships with overall T cell density yields a tumor inflammation score that correlates strongly with disease response, potentially reflecting an ongoing antitumor immune response rather than simple immunosuppression.⁶

## Therapeutic modulation

Multiple strategies restore or expand Treg populations in autoimmune and inflammatory disorders. Pharmacological inhibition of [[cdk8|CDK8]] and [[cdk19|CDK19]] with AS2863619 robustly converts CD4+CD25− effector T cells into CD4+CD25+Foxp3+ Tregs in [[immune-thrombocytopenia|primary immune thrombocytopenia (ITP)]], operating through rapid augmentation of [[stat5|STAT5]] phosphorylation and subsequent Foxp3 induction while simultaneously suppressing [[stat3|STAT3]] phosphorylation and [[th17|Th17]] polarization.⁸ Global transcriptomic analysis confirms broad suppression of effector differentiation programs coupled with upregulation of stable Treg signatures, with STAT5, STAT3, and FOXP3 identified as core mediators.⁸ Metabolic analyses reveal that CDK8/19 inhibition shifts T cells from glycolysis toward oxidative phosphorylation, a metabolic state compatible with Treg lineage stability.⁸

In [[type-1-diabetes|type 1 diabetes]], nanoassembly-mediated immunometabolic reprogramming of activated CD4+ T cells suppresses β-cell autoimmunity by redirecting differentiation away from pathogenic [[th1|Th1]]/[[th17|Th17]] lineages toward the protective Treg lineage, reducing CD4+ T cell infiltration into pancreatic islets and restoring blood glucose homeostasis.⁴

## Developmental and environmental influences

The maternal environment during fetal development exerts lasting influence on Treg generation and autoimmune disease susceptibility. In the [[nod-mouse|NOD mouse]] model of type 1 diabetes, embryo transfer experiments demonstrate that NOD fetuses gestated in C57BL/6 surrogate mothers exhibit approximately 50% reduction in cumulative diabetes incidence compared to NOD controls, despite similar insulitis severity.⁷ NOD/B6 offspring display a notable increase in CD4+[[cd25|CD25]]+ Tregs in blood and spleen, with restored CD25 expression, alongside altered autoantibody profiles including elevated IgM titers against insulin, DNA, and GAD65 and distinct IgG subclass distributions.⁷ These findings establish that prenatal factors imprint immune cell populations and influence autoimmune disease trajectories independently of genetic background.
