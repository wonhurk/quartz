**Herpes simplex virus (HSV)** is a latent human pathogen that establishes persistent infection in sensory ganglia, with recurrent reactivation events causing symptomatic disease across the lifespan. The predominant clinical manifestation is [[herpes-labialis|recurrent oral herpes labialis]] or "cold sores," which affects a substantial proportion of the global population including young children.¹ Currently, no effective preventive intervention exists, creating significant unmet clinical need. Two distinct therapeutic strategies have emerged: off-target immunomodulation via [[bcg-vaccine|BCG vaccination]] to enhance anti-viral immunity, and direct antiviral treatment through novel transdermal delivery systems employing [[acyclovir|acyclovir]]-loaded microneedle patches that achieve superior bioavailability compared to conventional formulations.²

---

## Latency and reactivation

HSV establishes a latent infection within sensory ganglia following primary infection, with reactivation of the virus leading to recurrent episodic disease. The clinical burden of these recurrent episodes—manifesting primarily as painful vesicular lesions on the oral mucosa—affects a substantial proportion of children and adults worldwide.¹ However, the precise molecular mechanisms governing reactivation frequency and severity remain incompletely understood, and existing interventions provide only symptomatic relief rather than prevention.

---

## Clinical presentation

Recurrent [[herpes-labialis|herpes labialis]] represents the most common manifestation of HSV disease, characterized by clusters of painful blisters that rupture into shallow ulcers on the lip vermilion and surrounding oral mucosa.¹ The lesions typically persist for 7–10 days and recur at variable intervals depending on individual immune status and viral load within the latent reservoir. The high prevalence of these recurrences across diverse populations underscores the pressing need for novel preventive and therapeutic approaches beyond symptomatic management.

---

## Immunomodulation via BCG vaccination

The [[bcg-vaccine|Bacille Calmette-Guérin (BCG) vaccine]], classically administered for tuberculosis prevention, exerts broad off-target immunomodulatory effects that extend beyond mycobacterial immunity. Recent evidence demonstrates that neonatal BCG vaccination may provide protection against recurrences of latent HSV infection through enhanced innate immune responses.¹ This immunological mechanism represents a promising non-antiviral strategy for reducing HSV reactivation burden in early childhood and potentially across the lifespan.

---

## Transdermal antiviral delivery

Direct antiviral intervention remains the cornerstone of HSV treatment, with [[acyclovir|acyclovir]] serving as the gold-standard nucleoside analog. However, conventional topical and oral formulations face bioavailability limitations. Acyclovir-loaded starch-based microneedle patches represent an innovative transdermal delivery system that circumvents these constraints through mechanical penetration of the stratum corneum.² The patches employ pyramidal-shaped starch-based microneedles (620–640 μm in length) fabricated from mungbean starch and polyvinyl alcohol, with arginine and mannitol as plasticizers to optimize mechanical properties.² Compression forces of 1.30–4.80 N per needle ensure robust dermal penetration without causing excessive tissue damage.

---

## Drug release kinetics

The acyclovir-loaded microneedle patches achieve drug release kinetics substantially superior to conventional film-type formulations, delivering 2.5–4.3 times higher acyclovir concentrations over the same timeframe.² Notably, over 98% of the loaded acyclovir is released within 80 minutes following application, driven by Fickian diffusion-mediated transport through the hydrated polymer matrix.² This rapid and complete drug release—validated through artificial skin models and ex vivo porcine skin studies—translates to high local drug bioavailability at the infection site, potentially enabling more effective viral suppression than topical formulations achieving lower dermal drug levels.

---

## Biocompatibility and efficacy

The starch-based microneedle platform demonstrates excellent biodegradability and maintains high cellular viability in in vitro assays, eliminating concerns about foreign-body reactions or chronic irritation from needle residue.² Antiviral efficacy studies confirm that acyclovir-loaded patches retain pharmacological activity, supporting their potential as a viable transdermal therapy for recurrent HSV infections. The combination of mechanical penetration efficiency, optimized drug release kinetics, and demonstrated biological safety positions these microneedle patches as promising candidates for next-generation HSV management, particularly in settings where patient compliance with multidose oral regimens or frequent topical application is challenging.
