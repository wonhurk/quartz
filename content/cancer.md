**Cancer** is a disease characterized by uncontrolled cellular proliferation and malignant tumor growth that remains a leading cause of global morbidity and mortality. Contemporary therapeutic strategies increasingly leverage nanomedicine-based platforms employing stimulus-responsive nanoparticles—including metal-organic frameworks, heterostructures, liposomes, and mesoporous silica—to achieve selective tumor targeting and triggered drug release through pH-, heat-, ultrasound-, or [[reactive-nitrogen-species|reactive oxygen and nitrogen species]]-sensitive mechanisms. These platforms integrate multimodal mechanisms of action combining photothermal therapy (PTT), photodynamic therapy (PDT), [[ferroptosis]], and immunotherapy while targeting critical features of the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment, including [[fap|cancer-associated fibroblasts]] and immunosuppressive immune cells.¹⁻² Central to emerging immunotherapeutic approaches is activation of the [[sting|stimulator of interferon genes (STING)]] pathway, which drives [[type-i-interferon|type I interferon]] production and establishes robust anti-tumor immunity.³

## Nanoparticle-based therapeutic platforms

Stimulus-responsive nanoparticle systems enable precise spatiotemporal control over drug release within the tumor microenvironment while minimizing systemic toxicity. The [[rcla|RCLA]] nanosystem—comprised of rare earth nanoparticles and Ce-UiO-66-NH₂—responds to 980-nm near-infrared laser irradiation to trigger cascade enzymatic catalysis, producing [[reactive-nitrogen-species|reactive nitrogen species]] that exert enhanced nitrification and oxidation damage compared to reactive oxygen species alone.⁴ This platform exhibits dual catalase- and oxidase-like activity, generating oxygen that converts to ROS, which subsequently cleaves thioketal bonds to release L-arginine for downstream RNS production. The [[sendful|SENDFUL]] formulation employs pyridinium betaine-capped lipids that reversibly switch from non-fusogenic to fusogenic states in response to acidic tumor regions (pH 6.5), enabling tumor-specific cytoplasmic delivery and potent [[sting|STING]] pathway activation with negligible systemic toxicity.³ Ultrasound-guided strategies further refine spatial control, as demonstrated by the AMINP system, which leverages localized vascular disruption to generate in situ fibrin and activated coagulation factor XIII, achieving 52.1-fold enhancement in active drug concentration and 98.9% tumor suppression.⁶

## Multimodal combination therapies

Integration of complementary therapeutic modalities has emerged as a cornerstone strategy for overcoming the limitations of single-agent approaches. Bi₂Se₃@BiSe heterostructures exemplify this paradigm, simultaneously activating PTT and PDT through efficient charge carrier separation at interfacial contacts while polarizing M0 macrophages toward the tumor-suppressive M1 phenotype to promote immunogenic cell death.¹ The iTempest platform—hollow mesoporous manganese dioxide nanoparticles loaded with tacrine and SR717—triggers [[ferroptosis]] and abundant type I interferon production through multiple convergent pathways, effectively suppressing tumor metastasis and establishing immunological memory.⁵ This system leverages lipid droplet accumulation to self-regulate late-stage inflammation by inhibiting dendritic cell maturation without compromising memory T cell populations. Thermoresponsive chitosan-based composite hydrogels extend this concept to sustained delivery, forming durable depots that sequentially release β-glycerophosphate and genipin to create double-network structures, enabling synergistic photothermal, ROS-mediated apoptosis, and tumor-associated macrophage repolarization.⁷

## Tumor microenvironment modulation

The dense extracellular matrix and immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment create dual physical and immunological barriers that severely compromise therapeutic efficacy. Carrier-free QN nanoparticles address this challenge by coordinating manganese ions with quercetin and the indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1 inhibitor NLG919.² Quercetin normalizes [[fap|cancer-associated fibroblasts]] and reduces extracellular matrix deposition, while manganese ions generate hydroxyl radicals to induce immunogenic cell death and activate the [[sting|STING]] pathway. Simultaneously, NLG919 disrupts the tryptophan-kynurenine metabolic pathway to alleviate immunosuppression. This coordinated mechanism enhances immune cell infiltration and effector function, generating robust synergistic activity when combined with [[anti-pd-l1-antibody|anti-PD-L1 antibodies]] to cooperatively suppress primary tumor growth and pulmonary metastases. The platform overcomes the traditionally insurmountable challenges posed by stromal fibrosis and immune evasion, establishing a new paradigm for stromal-targeted immunotherapy.

## Immune activation strategies

Activation of innate immune sensing pathways represents a pivotal mechanism for converting immunologically "cold" tumors into responsive targets for [[immune-checkpoint-blockade|immune checkpoint inhibition]]. The [[sting|STING]] pathway serves as a central node integrating diverse upstream signals—including ferroptotic damage-associated molecular patterns and manganese ions—to drive robust [[type-i-interferon|type I interferon]] production.³⁻⁵ Radiotherapy enhances adeno-associated vector tumor transduction through epigenetic modification of vector episomes, enabling spatial control of immunostimulatory cytokine expression.¹¹ The AAV-iIL12 platform delivers interleukin-12 through an interferon-inducible promoter, achieving efficient cytokine production without significant toxicity when combined with radiation. This combination generates a highly immunostimulatory tumor microenvironment leading to IFNγ- and FAS-dependent local and systemic antitumor responses capable of overcoming common immune-evasion mechanisms. Freeze-dried [[peptide-cancer-vaccine|peptide vaccines]] incorporating cobalt porphyrin-phospholipid with melanoma-associated antigens and QS21 adjuvant further demonstrate enhanced immunogenicity, inducing tumor inhibition comparable to liquid formulations while offering superior stability and scalability.¹²

## Overcoming therapeutic resistance

Adaptive resistance mechanisms—including radioresistance and chemoresistance—represent major obstacles to durable cancer control. Ionizing radiation induces lysosomal cholesterol deposition and cellular senescence with complex interrelationships that drive radioresistance.⁸ The U@HC nanomedicine system addresses this through sequential release of U18666A and β-cyclodextrin in response to acidic environments and elevated glutathione levels, inhibiting abnormal lysosomal cholesterol accumulation while reducing cellular senescence and senescence-associated secretory phenotypes to achieve potent radiosensitization. In multiple myeloma and other malignancies, overexpression of [[psmd1|PSMD1]]—a key 19S proteasome regulatory particle subunit—correlates with poor prognosis and proteasome inhibitor resistance.¹⁰ Genetic depletion of PSMD1 decreases cancer cell viability, induces polyubiquitinated protein accumulation, and promotes apoptosis while activating immune-related pathways. Lipid nanoparticle-delivered PSMD1 siRNA reduces tumor growth in cell lines and primary patient samples, overcomes proteasome inhibitor resistance, and significantly prolongs survival in xenograft models, positioning PSMD1 as a critical therapeutic target across diverse cancer types.

## Advanced drug delivery systems

Achieving independent and programmable release of multiple drugs from single formulations addresses the critical need for precise temporal coordination in combination therapies. Modular microgel platforms inspired by LEGO-like assembly combine distinct microsphere modules—gelatin methacrylate for hydrophilic drugs and alginate for hydrophobic agents—to enable programmable multi-drug delivery.⁹ These composite microgels exhibit outstanding injectability, rapid self-healing properties, and prolonged in vivo retention while enabling precise tuning of release profiles across a broad therapeutic spectrum. Sequential delivery of carboplatin followed by [[rsl3|RSL3]] overcomes chemoresistance by exploiting their distinct mechanisms, extending survival by ten days compared to conventional hydrogels. Controlled release of doxorubicin and paclitaxel reduces tumor mass 3.53-fold versus traditional systems. Reassessment of the enhanced permeability and retention (EPR) effect using second near-infrared aggregation-caused quenching probes reveals that microdistribution—rather than total tumor accumulation—serves as the more meaningful indicator of permeation efficiency, challenging classical macro-level EPR concepts and underscoring the necessity of microscopic particokinetic analysis.¹³

## Novel therapeutic targets

Beyond canonical oncogenic drivers, emerging targets exploit metabolic vulnerabilities and tissue-specific dependencies. Carbonic anhydrase 2 ([[ca2|CA2]]) suppression by luteolin—a bioactive compound from *[[celastrus-orbiculatus|Celastrus orbiculatus]]* stem—inhibits cervical cancer through mechanisms bridging traditional herbal medicine and molecular oncology.¹⁴ Hydrogen peroxide serves as a key biomarker of oxidative stress, and the [[a-bo-crispr|A-BO-CRISPR]] strategy enables real-time fluorescence imaging in living systems through a 4-bromomethylphenylboronic acid pinacol ester-caged DNA activator whose binding to crRNA is initially blocked by steric hindrance.¹⁵ Upon encountering endogenous H₂O₂, boronate ester hydrolysis restores activator-crRNA hybridization, triggering amplified signal generation with a detection limit of 0.64 μM and enabling monitoring of redox fluxes in tumor-bearing mice for imaging-guided diagnostics and therapeutic monitoring.
