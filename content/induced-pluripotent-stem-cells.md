**Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs)** are reprogrammed somatic cells derived through forced expression of pluripotency factors, capable of indefinite self-renewal and differentiation into all cell types of the body.¹ This technology has emerged as a transformative platform for cellular engineering and human disease modeling, combining the regenerative capacity of embryonic stem cells with the ethical advantages and patient-specific applicability of adult cell sources. Unlike terminally differentiated somatic cells, iPSCs maintain a malleable epigenetic state that permits precise genetic modification and faithful recapitulation of disease phenotypes in vitro.

---

## CTL engineering for immunotherapy

iPSC-derived [[cd8-t-cells|cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs)]] represent a particularly powerful application of iPSC technology for cancer immunotherapy. The introduction of tumor-specific T-cell receptor ([[tcr]]) genes into iPSC-derived CTLs via conventional viral vectors—retroviral or lentiviral approaches—can result in random, multicopy genomic integration that produces heterogeneous TCR expression and carries risk of oncogenic transformation depending on insertion site.¹ To overcome these limitations, genome editing at the TRAC (T-cell receptor alpha constant) locus enables precise, single-copy integration of TCR genes under native regulatory control, thereby ensuring stable expression of functionally competent receptors.¹ Engineered iPSC-derived CTLs display robust antigen-specific expansion and produce interferon-gamma and upregulate the degranulation marker CD107a in response to cognate peptide antigens, demonstrating potent cytolytic activity against target cells while avoiding the genotoxicity risks inherent in viral vector integration.¹

## Disease modeling and isogenic cell systems

Patient-derived iPSCs enable the generation of isogenic cell lines carrying wild-type, heterozygous-mutant, and biallelic-mutant genotypes within the same genetic background, providing a powerful approach to interrogate disease mechanisms. This application is exemplified in the study of hereditary multiple osteochondromas (HMO), a rare autosomal dominant skeletal disorder caused by loss-of-function mutations in [[ext1]] or EXT2, which encode glycosyltransferases essential for heparan sulfate biosynthesis. Direct genetic manipulation via [[crispr-cas9]] combined with PiggyBac transposon technology enabled the introduction of a second pathogenic mutation into patient-derived iPSCs carrying a heterozygous [[ext1]] mutation, generating a complete model system.² Critically, biallelic [[ext1]] inactivation—but not heterozygous mutations alone—drives disease-relevant phenotypes, including significant upregulation of [[sox9]], [[col2a1]], and [[acan]], elevated glycosaminoglycan levels, and markedly reduced heparan sulfate production.² This finding resolves the long-standing question of whether haploinsufficiency suffices for osteochondroma formation, demonstrating instead that complete gene inactivation is required to initiate pathological chondrocyte differentiation.

## Three-dimensional organoid modeling

iPSC-derived three-dimensional organoid cultures extend disease modeling beyond monolayer systems to recapitulate tissue-level architecture and disease phenotypes. Chondrogenic organoids generated from iPSCs carrying biallelic [[ext1]] mutations display enlargement and structural disorganization, partially recapitulating key histopathological features of osteochondromas observed in patient tissue.² This capability to generate patient-specific, genetically defined 3D tissue analogs provides a tractable system for studying disease mechanisms, screening therapeutics, and understanding how mutations alter multicellular development and tissue homeostasis in a physiologically relevant context.

---

## Genome editing and precision engineering

The amenability of iPSCs to precise genetic manipulation underpins their utility across diverse applications. Integration of [[crispr-cas9]] genome editing with controlled mutagenesis via PiggyBac transposon technology enables researchers to introduce defined mutations, correct disease variants, or introduce therapeutic transgenes in a targeted and reversible manner.² Combined with iPSCs' capacity for unlimited clonal expansion and characterization, this toolset permits the generation of large panels of isogenic variants required to systematically dissect the genetic architecture of human disease and validate therapeutic hypotheses in patient-derived cellular contexts before clinical translation.
