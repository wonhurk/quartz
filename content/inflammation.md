**Inflammation** is a complex pathophysiological response characterized by elevated oxidative stress, dysregulated immune activation, and disruption of tissue homeostasis, underlying a broad spectrum of acute and chronic diseases. [[h2o2|Hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂)]] and reactive oxygen species (ROS) serve as both critical biomarkers and primary drivers of inflammatory pathology, creating pathological feedback loops with calcium dysregulation that amplify cellular damage.¹⁻⁴ The inflammatory cascade involves aberrant polarization of macrophages and microglia toward pro-inflammatory (M1) phenotypes, overproduction of pro-inflammatory cytokines including IL-6 and IL-8, and compromised epithelial barrier integrity through loss of tight-junction proteins and mucin production.⁵⁻⁷ Emerging therapeutic strategies target inflammation through antioxidant nanoparticles, immune cell reprogramming toward reparative (M2) phenotypes, restoration of epithelial homeostasis, and modulation of neuroimmune pathways.

---

## Pathophysiological mechanisms

Inflammation is driven by excessive ROS generation, which catalyzes lipid peroxidation, protein oxidation, and DNA damage across multiple tissue compartments. In acute settings, calcium dysregulation amplifies ROS production through positive feedback loops, wherein elevated intracellular Ca²⁺ stimulates mitochondrial ROS generation, which in turn further disrupts calcium homeostasis.³ The malondialdehyde-to-superoxide dismutase (MDA/SOD) ratio serves as a quantitative oxidative index that correlates positively with symptom severity across inflammatory conditions ranging from [[temporomandibular-disorder|temporomandibular disorder]] to age-related gut dysfunction.⁷⁻¹³

Immune cell polarization constitutes a central regulatory axis in inflammatory progression. Macrophages and microglia adopt pro-inflammatory M1 phenotypes characterized by secretion of IL-6, IL-8, and TNF-α, perpetuating tissue damage and preventing resolution. Therapeutic reprogramming toward M2 reparative phenotypes restores local homeostasis and accelerates tissue regeneration.³⁻⁶ In [[systemic-lupus-erythematosus|systemic lupus erythematosus]], dysregulated B cell and CD8⁺ T cell populations contribute to chronic immune activation and end-organ injury.⁸

Epithelial barrier dysfunction represents a shared pathogenic feature across mucosal inflammatory diseases. Chronic inflammation compromises intestinal and respiratory epithelial integrity through downregulation of tight-junction proteins, reduced mucin production, and impaired regenerative capacity.⁷⁻¹⁰ [[eicosanoids|Arachidonic acid]] metabolism via phospholipase A2 generates bioactive eicosanoids that modulate vascular tone, immune cell recruitment, and tissue remodeling, with dysregulation of this pathway contributing to inflammation-driven malignancy.¹⁴

## Acute inflammatory conditions

In sepsis-induced acute lung injury (ALI), dysregulated host responses to infection drive endotoxin accumulation, cytokine storm, and oxidative injury culminating in respiratory failure. Polymyxin B-antioxidant nanoassemblies ([[pv-nps|PV NPs]]) integrate antibacterial activity, lipopolysaccharide neutralization, and broad-spectrum ROS scavenging, attenuating lung inflammation and conferring 75% survival in murine polymicrobial sepsis models while mitigating the nephrotoxicity of polymyxin B monotherapy.⁴

Ischemic stroke pathology encompasses a dynamic cascade of calcium dysregulation, oxidative stress, and neuroinflammation during the ischemia-reperfusion phase. Multiaspect layered double hydroxide nanohybrid platforms ([[s-e-ldh|S-E@LDH]]) chelate Ca²⁺, scavenge reactive oxygen and nitrogen species, and deliver the anti-inflammatory agent [[salvianic-acid-a|salvianic acid A]] in a spatiotemporally controlled manner. This triple synergistic regulation disrupts Ca²⁺-ROS amplification loops acutely, then promotes microglial M2 polarization during the recovery phase, reducing neuronal damage and improving motor function.³

Infected wounds present a complex inflammatory milieu characterized by bacterial burden, persistent inflammation, and impaired angiogenesis. [[peptide-peg-hydrogel|Peptide-PEG hydrogels]] fabricated via dendritic polymerization generate stable, antibacterial crosslinked networks that eradicate bacteria, attenuate inflammation, and accelerate wound closure through enhanced vascular ingrowth.⁹

## Chronic inflammatory diseases

[[systemic-lupus-erythematosus|Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)]] manifests immune dysregulation, chronic inflammation, and hypertension linked to impaired neuroimmune communication. The cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway, which suppresses cytokine release via vagal efferents, is compromised in lupus. Long-term acetylcholinesterase inhibitor therapy with galantamine enhances this pathway, reducing blood pressure, systemic inflammation (soluble TNFR1), renal injury markers, and splenic B cell and renal CD8⁺ T cell populations in murine models.⁸

Cystic fibrosis lung disease features chronic Pseudomonas aeruginosa colonization with paradoxically excessive inflammation despite bacterial adaptation to a biofilm-associated, low-virulence phenotype. [[dksa|DksA]], a bacterial transcription factor, was identified as a novel pro-inflammatory mediator sustaining IL-8 secretion by airway epithelial cells under conditions mimicking chronic infection, contributing to persistent immunopathology independent of classical virulence factors.¹²

[[temporomandibular-disorder|Temporomandibular disorder (TMD)]] pain associates with peripheral inflammatory and oxidative alterations even in genetically identical individuals. Monozygotic twin studies discordant for painful TMD reveal elevated IL-6, IL-6/IL-10 ratio, MDA/SOD ratio, and reduced brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) in affected twins, demonstrating that environmental factors drive epigenetic inflammatory dysregulation underlying chronic orofacial pain.¹³

Age-related gut dysfunction stems from chronic low-grade inflammation, epithelial barrier deterioration, and microbiota dysbiosis. Dietary [[neu5ac|N-acetylneuraminic acid (Neu5Ac)]] preserves intestinal homeostasis by enhancing tight-junction proteins, suppressing macrophage infiltration and M1 polarization, enriching butyrate-producing bacteria, and reducing HIF-1α signaling in inflammatory cells, thereby restoring epithelial regeneration and mitigating senescence pathways.⁷

Obesity-associated inflammation is driven by hypoxic expansion of adipose tissue. Hypoxia-activated phototheranostic probes enable targeted imaging and photothermal modulation of visceral adipose tissue, inducing white adipose browning, enhancing lipolysis, and reducing pro-inflammatory markers (IL-6, MCP-1) while increasing anti-inflammatory IL-10, leading to metabolic improvement.¹¹

## Detection and monitoring

Real-time visualization of inflammatory biomarkers in living systems has been enabled by bioorthogonal biosensing platforms. A CRISPR/Cas12a strategy employing a sterically hindered, [[h2o2|H₂O₂]]-activatable DNA activator achieves sub-micromolar detection sensitivity and rapid response kinetics for monitoring oxidative stress in living cells and tumor-bearing mice. Upon encountering endogenous H₂O₂, boronate ester hydrolysis restores activator-crRNA hybridization, triggering amplified fluorescent signal generation via Cas12a-mediated collateral cleavage, providing a powerful tool for probing redox biology and imaging-guided diagnostics.¹

## Novel therapeutic approaches

Antioxidant nanoparticle platforms represent a cornerstone strategy for quenching ROS and interrupting oxidative-inflammatory cascades. Beyond sepsis and stroke applications, reactive oxygen species-scavenging microgels embedded within periosteum-mimetic membranes ([[pomegranate-inspired-bionic-microgel-periosteum-composite|pomegranate-inspired bionic composites]]) enable macrophage polarization toward reparative phenotypes and promote intercellular mitochondrial transfer during bone regeneration in critical-sized defects.⁶

Epithelial barrier restoration strategies address the root cause of mucosal inflammation. Storage-stable [[rnai|siRNA]] delivery systems incorporating N-acetylcysteine as a functional lyoprotectant enable dual-action therapy: extracellular mucolysis penetrates pathological mucus hypersecretion, while intracellular RNA interference suppresses inflammation-driving genes in mucus-hypersecretory lung diseases.⁵ Similarly, [[nootkatone|nootkatone]], a sesquiterpenoid from grapefruit, orchestrates mitochondrial redox homeostasis via Nrf2 activation, ameliorating sleep deprivation-induced colonic barrier dysfunction by upregulating epithelial proteins, inducing mucin production, and restoring beneficial microbiota.¹⁰

Neuroimmune modulation through the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway offers a systemic approach to chronic inflammation. Acetylcholinesterase inhibition enhances vagal anti-inflammatory signaling, reducing cytokine production and end-organ injury across multiple inflammatory contexts.⁸

Spatiotemporally controlled drug release maximizes therapeutic efficacy while minimizing off-target effects. Nanocarrier systems responsive to the acidic microenvironment of inflamed tissues enable targeted delivery during active inflammation, with subsequent resolution-phase dosing tailored to promote tissue repair.³
