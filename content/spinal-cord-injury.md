**Spinal cord injury (SCI)** is a traumatic neurological condition characterized by acute [[neuroinflammation]] and secondary neurodegeneration driven by aberrant [[astrocyte]] activation and dysregulated [[immune-infiltration|immune cell infiltration]].¹ Unlike primary mechanical damage, which occurs at the moment of impact, the secondary phase—initiated within hours to days post-injury—is an active degenerative process fueled by persistent [[astrocyte|glial]] inflammation and pro-inflammatory mediator production that exacerbates tissue loss and functional impairment.¹² The central regulatory brake controlling this self-perpetuating neuroinflammatory cascade is the [[ahr|aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR)]], which when activated suppresses the [[nf-kb|NF-κB]]/[[mapk|MAPK]] signaling axis in [[astrocyte|astrocytes]], thereby limiting secondary degeneration and enabling functional motor recovery.¹

---

## Pathophysiology

Acute spinal cord injury initiates a rapid and stereotyped neuroinflammatory cascade. [[astrocyte|Astrocytes]], once activated by trauma-associated molecular signals and cytokines including TNF-α, undergo morphological transformation from a resting ramified state to a reactive hypertrophic phenotype and initiate transcription of pro-inflammatory mediators including [[il-6|IL-6]], [[il-1b|IL-1β]], iNOS, COX-2, and chemokines (CCL2, CXCL2, CXCL10).¹ These mediators create a chemotactic gradient that recruits resident microglia and infiltrating monocytes from the peripheral blood, establishing a self-perpetuating inflammatory loop wherein [[immune-cell|immune cells]] further activate [[astrocyte|astrocytes]] through additional cytokine signaling. Recent multi-omics investigations have revealed that a functionally distinct C/EBPβ-S100a4⁺ [[macrophage|macrophage]] subset emerges early post-injury and acts as a critical regulatory hub, perpetuating secondary tissue damage through coordinated NF-κB signaling.² The spatial and temporal coordination of [[astrocyte|astrocyte]]-[[immune-cell|immune cell]] crosstalk during the acute window—particularly in the first days following injury—is the primary determinant of whether tissue preservation and functional recovery or widespread [[neurodegeneration|neuronal loss]] ensues.

---

## Neuroinflammatory signaling and the NF-κB/MAPK axis

The molecular engine driving aberrant [[astrocyte]] activation in SCI is the [[nf-kb|NF-κB]]/[[mapk|MAPK]] signaling cascade. Trauma-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) released from damaged neurons and endothelial cells, combined with elevated TNF-α and other danger signals, activate both [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] and [[mapk|MAPK]] pathways within [[astrocyte|astrocytes]], leading to rapid transcription and secretion of pro-inflammatory genes. This cascade perpetuates not only [[astrocyte]] activation but also recruitment and polarization of [[macrophage|macrophages]] toward a pro-inflammatory phenotype. Therapeutic suppression of this axis—particularly through activation of the [[ahr|aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR)]]—represents a mechanistically grounded strategy for mitigating secondary injury without globally suppressing immune function. The [[ahr|AhR]], a ligand-activated nuclear receptor traditionally involved in xenobiotic sensing and tissue homeostasis, directly antagonizes both [[nf-kb|NF-κB]] and [[mapk|MAPK]] signaling when bound to specific endogenous or microbial ligands, thereby restoring immune tolerance.

## IPA: A gut microbiota-derived therapeutic

[[indole-3-propionic-acid|Indole-3-propionic acid (IPA)]], a [[tryptophan|tryptophan]]-derived metabolite produced by commensal gut bacteria, has emerged as a potent [[ahr|AhR]] ligand capable of suppressing SCI-associated neuroinflammation.¹ In both in vitro models of TNF-α-stimulated [[astrocyte|astrocytes]] and in vivo mouse models of SCI, [[indole-3-propionic-acid|IPA]] treatment significantly attenuated the expression of the full pro-inflammatory mediator panel (IL-6, IL-1β, iNOS, COX-2, CCL2, CXCL2, CXCL10) through activation of the [[ahr|AhR]]/[[nf-kb|NF-κB]]/[[mapk|MAPK]] axis.¹ Beyond suppressing the [[astrocyte|astrocytic]] inflammatory phenotype, [[indole-3-propionic-acid|IPA]] treatment reduced [[glial-scar|glial scar]] formation and lesion volume while enhancing neuronal survival and preserving tissue integrity as demonstrated by MRI and histopathological analyses.¹ Functionally, [[indole-3-propionic-acid|IPA]]-treated animals exhibited substantially improved long-term motor recovery, with increased Basso Mouse Scale (BMS) scores, enhanced inclined plane performance, and improved gait coordination compared to vehicle-treated controls.¹ These findings underscore the potential of targeting gut microbiota-derived metabolites to engage upstream neuroinflammatory regulators and achieve meaningful functional restoration after SCI.

## Multi-omics immune profiling and precision targeting

Defining early immune cell states and their upstream regulatory circuits at multi-omics resolution is essential for identifying mechanistically grounded therapeutic targets in SCI.² Rather than broadly suppressing all immune responses, which can impair tissue repair and regeneration, emerging approaches integrate transcriptomic, epigenomic, and proteomic data to identify specific pathogenic [[immune-cell|immune cell]] states amenable to selective therapeutic intervention. The identification of the C/EBPβ-S100a4⁺ [[macrophage|macrophage]] axis as a critical hub in acute SCI neuroinflammation exemplifies this strategy, suggesting that precision targeting of specific [[macrophage|macrophage]] subsets or their regulatory transcription factors may yield superior therapeutic outcomes compared to pan-inflammatory suppression. This multi-omics framework establishes a roadmap for future therapeutic development aimed at selectively disabling pathogenic immune circuits while preserving protective immune responses essential for tissue repair.
