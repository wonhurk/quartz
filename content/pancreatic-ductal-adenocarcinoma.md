**Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)** is an aggressive epithelial malignancy of the pancreas characterized by [[kras|KRAS]] mutations in approximately 90% of cases, a profoundly immunosuppressive and fibrotic tumor microenvironment, and a five-year survival rate of approximately 13%.¹ The disease is marked by intrinsic resistance to conventional chemotherapy, radiotherapy, and immune checkpoint blockade, driven by KRAS-orchestrated metabolic reprogramming and inflammatory signaling that recruits immunosuppressive innate immune cells including neutrophils capable of forming extracellular traps.² Therapeutic strategies increasingly target the multilayered resistance mechanisms through rational combinations that disrupt KRAS effector pathways, reprogram the hostile microenvironment, and exploit alternative cell death modalities including ferroptosis.³

---

## Molecular drivers

KRAS mutations serve as the foundational oncogenic driver in PDAC, with KRASG12D representing the most prevalent variant.⁴ These mutations activate downstream MAPK signaling cascades that upregulate inflammatory mediators, particularly IL-8, which binds the [[anti-cxcr2-antibody|CXCR2]] receptor on tumor-associated neutrophils and sustains a chronically inflamed, immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment.² The [[kras|KRAS]]-driven inflammatory milieu not only excludes cytotoxic T cells but also promotes formation of [[nets|neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs)]] in hypoxic and necrotic tumor regions, further insulating malignant cells from immune surveillance.² Post-translational modifications add another regulatory layer: O-GlcNAcylation of [[cldn18.2|CLDN18.2]], a transmembrane protein and emerging therapeutic target, is enhanced by KRAS mutations and modulates tumor progression, potentially explaining the modest clinical efficacy of CLDN18.2-targeted therapies in PDAC relative to gastric cancer.¹

---

## Therapeutic resistance mechanisms

[[anti-pd-1-antibody|Anti-PD-1 antibody]] monotherapy is ineffective in PDAC due to the profoundly immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment maintained by constitutive KRAS-MAPK-IL-8 signaling.² Radioresistance is mediated in part by AURKA overexpression, which physically interacts with glycogen synthase kinase 3β (GSK3β) to phosphorylate the tumor suppressor PTEN at threonine 366, thereby impairing PTEN phosphatase activity and sustaining pro-survival PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling; AURKA expression levels robustly correlate with poor patient prognosis in clinical cohorts.⁵ Resistance to KRAS-selective inhibitors such as MRTX-1133 emerges rapidly through adaptive transcriptional reprogramming orchestrated by the stress-responsive transcription factor Fra-2, which directly upregulates mTOR expression and signaling, creating a mechanistic interplay between Fra-2 and the mTOR pathway that limits inhibitor efficacy.⁴ The [[ddx3|DDX3]]/[[paf1|PAF1]] axis further promotes therapeutic resistance by enhancing cancer stemness and epithelial-mesenchymal transition phenotypes, contributing to the limited efficacy of gemcitabine and 5-fluorouracil.⁶

---

## Combination strategies

Rational therapeutic combinations targeting KRAS effector pathways have demonstrated preclinical efficacy in overcoming resistance mechanisms. MEK1/2 inhibition combined with [[anti-pd-1-antibody|anti-PD-1 antibodies]] displays antitumor activity selectively in KRAS-mutant models, inducing recruitment of memory CD8+ T cells and IFN-γ production; however, larger tumors resist this combination due to hypoxia-driven NETosis and CD8+ T cell exclusion.² The subsequent addition of [[anti-cxcr2-antibody|anti-CXCR2 antibody]] overcomes this resistance by blocking tumor-associated neutrophil infiltration into hypoxic regions, restoring CD8+ T cell anti-tumor responses and validating the clinical relevance of a NETosis-MAPK risk score that correlates with poorer survival in human PDAC cohorts.² Targeting the [[ddx3|DDX3]]/[[paf1|PAF1]] axis with the small-molecule inhibitor [[rk-33|RK-33]] enhances the therapeutic efficacy of gemcitabine and 5-fluorouracil by mitigating stemness, clonogenic capacity, and extracellular matrix remodeling, while simultaneously reducing tumor growth and increasing apoptosis in xenograft and organoid models.⁶ Trikines—engineered trimeric cytokine receptor assemblies that induce bespoke STAT signaling programs—represent a novel immunotherapeutic platform; an IL-10-based Trikine promotes immune infiltration into poorly immunogenic PDAC tumors and demonstrates efficacy in preclinical models by rewiring STAT3 and STAT5 transcriptional signatures distinct from natural cytokines.⁷

## Comorbidities

Comorbid [[diabetes-mellitus|diabetes mellitus]] significantly accelerates PDAC progression through metabolic reprogramming of both cancer cells and the immune compartment. Spatially resolved multiomics analyses reveal that diabetes-associated metabolic alterations drive [[srebp2|SREBP2]]-dependent cholesterol biosynthesis in the tumor microenvironment, which in turn stimulates the CXCL1/CXCR1/CXCR2 signaling axis to promote neutrophil recruitment and accelerate [[nets|NET]] formation.³ Patients with PDAC and comorbid diabetes exhibit higher tumor stage, poorer differentiation, and worse clinical outcomes compared to non-diabetic counterparts; pharmacologic blockade of [[srebp2|SREBP2]], CXCL1 neutralization, or NET disruption markedly reduces PDAC growth in diabetic mouse models, highlighting cholesterol-induced NETosis as a tractable therapeutic vulnerability in this patient population.³

---

## Novel therapeutic approaches

Ferroptosis induction has emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy in PDAC given its intrinsic resistance to conventional cell death modalities. Integrative multi-omics analysis identified a core ferroptosis gene signature comprising GPX4, TFRC, CTSB, NOX4, HIF1A, and TGFB1; these genes are significantly enriched in ferroptosis, HIF-1, and TGF-β pathways and show notable correlation with M1/M2 macrophage infiltration patterns.⁸ Resveratrol was validated through molecular docking, biochemical assays, and inhibitor rescue experiments as a novel ferroptosis inducer that downregulates multiple key proteins, suppresses tumor cell viability, and enhances ferroptosis sensitivity by depleting glutathione; ferroptosis inhibitor (Ferrostatin-1) partially reverses resveratrol-induced effects, confirming mechanistic involvement of the ferroptotic pathway.⁸ Targeting Fra-2 to overcome KRASG12D inhibitor resistance represents another rational approach, as Fra-2 knockdown or mTOR pathway inhibition may synergize with KRAS-selective inhibitors to prevent adaptive transcriptional reprogramming.⁴ Modulation of [[cldn18.2|CLDN18.2]] O-GlcNAcylation patterns through upstream KRAS pathway inhibition may enhance the clinical efficacy of CLDN18.2-targeted antibodies in PDAC, addressing the regulatory mechanisms that have limited their impact relative to gastric cancer applications.¹
