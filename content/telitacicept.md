**Telitacicept** is a fusion protein therapeutic that dual-targets B-cell activating factor ([[baff|BAFF]]) and a proliferation-inducing ligand ([[april|APRIL]]), two critical TNF superfamily cytokines driving pathogenic B-cell activation in autoimmune kidney disease. The drug has demonstrated clinical efficacy in [[iga-nephropathy|IgA nephropathy (IgAN)]], a primary glomerulonephritis in which aberrant BAFF and APRIL signaling promotes IgA-dominant immune complex deposition in the glomerulus. In Phase 3 clinical trials, telitacicept monotherapy reduced proteinuria by 71.4% at six months, with combined treatment alongside [[glucocorticoids]] achieving significantly superior proteinuria reduction of 84.1%.¹

---

## Mechanism

Telitacicept functions as a soluble receptor fusion protein that simultaneously neutralizes both [[baff|BAFF]] and [[april|APRIL]], two essential ligands for B-lymphocyte survival, activation, and differentiation. In [[iga-nephropathy|IgA nephropathy]], dysregulated B-cell responses result in excess production of IgA-predominant immune complexes, which deposit in the glomerulus and trigger a destructive inflammatory cascade leading to progressive renal dysfunction. By blocking both BAFF and APRIL signaling pathways, telitacicept addresses the dual-ligand dysregulation that is central to IgAN pathogenesis.¹ This dual-targeting strategy distinguishes telitacicept from single-ligand antagonists and may account for its enhanced efficacy when combined with conventional immunosuppression.

---

## Clinical efficacy in IgA nephropathy

Telitacicept has been evaluated in Phase 3 clinical trials in untreated [[iga-nephropathy|IgA nephropathy]] patients. In a real-world analysis of 71 [[iga-nephropathy|IgAN]] patients receiving telitacicept monotherapy at 160 mg intravenous weekly, mean 24-hour proteinuria decreased by 71.4% after six months.² This substantial anti-proteinuric effect was accompanied by reductions in serum [[immunoglobulin-a|IgA]] and IgG levels, indicating suppression of the pathogenic B-cell immune response. Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), a marker of renal function, improved modestly by 8.1% in the monotherapy group, and most patients experienced resolution of hematuria, reflecting broad immunosuppressive benefit.

---

## Enhanced efficacy with glucocorticoid combination

When telitacicept was combined with [[glucocorticoids]], the anti-proteinuric effect was substantially potentiated. The telitacicept plus [[glucocorticoids]] cohort achieved a mean 24-hour proteinuria reduction of 84.1% at six months, representing an absolute 12.7 percentage point improvement compared to monotherapy (p = 0.043).² Remarkably, eGFR improvement was comparable between the combination and monotherapy groups (8.8% versus 8.1%, p = 0.91), indicating that the synergistic reduction in proteinuria with combined therapy does not confer enhanced short-term renal function recovery. This pattern suggests that [[glucocorticoids]] and telitacicept act cooperatively to accelerate proteinuria clearance, a key surrogate marker of disease control in [[iga-nephropathy|IgAN]].

---

## Biomarkers and safety

Telitacicept treatment significantly reduced urinary soluble CD163, an inflammatory biomarker of intrarenal macrophage infiltration and disease activity in [[iga-nephropathy|IgA nephropathy]].² The decline in urinary CD163 levels (−4.33 ng/mg; p = 0.001) suggests suppression of intrarenal inflammation and signals potential for durable renoprotection beyond the short-term proteinuria-lowering phase. Both monotherapy and combination therapy groups demonstrated favorable safety profiles, with no serious adverse events reported during the study period, establishing that telitacicept is well-tolerated when administered as monotherapy or in combination with [[glucocorticoids]] in [[iga-nephropathy|IgAN]] patients.
