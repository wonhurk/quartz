**Bromodomain-containing protein 4 (BRD4)** is a transcriptional coactivator that selectively recognizes acetylated histones through its bromodomain modules to regulate gene expression across multiple biological pathways.¹ The protein serves as a critical regulatory hub in both physiological contexts and disease states, with emerging applications in cancer therapy through selective protein degradation platforms and in metabolic disease via participation in chromatin-dependent gene regulation. BRD4 represents a tractable drug target, with small-molecule inhibitors and proteolysis-targeting chimeras (PROTACs) showing promising therapeutic efficacy, and its protein-ligand interactions now resolvable at near-atomic resolution through advanced single-molecule biosensing technologies.²

---

## Mechanism and function

BRD4 functions as a bromodomain-containing transcriptional coactivator that recognizes and binds acetylated lysine residues on histone proteins, thereby serving as a reader of the acetylation-based histone code.¹ This recognition enables BRD4 to integrate into transcriptional regulatory complexes that control gene expression in response to developmental and environmental signals. The protein's bromodomains are selective for acetylated [[histone-peptides]], allowing BRD4 to target specific genomic loci where transcriptional activity is required. In kidney disease pathways triggered by maternal obesity, BRD4 participates in a regulatory axis with [[p300]] and [[trb|thyroid hormone receptor β]], wherein TR-β binds to enhancer elements to promote transcription of target genes such as Ffar1 through a BRD4-P300-dependent mechanism.³

## Tumor-selective protein degradation

BRD4 has emerged as a major therapeutic target in cancer through the development of an [[hsp90|HSP90]]-dependent bioorthogonal [[hbprotac|PROTAC prodrug system]] called HBPROTAC.¹ This dual-component platform consists of [[tz-pu|Tz-PU]], a tetrazine-conjugated HSP90 inhibitor, and [[tco-mz1|TCO-caged PROTAC prodrugs]] (such as TCO-MZ1) that are activated through inverse electron demand Diels-Alder (IEDDA) reactions with Tz-PU. The system achieves tumor-selective degradation of BRD4 and other oncoproteins because Tz-PU preferentially accumulates in tumor tissues, creating a localized environment where PROTAC activation occurs. Notably, HSP90 inhibition by Tz-PU synergistically enhances BRD4 degradation efficiency through HSP90-mediated signaling pathways, yielding superior antitumor activity while minimizing off-target toxicity in normal tissues.¹ This HBPROTAC platform has been validated across multiple tumor cell lines and in vivo melanoma models, establishing a broadly applicable strategy for spatiotemporal control of targeted protein degradation.

## Single-molecule drug discovery

The interactions between BRD4 and small-molecule drugs can be resolved at near-atomic resolution using a YaxAB nanopore biosensor system.² This single-molecule sensing approach exploits the oscillating dynamics of BRD4 as it transits through a LiCl-modulated nanopore, generating two-level current transitions that encode information about ligand binding. The method achieves sensitivity sufficient to discriminate BRD4 complexes differing by as little as 2.5 Daltons in mass, enabling the detection of BRD4 binding to diverse drug molecules and [[histone-peptides|histone peptides]] with unprecedented resolution. Molecular dynamics simulations confirm that the narrow- and wide-pore regions generate distinct current signatures for ligand-bound and unbound states, permitting quantitative characterization of protein-ligand interactions at the single-molecule level. This nanopore sensing capability has implications for drug discovery, proteomics, and diagnostics, particularly in applications requiring label-free, high-resolution biomolecular interaction analysis.

## Kidney disease and metabolic dysfunction

BRD4 participates in a critical transcriptional regulatory axis that links maternal obesity to chronic kidney disease in male offspring.³ In this pathway, proximal tubule-derived 20-hydroxyeicosatetraenoic acid (20-HETE) promotes macrophage mitochondrial hyperactivity and macrophage-to-myofibroblast transition (MMT) through the G protein-coupled receptor Ffar1. Thyroid hormone (T3) acts as a transcriptional amplifier by binding to [[trb|thyroid hormone receptor β (TR-β)]], which in turn recruits [[p300]] and BRD4 to enhancer elements upstream of the Ffar1 gene, thereby promoting its transcription. The enhanced Ffar1 expression in macrophages sensitizes them to 20-HETE signaling, driving their differentiation into myofibroblasts and exacerbating kidney fibrosis. This multi-omics-defined mechanism highlights BRD4 as a critical component of the transcriptional regulatory machinery linking metabolic stress (maternal obesity) to organ pathology in offspring, and suggests therapeutic targeting of the BRD4-P300 axis as a potential intervention strategy for metabolic disease-associated kidney dysfunction.
