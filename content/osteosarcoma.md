**Osteosarcoma** is a primary malignant bone tumor characterized by unchanged survival rates for over four decades, necessitating urgent exploration of precision medicine approaches that exploit multiple tumor vulnerabilities.¹ The disease resists conventional therapy through hypoxic tumor microenvironments, robust antioxidant barriers, and metabolic reprogramming that sustains oxidative phosphorylation. Three complementary therapeutic strategies have emerged from recent preclinical research: [[sonodynamic-therapy|sonodynamic therapy]] utilizing ultrasound-responsive bone-targeting nanoliposomes that amplify reactive oxygen species generation while activating innate immune pathways; metabolic disruption via direct targeting of the [[atp5i|ATP synthase subunit e (ATP5I)]] to collapse oxidative phosphorylation; and receptor-targeted [[ac-225-macropa-peg4-if3|alpha particle radiotherapy]] against [[igf2r|insulin-like growth factor-2 receptor (IGF2R)]].

---

## Tumor microenvironment and metabolic vulnerabilities

Osteosarcoma tumors exist within a hostile physiological niche characterized by profound hypoxia, acidic extracellular pH, and elevation of the glutathione (GSH) antioxidant system — factors that collectively suppress reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation and limit therapeutic efficacy. The tumor vasculature is poorly developed due to abnormal bone remodeling, creating diffusion barriers that prevent drug penetration to osteolytic lesions. Furthermore, malignant osteoblasts reprogram their bioenergetics toward sustained oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) to fuel anabolic biosynthesis, differentiating them from many chemotherapy-sensitive tumors. This metabolic dependency presents a tractable vulnerability when disrupted at the F₁F₀-ATP synthase level.

## Sonodynamic therapy and innate immune activation

[[Sonodynamic-therapy|Sonodynamic therapy (SDT)]] harnesses low-intensity focused ultrasound to activate embedded sonosensitizers, generating ROS deep within bone lesions without systemic toxicity. The bone-targeting [[lpim|LPIM nanoliposomes]] integrate three mechanistic components: [[phytic-acid|phytic acid (PA)]] conjugation to DSPE-PEG enables targeted delivery to osteolytic regions through phosphate-mediated interactions with bone mineral matrix, while embedded manganese dioxide (MnO₂) nanoparticles simultaneously alleviate hypoxia and suppress GSH-mediated antioxidant barriers, thereby amplifying ROS generation efficiency.² Under ultrasound activation, LPIM achieves osteosarcoma targeting within 24 hours in vivo and achieves a 93.5% tumor growth inhibition rate.² Additionally, the release of Mn²⁺ ions activates the [[cgas-sting|cGAS-STING]] innate immune signaling pathway, triggering endogenous immunoamplification through pattern recognition receptor signaling.² The ROS-driven immunogenic cell death (ICD) induced by LPIM simultaneously presents damage-associated molecular patterns to resident dendritic cells and macrophages within bone lesions, converting the immunosuppressive microenvironment into an immunostimulatory niche.

## Metabolic targeting via ATP synthase disruption

[[Metformin]], a biguanide antidiabetic agent, has been repurposed for osteosarcoma through discovery of a direct interaction with [[atp5i|ATP5I]], the subunit e component of F₁F₀-ATP synthase dimers.³ ATP5I is essential for maintaining dimer stability and cristae morphology in the inner mitochondrial membrane. When ATP5I is inactivated — either by metformin binding or CRISPR-mediated knockout — the F₁F₀-ATP synthase undergoes oligomerization collapse, leading to accumulation of vestigial assembly intermediates in osteosarcoma cells.³ This disruption simultaneously inhibits oxidative phosphorylation capacity and impairs the NAD⁺/NADH ratio, forcing compensatory upregulation of glycolysis. Importantly, ATP5I expression is essential for metformin's antiproliferative activity; cells lacking ATP5I develop biguanide resistance despite maintained mitochondrial function through alternative ATP-generating pathways.³ This finding identifies ATP5I as a biomarker for therapeutic sensitivity and suggests that combinatorial approaches targeting both ATP5I-dependent OXPHOS and alternative energy pathways may overcome resistance.

## Targeted alpha particle radiotherapy

[[IGF2R]]-targeted alpha particle therapy represents a precision radionuclide approach designed to concentrate cytotoxic radiation directly within osteosarcoma tumors while sparing healthy bone and marrow. The [[ac-225-macropa-peg4-if3|[225Ac]Ac-Macropa-PEG4-IF3 radioimmunoconjugate]] combines the anti-IGF2R antibody IF3 with a Macropa-PEG4 chelator optimized for stable ²²⁵Ac coordination.¹ The radiolabeled conjugate achieves strong receptor affinity (Kd = 3.8 nM) and efficient receptor-mediated internalization in osteosarcoma cells.¹ In murine xenograft models, tumor uptake reaches 18.7%ID/g within 72 hours, with persistent retention of 15.2%ID/g at 168 hours and a tumor-to-blood ratio of 19.0 — values far exceeding those typical of conventional radiolabeled monoclonal antibodies.¹ A single 370 kBq dose causes complete tumor regression in 75% of treated mice with no evidence of hematologic toxicity, setting a new benchmark for osteosarcoma efficacy.¹ The alpha particles emitted by ²²⁵Ac (helium nuclei with extremely high linear energy transfer) induce complex DNA damage irreparable through homologous recombination, making alpha therapy effective even against chemotherapy-resistant populations.

## Therapeutic combination strategies

The distinct mechanisms of SDT-driven immunogenic cell death, metabolic ATP synthase targeting, and alpha particle cytotoxicity suggest synergistic potential when combined. LPIM therapy awakens dormant anti-tumor immunity through [[cgas-sting|STING]]-mediated interferon production, priming local immune infiltration that enhances the therapeutic window for subsequent radiation or metabolic disruption. Metformin's bioenergetic collapse forces tumors into energy-deficit states where the high-LET damage from alpha particles cannot be adequately repaired through energetically expensive DNA repair pathways. IGF2R targeting concentrates alpha particles precisely where osteosarcoma cells express maximal IGF2R density, amplifying local dose while minimizing systemic exposure. Sequential or concurrent deployment of these three modalities offers a rational path toward overcoming the historical stagnation in osteosarcoma survival and establishing durable disease control.
